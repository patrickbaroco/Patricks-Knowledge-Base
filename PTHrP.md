Evergreen concept note. PTH-related peptide in the non-PTH hypercalcemia workup. Teaching points cited per episode.

## What it points to

- **An elevated PTHrP points to a solid tumor** — squamous cell carcinoma (anywhere), renal cell, and breast are the classics — as the mechanism of [[Hypercalcemia of Malignancy|humoral hypercalcemia of malignancy]]. It sits on the non-PTH arm of the [[Hypercalcemia|hypercalcemia]] schema, reached once [[Parathyroid Hormone|PTH]] is suppressed. ([[RLR 007 - Hypercalcemia|RLR 007]], [[RLR 014 - Hypercalcemia and Immobilization|RLR 014]])

## Interpreting it (a Bayesian lead, not a verdict)

- **Weigh its positive predictive value against contradicting data.** PTHrP can be *falsely elevated in renal failure*, so a negative [[PET-CT|PET]], a stable weight, and a chronic course argue hard against occult cancer even with a positive result — hold it as *possible* while weighing its [[Positive Predictive Value|PPV]]. ([[RLR 007 - Hypercalcemia|RLR 007]])
- **Its performance is poor in kidney dysfunction**, a limitation to state explicitly before leaning on it. ([[RLR 014 - Hypercalcemia and Immobilization|RLR 014]], [[RLR 028 - Hypercalcemia and Vitamin D Toxicity|RLR 028]])
- **[[Hypercalcemia of Malignancy|Hypercalcemia of malignancy]] is usually an end-stage event** (median survival ~6 months), so a PET-negative, weight-stable, years-long hypercalcemia fits the malignancy [[Illness Script|illness script]] poorly. ([[RLR 007 - Hypercalcemia|RLR 007]])

## Its cousin: pathologic 1,25 conversion

- PTHrP is one way to raise the active vitamin D pathway; the other is a granuloma pathologically converting 25- to 1,25-vitamin D. Both are "conversion" states in which the [[25-Hydroxyvitamin D|25-hydroxy]] level runs *low* — the discriminator from a 25-hydroxy ingestion, where both are high (see [[Vitamin D Metabolism|vitamin D metabolism]] and [[Test Interpretation]]). ([[RLR 028 - Hypercalcemia and Vitamin D Toxicity|RLR 028]])
