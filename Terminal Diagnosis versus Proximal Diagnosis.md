## Definition

A proximal diagnosis explains the symptom. A terminal diagnosis explains the proximal diagnosis. The distinction matters because descriptive findings are easy to mistake for answers: "bilateral hydronephrosis" and "ureteral stricture" both sound like diagnoses and neither one is, because each still demands a *why*.

The reasoning task is to keep asking that question until the answer stops generating a new one. The chain ends when you reach something that explains itself, or something irreducible. Stopping early feels like closure and is usually [[Premature Closure]] wearing a radiology report.

## How it operates

- **Descriptive labels masquerade as endpoints.** A word that names a finding's shape rather than its cause will pass for a diagnosis if nobody presses it. "Stricture" describes a narrowing; it says nothing about what narrowed it.
- **Each answer becomes the next proximal finding.** The framework recurs within a single case. Solving one layer does not exit the chain, it advances one link along it.
- **Base rates flag the premature stop.** When the usual causes of a proximal finding do not fit the patient, that mismatch is the signal to keep going rather than to accept the label.
- **It is the engine behind unification.** Pushing every proximal finding toward its cause is often what reveals that several apparently separate problems share one, which is where this meets [[Unifying Diagnosis]].

## Examples from RLR episodes

- **Where the framework was named.** Reza refuses "bilateral hydronephrosis" and "bilateral ureteral strictures" as terminal, applying a luminal / extraluminal / infiltrative framework and questioning whether "stricture" is even the right word for a bilateral, stone-free, UTI-free presentation. The chain ends at IgG4-related disease, which unifies the ureteral strictures with chylous ascites and a calcified pericardium. The hosts note they have invoked this distinction many times. ([[RLR 137 - Bloody Ascites and a Calcified Pericardium- IgG4-Related Disease Uniting Ureteral Strictures, Chylous Ascites, and Constrictive Pericarditis|RLR 137]])
- **Applied twice inside one case.** Abdominal pain resolves to a clot, and Reza immediately reframes the clot as proximal rather than terminal. The two commonest causes of mesenteric venous thrombosis, cirrhosis-driven hypercoagulability and intra-abdominal inflammation, both fail to fit. *Fusobacterium* pylephlebitis is the next link, still not the last: the chain ends at an underlying colon cancer. ([[RLR 139 - Peri-Umbilical Pain, a Missed Clot, and Fusobacterium- Portomesenteric Thrombosis Unmasking Colon Cancer|RLR 139]])
- **"Idiopathic" as a proximal finding in disguise.** Recurrent small bowel intussusception carried the label idiopathic across repeat presentations. The intussusception was the proximal finding and the recurrence was the clue that something was driving it; the terminal answer was cannabinoid hyperemesis syndrome. ([[RLR 143 - Recurrent Idiopathic Small Bowel Intussusception Traced to Cannabinoid Hyperemesis Syndrome|RLR 143]])
