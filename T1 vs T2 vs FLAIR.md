Reading MRI brain sequences at a glance.

## How it operates

- **T1** shows anatomy — gray matter appears darker than white matter.
- **T2** is the visual "negative" of T1 — CSF is bright, and white matter appears darker than gray matter.
- **FLAIR** is a T2 sequence with the CSF signal subtracted out, making periventricular or juxtacortical lesions easier to see, since they might otherwise be obscured by adjacent bright CSF on standard T2.
- **Choose by location.** FLAIR is generally preferred supratentorially; T2 can be more useful in the brainstem and posterior fossa, where FLAIR's CSF suppression can create artifact.

## Examples from RLR episodes

- **FLAIR catches what plain T2 might obscure.** A patchy right temporal juxtacortical white matter lesion, ultimately found to be primary CNS vasculitis, is characterized on FLAIR sequences that make its subcortical, non-enhancing character clear — a sequence choice discussed explicitly as part of reading the imaging "like a radiologist" before layering on the clinical story.
