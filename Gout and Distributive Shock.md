An unusual manifestation in which a massive tophaceous gout burden drives a systemic inflammatory, vasodilatory shock that resolves with corticosteroids.

## The Phenomenon
A very large **tophaceous [[Gout|gout]] burden** can generate enough systemic inflammation to produce a **distributive (vasodilatory) shock** — hypotension with warm, well-perfused physiology and low systemic vascular resistance — that **resolves with corticosteroids (e.g., prednisone)** rather than antibiotics or pressors alone ([[RLR 062 - Oligoarthritis and Polyarticular Gout Flare|RLR 062]]).

- It is a **rare, striking manifestation** offered as a coda in RLR 062 to make the point that "gout can do anything" ([[RLR 062 - Oligoarthritis and Polyarticular Gout Flare|RLR 062]]).

## Why It Fits the Shock Schema
- The patient is hypotensive but has **no thoracic signature**, placing it on the **systemic** side of the systemic-vs-thoracic fork and within the **[[Distributive Shock|distributive]]** bucket — see [[Shock]] ([[RLR 062 - Oligoarthritis and Polyarticular Gout Flare|RLR 062]]).
- The mechanism is massive crystal-driven inflammatory cytokine release causing vasodilation, mirroring the physiology of septic/inflammatory distributive shock.

## Clinical Reasoning Value
- It is a reminder that a crystal arthropathy is a **systemic inflammatory disease**, not only a joint problem.
- Diagnostically it is largely one of **exclusion and treatment response** — sepsis and other distributive causes must be worked up first, and the resolution with steroids acts as [[Treatment as a Diagnostic Test|treatment-as-a-test]] ([[RLR 062 - Oligoarthritis and Polyarticular Gout Flare|RLR 062]]).

## Management
- Corticosteroids to quell the systemic flare, alongside supportive resuscitation; cover [[Septic Arthritis|infection]] empirically until it is excluded, since steroids are safe with concurrent antibiotics ([[RLR 062 - Oligoarthritis and Polyarticular Gout Flare|RLR 062]]).
