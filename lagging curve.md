# Lagging Curve

A slack-leader casting technique for dry flies (and a factor in leader design more broadly): instead of driving the cast to full turnover, the arm moves laterally so the leader lands with a deliberate curve rather than a straight line. The fly reaches the water first; the tippet and leader lay out in a curve upstream or to the side, giving the current something to remove gradually instead of creating instant drag. Also called a **candy-cane cast** for the shape it leaves on the water.

Promoted to its own note per the vault's concept-promotion rule (see [[_Fishing Notes Convention]]) after being referenced by two independent sources:

- [[Dry Flies in Fast Water - Fly Fishing Tips]] — pairs the lagging curve with a **crash cast** to force the curve down onto fast water quickly, so the leader and fly land in near-unison rather than settling gradually.
- [[Fishing Angles - Upstream or Downstream]] — frames it as the default up-and-across dry-fly presentation, protecting the drift while introducing slack that has to be managed at the hook set.

## Related techniques

- [[Leader Formulas]] — the **General Dry Fly** and **Ultra-Delicate Dry Fly** tapers are built to support this kind of slack, gradual-turnover leader; a lagging curve is hard to produce with a leader designed for full turnover instead.
