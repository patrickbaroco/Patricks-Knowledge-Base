Bilateral pyelonephritis and its atypical, sometimes localizing-symptom-free presentation.

## How it operates

- **A meaningful minority present without localizing symptoms.** Bilateral pyelonephritis can occur without flank pain or dysuria in roughly 15% of cases (per the discussant's estimate), presenting instead as an unexplained fever.
- **Imaging, not symptoms, often secures the diagnosis** when the classic urinary complaints are absent — CT abdomen/pelvis is frequently what closes the case.
- **Pyuria's significance is population-dependent** — see [[Test Interpretation]] and the age-based urinalysis discussion below; a finding that is "noise" in one population can be the diagnosis in another.

## Examples from RLR episodes

- **Fever without localizing symptoms, closed by imaging.** A young woman with five nonspecific days of fever, myalgias, nausea, and vomiting — no dysuria, no flank pain — has asymptomatic pyuria with nitrites; CT abdomen/pelvis confirms bilateral pyelonephritis, and urine and blood cultures grow Klebsiella pneumoniae, revealing bacteremic disease behind an initially nonspecific presentation.
