The spillover phenomenon: when the sampled fluid is *adjacent* to the inflammation rather than *at* it, the tap comes back deceptively bland — so a quiet sample next to a raging structure points you to the neighbor, not away from disease.

## The principle

Body cavities and their fluids report faithfully only on what is happening *inside* them. When a disease process sits in a structure *next to* a fluid compartment, only a fraction of its inflammation "spills over" into the fluid you can reach with a needle — so the sampled fluid is mildly inflamed or normal even though severe disease is a centimeter away. The corollary for reasoning: a **bland tap does not exclude severe adjacent pathology**, and a striking mismatch between how sick the structure looks and how quiet the fluid reads should redirect you to the neighboring compartment.

## The canonical analogy — the brain abscess LP

A brain abscess characteristically produces a **near-normal lumbar puncture** (a few white cells, mildly elevated protein) because the pus is walled off in the parenchyma, not free in the CSF. Reasoning from "the LP is bland, so there's no serious CNS infection" is exactly the trap the principle warns against. ([[RLR 061 - Knee Swelling and Calcific Tendinitis|RLR 061]])

## Examples across compartments

- **Joint vs tendon.** Severe [[Calcific Tendinitis|calcific tendinitis]] of the quadriceps tendon spilled only a little inflammation into the synovium, so a knee tap showed a *non-inflammatory* cell count (a few hundred white cells) with incidental [[CPPD|CPPD]] crystals — while the tendon next door was the true, raging disease. The disproportion (plus a dry, hard-to-aspirate tap) was the tell that the problem was **periarticular**, not intra-articular. ([[RLR 061 - Knee Swelling and Calcific Tendinitis|RLR 061]])
- **Bone/marrow vs aspirate.** A "dry tap" on bone-marrow aspiration in the setting of marrow fibrosis is a parallel — the needle can't sample what has been replaced, so the yield misrepresents the pathology. ([[RLR 061 - Knee Swelling and Calcific Tendinitis|RLR 061]])
- **Pleura/pericardium.** Inflammation of adjacent structures (pneumonia beside the pleura) can produce a reactive, non-diagnostic effusion — the fluid reflects proximity, not the primary process.

## How to use it

When a tap is **discordant** with the clinical picture — the joint/space looks severely inflamed but the fluid is bland, or the fluid is hard to obtain where a real effusion should be easy — resist attributing it to sampling error or to "mild disease." Instead ask *what sits next to this compartment*, and image the neighbor (MRI, cross-sectional imaging). The [[Law of Proportionality|law of proportionality]] is the trigger; the spillover principle supplies the redirection.
