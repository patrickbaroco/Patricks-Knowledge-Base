Evergreen concept note. Immobilization hypercalcemia — one of the "two I's" on the [[Hypercalcemia|hypercalcemia]] schema. Teaching points cited per episode.

## The illness script

- **A diagnosis of exclusion marked by spontaneous resolution and no other lab abnormality.** Uncoupled bone resorption during immobility releases calcium; when the patient re-mobilizes (or is simply hydrated) the calcium normalizes on its own — spontaneous resolution *defines* it. ([[RLR 014 - Hypercalcemia and Immobilization|RLR 014]])
- **Notoriously severe, hard to predict, and relapsing-remitting.** It can be dramatic and can wax and wane, which is why it belongs on the schema even when the calcium is very high. ([[RLR 028 - Hypercalcemia and Vitamin D Toxicity|RLR 028]])

## When to think of it

- **Prioritize the "two I's" — ingestion and immobilization — for silent, indolent hypercalcemia in an otherwise well patient.** Malignancy and granulomatous disease usually announce themselves; the patient "minding his own business" is more likely milk-alkali or immobilization. It is under-weighted in chronically bedbound, contracted, demented patients where its pretest probability is actually high. ([[RLR 007 - Hypercalcemia|RLR 007]])
- **In adults it is uncommon as the *sole* cause** — investigate for a second contributing bone process (e.g., Paget) before attributing hypercalcemia entirely to immobility; an elevated bone-specific alkaline phosphatase can be the clue. ([[RLR 007 - Hypercalcemia|RLR 007]])
- **An atypical trigger can hide the textbook mechanism** — sequential bilateral cataract surgery (functional blindness → two couch-bound weeks) produced pure adult immobilization hypercalcemia, missed via a [[Representativeness Heuristic|representativeness]] trap; a long flight can immobilize too. ([[RLR 014 - Hypercalcemia and Immobilization|RLR 014]], [[RLR 028 - Hypercalcemia and Vitamin D Toxicity|RLR 028]])
