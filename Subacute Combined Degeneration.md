Vitamin B12 (cobalamin) deficiency myelopathy — degeneration of the dorsal columns and corticospinal tracts, often with a coexisting peripheral neuropathy.

## Definition and Pathophysiology
Subacute combined degeneration (SCD) is the neurologic consequence of **B12 deficiency** (and, similarly, copper deficiency or nitrous-oxide toxicity): impaired myelin maintenance produces **combined** demyelination of the **dorsal columns** and **lateral corticospinal tracts**, plus a **peripheral neuropathy** ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).

- **"Combined"** refers to simultaneous involvement of sensory (dorsal column) and motor (corticospinal) tracts within the cord.
- **Causes of B12 deficiency:** pernicious anemia, malabsorption, gastric/ileal surgery, strict vegan diet, and metformin.

## Clinical Features
- **Dorsal column loss first:** **vibration and proprioception** deficits, distal paresthesias, a positive Romberg, and a sensory (ataxic) gait — vibration/proprioception testing is exactly the exam step not to skip ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).
- **Corticospinal signs:** spastic weakness, hyperreflexia, and extensor plantar responses; a mixed picture with the peripheral neuropathy can blunt reflexes.
- **What it spares:** SCD **usually spares bowel/bladder and the face** — features that, when present, argue for a different or additional process (as in leptomeningeal or cauda-equina disease) ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).
- May accompany cognitive change and, hematologically, macrocytic anemia — though **neurologic disease can precede or occur without anemia**.

## The Neuropathy Is Length-Dependent
- **B12 neuropathy follows the length principle** — feet before hands (see [[Length-Dependent vs Non-Length-Dependent Neuropathy]]); a **non-length-dependent** pattern (hands and feet together) argues against pure B12 and toward a demyelinating or central cause ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).

## Diagnosis
- **Serum B12**, with **methylmalonic acid and homocysteine** (both elevated) to catch low-normal B12; check copper if B12 is normal but the picture fits.
- **MRI** may show **dorsal-column T2 hyperintensity** (an "inverted V"/"rabbit ears" sign) in the cervical/thoracic cord.
- Evaluate the cause (anti-intrinsic-factor/parietal-cell antibodies for pernicious anemia).

## Management
- **B12 repletion** (parenteral, then maintenance) — earlier treatment yields better recovery; some deficits may be permanent if long-standing.
- Treat the underlying cause and monitor the hematologic response.
