Evergreen concept note. "When to worry" — the gestalt that separates a benign version of a common complaint from a dangerous one. Common symptoms (back pain, numbness, headache, fatigue) are mostly benign; a small set of accompanying features flips them into red-flag territory.

## The idea

Most patients with a common complaint have a common, benign cause. The clinical skill is not to work every one up exhaustively but to recognize the **features that should raise alarm** — and then escalate. The dominant red flag is often **tempo** (a rapidly progressive course), reinforced by **constitutional signs** (weight loss, cachexia, fever) and an **objective anchor** (an imaging lesion, a hard exam finding, an abnormal lab). When several stack, the prior for serious disease rises sharply.

## The recurring red flags

- **Tempo out of keeping with a benign process** — rapid progression, or a course that keeps escalating. Read the pace from the physiology, not only the history. ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]])
- **Constitutional features** — significant weight loss, cachexia, night sweats, unexplained fever.
- **An objective anchor** — a mass or lesion on imaging, a focal neurologic deficit, a cytopenia, a markedly abnormal inflammatory marker.
- **Age and background** — older age, immunocompromise, cancer history, or anticoagulation ([[Background Affects Foreground|background reframes the foreground]]).

## Worked example (RLR 059)

Numbness, tingling, and back pain are mundane — but in an 82-year-old with a **rapidly progressive** course, **cachectic weight loss**, and a **lung nodule**, the same complaints become a "when to worry" pattern that reframes the workup toward malignancy (metastatic or [[Paraneoplastic Neurologic Syndrome|paraneoplastic]]). The lesson the hosts drew: teach learners *when to worry*, not to fear every symptom. ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]])

## How to use it

Treat the red flags as a checklist layered over the common-things-common default: if none are present, a measured/benign approach is reasonable; if one or more are present, escalate imaging and workup and shorten the leash. The absence of red flags is reassuring; their presence is a call to act.
