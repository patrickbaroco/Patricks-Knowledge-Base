Evergreen concept note. Definition plus worked examples, drawn from the RLR / Clinical Problem Solvers series and elsewhere. Source episodes are cited per example.

## Definition

A cognitive autopsy is a structured retrospective of one's own reasoning on a case — after the answer is known, going back through the workup to name which clues were rationalized away, which schema branch was missing, and which single datum actually did the diagnostic work. It is the CP Solvers' signature closing discipline (popularized with Gurpreet Dhaliwal): the point is not to celebrate the hit but to dissect the *delay to* the hit, so the same reasoning arrives faster next time.

It differs from a morbidity-and-mortality review in three ways: it is blame-free, it is run on *successes* as often as misses, and it targets the reasoning process rather than the outcome.

## How it operates

- **It treats the miss, not just the hit.** The object of study is the gap between when the answer *could* have been reached and when it *was* — the rationalized-away lab, the muted cue, the branch not on the schema.
- **It names the failure type.** Most autopsies land on a recognizable cousin: an [[Anchoring Bias|anchor]], a [[Framing Effect|framing]] slip, [[Diagnostic Momentum|momentum]], a [[Search Satisficing|satisficed]] search, or a plain [[Illness Script|schema gap]].
- **It converts reflection into a schema edit.** The deliverable is a concrete change — add hereditary ATTR to the polyneuropathy list — not a resolution to "be more careful."
- **It can run in real time.** Several hosts narrate the autopsy *mid-case* ("the heart should have been prioritized once the effusion appeared"), turning metacognition into a live instrument.

## Examples from RLR episodes

- **The case framed explicitly as an autopsy.** Bob Centor presents the whole case as a self-audit of *why the CT was delayed* in a nausea/vomiting/hyponatremia patient who turned out to have NSAID gastric ulcers — modeling non-punitive review of a delay that caused no harm. ([[RLR 002 - Nausea, Vomiting, and Hyponatremia|RLR 002]])
- **Don't blame the abnormal number too early.** Reza's autopsy draws the hypoglycemia analogy — a mildly abnormal value gets over-blamed for a presentation; bradycardia earned the blame only because it was unphysiologically severe. ([[RLR 006 - Bradycardia and Urinary Retention|RLR 006]])
- **The whiteboard re-examination.** After landing on lupus nephritis, the team revisits every clue it had soothed away — the losartan, the 3+ protein, the five-year rule — to see how each had been explained off. ([[RLR 008 - Dyspnea|RLR 008]])
- **The autopsy that edits the schema.** The miss is diagnosed as a *schema gap* — hereditary ATTR was absent from the polyneuropathy list — and the fix is to add it so the next such patient is tested up front. ([[RLR 009 - Progressive Sensorimotor Polyneuropathy|RLR 009]])
- **A framing slip surfaced in hindsight.** Encoding the complaint as *nausea* rather than *vomiting* muted the mechanical cue that would have raised Boerhaave — visible only on the retrospective pass. ([[RLR 012 - Dysuria and Esophageal Perforation|RLR 012]])
- **Real-time autopsy and the pre-articulate alarm.** Reza narrates mid-case that the heart should have led once the effusion and edema appeared; Rabih valorizes sensing "something is wrong" before it can be named. ([[RLR 013 - Right Upper Quadrant Pain and Heart Failure|RLR 013]])
- **Distributed expertise.** The closing reframe credits the neurosurgeon's off-the-MRI read — a reminder that whoever reads a given film most often may outperform the nominal expert. ([[RLR 016 - Neck Pain and Crowned Dens Syndrome|RLR 016]])
- **Two named misses without defensiveness.** Reza accounts for never integrating an isolated LDH of 550 and never entertaining a congenital cancer-predisposition syndrome. ([[RLR 018 - Gross Hematuria in End-Stage Renal Disease|RLR 018]])
- **The non-cognitive contributors.** Rabih extends the autopsy past cognition to advocacy, gender, and immigration status — which patients get their abnormal labs pursued. ([[RLR 021 - Chronic Pruritus and Primary Biliary Cholangitis|RLR 021]])
- **Accounting for the clinching datum.** Reza recognizes he was "one step short," and that the piece that finally clinched confidence was the dramatic improvement on drug withdrawal. ([[RLR 024 - Confusion and Ertapenem-Induced Encephalopathy|RLR 024]])
- **The autopsy on an unsolved case.** A rigorous workup can end without a diagnosis; the hosts note these cases "never get told," making the retrospective itself the teaching. ([[RLR 010 - Postprandial Pain|RLR 010]])
