Evergreen concept note. Definition plus worked examples, drawn from the RLR / Clinical Problem Solvers series and elsewhere. Source episodes are cited per example.

## Definition

"Return to the bedside" is the move of going back to the patient — re-taking the history, re-examining, or heeding a bedside observer — when the workup stalls or the data don't cohere. It rests on a recurring RLR observation: the answer often lives in a history no one took, an exam no one repeated, or a nurse's concern no one acted on, not in another test. The intake encounter is a first pass, not the final word; when you have no anchor, the highest-yield next step is frequently a person, not a scan.

## How it operates

- **The first history is a draft.** Re-interviewing — especially with better collateral or an interpreter — can reweight the whole case even when it yields no strictly new fact; emphasis shifts, and a bracketed symptom becomes the center of gravity.
- **History precedes workup.** A workup assembled entirely off the chart, before anyone asks the patient how they are functioning, can miss the one question that resolves the case.
- **The exam is repeatable — and expands.** A second, more thorough exam (a full-body skin exam, reflexes in an un-examinable patient) uncovers what the first missed.
- **Trust the bedside observer.** Nurses spend far more time at the bedside and are often steps ahead; missed nursing concern is itself a route to [[Diagnostic Error|diagnostic error]].
- **When anchorless, go see the patient.** "I don't know what to focus on" is the specific trigger — the bedside is where you re-derive an anchor.

## Examples from RLR episodes

- **The diagnosis in an untaken history.** The entire hypercalcemia workup was built off the chart and an illegible "green sheet" before anyone asked the man how he was functioning; the single question ("what have you been doing?") — revealing two weeks immobile after cataract surgery — resolved it. ([[RLR 014 - Hypercalcemia and Immobilization|RLR 014]])
- **The bedside observer rescues the case.** A first-day nurse, Bella, flags that the patient is suddenly febrile, confused, and "not who he was thirty minutes ago"; Reza applauds the instinct and names missed nursing concern as a genuine route to diagnostic delay — the fever recasts the case toward ascending cholangitis. ([[RLR 020 - Chest Pain and Ascending Cholangitis|RLR 020]])
- **The exam that found the answer on the surface.** Will's full-body skin exam — after thousands of dollars of imaging — finds the nodular rash that biopsies to leukemia cutis and unlocks the CMML. ([[RLR 032 - Fever and Chronic Myelomonocytic Leukemia|RLR 032]])
- **Re-history reframes, without new data.** Stuck with "no anchor other than altered mental status," Will goes back and re-interviews the husband; nothing is strictly new, but reweighting around progressive back pain and new leg weakness surfaces the cord syndrome hiding under the encephalopathy. ([[RLR 033 - Persistent Altered Mental Status and Spinal Epidural Abscess|RLR 033]])
