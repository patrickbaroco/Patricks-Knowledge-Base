Evergreen concept note. Lyme carditis is Borrelia-mediated cardiac involvement that characteristically causes reversible high-grade AV block — the U.S. "champion" of infectious conduction disease. RLR / Clinical Problem Solvers teaching points are cited per episode; general clinical detail is scaffolding.

## Concept and Pathophysiology
Disseminated infection with **Borrelia burgdorferi** (from an Ixodes tick bite) can invade the myocardium, with a striking tropism for the **conduction system**. Inflammation at the AV node produces block that is typically **reversible** with treatment.

- **The U.S. "world champion" of infectious conduction disease** — the leading infectious cause of high-grade AV block to keep on the list, especially in endemic regions and warmer months ([[RLR 060 - Transient Loss of Consciousness and Cardiac Sarcoidosis|RLR 060]]).
- Occurs in **early disseminated** Lyme, weeks after the bite; a preceding erythema migrans rash and tick exposure support the diagnosis but are often absent or unrecalled.

## Presentation
- **Fluctuating, often high-grade or complete [[AV Block]]** — the degree of block can change rapidly, which matters for monitoring.
- Lightheadedness, syncope ([[Transient Loss of Consciousness]]), dyspnea, palpitations; less often myopericarditis with chest pain.
- Fits the **AV-block + fever** frame that also raises **[[Endocarditis]]** (perivalvular abscess) and rheumatic fever as the not-to-miss and differential ([[RLR 060 - Transient Loss of Consciousness and Cardiac Sarcoidosis|RLR 060]]).

## Diagnosis
- **Two-tier serology** (screening EIA followed by confirmatory immunoblot) in a patient with compatible exposure/epidemiology and conduction disease.
- EKG and continuous telemetry to characterize and track the block.
- Consider it in any young or otherwise-healthy patient with unexplained high-grade block, where the payoff of a reversible cause is high.

## Management
- **IV antibiotics** (e.g., ceftriaxone) for high-grade block or symptomatic carditis; oral doxycycline for milder disease.
- **Temporary pacing** (transcutaneous/transvenous) bridges the reversible block — a **permanent pacemaker is usually unnecessary** because conduction recovers with therapy ([[RLR 060 - Transient Loss of Consciousness and Cardiac Sarcoidosis|RLR 060]]).
- Telemetry monitoring until the block resolves.
