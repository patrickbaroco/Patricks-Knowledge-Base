Evergreen concept note. The Watchman procedure is percutaneous left atrial appendage occlusion for stroke prevention in atrial fibrillation when long-term oral anticoagulation is contraindicated. RLR / Clinical Problem Solvers teaching points are cited per episode; general clinical detail is scaffolding.

## Rationale
In non-valvular atrial fibrillation, the great majority of thrombi form in the **left atrial appendage (LAA)**. Occluding that pouch mechanically removes the dominant source of cardioembolic stroke, offering an alternative for patients who cannot tolerate anticoagulation.

- **Mechanical alternative to a drug.** Just as a **temporary IVC filter** substitutes for anticoagulation in a patient who cannot be anticoagulated for DVT/PE, LAA occlusion substitutes for anticoagulation in AFib — a useful mental analogy ([[RLR 058 - Inguinal Bulge and Rectus Sheath Hematoma|RLR 058]]).
- **Durable, not temporary.** Unlike a retrievable filter, the device is a permanent implant endothelializing over weeks to months.

## Indications and Patient Selection
- **Non-valvular AFib** with an elevated stroke risk by **[[CHA2DS2-VASc]]** who have a **contraindication to, or intolerance of, long-term anticoagulation** — classically a serious or recurrent bleed. In the episode, a Watchman is floated for a patient whose rectus sheath hematoma may preclude re-anticoagulation ([[RLR 058 - Inguinal Bulge and Rectus Sheath Hematoma|RLR 058]]).
- Weigh the same competing risks that govern anticoagulation itself: stroke risk (CHA2DS2-VASc, which climbs with age) against bleeding risk. See [[Anticoagulation Reversal]].
- Cardiac sarcoidosis and other structural disease can produce AFib and, more broadly, complex device decisions ([[RLR 060 - Transient Loss of Consciousness and Cardiac Sarcoidosis|RLR 060]]).

## Procedure and Peri-Procedural Course
- **Transvenous, transseptal** delivery of a self-expanding occluder into the LAA ostium under TEE/fluoroscopic guidance.
- Pre-procedure imaging (TEE or cardiac CT) confirms LAA anatomy and excludes a pre-existing appendage thrombus.
- A defined **post-implant antithrombotic bridge** (short-term anticoagulation or dual antiplatelet therapy, per protocol) covers the endothelialization window, then typically steps down to aspirin.

## Limitations
- Addresses **appendage** thrombus only — not atrial thrombus from other sites or non-cardioembolic stroke mechanisms.
- Procedural risks: pericardial effusion/tamponade, device embolization, and peri-device leak.
- Does not treat the arrhythmia itself (rate/rhythm control and symptom management remain separate).
