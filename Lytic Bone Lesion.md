Evergreen concept note. Working up a lytic bone lesion. Teaching points cited per episode.

## The forks

- **First fork: primary bone process vs systemic disease.** Systemic = metastasis, [[Multiple Myeloma|myeloma]], infection (pyogenic or [[Tuberculosis|TB]]), or autoimmune (sarcoid, crystalline). A *solitary* lesion in a well-appearing patient with no systemic features points to a **primary** bone process. ([[RLR 031 - Headache and Gorham-Stout Disease|RLR 031]])
- **Second fork (primary): benign vs malignant.** Benign — [[Paget Disease of Bone|Paget]], bone island/cyst, [[Fibrous Dysplasia|fibrous dysplasia]]/McCune-Albright. Malignant — osteosarcoma, Ewing, osteoblastoma. ([[RLR 031 - Headache and Gorham-Stout Disease|RLR 031]])

## Pruning the systemic arm

- **Normal SPEP/serum free light chains, PSA, and alkaline phosphatase, plus a solitary lesion on PET, prune the systemic causes** (no secretory [[Multiple Myeloma|myeloma]], no metastatic prostate, argues against Paget). ([[RLR 031 - Headache and Gorham-Stout Disease|RLR 031]])
- **FDG-avidity is not synonymous with malignancy.** Hypermetabolism reflects cellular glucose uptake — a benign lesion densely replaced by proliferating vessels/lymphatics (e.g. [[Gorham-Stout Disease|Gorham-Stout]]) can be intensely avid; let overall wellness weigh against malignancy. ([[RLR 031 - Headache and Gorham-Stout Disease|RLR 031]])
- **Biopsy is the arbiter, and radiology is a partner** — advanced imaging characterization plus tissue settle the benign/malignant question when clinical data cannot. ([[RLR 031 - Headache and Gorham-Stout Disease|RLR 031]])
