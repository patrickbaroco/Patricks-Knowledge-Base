Chronic occupational silica exposure as a driver of both lung disease and autoimmune vasculitis.

## How it operates

- **Mechanism.** Silicosis impairs macrophage function, raising tuberculosis risk, and is an established trigger for ANCA-associated vasculitis; documented spikes in regional ANCA vasculitis incidence have followed earthquakes that aerosolize ambient silica, supporting a causal, dose-related relationship between exposure and autoimmune disease.
- **Radiographic clue.** Eggshell calcification of mediastinal or hilar lymph nodes is a recognized sign of silicosis and can be the finding that prompts a diagnostic biopsy.
- **Ask about occupation explicitly.** Construction, sandblasting, mining, and other silica-dust-generating work are the relevant exposure history — easy to omit unless asked directly.
- **Management requires both immunosuppression and source control.** Standard vasculitis immunosuppression (e.g., rituximab) treats the autoimmune process, but durable improvement also requires removing or reducing ongoing occupational exposure.

## Examples from RLR episodes

- **An unelegant serology solved by an occupational history.** Simultaneous strong cANCA/PR3 and pANCA/MPO positivity — an atypical, "inelegant" pattern — prompts a search for a trigger rather than a forced overlap-syndrome label; eggshell mediastinal lymph node calcifications and a transbronchial biopsy showing massive silicosis reveal chronic construction-related silica exposure as the driver of the ANCA-associated pulmonary-renal vasculitis.
