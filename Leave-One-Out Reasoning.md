Evergreen concept note. Leave-one-out reasoning: when a set of findings won't localize or unify, provisionally *remove one* and ask whether the rest cohere — a disciplined way to reason under noisy, conflicting data.

## The move

Complex presentations often contain one finding that refuses to fit any single explanation. Rather than force a bad unifier or freeze, **temporarily set one finding aside** and test whether the remainder points cleanly somewhere. Then repeat with a different finding. Each pass yields a conditional ("*if* the bowel/bladder is real, we're in the cord; *if* the face is real, we're not in the cord alone"), and the pattern of conditionals reveals which findings are load-bearing and which may be soft, over-reported, or a second process. The essential discipline is to **name what you are discarding and why**, so a provisional exclusion never hardens silently into a fact.

## Worked example (RLR 059)

Facing an 82-year-old with weakness, numbness, back pain, bowel/bladder changes, and facial numbness — across three disagreeing exams — Aaron Berkowitz reasoned: if the **urinary retention** is real, the process is in the cord/roots and a peripheral neuropathy can't explain it; if the **facial numbness** is real, it can't be the cord alone (the trigeminal is at brainstem/meninges). Holding each finding in and out in turn kept the localization honest until imaging showed a *dual* process (compressive cauda equina **plus** leptomeningeal disease) that a single forced unifier would have missed. ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]])

## Why it works

- **It exposes soft data.** Sensory symptoms, reported (not examined) findings, and bowel/bladder changes in a patient who can't walk are notoriously unreliable; leave-one-out makes their weight explicit rather than letting them silently drive the localization.
- **It preserves [[Hickam's Dictum|two-process]] answers.** By revealing which findings *can't* be reconciled under one lesion, it flags when the truth is dual pathology rather than a single elegant diagnosis.
- **It is transparent.** The conditionals are shareable and falsifiable, which is why it doubles as a teaching method.

## Cautions

Leave-one-out is a scaffold for thinking, not permission to ignore an inconvenient finding. The discarded datum must be *revisited* — often it is the very clue (the face, here) that forces the correct, more complex answer. Pair it with [[Diagnostic Humility|humility]] about exam reliability and a plan to [[Return to the Bedside|re-examine]].
