## Definition

Treatment as a diagnostic test is reading a patient's *response to therapy* as evidence about the diagnosis — the classic **diagnosis ex juvantibus** ("from that which helps"). A response that is faster or more complete than the presumed disease could plausibly produce becomes retrospective confirmation of a different mechanism; conversely, a pre-committed plan to broaden if empiric treatment fails turns the therapeutic trial itself into an ongoing test. Imaging and empiric drugs behave the same way — "soft tests" that shift post-test probability rather than settle it.

## How it operates

- **Speed and completeness are the signal.** Resolution faster than the named disease could manage (transaminases clearing in a day; pain gone in an hour) points elsewhere.
- **A bolus is both therapy and test.** Normalizing vitals with 500 cc converts "why syncope?" into "why hypovolemic?"
- **Dechallenge is powerful but confoundable.** Improvement after removing a drug is a strong clue, not proof — reckon with hydration, regression to baseline, or a home exposure simply no longer accessible in hospital.
- **Empiric therapy and imaging are soft tests.** Response to empiric antibiotics adjusts probability; a poorly timed or incomplete study (gadolinium MRI, non-contrast "stone" CT) can falsely reassure, so a therapeutic response is used to keep the differential honest.

## Examples from RLR episodes

- **Diuresis as confirmation.** Complete resolution of pain, transaminases, and bilirubin after 3 L of diuresis in 24 hours is faster than treated cholecystitis could resolve — confirming a congested state. ([[RLR 013 - Right Upper Quadrant Pain and Heart Failure|RLR 013]])
- **One dose of ketorolac.** Near-complete relief within an hour of IV ketorolac is faster than fracture, infection, or malignancy could resolve — retrospectively confirming a crystalline process. ([[RLR 016 - Neck Pain and Crowned Dens Syndrome|RLR 016]])
- **Soft tests, not proofs.** Response to empiric antibiotics/antimalarials adjusts probability, and a poorly timed gadolinium MRI can miss an epidural abscess — refusing to let therapy or imaging falsely exclude infection. ([[RLR 017 - Back Pain and Disseminated Nocardiosis|RLR 017]])
- **Failure as data.** The admitting plan pre-commits to broadening if supportive management fails, treating the therapeutic response itself as a test; a non-contrast "stone" CT is read as incomplete. ([[RLR 018 - Gross Hematuria in End-Stage Renal Disease|RLR 018]])
- **Dechallenge, read cautiously.** Improvement after withdrawing a drug is powerful but confoundable, so Reza still leads with the history rather than treating the response as proof. ([[RLR 024 - Confusion and Ertapenem-Induced Encephalopathy|RLR 024]])
- **The bolus that reframes the question.** 500 cc normalizing heart rate, pressure, and symptoms confirms a volume-deficient state and sharpens "why syncope?" into "why hypovolemic?" ([[RLR 026 - Syncope and Occult Gastrointestinal Bleeding|RLR 026]])
