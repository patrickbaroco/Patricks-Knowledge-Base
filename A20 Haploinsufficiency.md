Evergreen concept note. A20 haploinsufficiency (HA20) is a monogenic autoinflammatory disease from loss-of-function variants in TNFAIP3 that produces an early-onset, heritable Behçet-like phenotype. RLR / Clinical Problem Solvers teaching points are cited per episode; general clinical detail is scaffolding.

## Definition and Genetics
HA20 is a **dominantly inherited autoinflammatory disorder** caused by **loss-of-function (haploinsufficient) variants in TNFAIP3**, the gene encoding the **A20 protein**.

- **TNFAIP3 / A20 is a negative regulator of NF-κB signaling.** A single defective allele reduces A20 activity below the threshold needed to restrain inflammation — hence "haploinsufficiency" ([[RLR 063 - Diarrhea and Behçet's Disease|RLR 063]]).
- Loss of A20's brake on **NF-κB** drives excess proinflammatory cytokine production (including inflammasome activation and IL-1/TNF pathways), producing a systemic autoinflammatory state.
- Inheritance is **autosomal dominant** with variable expressivity, so a family history of recurrent ulcers or autoinflammatory features may be present.

## Clinical Phenotype
HA20 characteristically presents as **early-onset Behçet-like disease**.

- **Recurrent oral and genital ulcers**, and often gastrointestinal inflammation, overlapping the Behçet's phenotype (see [[Behçet's Disease]]).
- **Unusually young age of onset** is the key clinical flag — often in early childhood, earlier than typical Behçet's. In the RLR case, onset around **age 11** prompted local experts to raise HA20 ([[RLR 063 - Diarrhea and Behçet's Disease|RLR 063]]).
- Additional features can include recurrent fevers, arthralgia/arthritis, skin lesions, and autoimmune/autoinflammatory overlap phenomena.

## Diagnosis and When to Consider It
- Suspect HA20 when Behçet-like disease is **early-onset, familial, or atypically severe** in a young patient ([[RLR 063 - Diarrhea and Behçet's Disease|RLR 063]]).
- Confirmation is by **genetic testing** for a heterozygous loss-of-function TNFAIP3 variant.
- On the RLR case, testing was framed as **not changing acute management** — it was raised for classification and counseling rather than to alter immediate therapy ([[RLR 063 - Diarrhea and Behçet's Disease|RLR 063]]).

## Management
- Treatment mirrors autoinflammatory/Behçet-like disease: **colchicine** and immunosuppression for mucocutaneous and systemic features.
- **Cytokine-targeted biologics** (anti-TNF, IL-1 blockade) are used for more severe or refractory disease, reflecting the underlying NF-κB/cytokine dysregulation.
- A genetic diagnosis carries value for **family counseling** and long-term classification even when it does not change the acute regimen.
