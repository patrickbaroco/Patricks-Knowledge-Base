## Definition

Case-based recall is a specific remembered patient firing before any schema does, and redirecting the workup. Not a category, not a differential, but one prior case retrieved whole on a surface cue, and carrying with it the vigilance that case earned.

It shares its machinery with the [[Availability Heuristic]], which names the failure mode: the vivid case that distorts probability. This note names the same mechanism working correctly. The distinction is not in the retrieval, which is identical, but in what happens next. Recall that opens an investigation is a trigger. Recall that closes one is a bias.

## How it operates

- **A single formative case encodes harder than a category.** The near-miss, the case that went wrong, the one a mentor made memorable. These are stored with an emotional weight that textbook entries never acquire, and they are retrieved faster.
- **It fires on a surface cue, not on a full pattern.** A word in the history, a lab trending the wrong way. The cue is often too thin to justify a diagnosis and exactly thick enough to justify looking harder.
- **Transmitted cases work as well as lived ones.** A mentor's teaching case, a colleague's story, a recorded lecture. The case does not have to be yours to be retrievable.
- **The discipline is to treat recall as a prompt, not a conclusion.** The remembered case tells you what to rule out. It does not tell you what the patient has. Skipping that step is where this becomes [[Premature Closure]].

## Examples from RLR episodes

- **A residency near-miss driving present vigilance.** Reza acts on a trending white count rather than the fever, and traces the instinct to a specific case from residency: back pain with a rising white count and an outside MRI read as negative, nearly discharged, re-imaged, spinal epidural abscess. ([[RLR 149 - A Post-CABG Fever and Neutrophilic Leukocytosis- Hidden Spaces, Painless Pyelonephritis, and the Limits of Diagnostic Certainty|RLR 149]])
- **A mentor's case, retrieved on one word.** The diagnosis lands the moment Reza hears "amputation," traced directly to a prior teaching case from a mentor. The falsely reassuring creatinine in a patient with an above-knee amputation is the kind of trap that a category never teaches and one case teaches permanently. ([[RLR 150 - The Falsely Reassuring Creatinine- Cefepime Neurotoxicity Unmasked by an Above-Knee Amputation|RLR 150]])
- **A remembered pearl used to refuse a settled label.** Reza declines to accept "osteomyelitis" from CT morphology alone, recalling a Lawrence Tierney teaching pearl about vertebral disease. The recall does no diagnostic work here; it keeps a question open that was about to close. ([[RLR 154 - The Bulge on His Forehead- Syphilitic Osteomyelitis of the Skull Recalled from a Colleague's Case|RLR 154]])
- **Recorded teaching content retrieved mid-emergency.** Struck by pulse-telemetry discordance at the bedside, Reza remembers that Robbie had recorded a teaching video on exactly this phenomenon years earlier, and with the blood pressure stable, steps away to rewatch it. A rare closed loop where the show's own material becomes the bedside tool. ([[RLR 155 - The Pulse That Wasn't There- Rate, Diastolic Filling, and a Real-Time Pulse-Deficit Rapid Response|RLR 155]])
- **A tangential case shaping how a stray lab is handled.** Facing an unexpectedly obtained inflammatory marker, Robbie recalls his own patient with *C. difficile* whose elevated ESR and CRP turned out to reflect an incidentally discovered glomerulonephritis. The recalled case argues for seeking an explanation rather than dismissing or uncritically accepting the value. ([[RLR 158 - Two Legs, One Cat- Pasteurella Multocida Bacteremia Causing Multifocal Cellulitis|RLR 158]])
