## Definition

Illness tempo is the rate at which a syndrome develops and evolves, used as a primary diagnostic variable. The pace of the workup should match the pace of the illness — seconds-to-hours complaints demand seconds-to-hours answers, a decade-long course grants time — and the tempo also runs in reverse, excluding diagnoses whose natural history is incompatible with it. Above all, an *evolving* illness is an *incomplete* history: a day-one story of a still-declaring disease is structurally partial.

## How it operates

- **Match workup pace to illness pace.** The urgency of the evaluation is set by how fast the process is moving, not by how dramatic it sounds.
- **Tempo is only interpretable against the syndrome.** Seconds-to-minutes of confusion means vascular/hypoglycemia/seizure; hours-to-days means acute delirium; weeks-to-months paints neurodegeneration.
- **Tempo excludes as well as includes.** A tempo of days is nearly incompatible with malignancy; a rhythm that *bounces and jumps* points to "plumbing" (drainage/perfusion) over parenchymal injury.
- **Evolution is data, sometimes the best data.** How the syndrome *changes* — dramatic improvement, a lesion resolving on serial imaging — can outweigh a single cross-sectional test ("like a non-contrast CT" of one moment).

## Examples from RLR episodes

- **The tempo rule, both directions.** A seconds-to-hours complaint must be solved in seconds to hours; a days-long tempo is nearly incompatible with malignancy. ([[RLR 022 - Vulvar Pain and Behçet Disease|RLR 022]])
- **Tempo as the master variable in confusion.** The same "confusion" resolves to entirely different differentials at seconds, days, or months — and is only interpretable against the syndrome. ([[RLR 024 - Confusion and Ertapenem-Induced Encephalopathy|RLR 024]])
- **Bouncing vs steady injury.** Up to half of acute liver injury is "plumbing" (a stone that lodges then pops, or a perfusion drop); plumbing bounces and jumps, parenchymal injury does not. ([[RLR 025 - Liver Chemistry Abnormalities and Intrahepatic Cholestasis of Pregnancy|RLR 025]])
- **An evolving illness is an incomplete history.** A day-one/day-two history of a still-declaring disease is structurally partial — wait for the story or lean on subtle clues. ([[RLR 026 - Syncope and Occult Gastrointestinal Bleeding|RLR 026]])
- **Evolution outweighs imaging.** The steady, dramatic improvement of the syndrome is more informative than the CT, CTA, or MRI — the story getting better is itself the most reassuring result. ([[RLR 027 - Dizziness and Peripheral Vertigo|RLR 027]])
