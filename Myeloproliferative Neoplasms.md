The four core myeloproliferative neoplasms, organized against the CBC as a memory device.

## How it operates

- **Map each disease to the CBC parameter it distorts:** chronic myeloid leukemia against the white count, polycythemia vera against the hemoglobin, essential thrombocythemia against the platelet count, and myelofibrosis (often a secondary progression of one of the other three) against the hematocrit.
- **Confirmatory mutations:** BCR-ABL (100% specific for CML) and JAK2, MPL, and calreticulin (CALR) for polycythemia vera, essential thrombocythemia, and myelofibrosis respectively.
- **Bone marrow biopsy is required for confirmation in all but CML and polycythemia vera**, which can often be diagnosed without it once the defining mutation is found.
- **Chronic urate elevation is a recognized complication** — patients with polycythemia vera have higher rates of gout and low-grade kidney dysfunction from chronic urate elevation, the chronic analogue of acute [[Tumor Lysis Syndrome|tumor lysis]] physiology.

## Examples from RLR episodes

- **A myeloid, not lymphoid, signature.** Marked leukocytosis with bands and monocytosis, splenomegaly, and thrombocytosis without blasts or schistocytes point toward a chronic myeloid process; BCR-ABL returns negative while JAK2 V617F is strongly positive, landing the diagnosis as a JAK2-positive, BCR-ABL-negative CML-pattern myeloproliferative neoplasm — with chronic urate elevation from the underlying disease driving both stone formation and subacute tumor lysis physiology.
