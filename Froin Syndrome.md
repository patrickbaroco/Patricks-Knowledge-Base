The CSF signature of a spinal subarachnoid block — markedly elevated protein with xanthochromia and spontaneous clotting in fluid sampled below a lesion.

## Definition
Froin syndrome (loculation syndrome) is the constellation of CSF findings obtained **below a spinal subarachnoid block**: **very high protein**, **xanthochromia**, and a tendency to **spontaneous clotting**, in **non-circulating** CSF isolated from the rest of the neuraxis ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).

- **The classic triad:** cytoalbuminologic dissociation (high protein, few cells), xanthochromia, and hypercoagulability of the fluid.

## Pathophysiology
When a lesion blocks the subarachnoid space, the CSF caudal to it becomes a **stagnant, sequestered pool** ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]):

- **Protein accumulates** because it is not cleared by normal CSF circulation and because the blocking lesion (and disrupted blood–CSF barrier) leaks protein locally — values can climb into the hundreds of mg/dL.
- **Xanthochromia and clotting** arise from the high protein (notably fibrinogen) and any local hemorrhage; the fibrinogen lets the sample gel.

## Causes of the Block
- **Compressive:** spinal tumors (epidural, intradural), severe stenosis, epidural abscess or hematoma, and disc disease — anything that seals the subarachnoid space.
- **Infiltrative/adhesive:** [[Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|leptomeningeal carcinomatosis]] and arachnoiditis, where roots and meninges are matted together. A markedly elevated CSF protein below a compression is Froin, not a separate disease ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).

## Why It Matters for Interpretation
- **Read the high protein correctly.** A very high CSF protein in the right setting signals a block and points you *up* the canal to find the obstructing lesion ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).
- **It coexists with other clues.** In leptomeningeal cancer, a Froin-pattern protein sits alongside an **insensitive, often negative CSF cytology** — the negative cytology reflects test limits, not absence of disease (see [[Test Interpretation]]) ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).
- **Procedural caution:** LP below a complete block can rarely worsen a compressive myelopathy by pressure shifts; image before tapping when compression is suspected.
