## Definition

The center of gravity is the single finding a reasoner deliberately makes the organizing hub of a case — the one that most constrains the differential and that the rest of the picture should be tested against. Choosing it well converts a scattered problem list into a coherent hypothesis: instead of cataloguing each abnormality, you ask whether *one* driver, through a pathophysiologic cascade, can account for the whole panel.

## How it operates

- **Let the hardest constraint choose the hub.** When findings compete, anchor on the one that *can't* be explained loosely (air in a hydropneumothorax; a chronic consolidation) rather than the broad one.
- **Prefer a cascade to a list.** One driver — hypercalcemia → nephrogenic DI → hypovolemia → prerenal AKI → low-EPO anemia and acidosis — can unify a new anemia, a bumped creatinine, and a low bicarbonate.
- **Discard the non-localizing.** Deliberately setting aside a non-specific finding (a neutrophilia) sharpens the center of gravity.
- **Re-check whether the chief complaint was the whole mountain.** When new "peaks" appear, the center of gravity may shift from a local problem to a systemic one.
