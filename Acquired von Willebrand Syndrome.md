Evergreen concept note. An acquired defect of von Willebrand factor — most often from mechanical shear destroying high-molecular-weight multimers — presenting as mucocutaneous bleeding despite normal platelet count and PT/PTT. RLR / Clinical Problem Solvers teaching points are cited per episode; general clinical detail is scaffolding.

## Concept
Von Willebrand factor (vWF) is the **bridge** that tethers platelets to the injured vessel wall and carries factor VIII (see [[Bleeding Framework]]). Its largest, **high-molecular-weight (HMW) multimers** are the most hemostatically active. Acquired von Willebrand syndrome (AVWS) is a loss or dysfunction of vWF developing in a previously unaffected adult — distinguishing it from the far more common congenital von Willebrand disease. Suspect it when bleeding is mucocutaneous and out of proportion, yet platelets and PT/PTT are normal ([[RLR 064 - Hematuria and Post-TAVR Perivalvular Leak|RLR 064]]).

## Mechanisms
Three broad flavors ([[RLR 064 - Hematuria and Post-TAVR Perivalvular Leak|RLR 064]]):

- **Vascular shear (mechanical).** High shear stress unfolds and cleaves HMW multimers. Culprits include **aortic stenosis** (the basis of **Heyde syndrome** — AS plus GI angiodysplasia bleeding), **prosthetic/mechanical valves** and **perivalvular leaks**, **LVADs**, and high-flow **AV fistulas** (including dialysis access). A patient with both a prosthetic valve and dialysis access has two shear sources ([[RLR 064 - Hematuria and Post-TAVR Perivalvular Leak|RLR 064]]).
- **Autoantibody.** **Anti-vWF antibodies** accelerate clearance or block function; associated with lymphoproliferative and autoimmune disease ([[RLR 064 - Hematuria and Post-TAVR Perivalvular Leak|RLR 064]]).
- **Adsorption / chelation by malignant cells.** Notably **myeloproliferative neoplasms** (and high platelet counts / paraproteins) bind and remove vWF ([[RLR 064 - Hematuria and Post-TAVR Perivalvular Leak|RLR 064]]).

## Clinical Clues
- **Mucocutaneous, primary-hemostasis pattern.** Epistaxis, bruising, GI bleeding (especially angiodysplasia in Heyde), procedural/mucosal bleeding.
- **Normal screening labs.** Platelet count and PT/PTT are typically normal, which is precisely why vWF must be interrogated separately.
- **A structural driver in the history.** Valve, LVAD, fistula, or MPN reframes "unexplained" bleeding.

## Testing
Because assays are nuanced, involve hematology. Key studies:
- **vWF antigen** (quantity) and **vWF activity** (function, e.g., ristocetin cofactor or GPIbM assay); an **activity-to-antigen ratio** below ~0.7 suggests a qualitative/multimer problem.
- **vWF multimer analysis** — the diagnostic hallmark of shear-mediated AVWS is selective loss of the **HMW multimers**.
- **Factor VIII** level; supportive workup for the underlying cause (echocardiography, MPN evaluation, antibody testing).

## Management
- **Treat the underlying driver** — this is definitive. Correcting the mechanical source (valve repair/replacement, addressing a perivalvular leak, LVAD management) can reverse the syndrome.
- **DDAVP (desmopressin)** transiently releases stored vWF and can support minor bleeding or procedures ([[RLR 064 - Hematuria and Post-TAVR Perivalvular Leak|RLR 064]]).
- **vWF-containing factor concentrates** for significant bleeding; **antifibrinolytics** as adjuncts. IVIG helps antibody-mediated forms; addressing the MPN helps adsorptive forms.
