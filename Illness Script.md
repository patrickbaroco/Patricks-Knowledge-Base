## Definition

An illness script is the structured knowledge package a clinician holds for a disease — its predisposing conditions and epidemiology, its pathophysiology and time course, and its typical manifestations and management. Diagnosis is largely pattern-matching the patient against stored scripts; the quality of reasoning therefore tracks the quality and completeness of the scripts, not raw intelligence. Two properties of scripts recur across the series and have their own notes: [[Illness Script Command|command]] (fluency with a script) and [[Illness Script Flexibility|flexibility]] (adjusting a script's boundaries).

## How it operates

- **Diagnosis is script-matching.** A presentation is recognized when it fits a stored script; the missing or thin script is where reasoning fails.
- **A *schema* organizes; a *script* recognizes.** [[Schema-Based Reasoning|Schemas]] partition a complaint into buckets; scripts populate those buckets with disease-level detail. The two work together.
- **A "schema gap" is a missing branch.** When a diagnosis isn't on the list at all, no amount of careful reasoning retrieves it — the fix is to add the script (see [[Cognitive Autopsy]]).
- **Fit is graded, not binary.** A case fits a script well, poorly, or partially; a poor fit ("end-stage manifestation in an otherwise well patient") should lower that script even when the label is tempting.

## Facets with their own notes

- **[[Illness Script Command]]** — fluency with a script and the predictable places command fails (a falsely normal test during the acute phase; stopping at "not deficient").
- **[[Illness Script Flexibility]]** — widening a script's severity, tempo, or histology so a textbook host with an atypical course isn't under-weighted.

## Examples from RLR episodes

- **Poor fit lowers the script.** Hypercalcemia of malignancy is usually end-stage (median survival ~6 months), so a PET-negative, weight-stable, years-long course fits the malignancy script poorly. ([[RLR 007 - Hypercalcemia|RLR 007]])
- **The schema gap.** The miss was not faulty reasoning but a missing branch — hereditary ATTR was absent from the polyneuropathy list, so the search satisficed. ([[RLR 009 - Progressive Sensorimotor Polyneuropathy|RLR 009]])
- **A tear's earliest signature, missed for want of a script.** A day-one "mild esophagitis" was the Boerhaave tear's first sign, moved past because the script wasn't active. ([[RLR 012 - Dysuria and Esophageal Perforation|RLR 012]])
