Evergreen concept note. Recognizing, localizing, and excluding spinal-cord disease. Teaching points cited per episode.

## Localizing to (or away from) the cord

- **Use [[Neurologic Equation|localization × time course]] first.** Aaron Berkowitz's equation forces *where* and *how fast* before *why*; a myelopathy is a cord-level lesion, distinguished from peripheral nerve and brain by its signature. ([[RLR 009 - Progressive Sensorimotor Polyneuropathy|RLR 009]])
- **Bowel and bladder involvement, a sensory level, and upper-motor-neuron signs point *to* the cord.** Conversely, intact bowel/bladder and a lower-motor-neuron picture (profound distal atrophy, areflexia) argue *against* myelopathy and toward peripheral nerve — the pertinent negatives that excluded an atypical myelopathy in a length-dependent polyneuropathy. ([[RLR 009 - Progressive Sensorimotor Polyneuropathy|RLR 009]])
- **A normal neurologic exam localizes by exclusion.** Intact distal strength, sensation, gait, and no Horner's make cord and root disease unlikely and push reasoning toward the musculoskeletal column itself. ([[RLR 016 - Neck Pain and Crowned Dens Syndrome|RLR 016]])

## The compressive emergency

- **Spinal cord compression is time-critical, reasoned like ACS or stroke** — the earlier the decompression, the better the recovery ("walk in with paresis, walk out"). In an un-examinable, encephalopathic patient, reflexes and withdrawal-to-pain substitute for a participatory motor exam. ([[RLR 033 - Persistent Altered Mental Status and Spinal Epidural Abscess|RLR 033]])
- **A febrile back demands the epidural space on the differential**, and [[Spinal Epidural Abscess|epidural abscess]] is poorly seen on non-contrast CT — match the modality (contrasted MRI) to the target. ([[RLR 017 - Back Pain and Disseminated Nocardiosis|RLR 017]], [[RLR 033 - Persistent Altered Mental Status and Spinal Epidural Abscess|RLR 033]])
