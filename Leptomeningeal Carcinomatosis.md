Evergreen concept note. Metastatic seeding of the leptomeninges (arachnoid and pia) and cerebrospinal fluid, producing a multifocal neurologic syndrome across brain, cranial nerves, cord, and roots. RLR / Clinical Problem Solvers teaching points are cited per episode; general clinical detail is scaffolding.

## Definition and Pathophysiology
Leptomeningeal carcinomatosis (also called carcinomatous meningitis or leptomeningeal metastases) is dissemination of malignant cells throughout the subarachnoid space and CSF, coating the pia-arachnoid over the brain, spinal cord, cranial nerves, and spinal nerve roots. Because the roots are sheathed in leptomeninges, root enhancement is itself a leptomeningeal sign ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).

- **Routes of spread:** hematogenous, direct extension from parenchymal/dural metastases, perineural, or via choroid plexus, with subsequent CSF-borne showering along the neuraxis.
- **The unifying-lesion insight:** when a single process seems to reach the face, arms, legs, and roots all at once, ask "how would one thing get *everywhere*?" — CSF-borne leptomeningeal spread is the anatomy that answers that ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).

## Clinical Presentation
The hallmark is **multifocal, multi-level disease** that cannot be localized to one site.

- **Cerebral:** headache, altered mentation, gait disorder, communicating hydrocephalus from impaired CSF resorption.
- **Cranial nerves:** diplopia, facial numbness or weakness, hearing loss; a unilateral trigeminal division numbness (see [[Numb Chin Syndrome]]) in a cancer patient should raise leptomeningeal coating of that nerve ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).
- **Spinal roots:** radicular pain, asymmetric weakness, sensory loss, and bowel/bladder dysfunction from cauda-equina root involvement ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).

## Common Primaries
- **Solid tumors:** adenocarcinoma of **lung** and **breast**, and **melanoma** are the classic solid-tumor culprits; GI primaries occur as well ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).
- **Hematologic:** leukemia and lymphoma seed the CSF readily and are a leading cause in that population.

## Diagnosis
Diagnosis rests on the pattern, contrast MRI, and CSF — each read for its limits ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).

- **MRI with gadolinium** of the entire neuraxis: leptomeningeal enhancement over cortical sulci, basal cisterns, cranial nerves, cord surface, and cauda equina roots (see [[Pachymeningeal vs Leptomeningeal Enhancement]]). MRI can still be negative in thin disease.
- **CSF cytology is insensitive:** a single tap misses many cases; sensitivity climbs with **large-volume taps repeated up to three times**. A negative cytology does *not* exclude disease ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]). CSF typically shows elevated protein, low-to-normal glucose, and a lymphocytic pleocytosis; a coexisting spinal block adds a [[Froin Syndrome|Froin]] pattern of markedly high protein.
- **PET may be negative** because thin leptomeningeal disease is not sufficiently avid; a negative PET should not dissuade the diagnosis when imaging and CSF fit ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).

## Management and Prognosis
- **Treatment** is generally palliative and multimodal: intrathecal and/or systemic chemotherapy, radiation to symptomatic or bulky sites, and CSF diversion for hydrocephalus. **Molecularly targeted agents** with CNS penetration (chosen by the primary tumor's driver mutation) have improved options for select patients.
- **Prognosis** is poor, historically measured in months; it is a marker of advanced (stage IV) disease. Goals-of-care and symptom-directed conversations are central.
