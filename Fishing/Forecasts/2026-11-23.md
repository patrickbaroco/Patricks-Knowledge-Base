# Mossy Creek forecast, week of Nov 23

Summarized from the Mossy Creek Fly Fishing weekly post and the video transcript; the day headings below are read by the Today dashboard, so keep them as they are.

## Week summary

- Still very dry. The storm expected over the weekend stayed off the coast and brought clouds and maybe a drizzle, so water everywhere is low and clear and many mountain streams are down to trickles.
- Cold early in the week: Tuesday night in the Valley could reach the upper teens (around 18 to 19 degrees in the video). The next rain chance is Wednesday night into Thursday, possibly a nor'easter on Thanksgiving, though the video is openly skeptical it will arrive.
- Best trout bets over the holiday are managed and Delayed Harvest water (Passage Creek, Back Creek, North River, the Pedlar, South River) and the spring creeks (Buffalo, Beaver, Mossy), fished in the warmer hours.
- Musky on the James and Shenandoah are shy in the clear water and are being caught on downsized flies and by sight-fishing. Mountain lakes such as Elkhorn and Switzer are a good option on a calm day while trout are still shallow.
- Brook trout are spawning in very low water; leave them alone. Shop news: closed Thanksgiving Day and open the rest of the week, with the annual holiday promotion running Thanksgiving through Cyber Monday and the Virginia Fly Fishing Festival in early January.

Neither source gives Monday weather beyond dry, low and clear conditions. Head to Delayed Harvest streams and the spring creeks and fish the warmest part of the day.

## Tuesday

Turning cold, with Tuesday night possibly around 18 to 19 degrees in the Valley, the first lows in the teens this season. Fish midday, check a stream thermometer, and look for spring-fed water where fish stay more active.

Neither source gives day-level weather. Same options as Saturday: Delayed Harvest water and spring creeks for trout, musky on the big rivers, or a mountain lake on a calm day.

Neither source gives weekend weather, so whether Thursday's possible rain lifts flows is the open question. Trout options are the Delayed Harvest streams and spring creeks in the warmer hours. Musky on the James and Shenandoah call for smaller flies and sight-fishing if the water stays low and clear. On a calm day, Elkhorn Lake and Switzer Reservoir hold trout in the top five to ten feet of water, which will change once it gets really cold and they go deep.

## Water

- Overall: very dry, everything low and clear; the weekend storm stayed offshore. Possible significant rain Wednesday night into Thursday.
- Delayed Harvest and managed trout water: Passage Creek, Back Creek, North River, the Pedlar and South River.
- Spring creeks: Buffalo, Beaver and Mossy, which stay warmer and more active in the cold.
- Mountain brook trout streams: many are just trickles and fish are spawning; stay away until rain lets them spread out and feed before winter.
- James and Shenandoah: low, clear musky water where fish are timid; fish can often be seen lying on the bottom.
- Mountain lakes and impoundments: Elkhorn Lake and Switzer Reservoir have both warm and cold water species near the surface right now, with trout in the top five to ten feet; best on a calm day before hard cold pushes them deep.

## Flies and tactics

- Cold weather: watch stream thermometers, target the warmer hours, and find springs.
- Trout surface activity: fish may cruise for small mayflies or midges, especially on overcast days (from the post; the video does not name trout hatches or patterns).
- Musky: downsize from large Game Changers to smaller flies such as the spoon fly, big Cree-Lex patterns and the shop's own fluke fly. For fish sitting on the bottom, bounce oversized jig flies such as large crayfish patterns along the bottom in front of them.
- Low, clear water generally: use the visibility to spot fish before they spot you, avoid spooking them, and move on when a fish swims off.
- Brook trout: give spawning fish a rest.
