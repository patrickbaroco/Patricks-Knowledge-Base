# Mossy Creek forecast, week of Oct 19

Summarized from the Mossy Creek Fly Fishing weekly post and Brian Trowe's video forecast (transcript). Sections are read by the Today dashboard and the Week Ahead note, so keep the day headings as they are.

## Week summary

- A strong cold front arrived over the weekend: 30°F and a hard freeze on Mossy Creek Monday morning. Expect nights in the 30s with frost for at least the next four or five nights, and sunny days in the 60s to low 70s. The post's 10-day outlook shows no rain.
- Last week's hurricane dropped three to four inches of rain in the mountains and the valley, plus another half inch from a second storm. Rivers and streams are back to roughly average fall levels. The drought is not over, and flows may drop again without more rain.
- This is the last good week for brook trout before the spawn. Wild browns, including on Mossy Creek, are starting to pair up, so spring creek fishing will get harder.
- DWR stocking has ramped up. Passage Creek and Back Creek delayed harvest sections are open, and the Jackson special regulation water and the South River in Waynesboro are well stocked, some with browns.
- Musky fishing is good with the best color and levels in months. Bass are in wintering lies and best in the warm afternoon hours.

## Monday

Hard freeze to start (30°F), then sunny and cool. Spring creeks still have tricos early, though the cold mornings will slow terrestrials. Brook trout streams have good water after last week's rain.

## Tuesday

Frosty night, sunny day in the 60s. Brook trout in the mountains are the priority this week; leave paired fish alone. Delayed harvest and freshly stocked streams are the main alternative.

## Wednesday

Cold night in the 30s, sunny and mild. Musky water is in good shape. For bass, fish deep pools slowly around 2 to 3 pm when water temperatures peak.

## Thursday

Another frosty night and a sunny, stable day. Dry fly fishing should be good in the stable sunshine. Calm days carry less leaf litter in the water than breezy ones.

## Friday

Nights still in the 30s through about Friday, days in the 60s to low 70s, no rain in the outlook. Stocked waters (Passage Creek DH, Back Creek DH, South River, Jackson) are a good bet with attractors under an indicator.

## Saturday

Sunny and cool with no rain expected. Brook trout window is closing; stocked streams, mountain lakes like Switzer Reservoir, musky, or afternoon bass. The Legends of the Fly tournament runs this weekend in Virginia Beach.

## Sunday

Same pattern: cool and dry. Stocked trout, musky or afternoon bass, and avoid spawning browns and brookies.

## Weekend

Cool, sunny and dry, with frosty mornings. Stocked delayed harvest streams and the Jackson are the most reliable trout options, and brook trout are near the end of their window. Musky should be good if the wind stays down and leaves are not dropping heavily. Bass fishing means slow, deep presentations in mid-afternoon, ideally from a boat.

## Water

- Mountain brook trout streams: good levels after last week's rain. Fish this week, then leave them for the spawn; watch for paired fish.
- Mossy Creek and other wild brown waters: fish daisy-chaining and pairing. Mostly juveniles and the odd rainbow will be catchable during the spawn.
- Spring creeks: in good shape; tricos still coming off early.
- Delayed harvest: Passage Creek (near Front Royal) and Back Creek are open and stocked. DH streams are usually stocked in fall, winter and spring, catch and release, single hook, artificials only, until they open to harvest.
- Other stocked water in the past two weeks (post): Jackson special regulation section, South River in Waynesboro, Elkhorn Lake, Bullpasture, Spring Run, Tye River, Hawksbill.
- Mountain lakes such as Switzer Reservoir: trout up in the water column and reachable with a fly rod; uncrowded during hunting season.
- Big rivers: musky water has better color and levels than in months, with ideal water temperatures. Heavy leaf drop and wind will make it harder over the next couple of weeks.
- Leaves are turning and in places dropping, which matters most in the national forest and the national park.

## Flies and tactics

- Spring creeks: trico spinners early; dry flies on stable sunny days. Hoppers and terrestrials will fade with the frosts.
- Stocked trout: attractor patterns under indicators with split shot; nymphs or small jig streamers bounced along the bottom.
- Brook trout: fish them this week before the spawn and stay off paired fish.
- Bass: slow and deep in big pools around 2 to 3 pm; a boat helps reach the deeper holes.
- Musky: should pick up. Calm days fish better because of the leaves.
- Coast: bull drum were caught at Virginia Beach last week between nor'easter blows. Speckled trout, stripers, puppy drum and bluefish are also around the Lynnhaven and the Bay.
