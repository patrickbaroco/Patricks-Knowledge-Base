# Mossy Creek forecast, week of Oct 12

Summarized from Nick's video forecast for Mossy Creek Fly Fishing (transcript only; no blog post was up for this week when this was written). Sections are read by the Today dashboard and the Week Ahead note, so keep the day headings as they are.

## Week summary

- The drought broke over the weekend: a hurricane brought a widespread soaking, about three inches at the shop and in Bridgewater and two to three inches across most of the valley. Expect high, stained water nearly everywhere for the next couple of days.
- Weather: warm and sunny for the first few days, with highs reaching the 80s on a couple of them. A cold front arrives later in the week and into the weekend, with lows in the 30s and possibly the first frost.
- Spring creeks are the best bet early in the week. Mossy Creek was stained and bankfull but fishable Monday morning. Tricos are still hatching and some terrestrial fishing remains, but the coming frost usually starts the end of terrestrial season, and the pattern shifts mostly subsurface.
- Big rivers are high and flooding, with secondary rises still coming. The rain flushed out summer moss and grass and should set up good musky fishing as the water drops and keeps some color.
- Stocking: the state had held off on many waters for lack of flow; the rain should help fall stockings. Lakes and reservoirs are the fallback where streams are blown out.

## Monday

The video gives no day-by-day weather; the first few days are warm and sunny. Mossy Creek was stained and bankfull but fishable this morning. Spring creeks are the place to go; most other water is high and dirty.

## Tuesday

Warm and sunny, with highs possibly in the 80s. The South River is falling and should come into shape soon, along with Spring Run and Beaver Creek over the next couple of days. Fish streamers on a sinking leader with heavy tippet in the stained water.

## Wednesday

Warm and sunny, possibly in the 80s. Mountain streams are still expected to be full; lower-gradient water such as the Dry River may have fishable slower pools. If fish won't chase streamers, dead-drift rubber-leg and attractor patterns under an indicator or on a tight line.

## Thursday

The cold front is due "later in the week"; the video does not name the day. As the spring creeks clear, expect more dry-fly fishing (tricos, plus the last of the hoppers and other terrestrials).

## Friday

Cooling as the front moves in. The big rivers should be falling by now, though secondary rises from tributaries can slow that. Brook trout streams should be dropping toward fishable levels.

## Saturday

Cold front, with lows in the 30s and possibly the first frost. Terrestrial fishing begins to fade after a frost; plan on subsurface fishing and streamers. Musky should be worth a look on the dropping, colored rivers.

## Sunday

Cool after the front, lows in the 30s possible. Same plan as Saturday: subsurface and streamers, brook trout streams as they drop, musky on the larger rivers.

## Weekend

A cold front brings lows in the 30s and maybe the first frost, which usually marks the start of the end for terrestrials. By then the spring creeks should be clearing and the freestone and brook trout streams dropping into shape after the flood, so streamers and dead-drifted subsurface flies are the main plan. The big rivers should be falling with good color and cleaner bottoms, which favors musky. Reservoirs remain a fallback if a stream is still too high.

## Water

- Mossy Creek: stained and bankfull Monday morning but fishable. Beaver Creek wasn't checked; it and Spring Run should be among the first waters fishable over the next couple of days.
- Spring creeks generally: the best option early in the week. Tricos continue; dry-fly fishing may improve for a couple of weeks as the water clears.
- South River: falling, should come online soon.
- Mountain and brook trout streams: quite full, many ungauged, few reports yet. Expect good fishing as they drop, before the spawn and leaf litter set in.
- Dry River and other lower-gradient streams: probably fishable in the slower pools.
- South Fork Shenandoah and James: high and flooded. Gauges may be cresting or falling, but expect secondary rises as tributaries come in. On the South Fork the first surge came from the North River; the Middle River was still rising and expected to peak Monday night. Some reaches were already flushed of the summer's moss and grass.
- Lakes and reservoirs: a fallback when streams are too full; many hold trout.
- Stocking: the state had skipped many places this summer for lack of water; the rain should help fall stockings.

## Flies and tactics

- High, dirty water: sinking leader or sinking line, heavy tippet, dark heavy streamers, split shot. Streamer fishing was already good in recent weeks and should improve with the stain.
- Fish not chasing: Squirmy Wormies, Zirdle Bugs, TJ Hookers and other rubber-leg attractors, dead-drifted under an indicator or on a tight line.
- Spring creeks: tricos, and hoppers and other terrestrials until the frost. Mostly subsurface after that.
- Musky: good fishing expected as the rivers fall and keep color.
- Brook trout: fish the mountain streams as they drop, ahead of the spawn.
