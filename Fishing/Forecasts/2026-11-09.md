# Mossy Creek forecast, week of Nov 9

Summarized from the Mossy Creek Fly Fishing weekly post and Brian Trowe's video forecast (transcript). Sections are read by the Today dashboard and the Week Ahead note, so keep the day headings as they are.

## Week summary

- Sunny, dry and pleasant, with the drought and low water continuing. Both sources give Thursday a shot of rain that could bring some relief, and the video expects colder weather this weekend.
- Spring creeks and state-stocked delayed harvest water are the best bets, since neither depends on day-to-day rain. Larger freestone streams are the other option.
- Mossy Creek browns are spawning and mostly missing for another week or two. They usually return around the 15th to 20th of the month and chase streamers hard.
- Brook trout streams are low and clear with fish on redds; leave them alone. Smallmouth are tough after nights in the upper 20s and low 30s, but musky have been cooperative.
- Sunday, November 15: the fly truck is at Hardywood West Creek in Richmond all day with free casting lessons, tune-ups and deals.

## Monday

Sunny and dry; neither source gives day-level temperatures. Fish delayed harvest water with attractor nymphs, squirmy worms, eggs and streamers, or the rainbow spring creeks with nymphs.

## Tuesday

Sunny and dry. Same plan as Monday: stocked water, or Buffalo Creek, Beaver Creek and the South River with nymphs and a careful approach.

## Wednesday

Sunny and dry. Musky are an option from a boat, kayak or canoe if the wind is light.

## Thursday

Both sources give Thursday a shot of rain, which may give low streams some relief. A bump in flow would spread stocked fish out and give cover on glassy pools.

## Friday

No day-level weather in either source. If Thursday's rain arrives, stocked and freestone water should fish better; otherwise expect spooky fish in clear pools.

## Sunday

Colder, per the video. The fly truck is at Hardywood West Creek in Richmond all day with free casting lessons and tune-ups. Mossy browns may start feeding again around the 15th.

On Sunday the shop is at Hardywood West Creek in Richmond with the fly truck, casting lessons and gear deals; call or email the shop to have a specific item brought along.

## Water

- Mossy Creek (browns): the post says the browns are starting to spawn; the video says they are paired and largely absent for another week or two. Immature browns and a few rainbows are still eating. Once the big browns finish, usually around the 15th to 20th, they feed hard and chase streamers, if enough moss has died back to fish them.
- Buffalo Creek, Beaver Creek and South River: spring-influenced, mostly rainbow fisheries with okay flow. Fish are spooky but feeding on nymphs.
- Spring creeks generally: flowing fairly well, since they depend on longer-term groundwater rather than day-to-day rain.
- Delayed harvest and state-stocked water: the best option right now. Rain would help spread fish out.
- Larger freestone streams: the other main option while small streams are low.
- Brook trout streams: low, clear and full of spawning fish on redds. Leave them be.
- Warm-water rivers: water temperatures keep falling after nights in the upper 20s and low 30s. Smallmouth are very tough.
- Leaves: gone in the western mountains, still falling on the valley floor and plentiful in the Piedmont, which fouls big flies. This should ease in a week or two.

## Flies and tactics

- Stocked trout: attractor nymphs, squirmy worms, egg patterns and other bright flies, plus streamers.
- Mossy Creek browns: streamers once the fish return and the moss allows.
- Rainbow spring creeks: nymphs, with a careful approach for spooky fish.
- Musky: big flies and long casts from a boat, kayak or canoe. Pick a day with light wind, and expect leaves to snag the fly in the valley and Piedmont.
- Smallmouth: a long wait until conditions change.
