# Mossy Creek forecast, week of Nov 16

Summarized from the Mossy Creek Fly Fishing weekly post and Brian Trowe's video forecast (transcript). Sections are read by the Today dashboard and the Week Ahead note, so keep the day headings as they are.

## Week summary

- Dry all week, with no rain in the five- to seven-day outlook. Cold nights and mild days: about 23°F on the valley floor Tuesday morning, and highs near 70 on one or two days that neither source names.
- Best window each day is the warmest part of the afternoon, around 2 to 3 pm, since water temperature peaks a little after the air does. Carry a stream thermometer.
- Spring creeks hold about 55°F groundwater year-round, the warmest water around as it gets colder. Find the springs and seeps, because fish may concentrate in or near them.
- Mossy Creek browns: no reports yet of the big fish coming out to feed after the spawn, but the shop expects it any day.
- Stocking is thin this year. Cutbacks, shortfalls and a major loss of trout (mostly rainbows) at Coursey Springs Hatchery mean fewer fish in managed water and more brown trout, likely into next year.

Musky are a good option if you can float, though low clear water makes them harder to fool. Crowds are light ahead of the holidays.

## Water

- Spring creeks (Mossy Creek and others): around 55°F groundwater makes them the warmest water as it cools. No reports yet of big browns feeding on Mossy, but expected any day.
- Stocked trout streams and delayed harvest water: among the best bets, though managed water has fewer fish this year and more brown trout. Target the places with the most fish.
- Brook trout streams: the shop is leaving brook trout alone for now.
- Musky rivers: water temperatures are in the range musky like, and November and December are good months. Rivers are low and very clear. Musky are packed into deep community pools with suckers, chubs and fallfish.
- Warm-water rivers and lakes (smallmouth, largemouth, sunfish): fishing has grown steadily harder since early October.
- Marginal trout water: trout are happiest around 55 to 64°F; near-freezing water slows them down.
- Leaves: mostly off the trees in the Shenandoah Valley and to the west, still coming down east of the Blue Ridge in the Piedmont and around Richmond.
- West Virginia: the post points to fall-stocked streams there for brood trout.

## Flies and tactics

- Neither source names fly patterns this week.
- Trout, general: fish the warmest two or three hours of the afternoon. Take water temperatures at a couple of locations and depths, and look for springs and spring seeps that warm the water around them.
- Spring creeks: watch for big browns coming off the spawn on Mossy Creek; waiting for next week's forecast may mean missing the first days.
- Stocked and delayed harvest water: concentrate time where the most fish have been put in, since stocking is reduced.
- Musky: work the deep winter pools where forage gathers; more fish per pool means more fish see each cast. Low clear water is the main difficulty.
- Lakes: in fall they turn over and the coldest water ends up on top, so find where fish sit in the water column.
