# Mossy Creek forecast, week of Sep 28

Summarized from the Mossy Creek Fly Fishing weekly post and Brian Trowe's video forecast (transcript). Sections are read by the Today dashboard and the Week Ahead note, so keep the day headings as they are.

## Week summary

- Warm through Wednesday (low 80s by Wednesday), then a cooldown to highs near 70 from Thursday. Rain is possible over the weekend.
- Thursday and Friday are the stable window before the next front; the shop's pick for bass.
- Spring creeks: the hex emergence is over, so mornings fish again. Tricos are heavy but falling later as the sun angle drops, some spinner falls not until 10 to 10:30. Terrestrials are excellent, with very large hoppers and crickets.
- Drought: the Shenandoah is very low and tough, and last week's storms barely moved the gauges. The video sees little rain in the next three to five days; the post allows for weekend rain.
- DWR stocking starts next week, mostly lakes while streams are this low. Delayed harvest waters come online once rain brings flow.
- Brook trout: scouted streams were wetter than expected and fish were caught. Fish them before the spawn later in October.

## Monday

Warming. Water temperatures dropped over the weekend and are expected to climb slightly this week. Spring creek tricos in the morning, terrestrials midday.

## Tuesday

Continued warming. Same spring creek pattern: tricos (the post says 8:30 to 9:30 am; the video says falls are drifting toward 10 to 10:30), then hoppers, crickets, beetles and ants. On bright days, run a dropper or fish sculpins, crayfish and jig streamers along the bottom.

## Wednesday

Warmest day of the week, highs in the low 80s. Low water makes bass spooky. Spring creek pattern unchanged from Tuesday.

## Thursday

Cooldown begins, highs near 70, stable. Good bass day ahead of the front. Brook trout are worth targeting before the spawn starts next month; east-side Blue Ridge streams are the better bet. Dry River and Skidmore Fork are low but have fishable runs.

## Friday

Highs near 70 and stable, the last good window before the next front. Bass remain the shop's pick. Carp are active and feeding in shallow water.

## Saturday

Highs near 70, rain possible as the front arrives. Musky expected to fish well this weekend, though the leaf drop has started and wind will complicate it. Overcast or rain means streamers on the spring creeks.

## Sunday

Highs near 70, rain possible. Musky and spring creek streamers as on Saturday.

## Weekend

Cooler, highs around 70. The post allows for rain with a front; the video expects little rain over the next several days. Musky should be good despite leaves in the water and breezy conditions. On the spring creeks, streamers if it's overcast or wet; tricos and terrestrials if it stays bright. The Shenandoah is low, spooky and collecting early leaves, so smallmouth there are a hard day; the shop's bass window was Thursday and Friday. Lakes are the fallback if DWR has stocked.

## Water

- Dry River and Skidmore Fork: low, fishable runs.
- Shenandoah: very low and clear, gauges flat despite last week's storms. Walnut leaves are already collecting in the foam lines. Floating it is hard going. Warm-water season there winds down in about a week; the first hard October cold front usually pushes smallmouth off their summer lies.
- James and larger warm rivers: warmer, smallmouth stay on summer lies a couple more weeks.
- Brook trout streams: hit or miss, but scouted streams had water and fish. Fish are colorful, feeding, easily spooked in low clear water.
- Mossy Creek (browns): fish begin pairing late October; fish them before then and leave spawners alone.
- East-side Blue Ridge streams: better than western-slope drainages.
- Water temperatures fell last weekend and should rise slightly through midweek.
- Lakes: heavy DWR stocking expected next week. Check the DWR daily stocking list and know the management type (put-and-take, special regulation, delayed harvest) before driving to a stream.

## Flies and tactics

- Spring creeks, morning: trico spinners, later in the morning than in summer.
- Spring creeks, midday: hoppers (oversized), crickets, beetles, ants. Terrestrials usually fish until the second to fourth hard freeze, into November in the valley.
- Bright days: dropper rigs; sculpins, crayfish and jig streamers on the bottom.
- Overcast or rain: streamers.
- Bass: low water, spooky fish. Fish small: Critter Mites and small crayfish, plus surface bugs. Work long shade lines where shade and habitat overlap.
- Stocked trout: streamers (stockers eat each other), bright egg patterns, and attractors such as squirmy worms, sucker spawn and Cree-Lex-style streamers.
- Musky: water temperatures falling and fish on the move. Odds improve as baitfish (suckers, chubs, fallfish, small carp) consolidate in winter lies and musky follow them. Leaves and wind are the complications.
- Carp: shallow and feeding.
- Brook trout: before the spawn next month.
