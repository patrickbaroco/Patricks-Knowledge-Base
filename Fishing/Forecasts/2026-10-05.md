# Mossy Creek forecast, week of Oct 5

Summarized from the Mossy Creek Fly Fishing weekly post and Brian Trowe's video forecast (transcript). Sections are read by the Today dashboard and the Week Ahead note, so keep the day headings as they are.

## Week summary

- Sunny all week with highs in the 70s and Thursday near 80. Nights turn cold: the video notes a frost watch for the valley floor Monday night, near 35°F. The post allows for rain late Sunday; the video sees little rain over the next four or five days.
- Drought continues. A weekend soaking gave small brook trout streams a few days' bump, but rivers fall back within three or four days and most mountain streams are low again, with spooky fish.
- Bass are about to move to winter lies as the cold nights arrive, possibly this week and within the next week or two at most. Warmer rivers to the south (James, New) run a week or two behind. Musky are eating well.
- Spring creeks: tricos in the mornings for another two or three weeks in good numbers, terrestrials midday. Mossy Creek browns start pairing over the next couple of weeks.
- DWR stocking has begun (the Jackson River has had fish) and delayed harvest waters are opening. With streams this low, expect larger streams and lakes first. Brook trout have a week or two left before the spawn.

## Monday

Sunny, highs in the 70s, with a frost watch on the valley floor tonight (near 35°F). Spring creek tricos in the morning, then hoppers, crickets, beetles and ants.

## Tuesday

Sunny, highs in the 70s after a cold night. Same spring creek pattern; if fish won't look up midday on Mossy Creek, a well-drifted nymph in the strike zone works. Bass will start pulling toward deep winter pools.

## Wednesday

Sunny, highs in the 70s, cool night. Brook trout are worth a trip in the week or two before they pair up; small ants, caddis and attractors like a Purple Haze.

## Thursday

Warmest day, near 80, and sunny. The warmer afternoon is the best bass window of the week; fish subsurface near deep winter lies. Spring creek tricos and terrestrials as earlier in the week.

## Friday

Sunny, highs in the 70s, cool night. Check the DWR daily stocking list by county before heading to stocked water. Musky are active; leaves on the water are the main nuisance.

## Saturday

Sunny, highs in the 70s. Stocked streams, spring creeks, or a last brook trout outing; streams are lightly fished this time of year with many anglers hunting.

## Sunday

The post allows for rain toward the end of the weekend; the video expects a mostly dry stretch. Spring creeks, stocked water, or musky on the rivers.

## Weekend

Saturday looks sunny in the 70s; Sunday may turn wet late, per the post, though the video expects little rain. Stocked streams and lakes, spring creeks, and brook trout before the spawn are the trout options. Musky continue to eat on the rivers, but walnut leaves and wind will complicate it. Bass will be harder by the weekend as fish settle into winter lies; work warmer hours with a boat if possible.

## Water

- Mountain streams (east slope of the Blue Ridge): mostly low after the weekend's limited rain; some fishable runs, spooky fish. Small brook trout streams got a short bump from the rain.
- Valley rivers (bass water): low, rebound for only three or four days after rain, then drop back. The post notes low water is a problem for boat access. Leaf litter from early walnuts is starting.
- James and New rivers: warmer, so the bass transition runs a week or two behind.
- Spring creeks (Mossy Creek): fishing well. Browns pair up over the next couple of weeks and are hard to catch until they finish, around the first week of November.
- Stocked water: DWR stocking under way, delayed harvest opening. Jackson River stocked. Larger streams and lakes first while streams are low.
- Brook trout streams: some fishing well; another week or two before the spawn, then leave them alone.

## Flies and tactics

- Spring creeks, morning: tricos.
- Spring creeks, midday: hoppers (there are big ones out), crickets, beetles, ants; terrestrials can fish into Thanksgiving. If fish won't rise, nymphs on a good drift. The post suggests skating bushy dries when rises are aggressive.
- Stocked trout: heavy presentations with worms, eggs, mops and attractors.
- Brook trout: small ants, small caddis, attractors such as a Purple Haze.
- Bass: subsurface, at deep winter lies, during the warmest hours; a boat helps. Summer foods (damselflies, dragonflies, cicadas) are dying off.
- Musky: eating well; expect leaves and wind to complicate presentations.
