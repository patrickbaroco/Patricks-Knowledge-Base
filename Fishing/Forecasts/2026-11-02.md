# Mossy Creek forecast, week of Nov 2

Summarized from the Mossy Creek Fly Fishing blog post for 11/2/2026 and the transcript of Andy's video forecast, and the day headings are read by the Today dashboard.

## Week summary

- Sunny and warm Monday through Wednesday with highs in the 70s, rain chances Wednesday into Thursday, then cooler for the weekend with highs in the 60s.
- Water is low and clear after a stretch of stable high-sun days, so fish are spooky; both sources call for a stealthy approach. Any midweek rain should improve flows and fishing noticeably by Friday.
- Spring creeks: hoppers and a few tricos are still around but fish are paying them less attention. Nymphs and streamers are working better on Mossy and Beaver Creek, with the odd fish still rising to an ant or caddis.
- Brown trout are starting to move for the spawn on Mossy and the Jackson, and brook trout in the mountains are close to spawning. Leave paired fish and redds alone.
- Delayed harvest waters nearby have pretty much all been stocked. In low water the stockers are stacked in the big pools.

## Monday

Sunny and warm, highs in the 70s, water low and clear. Fish the spring creeks with nymphs and streamers, size down in delayed harvest water, and use caddis or attractor dry-droppers in the mountains.

## Tuesday

Same sunny, warm pattern with highs in the 70s. Beaver Creek has the better flows; on Mossy keep watching for a rise to an ant or caddis on a dry-dropper.

## Wednesday

Sunny to start with highs in the 70s, then rain chances arrive from Wednesday into Thursday. Low-water tactics still apply: small flies and midges in stocked water, big pools for stockers.

## Thursday

Rain chances continue. Overcast, cooler days favor blue-winged olives on the mountain streams.

## Friday

The video gave no specific weather for Friday. If Wednesday and Thursday bring rain and flows rise, size up in delayed harvest water with Pat's Rubber Legs or a streamer; if not, stay small.

## Saturday

Cooler, highs in the 60s and still pleasant. Musky are improving into the season, and higher water would help them feed.

## Sunday

Highs in the 60s. Same options as Saturday: spring creek nymphs and streamers, delayed harvest water, or musky.

## Weekend

Cooler, with highs in the 60s but good weather for fishing. If midweek rain raised the water, stocked fish will have spread out of the big pools and larger flies will fish better; musky should also feed more readily. If it stays low and clear, keep flies small and expect spooky fish, especially musky. The shop said it would post updates during the week if the rain comes.

## Water

- Mountain brook trout streams: water temperatures haven't dropped much, so fish aren't fully spawning yet, and recent visitors saw no redds. Spawning fish use shallow tail-outs; target the heads of pools and deeper, faster riffles instead.
- Mossy Creek: fishing well enough, but browns are starting to move for the spawn and may feed less, which could explain slow days over the next few weeks. The creek isn't waded, and reproduction is poor in the silt.
- Beaver Creek: fishing really well, with better flows than most water around.
- Spring creeks generally (Mossy, Beaver, Spring Run): some of the best flows available right now.
- Jackson River tailwater: good flows; browns moving to spawn but plenty of rainbows. Worth the drive.
- Delayed harvest: nearly all nearby water has been stocked, but it is low and clear. Fish concentrate in big pools until rain lets them spread out.
- Smallmouth rivers: season is essentially over. Fish are podded up in deeper areas and fishing is slow.
- Musky water: low water has made fish spooky, but fishing is getting better each week.

## Flies and tactics

- Mountain brook trout: attractor dry-droppers. Caddis on sunny days, blue-winged olives on overcast, cooler days.
- Low, clear water anywhere: approach quietly and use light tippet (from the post).
- Spring creeks: nymphs and streamers; an ant or caddis on a dry-dropper for the occasional riser. Hoppers and tricos are fading.
- Freshly stocked delayed harvest: bright, flashy attractors, small streamers, egg patterns, squirmy worms, hot-head pheasant tails.
- Delayed harvest stocked two or more weeks ago: fish switch to naturals, so pick flies by water conditions. Low and clear, size down to midges; high water, size up to Pat's Rubber Legs, a big stonefly or a streamer.
- Smallmouth: fish the deeper holes slowly.
- Musky: sight fish where you can and bounce a small jig-style fly in front of them; enough presentations usually draw an eat.
