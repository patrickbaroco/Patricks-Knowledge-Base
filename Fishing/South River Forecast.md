# South River forecast

Rewritten each morning. The Now, Today, Tomorrow and Weekend headings are read by the Today dashboard.

## Now

The Waynesboro gauge (WAYV2) read 24 cfs and 2.34 ft at 4:15am, a small uptick from the 22 cfs it held for the past six days and about level with the 25 cfs logged a week ago, so the river remains at a very low base flow and should be clear. The NWS forecast shows a modest bump to a peak of 2.5 ft and about 45 cfs around 7pm tonight with today's showers, then a fall back to 2.3 ft and 20 cfs by Tuesday morning. A mild, cloudy night in the upper 40s means the water starts the day cool and comfortable for trout rather than cold.

## Today

Sunday is cloudy all day with on-and-off light showers, about 0.06 in in total, a high of 61° around 2 to 3pm and only a light north breeze. Under full cloud and soft rain the fish in the clear, low water should be less wary, so work small nymphs or a lightly weighted streamer through the deeper runs and pools at Ridgeview Park and in the catch-and-release water from Wayne Avenue to North Park, favoring the warmest hours from late morning to mid-afternoon. Any tint from tonight's small rise should come after dark.

## Tomorrow

Monday is partly sunny and warm with a high of 68°, no rain and a west-northwest breeze gusting to about 14 mph. The river should be at or just past tonight's small peak in the morning and dropping back toward base flow, possibly with a slight tint that helps in this very clear water; fish the afternoon, which Mossy Creek names as the best window, and keep low and back from the bank in the slower pools as the light brightens.

## Weekend

Next Saturday, November 28, is cloudy and dry with a high of 52°, and Sunday, November 29, brings occasional rain of about 0.17 in with a high of 57°. Coming two days after the Thanksgiving rain, Saturday should find the river falling and clearing and is the better of the two days; a cold night with a slight chance of snow flurries Saturday evening points to fishing the afternoon.

## Next rain

The next meaningful rain arrives Wednesday night, November 25, about 0.27 in (77% chance), and continues as steady rain on Thanksgiving Day, Thursday, November 26, about 0.42 in more (91% chance), with a breezy north wind gusting near 31 mph and a high of only 48°; that is roughly 0.7 in in all. The NWS forecast for WAYV2 does not yet reach those dates, and after the long dry spell much of the rain will likely soak in, so the size of the rise is uncertain, though the river should come up and color for a day or so, and the cold rain will chill the water. Friday, November 27 (sun through high clouds, 54°, breezy), and Saturday, November 28 (cloudy, 52°, dry), look like the best days as the water falls and clears.

## Regional (Mossy Creek)

- From the week of Nov 16 note (blog post and video); the next post is due Monday, November 23. It called for a dry week with cold nights and mild afternoons, which held until today's light showers.
- Stocking: no South River stocking listed in the last two weeks. DWR's daily list covers November 13 to 20 with nothing for Augusta County or Waynesboro; the only South River entry (November 20) is the Rockbridge County water, not this river. Nearby valley stockings were Tomahawk Pond in Shenandoah County (Nov 17), Hogue Creek in Frederick County (Nov 18) and Happy Creek in Warren County (Nov 19). Mossy Creek reports thin stocking this year after cutbacks and a major loss of mostly rainbow trout at Coursey Springs Hatchery, so managed water holds fewer fish and more browns.
- Valley water: rivers are low and very clear; stocked and delayed harvest water are among the best bets, warm-water fishing has grown steadily harder, and musky are packed into deep winter pools with forage.
- For stocked trout: fish the warmest two or three hours of the afternoon, take water temperatures in a couple of spots and depths, and concentrate where the most fish were put in. Neither source names fly patterns this week.
- Spring creeks: around 55° groundwater makes them the warmest water as it cools; Mossy Creek's big browns had not been reported feeding after the spawn but were expected any day.

## Water and rules

- Wayne Avenue to 2nd Street (North Park), about 2 miles: catch-and-release stocked trout water, year-round, no bait, single-hook artificial lures only.
- Ridgeview Park: put-and-take stocked water, 6 trout per day, 7-inch minimum.
- Basic Park: youth only, April 1 to June 15.
- Lyndhurst, upstream: about 3 miles of fly-fishing-only water on private land, stocked with fingerlings; free permit required.
- Grand Caverns to Grottoes: stocked trout water.
- Mercury advisory: eat no fish from the South River except trout.
