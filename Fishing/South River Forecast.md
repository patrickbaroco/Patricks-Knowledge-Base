# South River forecast

Rewritten each morning. The Now, Today, Tomorrow and Weekend headings are read by the Today dashboard.

## Now

The Waynesboro gauge (WAYV2) read 33 cfs and 2.42 ft at 4:15am, up from 21 cfs yesterday and 22 cfs a week ago, so the Thanksgiving rain has started the rise, and Dooms has nearly tripled overnight to 102 cfs. The NWS forecast has WAYV2 climbing through the day to a peak near 4.2 ft and 600 cfs around 7pm tonight, so the water will turn from clear to stained this morning and muddy by afternoon. After this week's hard freeze and with a cold rain falling, the water is cold and will get colder, and trout will be sluggish.

## Today

Thanksgiving Day is wet and windy, with rain heavy at times and about 1.8 in in all (100% chance), a high of 49°, a north-northeast wind near 17 mph and a Wind Advisory for Augusta County until 4pm, with gusts around 55 mph that could bring down trees. The river will be rising and muddy all day toward this evening's peak, so this is a day to stay off the water and away from wooded banks.

## Tomorrow

Friday has a shower in spots in the morning, then mostly cloudy skies, a high of 47° and a west-southwest wind near 12 mph with gusts to 30 mph. The river will be falling from the overnight peak but still high and muddy, from about 314 cfs at 7am to about 148 cfs by 1pm in the NWS forecast, so the South River is a poor choice. Mossy Creek notes that the spring creeks usually clear sooner than freestone water after rain, which makes them the better bet if you want to fish.

## Weekend

Saturday, November 28, has intervals of clouds and sun, a high of 55° and no rain, and Sunday, November 29, has clouds and sun, a high of 56° and a couple of evening showers of about 0.08 in. The NWS forecast has WAYV2 near 2.6 ft and 61 cfs by Saturday morning, about three times its recent base flow, so expect cold, stained water on Saturday that suits slower, deeper runs, the edges and larger, more visible flies fished in the warmest hours. Sunday should be lower and clearer, and the forecast ends Saturday morning, so check the gauge before going.

## Next rain

The meaningful rain is today, Thursday, November 26: about 1.8 in at Waynesboro, following last night's rain, with only a few light showers in the rest of the 10-day forecast and nothing near 0.25 in. The NWS forecast, issued yesterday morning when the expected total was lower, peaks at 4.2 ft and 599 cfs this evening, still well below the 7.5 ft action stage, and falls back to 61 cfs by Saturday morning; after a long dry spell some of the rain will soak in, but with the forecast total now higher the actual crest could differ, so watch the gauge. Saturday is the first fishable day, Sunday should be better, and the dry stretch from Monday, November 30, to Thursday, December 3, looks best as the river drops and clears, though Monday night's low near 27° will chill the water again.

## Regional (Mossy Creek)

- From the week of Nov 23 note (blog post and video); the next post is due Monday, November 30. It described the valley as very dry, with everything low and clear and many mountain streams down to trickles, and called the Wednesday-night-to-Thanksgiving rain possibly significant, which the forecast and this morning's gauges now confirm.
- Stocking: no South River stocking listed in the last two weeks. DWR's list runs from November 17 to 24 with nothing for Augusta County or Waynesboro; the only South River entry (November 20) is the Rockbridge County water, not this river. Mossy Creek lists the South River with Passage Creek, Back Creek, the North River and the Pedlar as the managed and Delayed Harvest waters that are the best trout bets.
- Valley water: the James and Shenandoah had been low and clear, with shy musky taken on downsized flies and by sight-fishing; this rain will raise and color them for several days. Brook trout are spawning, and the shop asks anglers to leave them alone.
- For stocked trout: fish the warmest hours, carry a stream thermometer, and expect fish to cruise for small mayflies or midges on overcast days. The post names no specific trout patterns.
- Spring creeks: Buffalo, Beaver and Mossy stay warmer and fish well in the cold, and they usually clear sooner than freestone water after rain.

## Water and rules

- Wayne Avenue to 2nd Street (North Park), about 2 miles: catch-and-release stocked trout water, year-round, no bait, single-hook artificial lures only.
- Ridgeview Park: put-and-take stocked water, 6 trout per day, 7-inch minimum.
- Basic Park: youth only, April 1 to June 15.
- Lyndhurst, upstream: about 3 miles of fly-fishing-only water on private land, stocked with fingerlings; free permit required.
- Grand Caverns to Grottoes: stocked trout water.
- Mercury advisory: eat no fish from the South River except trout.
