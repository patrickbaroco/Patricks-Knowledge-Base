# South River forecast

Rewritten each morning. The Now, Today, Tomorrow and Weekend headings are read by the Today dashboard.

## Now

The Waynesboro gauge (WAYV2) read 1,190 cfs and 5.35 ft at 4:15am, up from 33 cfs yesterday morning and 22 cfs a week ago, so Thanksgiving's rain produced about twice the crest that yesterday's NWS forecast called for, though it stayed below the 7.5 ft action stage. The new NWS forecast, issued at 2am, has the river already falling, to 775 cfs at 7am and 434 cfs by 1pm, so the water will be high and muddy today and only beginning to clear on Saturday. After this week's hard freeze and a day of cold rain, the water is cold and fish will be sluggish.

## Today

Friday is mostly cloudy and breezy, with a high of 47°, a 25% chance of a shower but no measurable rain expected, and a southwest wind near 14 mph with gusts to 29 mph. The South River will be high, fast and muddy all day, so this is a day to stay off it; if you want to fish, Mossy Creek's point that the spring creeks clear sooner than freestone water after rain makes Buffalo, Beaver or Mossy the better bet.

## Tomorrow

Saturday has intervals of clouds and sun, a high of 58°, no rain and a west-southwest wind near 8 mph with gusts to 20 mph. The NWS forecast has WAYV2 near 176 cfs at 7am and 148 cfs by 1pm, still six to seven times its recent base flow, so expect cold, stained, pushy water that is fishable only along slow edges and in eddies, with larger, darker or brighter flies fished in the warmest hours and careful wading.

## Weekend

Saturday is dry with clouds and sun and a high of 58°, and Sunday has intervals of sunshine, a high of 58° and a couple of evening showers totaling about 0.19 in, with gusts near 27 mph. The NWS forecast holds WAYV2 near 3.0 ft and 148 cfs from Saturday afternoon through Sunday, so both days will have high, cold, slowly clearing water, with Sunday likely a little clearer than Saturday. Fish the edges and slower runs at midday, and check the gauge before going.

## Next rain

The next meaningful rain is Friday, December 4, with about 0.25 in in the afternoon (78% chance); Sunday evening's showers come to about 0.19 in and should not change the river much. With the ground now wet from Thanksgiving, more of next Friday's rain may run off than during the dry spell, but the size of any rise is uncertain and the NWS forecast does not reach that far. The dry stretch from Monday, November 30, to Thursday, December 3, looks best as the river keeps falling and clearing, though lows of 27° Monday night and 29° Tuesday night will keep the water cold; after the December 4 rain, Saturday, December 5, and Sunday, December 6, are dry and sunny.

## Regional (Mossy Creek)

- From the week of Nov 23 note (blog post and video); the next post is due Monday, November 30. It described the valley as very dry, with everything low and clear and many mountain streams down to trickles, and was unsure whether the Thanksgiving rain would arrive; it did, and the gauges show a large rise.
- Stocking: no South River stocking listed in the last two weeks. DWR's list runs from November 18 to 24 with nothing for Augusta County or Waynesboro; the only South River entry (November 20) is the Rockbridge County water, not this river. Mossy Creek lists the South River with Passage Creek, Back Creek, the North River and the Pedlar as the managed and Delayed Harvest waters that are the best trout bets.
- Valley water: the James and Shenandoah had been low and clear, with shy musky taken on downsized flies and by sight-fishing; this rain will raise and color them for several days, so the sight-fishing advice no longer applies. Brook trout are spawning, and the shop asks anglers to leave them alone.
- For stocked trout: fish the warmest hours, carry a stream thermometer, and expect fish to cruise for small mayflies or midges on overcast days. The post names no specific trout patterns.
- Spring creeks: Buffalo, Beaver and Mossy stay warmer and fish well in the cold, and they usually clear sooner than freestone water after rain.

## Water and rules

- Wayne Avenue to 2nd Street (North Park), about 2 miles: catch-and-release stocked trout water, year-round, no bait, single-hook artificial lures only.
- Ridgeview Park: put-and-take stocked water, 6 trout per day, 7-inch minimum.
- Basic Park: youth only, April 1 to June 15.
- Lyndhurst, upstream: about 3 miles of fly-fishing-only water on private land, stocked with fingerlings; free permit required.
- Grand Caverns to Grottoes: stocked trout water.
- Mercury advisory: eat no fish from the South River except trout.
