# South River forecast

Rewritten each morning. The Now, Today, Tomorrow and Weekend headings are read by the Today dashboard.

## Now

The Waynesboro gauge (WAYV2) read 616 cfs and 4.24 ft at 4:15am, down from 1,190 cfs yesterday morning but still far above the 22 cfs of a week ago. The river is falling more slowly than the NWS predicted: yesterday's forecast called for about 150 cfs by this morning, and the new forecast's 314 cfs at 7am looks optimistic against a 4:15am reading of 616, so expect the water to stay high and stained through today and clear only gradually. After the hard freeze and a day of cold rain the water is cold, and fish will be slow except in the warmest part of the afternoon.

## Today

Saturday has intervals of clouds and sun, a high of 58°, no rain and a west-southwest wind near 8 mph with gusts to 22 mph. The South River will be high, pushy and off-color, so wade carefully and fish only the slow edges, eddies and inside bends, for example in the Waynesboro catch-and-release water, with larger dark or bright flies or streamers worked slowly in the early afternoon. Mossy Creek's point that the spring creeks clear sooner than freestone water after rain makes Mossy, Beaver or Buffalo the more reliable choice today.

## Tomorrow

Sunday has intervals of bright sunshine, a high of 58° and a south wind near 6 mph, staying dry by day before about 0.28 in of rain overnight (91% chance). The NWS forecast holds WAYV2 near 3.3 to 3.4 ft and 240 to 276 cfs through the day, still many times its base flow but likely clearer than Saturday if the river catches up with the forecast. Fish the softer water at midday and be off the river before the evening rain; the lower river at Harriston carries more water than Waynesboro and will be slower to fish.

## Weekend

Next Saturday, December 5, has clouds and sun with a high of 63°, and Sunday, December 6, is mostly cloudy with a high of 56°, both dry. With only a trace of rain forecast Friday evening, the river should have had most of the week to fall and clear, so this looks like the best South River weekend since the Thanksgiving storm. Expect cold mornings, and plan to fish late morning through mid-afternoon.

## Next rain

The next meaningful rain is Sunday night, November 29, with about 0.28 in (91% chance). With the ground now wet, the NWS forecast shows a modest bump at WAYV2, from 240 cfs on Sunday to 394 cfs and 3.7 ft on Monday morning and early afternoon, easing to 354 cfs by Monday evening. After that the forecast is dry from Tuesday, December 1, through Thursday, December 3, with only about 0.05 in Friday evening, so Wednesday, December 2 (high 56°), and Thursday, December 3 (high 62°), look like the best weekdays as the water falls and clears, followed by the dry weekend of December 5 and 6.

- Valley water: the James and Shenandoah had been low and clear, with shy musky taken on downsized flies and by sight-fishing; the storm has raised and colored the valley rivers, so the low-water sight-fishing advice does not apply this weekend. Brook trout are spawning, and the shop asks anglers to leave them alone.
- For stocked trout: fish the warmest hours, carry a stream thermometer, and expect fish to cruise for small mayflies or midges on overcast days. The post names no specific trout patterns.
- Spring creeks: Buffalo, Beaver and Mossy stay warmer and fish well in the cold, and they usually clear sooner than freestone water after rain.

## Water and rules

- Wayne Avenue to 2nd Street (North Park), about 2 miles: catch-and-release stocked trout water, year-round, no bait, single-hook artificial lures only.
- Ridgeview Park: put-and-take stocked water, 6 trout per day, 7-inch minimum.
- Basic Park: youth only, April 1 to June 15.
- Lyndhurst, upstream: about 3 miles of fly-fishing-only water on private land, stocked with fingerlings; free permit required.
- Grand Caverns to Grottoes: stocked trout water.
- Mercury advisory: eat no fish from the South River except trout.
