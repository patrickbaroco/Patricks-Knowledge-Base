Diabetes insipidus — the water-diuresis branch of [[Polyuria|polyuria]], and the central-vs-nephrogenic fork within it.

## The two mechanisms

- **DI is ADH failure, and it comes in two flavors: deficiency vs resistance.** **Central DI** is a failure to *make/secrete* ADH (the hypothalamus makes it; the *posterior* pituitary releases it). **Nephrogenic DI** is renal *resistance* to ADH — the hormone is present but the collecting duct cannot respond. Both produce dilute polyuria and a tendency to hypernatremia. ([[RLR 034 - Polyuria and Post-Obstructive Nephrogenic Diabetes Insipidus|RLR 034]])

## Splitting central from nephrogenic

- **Base rate favors central — more common, more abrupt — but don't anchor on an incidental scan.** A pituitary abnormality on MRI pulls toward central DI, yet an intact anterior-pituitary panel (TSH, free T4, FSH, LH, prolactin, ACTH, cortisol) and an intact visual field (no optic-chiasm compression) drop the probability of a pituitary cause sharply. A [[Pituitary Macroadenoma|macroadenoma]] can be a non-functioning [[Anchoring Bias|incidentaloma]], not the cause. ([[RLR 034 - Polyuria and Post-Obstructive Nephrogenic Diabetes Insipidus|RLR 034]])
- **[[Copeptin|Copeptin]] (an ADH surrogate) or a water-deprivation-plus-vasopressin challenge formally splits them** — low in central deficiency, high in nephrogenic resistance. Use them when the distinction changes management. ([[RLR 034 - Polyuria and Post-Obstructive Nephrogenic Diabetes Insipidus|RLR 034]])
- **A slam-dunk nephrogenic trigger reweights the fork.** [[Post-Obstructive Uropathy|Post-obstructive uropathy]] downregulates [[Aquaporin|aquaporin]] channels, causing nephrogenic DI; when that sits in the timeline (relieved obstruction → polyuria), the sequence of events beats the incidental image. Nephrogenic DI is rarely acute *except* with diuretics or post-obstructive uropathy. ([[RLR 034 - Polyuria and Post-Obstructive Nephrogenic Diabetes Insipidus|RLR 034]])

## Don't miss

- **Screen for occult anterior-pituitary deficiency before blaming the kidney.** A low cortisol, a high potassium, or hyperpigmentation should force an urgent [[Adrenal Insufficiency|adrenal insufficiency]] workup. And hold [[Hickam's Dictum|AND, not OR]] — a patient with a macroadenoma (the commonest risk factor for central DI) and a post-obstructive kidney can genuinely harbor *both* mechanisms. ([[RLR 034 - Polyuria and Post-Obstructive Nephrogenic Diabetes Insipidus|RLR 034]])
