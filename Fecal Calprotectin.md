Fecal calprotectin is a neutrophil-derived stool protein that flags intestinal inflammation and helps separate inflammatory from functional bowel disease.

## What It Measures
Calprotectin is a **calcium-binding protein released by neutrophils**. When neutrophils migrate into inflamed intestinal mucosa, calprotectin is shed into the stool in proportion to the inflammatory burden.

- **Neutrophil-derived marker of mucosal inflammation** — a stool signal that white cells are trafficking into the gut wall ([[RLR 063 - Diarrhea and Behçet's Disease|RLR 063]]).
- It is **stable in stool** for days at room temperature, making collection and quantitative (ELISA-based) measurement practical.

## Clinical Use
- **Distinguishes inflammatory from functional bowel disease** — it is a leading tool to separate IBD from irritable bowel syndrome and to avoid unnecessary endoscopy in low-risk patients ([[RLR 063 - Diarrhea and Behçet's Disease|RLR 063]], see [[Inflammatory Bowel Disease]]).
- **Monitors IBD activity** — tracks mucosal inflammation, helps predict relapse, and assesses treatment/mucosal-healing response over time.
- A **low value** in a low-risk patient reassures against significant mucosal inflammation; a **high value** warrants endoscopic evaluation.

## Interpretation Depends on Pretest Probability
- **The result's value hinges on pretest probability** — calprotectin is most useful in the intermediate-probability patient and can mislead at the extremes ([[RLR 063 - Diarrhea and Behçet's Disease|RLR 063]]).
- It is **sensitive but not specific**: any neutrophilic intestinal process raises it — **infectious colitis, NSAID enteropathy, and colorectal neoplasia** among them — so an elevated value is not synonymous with IBD.
- Because infection also raises it, calprotectin does **not substitute for excluding infection** before immunosuppression (see [[Colitis]]).

## Relationship to Stool Lactoferrin
- Both are **neutrophil-derived stool markers** of intestinal inflammation and are often discussed together; **[[Stool Lactoferrin]]** is framed as the "leukocyte esterase of the gut" ([[RLR 063 - Diarrhea and Behçet's Disease|RLR 063]]).
- They serve overlapping roles in separating inflammatory from functional diarrhea and in flagging fecal white cells.
