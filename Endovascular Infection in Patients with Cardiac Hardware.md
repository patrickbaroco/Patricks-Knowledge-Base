A low threshold for blood cultures, and how to read the results, in patients with indwelling cardiac or dialysis hardware.

## How it operates

- **Low threshold for cultures regardless of classic stigmata.** Any patient with endovascular hardware (ICD, tunneled dialysis catheter) and a nonspecific systemic complaint deserves blood cultures even without fever or Osler's nodes — organisms like coagulase-negative staphylococcus and enterococcus can cause minimally symptomatic bacteremia in this population.
- **Gram stain morphology is near-diagnostic.** Gram-positive cocci in chains or pairs (streptococcus or enterococcus) should essentially never be dismissed as a contaminant; gram-positive cocci in clusters (coagulase-negative staphylococcus) are the most common true contaminant, followed by gram-positive rods (Bacillus, Corynebacterium).
- **A "sticky" organism reaching the bloodstream by an unexpected route.** Streptococcus mitis (also Streptococcus oralis) is part of normal oral flora but is also found throughout the GI tract; its adhesion properties let it bind readily to tissue and to foreign material, giving it a strong predilection for infecting indwelling cardiac hardware once bacteremic, via either an oral or GI mucosal route (the latter especially relevant after gastrectomy or with mucosal disruption such as duodenitis).
- **Hardware management is conditional, not formulaic.** Whether to remove or exchange a tunneled catheter may depend on whether an AV fistula is mature and functional; decisions about explanting an ICD weigh the organism's adherence properties and infection risk against procedural complexity.

## Examples from RLR episodes

- **A benign syndrome that hid real bacteremia.** Blood cultures sent despite a normal white count — justified purely by the burden of indwelling hardware (ICD, tunneled catheter) — grow gram-positive cocci in chains and pairs, speciated as Streptococcus mitis; a prior gastrectomy and CT-demonstrated duodenitis point to GI mucosal translocation as the portal of entry, raising urgent concern for endovascular infection of the ICD.
