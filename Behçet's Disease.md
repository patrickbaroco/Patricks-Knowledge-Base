Behçet's disease is a variable-vessel systemic vasculitis defined clinically by recurrent oral and genital ulceration with ocular, cutaneous, articular, neurologic, vascular, and gastrointestinal involvement.

## Definition and Core Features
Behçet's disease is a chronic, relapsing, multisystem inflammatory disorder classified among the variable-vessel vasculitides (it can involve arteries and veins of any size). Its clinical signature is **recurrent aphthous ulceration**, and the diagnosis is fundamentally clinical.

- **Oral ulcers** are the near-universal, usually first, and most frequent manifestation — painful, recurrent aphthae that heal without scarring.
- **Genital ulcers** are more specific and tend to scar (scrotum, vulva), an important discriminator from ordinary aphthosis.
- **Ocular disease** — classically relapsing **uveitis** (anterior and posterior/panuveitis) and retinal vasculitis — is a leading cause of morbidity and vision loss.
- **Skin lesions** include erythema nodosum, papulopustular/acneiform eruptions, and **pathergy** (see [[Pathergy]]).
- On the RLR case, undisclosed **recurrent oral and genital ulcers** surfaced only after a pathergy reaction prompted targeted questioning ([[RLR 063 - Diarrhea and Behçet's Disease|RLR 063]]).

## Intestinal Behçet's and the IBD Mimic
Gastrointestinal Behçet's ("intestinal Behçet's") is a great mimic of Crohn's disease and can dominate the presentation.

- **Ileocecal predilection** with **deep, focal, "punched-out" ulcers** and a **neutrophilic infiltrate** on biopsy — a picture that overlaps heavily with Crohn's ([[RLR 063 - Diarrhea and Behçet's Disease|RLR 063]]).
- Features favoring Behçet's over Crohn's include few, deep, round ulcers with discrete margins and absence of the diffuse transmural cobblestoning/granulomas more typical of Crohn's, though overlap is real and distinction is often difficult.
- Because it "mimics IBD to a T," intestinal Behçet's should stay on the [[Colitis]] and [[Inflammatory Bowel Disease]] differential, especially in a patient from an endemic region.

## Epidemiology
- Behçet's follows the historical **Silk Road** distribution — highest prevalence from the eastern Mediterranean (Turkey) through the Middle East to East Asia.
- **Japan** has notably high incidence, with the **northern island (Hokkaido)** among the highest in the world; local clinicians reach for it the way others reach for community-acquired pneumonia — an example that **epidemiology reweights autoimmune priors, not just infectious ones** ([[RLR 063 - Diarrhea and Behçet's Disease|RLR 063]], see [[Base Rate]]).
- **HLA-B51** is the strongest known genetic association and is enriched in high-prevalence populations; it is supportive but neither necessary nor sufficient — the diagnosis remains clinical ([[RLR 063 - Diarrhea and Behçet's Disease|RLR 063]]).

## Diagnosis
- **Clinical diagnosis.** The **International Study Group** criteria (and the later International Criteria for Behçet's Disease) are well established: recurrent oral ulceration plus two of — recurrent genital ulceration, eye lesions, skin lesions, or a positive pathergy test.
- There is **no confirmatory laboratory test**; HLA-B51 and pathergy testing support but do not establish the diagnosis.
- Early-onset, familial, or Behçet-like disease in the young should prompt consideration of monogenic autoinflammatory mimics such as **[[A20 Haploinsufficiency]]** ([[RLR 063 - Diarrhea and Behçet's Disease|RLR 063]]).

## Management
- **Colchicine** is a mainstay for mucocutaneous and articular disease and can produce dramatic improvement — the RLR patient improved markedly within three days ([[RLR 063 - Diarrhea and Behçet's Disease|RLR 063]]).
- Organ- and severity-tailored **immunosuppression**: topical/systemic corticosteroids, and steroid-sparing agents (e.g., azathioprine); **TNF-alpha inhibitors** and other biologics for severe ocular, neurologic, vascular, or refractory intestinal disease.
- Sight-threatening, neurologic, and major vascular disease drive aggressive therapy; mucocutaneous-predominant disease is managed more conservatively.
