Evergreen concept note. Colitis is inflammation of the colon; a compact "four I's" schema organizes its causes and drives a disciplined imaging-and-endoscopy workup. RLR / Clinical Problem Solvers teaching points are cited per episode; general clinical detail is scaffolding.

## The Four I's Schema
When colitis is found on imaging or endoscopy, a durable framework sorts the differential into four buckets ([[RLR 063 - Diarrhea and Behçet's Disease|RLR 063]]):

- **Infection** — bacterial (Campylobacter, Salmonella, Shigella, Yersinia, E. coli including STEC, C. difficile), viral (CMV in the immunocompromised), and parasitic (Entamoeba). Some organisms favor the **ileum/right colon** (Yersinia, tuberculosis) ([[RLR 063 - Diarrhea and Behçet's Disease|RLR 063]]).
- **IBD (and immune-mediated)** — Crohn's disease, ulcerative colitis, microscopic colitis, and immune mimics such as **Behçet's** disease (see [[Inflammatory Bowel Disease]], [[Behçet's Disease]]).
- **Infiltrative** — eosinophilic colitis/enteritis and neoplastic infiltration (e.g., lymphoma). A **decade of waxing-and-waning symptoms argues against** an infiltrative/malignant process ([[RLR 063 - Diarrhea and Behçet's Disease|RLR 063]]).
- **Ischemia** — ischemic colitis, classically at watershed zones (splenic flexure, rectosigmoid); usually older patients or those with vascular risk, so it **fits poorly in the young** ([[RLR 063 - Diarrhea and Behçet's Disease|RLR 063]]).

## Reading the Pattern
The **distribution and depth** of inflammation narrow the four I's before biopsy returns.

- **Segmental/watershed** involvement suggests ischemia; **continuous distal** disease suggests ulcerative colitis; **skip lesions with ileal involvement and rectal sparing** suggest Crohn's ([[RLR 063 - Diarrhea and Behçet's Disease|RLR 063]]).
- **Deep, discrete, "punched-out" ulcers** raise Behçet's or Crohn's; **pseudomembranes** raise C. difficile.
- On the RLR case, CT showed **pancolitis plus small-bowel enterocolitis with ileal and splenic-flexure enhancement**, launching the four-I's analysis ([[RLR 063 - Diarrhea and Behçet's Disease|RLR 063]]).

## Approach on Imaging and Endoscopy
- **Cross-sectional imaging (CT/MR enterography)** localizes the inflamed segments, defines small-bowel involvement, and flags complications (perforation, abscess, obstruction).
- **Colonoscopy with biopsy** is the anchor test — it characterizes mucosal ulceration, samples for histology (granulomas, neutrophilic infiltrate, eosinophils, viral inclusions), and reaches the terminal ileum.
- **Exclude infection first** — stool studies (C. difficile, cultures, multiplex/NAAT PCR) precede immunosuppression, because infection can also **trigger** an underlying inflammatory flare ([[RLR 063 - Diarrhea and Behçet's Disease|RLR 063]]).

## Clinical-Reasoning Notes
- Colitis is a **finding, not a diagnosis** — the schema converts a broad CT read into ordered forks (see [[Schema-Based Reasoning]]).
- **Tempo** matters: an acute presentation with a long relapsing history reweights the buckets away from acute infection and toward immune-mediated disease ([[RLR 063 - Diarrhea and Behçet's Disease|RLR 063]], see [[Illness Tempo]]).
