Discriminating a spinal-cord process from a nerve-root (radicular/cauda-equina) process from a peripheral neuropathy — the three compartments a below-the-neck sensorimotor syndrome can occupy.

## The Three Compartments
Once muscle and NMJ are excluded (sensory *and* motor involvement), a syndrome below the neck lives in the **cord**, the **roots (cauda equina)**, or the **peripheral nerves** ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).

- **Cord (myelopathy):** upper motor neuron. A **sensory level** on the trunk, **hyperreflexia**, spasticity, upgoing toes, and early bowel/bladder involvement.
- **Root / cauda equina (radiculopathy):** lower motor neuron in a **dermatomal/myotomal** pattern — radicular pain, segmental weakness, and reflex loss at the involved level.
- **Peripheral nerve (neuropathy):** lower motor neuron in a **length-dependent** stocking-glove pattern (usually), with distal-predominant sensory loss and reflex loss.

## Reflexes as the Pivotal Sign
- **UMN = brisk; LMN = absent/diminished.** A chronic cord process should be *hyperreflexic*; absent reflexes point to root or nerve ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).
- **The level sets the expectation.** Below L1 the canal contains roots (lower motor neurons), so a cauda-equina lesion gives **absent reflexes** — reconciling a "cord-like" story with areflexia ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).
- **Age caveat:** in an elderly patient with expected age-related neuropathy, even *present* ankle reflexes may represent relative hyperreflexia and hint at a superimposed cord process ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).

## The Sensory Pattern
- **Sensory level** (a horizontal cutoff on the trunk) ⇒ cord.
- **Dermatomal band** ⇒ root.
- **Stocking-glove, distal-graded** ⇒ peripheral nerve — unless it is **non-length-dependent** (hands and feet together), which breaks the neuropathy expectation and shifts toward a demyelinating or a central process (see [[Length-Dependent vs Non-Length-Dependent Neuropathy]]) ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).

## Sphincter Involvement
- **Bowel/bladder dysfunction pulls "into the spine."** True urinary retention or fecal incontinence points to cord or cauda equina, not to a peripheral neuropathy, which characteristically **spares** the sphincters ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).
- Use this as a tie-breaker: if bowel/bladder is real, a pure peripheral neuropathy is out.

## Putting It Together
When findings conflict, apply [[Leave-One-Out Reasoning|leave-one-out]] reasoning — provisionally discard one symptom and see whether the rest cohere — and re-examine for the reflexes, sensory level, and sphincter tone that adjudicate between compartments ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).
