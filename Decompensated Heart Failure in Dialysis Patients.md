A structured approach to decompensated heart failure in a hemodialysis-dependent patient.

## How it operates

- **Exclude the three emergent causes first** — an endocardial process (endocarditis or acute valve rupture), an acute myocardial process, and a pericardial effusion — with a murmur check, EKG, and point-of-care ultrasound, before attributing the presentation to the more common, non-emergent explanation.
- **Once emergent causes are excluded, organize around the three A's** — arrhythmia, acute kidney injury, and afterload (e.g., significant hypertension) — all of which can independently or jointly precipitate decompensation in a dialysis-dependent patient, alongside the default explanation of under-dialysis itself ("dialysis is the medication").
- **Missed or shortened dialysis is a diagnosis of exclusion**, not a first-pass default, however common it is in practice.
- **A hardware-laden patient deserves a low threshold for blood cultures** even when the presenting syndrome looks mundane — see [[Endovascular Infection in Patients with Cardiac Hardware]].

## Troponin kinetics

- **Trend it, don't snapshot it.** A troponin that is actively rising and falling signals an evolving vascular process — plaque rupture or increased demand on vulnerable vessels — and should be trended serially alongside EKGs rather than interpreted as a single value.
- **A clear demand driver does not exclude concurrent plaque rupture.** Systemic illness with a catecholamine surge (e.g., sepsis from a respiratory virus) can itself precipitate plaque rupture, so "demand" should not be assumed just because hypertension or volume overload is present.
- **Bedside conversion.** A high-sensitivity troponin can be roughly converted to the older-generation scale by dividing by 1,000 (e.g., 2,000 ≈ 2.0 on the older scale) — useful for comparing values across assay eras or institutions.

## Congestive hepatopathy

- **AST greater than ALT, from right-sided venous congestion, is the leading explanation** for a transaminitis in decompensated heart failure — but an acute congestive process alone would not be expected to elevate the MCV, which typically reflects a more chronic process; an elevated MCV alongside this pattern should prompt consideration of a second, more chronic contributor (e.g., unrecognized alcohol use).
- **AST/ALT can trend fast and reflect a cardiac, not purely hepatic, process** — a prior case where an elevated AST attributed to a biliary source was ultimately traced to an unrecognized STEMI is a cautionary parallel.

## Examples from RLR episodes

- **The default explanation, correctly reached only after ruling out emergencies.** A dialysis patient with two weeks of respiratory symptoms and dark stools is found to have decompensated heart failure; murmur check, EKG, and POCUS exclude the three emergent causes, and reduced dialysis adherence is favored — while a rising-then-falling demand-pattern troponin and an AST-predominant transaminitis with unexplained macrocytosis are worked through as parallel, ultimately secondary, threads to the case's real answer (endovascular infection).
