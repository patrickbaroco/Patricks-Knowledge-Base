Evergreen concept note. A facet of the [[Illness Script]] hub. Source episodes are cited per example.

## Definition

Illness-script command is *fluency* with a script — knowing a disease well enough to deploy it structured and complete at the bedside, including the places where the script predictably fails. Having a diagnosis merely *listed* on a schema is not the same as owning its script well enough to act on it; command is the difference between recognizing a name and being able to run it.

## How it operates

- **Listed ≠ commanded.** A branch can be present on the schema yet be forgotten in the moment or held only tentatively because its script is thin.
- **Command includes the failure modes.** A well-held script carries its own traps — the test that is falsely normal during the acute phase, the reflex to stop at the first negative.
- **A structured script travels.** Organizing a script by fixed questions (how / who / manifestation / what-to-do) makes it retrievable and comparable across related diseases.

## Examples from RLR episodes

- **Listed but not commanded.** The immobilization branch existed on both hosts' hypercalcemia schemas yet was forgotten or held only tentatively — Reza admits he lacks command of that script. ([[RLR 014 - Hypercalcemia and Immobilization|RLR 014]])
- **Command includes the traps.** Reza structures the G6PD script by four questions yet flags where command fails: the *falsely normal* G6PD level during active hemolysis, and stopping at "no G6PD deficiency." ([[RLR 015 - Hemolysis and Heinz Body Hemolytic Anemia|RLR 015]])
- **Command across a family of scripts.** Carrying gout, pseudogout, and RA scripts with their differing joint predilections and cervical manifestations is what lets knee chondrocalcinosis snap the cervical lesion into place. ([[RLR 016 - Neck Pain and Crowned Dens Syndrome|RLR 016]])
