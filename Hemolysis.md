Evergreen concept note. The hemolysis schema and its worked instances across the RLR / Clinical Problem Solvers series. General physiology is scaffolding; teaching points are cited per episode.

> Schema: [CP Solvers — Anemia / hemolysis](https://www.rlrcpsolvers.com/schemas-scripts-and-videos/blood-and-cancer-diagnostic-schemas/)

## Suspecting hemolysis (the two-step)

- **First establish cell destruction, then prove the cells are red.** The first signal is anemia; the confirmatory panel is indirect bilirubin, AST out of proportion to ALT, LDH, reticulocyte count, and a [[Peripheral Blood Smear|smear]] — but a low [[Haptoglobin|haptoglobin]] is the value that "screams" it. A very high LDH establishes *some* cell is being destroyed; the red-cell-specific signs (anemia, indirect hyperbilirubinemia, low haptoglobin, reticulocytosis) prove they are erythrocytes. ([[RLR 015 - Hemolysis and Heinz Body Hemolytic Anemia|RLR 015]], [[RLR 030 - Dark Urine and Delayed Hemolytic Transfusion Reaction|RLR 030]])
- **A normal hemoglobin does not exclude hemolysis.** In [[Compensated Hemolysis|compensated hemolysis]] the marrow replaces cells as fast as they are lost (Rabih's "Aha-can": a small enough hole and the level stays flat) — compensated hemolysis is *slow* hemolysis, reported with low-grade [[Hereditary Spherocytosis|hereditary spherocytosis]] and [[G6PD Deficiency|G6PD]] but essentially never with malaria, babesia, [[Microangiopathic Hemolytic Anemia|MAHA]], or PNH. Reticulocytosis is the marrow's compensation and is a "pulled," not "pushed," finding. ([[RLR 015 - Hemolysis and Heinz Body Hemolytic Anemia|RLR 015]], [[RLR 030 - Dark Urine and Delayed Hemolytic Transfusion Reaction|RLR 030]])
- **The numbers are not binary — an indirect bilirubin from hemolysis is rarely above 5.** Ordinary hemolysis doesn't destroy cells at the rate of a massive hemorrhage, so a bilirubin above 5 implies a *second hit*, most commonly coexisting [[Gilbert Syndrome|Gilbert syndrome]] (the sickle-cell patient with an indirect bili of 13 had transfusion-acquired [[Babesiosis|babesiosis]] plus sickle disease). ([[RLR 015 - Hemolysis and Heinz Body Hemolytic Anemia|RLR 015]])

## The smear as the branch point

- **Every CBC abnormality earns a smear, the way every acid–base question earns a VBG.** In hemolysis the smear localizes the mechanism: schistocytes/helmet cells → [[Microangiopathic Hemolytic Anemia|MAHA]]; spherocytes → [[Autoimmune Hemolytic Anemia|AIHA]] or hereditary spherocytosis; sickle cells → sickle disease; bite cells → [[G6PD Deficiency|G6PD]]; intracellular parasites → babesia/malaria; ghost cells → *Clostridium perfringens*. One schistocyte you were not looking for is one too many. ([[RLR 015 - Hemolysis and Heinz Body Hemolytic Anemia|RLR 015]])
- **Smear-negative hemolytic anemia is the trap you graduate into.** "If it's in the name," the smear is near-100% sensitive (sickle cell → sickling, spherocytosis → spherocytes); "if it's not in the name" (AIHA, MAHA/DIC) it is ~50% and can be blank. When the smear is silent, add the [[Heinz Bodies|Heinz-body stain]] for oxidatively denatured hemoglobin. ([[RLR 015 - Hemolysis and Heinz Body Hemolytic Anemia|RLR 015]])

## Intravascular vs the immune split

- **Intravascular hemolysis is the rarer, narrower category.** Free hemoglobin spills into plasma and urine (heme-positive dipstick with few RBCs — see [[Law of Proportionality|proportionality]]), pointing away from the usual extravascular (splenic) hemolysis; a [[Delayed Hemolytic Transfusion Reaction|delayed hemolytic transfusion reaction]] is a classic cause. ([[RLR 030 - Dark Urine and Delayed Hemolytic Transfusion Reaction|RLR 030]])
- **Structure the immune causes as auto / allo / drug** — see [[Immune-Mediated Hemolysis]] — and split spherocyte-predominant hemolysis with a [[Direct Coombs Test|direct Coombs]]. ([[RLR 030 - Dark Urine and Delayed Hemolytic Transfusion Reaction|RLR 030]])

## Hemolysis as a "liver" and an "anemia+thrombocytopenia" mimic

- **"Liver enzymes" are not liver-specific — AST especially is ubiquitous.** An AST-predominant transaminitis has extrahepatic sources — [[Myocardial Infarction|MI]], [[Rhabdomyolysis|rhabdomyolysis]], hemolysis — so a CK (and the hemoglobin/haptoglobin) belong in the workup; fractionating even a mildly elevated bilirubin redirects it (indirect-predominant → hemolysis vs Gilbert). ([[RLR 020 - Chest Pain and Ascending Cholangitis|RLR 020]], [[RLR 021 - Chronic Pruritus and Primary Biliary Cholangitis|RLR 021]])
- **Anemia with thrombocytopenia should reflexively raise MAHA** — the way chest pain raises ACS/PE/dissection — within the broader [[Anemia and Thrombocytopenia|five-cause schema]]. ([[RLR 015 - Hemolysis and Heinz Body Hemolytic Anemia|RLR 015]], [[RLR 032 - Fever and Chronic Myelomonocytic Leukemia|RLR 032]], [[RLR 033 - Persistent Altered Mental Status and Spinal Epidural Abscess|RLR 033]])
