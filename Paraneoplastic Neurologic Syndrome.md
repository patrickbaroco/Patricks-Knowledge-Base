Immune-mediated neurologic disease triggered by a remote cancer — distinct from direct metastasis — and the antibody framework used to recognize it.

## Definition and Mechanism
A paraneoplastic neurologic syndrome is nervous-system injury caused not by tumor invasion but by an **autoimmune response** in which onconeural antigens shared by the tumor and neurons provoke a cross-reactive attack ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).

- **Often heralds the cancer:** the neurologic syndrome frequently **precedes** the tumor diagnosis, so recognizing it prompts a cancer search.
- **Tempo:** typically subacute and progressive, out of proportion to any visible tumor burden.

## The Antibody Framework
Antibodies fall broadly into two mechanistic groups, which guides prognosis and treatment ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]):

- **Intracellular-antigen ("onconeural") antibodies** — markers of a T-cell–mediated process, less reversible: e.g., **anti-Hu (ANNA-1)** associated with **small-cell lung cancer**, causing sensory neuronopathy, limbic/brainstem encephalitis, and autonomic failure ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).
- **Surface/synaptic-antigen antibodies** — often more treatment-responsive: e.g., NMDA-receptor, VGKC-complex (LGI1/CASPR2) syndromes.
- **GFAP astrocytopathy** presents as a **meningoencephalomyelitis** with characteristic radial perivascular enhancement and can be paraneoplastic ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).

## Representative Syndromes
- **Sensory neuronopathy** (dorsal root ganglionopathy) — non-length-dependent sensory loss (see [[Length-Dependent vs Non-Length-Dependent Neuropathy]]).
- **Limbic/brainstem encephalitis, cerebellar degeneration, opsoclonus-myoclonus, Lambert-Eaton myasthenic syndrome, and myeloneuropathy.**

## The Cancer-and-Neurology Schema
When a cancer patient develops a neurologic problem, sort it into four buckets before reaching for a rare antibody ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]):

- **Unrelated** (a coincidental process — a diagnosis of exclusion).
- **Direct compression or metastasis** (including [[Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|leptomeningeal spread]]).
- **Treatment-related** (chemo/radiation toxicity, immunotherapy effects).
- **Paraneoplastic.**
- **Ask the base-rate question first:** "how could *one* process reach all these sites?" — common metastatic/leptomeningeal spread often explains a multifocal picture better than a rare paraneoplastic syndrome, a guard against the seductive zebra ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).

## Workup and Treatment
- **Serum and CSF paraneoplastic antibody panels**, plus tumor search (CT/PET, age/sex-appropriate screening).
- **Treat the tumor** and add **immunotherapy** (steroids, IVIG/PLEX, and further immunosuppression); surface-antibody syndromes respond best.
