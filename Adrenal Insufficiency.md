Evergreen concept note. Adrenal insufficiency as a recurring "sneaky" cause across the RLR / Clinical Problem Solvers series. General physiology is scaffolding; teaching points are cited per episode.

## Why it hides

Cortisol deficiency produces non-specific, multi-system findings — fatigue, nausea, weakness, hypotension, hyponatremia, hypoglycemia, eosinophilia, and (in primary disease) hyperpigmentation — none pathognomonic, so it lives on several different schemas at once and is easy to miss unless deliberately sought. Distinguish **primary** (adrenal; low cortisol, high ACTH, with aldosterone loss → hyperkalemia and salt-wasting) from **secondary/central** (pituitary-hypothalamic; low ACTH, aldosterone largely intact).

## Where it sits on the schemas

- **On the inappropriate-ADH (high-urine-sodium) hyponatremia list.** One of the five causes of inappropriate ADH; a morning cortisol screens for it, and cortisol deficiency impairs free-water excretion. ([[RLR 003 - Hypertension and Severe Hyponatremia|RLR 003]], [[RLR 029 - Dyspnea and Disseminated Varicella-Zoster|RLR 029]])
- **On the image-negative abdominal pain schema.** When a CT is normal, adrenal insufficiency sits in the *metabolic* branch alongside DKA, hypercalcemia, and acute intermittent porphyria — an extra-abdominal cause of abdominal pain. ([[RLR 019 - Abdominal Pain and Cannabinoid Hyperemesis Syndrome|RLR 019]])
- **As the mirror of cortisol excess.** In the Cushing/adrenal axis warm-up, the same reasoning that localizes cortisol *excess* (pituitary/ectopic ACTH/exogenous) frames the deficiency states and their testing. ([[RLR 026 - Syncope and Occult Gastrointestinal Bleeding|RLR 026]])

## Reasoning points

- **Screen with a morning cortisol, confirm with stimulation.** A clearly low morning cortisol is diagnostic; a normal-ish value in a sick patient does not exclude it, and a cosyntropin (cort-stim) test is often needed — a low pretest probability (no nausea, weakness, eosinophilia, or hyperpigmentation) can down-weight it before testing. ([[RLR 029 - Dyspnea and Disseminated Varicella-Zoster|RLR 029]])
- **Consider it in unexplained hypotension, hyponatremia, or hypoglycemia** — especially with a compatible multi-system story — precisely because it masquerades as a bystander. ([[RLR 003 - Hypertension and Severe Hyponatremia|RLR 003]])
