Approach to a monocytosis.

## The short differential

- **Sustained monocytosis is [[Chronic Myelomonocytic Leukemia|CMML]] until proven otherwise** — everything else is reactive (classically [[Tuberculosis|TB]], and other chronic inflammation/infection). Gurpreet Dhaliwal's teaching: for monocytosis, think CMML, then everything under the sun that inflames. ([[RLR 032 - Fever and Chronic Myelomonocytic Leukemia|RLR 032]])
- **Chronicity is the strongest discriminator** — monocytes persistently >1×10⁹/L for >6 months points hard at a chronic marrow process; a look-back at prior counts is the single most powerful step. ([[RLR 032 - Fever and Chronic Myelomonocytic Leukemia|RLR 032]])

## Reasoning points

- **Verify the monocytes on the smear** — automated counters misclassify monocytes as blasts or activated lymphocytes, so confirm before tunneling into a CMML workup. ([[RLR 032 - Fever and Chronic Myelomonocytic Leukemia|RLR 032]])
- **A striking marrow output in an elderly patient should give pause** — the aging marrow is largely replaced by fat, so a florid leukocytosis/monocytosis is itself a reason to consider a primary white-cell disorder over a purely reactive one. ([[RLR 032 - Fever and Chronic Myelomonocytic Leukemia|RLR 032]])
- **"Monocytes love tissue"** — a blood monocytosis can be accompanied by tissue infiltration ([[Leukemia Cutis|skin]], organs), linking the CBC to a [[Skin Nodule|cutaneous nodule]]. ([[RLR 032 - Fever and Chronic Myelomonocytic Leukemia|RLR 032]])
