Evergreen concept note. Multiple myeloma across the hypercalcemia, hematuria, and bone-lesion episodes. Teaching points cited per episode.

## Workup and imaging

- **Myeloma is purely lytic — pick imaging that sees lysis.** A [[Bone Scan|bone scan]] measures osteoblastic activity and can miss it entirely; a [[Skeletal Survey|skeletal survey]] is better but still misses ~10%, and CT/MRI are first-tier when concern is high. Send an [[Serum Protein Electrophoresis|SPEP]] and serum free light chains; normal SPEP/FLC exclude *secretory* myeloma. ([[RLR 007 - Hypercalcemia|RLR 007]], [[RLR 014 - Hypercalcemia and Immobilization|RLR 014]], [[RLR 028 - Hypercalcemia and Vitamin D Toxicity|RLR 028]], [[RLR 031 - Headache and Gorham-Stout Disease|RLR 031]])
- **Interpret free light chains by the ratio, corrected for renal function.** Both chains up = inflammation; a markedly *asymmetric* elevation = a monoclonal process. ESRD clears kappa more poorly, skewing the ratio toward kappa, so even a mild lambda-predominant skew in a dialysis patient is worrisome. ([[RLR 007 - Hypercalcemia|RLR 007]])

## Tempo and the CRAB trap

- **CRAB encodes myeloma's features but misleads on tempo — hypercalcemia is a *late* event.** Reza's tempo-corrected reordering, [[BARC Mnemonic|"BARC"]] (Bone → Anemia → Renal → Calcium), keeps an early hypercalcemia+anemia+renal picture from being over-read as classic myeloma when the sequence is wrong. ([[RLR 014 - Hypercalcemia and Immobilization|RLR 014]])
- **It sits on the lytic-bone-lesion and the AKI-with-hypercalcemia schemas** — send SPEP/FLC when hypercalcemia meets AKI, or when a solitary [[Lytic Bone Lesion|lytic lesion]] appears (pruned here by a normal SPEP toward a primary bone process). ([[RLR 028 - Hypercalcemia and Vitamin D Toxicity|RLR 028]], [[RLR 031 - Headache and Gorham-Stout Disease|RLR 031]])
