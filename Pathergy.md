Evergreen concept note. Pathergy is an exaggerated, disproportionate inflammatory response of the skin to minor trauma or penetration, serving as a physical-exam clue to certain neutrophilic and autoinflammatory diseases. RLR / Clinical Problem Solvers teaching points are cited per episode; general clinical detail is scaffolding.

## Concept
Pathergy is **hyperreactivity of the skin to minor injury**: a trivial breach of the skin provokes an inflammatory papule, pustule, or ulcer out of proportion to the insult.

- **Disproportionate reaction at a minor-penetration site** is the defining idea — an inflammatory lesion where only a small trauma occurred ([[RLR 063 - Diarrhea and Behçet's Disease|RLR 063]]).
- Common real-world triggers include an **IV/venipuncture site**, **EKG-lead contact points**, and other needle-pricks or adhesive/pressure contacts ([[RLR 063 - Diarrhea and Behçet's Disease|RLR 063]]).
- In the RLR case, a **new erythematous patch at the antecubital IV site** was recognized as pathergy and reframed the entire differential toward Behçet's ([[RLR 063 - Diarrhea and Behçet's Disease|RLR 063]]).

## The Pathergy Test
- A **standardized skin-prick test**: a sterile needle is inserted obliquely into the forearm dermis, and the site is read at **24–48 hours**.
- A **positive** result is formation of an erythematous papule or pustule at the puncture site, reflecting the underlying hyperreactive state.
- Sensitivity is **population-dependent** — historically higher in Mediterranean/Middle Eastern and East Asian cohorts and lower elsewhere — so a negative test does not exclude disease. A positive test is one of the classic supportive criteria for Behçet's.

## Disease Associations
- **Behçet's disease** — pathergy is a recognized diagnostic criterion and a prompt to screen for oral ulcers, genital ulcers, and ocular disease ([[RLR 063 - Diarrhea and Behçet's Disease|RLR 063]], see [[Behçet's Disease]]).
- **Pyoderma gangrenosum** — the pathergy phenomenon underlies the classic caution against aggressive debridement, which can enlarge lesions.
- Other **neutrophilic dermatoses** (e.g., Sweet syndrome) and inflammatory bowel-associated skin disease can display pathergy-like behavior.

## Clinical Value
- Pathergy is a **soft but powerful clue**: a lesion at a monitoring/access site is easy to dismiss as irritation, yet recognizing it can pivot the diagnosis ([[RLR 063 - Diarrhea and Behçet's Disease|RLR 063]]).
- Practically, it should trigger a **targeted mucocutaneous and ocular history** rather than being treated as incidental — an illustration of staying ready to leave a working track when a soft clue appears (see [[Diagnostic Momentum]]).
