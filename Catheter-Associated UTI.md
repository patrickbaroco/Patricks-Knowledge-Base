Catheter-associated UTI (CA-UTI) — how an indwelling Foley rewrites the symptom set and behaves like an obstructed conduit.

## The altered symptom set

- **A catheter connecting bladder to outside removes the usual urinary symptoms.** Dysuria, frequency, and urgency need a bladder that fills and voids; with a Foley in place, a CA-UTI declares itself instead by working *up* the [[Anatomical Approach|catheter anatomy]] — **obstruction first, then suprapubic tenderness, then flank pain** — with extra-urinary inflammation less specific. Don't hunt for dysuria; check the catheter. ([[RLR 034 - Polyuria and Post-Obstructive Nephrogenic Diabetes Insipidus|RLR 034]])
- **A Foley being *in* does not mean it is *working*.** An [[Law of Proportionality|AKI out of proportion]] to the patient's hemodynamics and intake should prompt two bedside checks — watch the bag drain and bladder-scan — for a plugged catheter (e.g., 600 cc behind it). ([[RLR 034 - Polyuria and Post-Obstructive Nephrogenic Diabetes Insipidus|RLR 034]])

## The obstruction–infection loop and the cholangitis analogy

- **The CA-UTI can *cause* the obstruction rather than the reverse.** Debris and inflammation plug the catheter, and the obstructed catheter under pressure then seeds bacteremia — the same physiology as [[Ascending Cholangitis|ascending cholangitis]] (obstruction under pressure). Klebsiella growing in *both* urine and blood is the signature. ([[RLR 034 - Polyuria and Post-Obstructive Nephrogenic Diabetes Insipidus|RLR 034]])
- **Relief resolves it as fast as it declared.** Replacing the catheter plus antibiotics clears the confusion, leukocytosis, and AKI within days — and can be followed by a [[Post-Obstructive Uropathy|post-obstructive diuresis]]. ([[RLR 034 - Polyuria and Post-Obstructive Nephrogenic Diabetes Insipidus|RLR 034]])
