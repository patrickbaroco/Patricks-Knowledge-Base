Evergreen concept note. The "length principle" of peripheral neuropathy — most neuropathies strike the longest axons first — and why breaking that rule is a diagnostic clue. RLR / Clinical Problem Solvers teaching points are cited per episode; general clinical detail is scaffolding.

## The Length Principle
The longest axons are metabolically the most vulnerable, so most **axonal** neuropathies are **length-dependent**: symptoms begin in the **feet**, ascend the shins, and only reach the **fingers** once the legs are involved to about mid-calf ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).

- **Stocking-then-glove sequence:** the classic distal-to-proximal, feet-before-hands gradient.
- **Prototypes:** **diabetic** and **B12-deficiency** axonal neuropathies, alcohol, and most toxic/metabolic neuropathies ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).

## When the Rule Is Broken
**Hands and feet declaring together** (non-length-dependent) is atypical and should redirect the differential ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]):

- **Demyelinating polyradiculoneuropathies** attack roots and nerves non-length-dependently — **acquired demyelinating disease such as [[Guillain-Barré Syndrome|GBS]] and [[CIDP|CIDP]]** can involve proximal and distal, upper and lower limbs early ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).
- **A central (cord or root) process** does not obey the length principle at all; non-length-dependent limb symptoms plus bowel/bladder or a sensory level pull the localization "into the spine" (see [[Spine vs Peripheral Nerve]]) ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).
- **Non-length-dependent sensory patterns** also occur in sensory neuronopathies (dorsal root ganglionopathies), which are frequently **paraneoplastic** (see [[Paraneoplastic Neurologic Syndrome]]).

## Why It Matters Diagnostically
- **A pattern is data.** Recognizing that a distribution is non-length-dependent is what forces the reasoner off the common-neuropathy path (diabetes, B12) and toward demyelinating or central disease ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).
- **Pair with tempo:** an acute non-length-dependent course suggests GBS; a chronic/relapsing one suggests CIDP; a subacute one with red flags raises a malignant or infiltrative cause.

## Confirming the Pattern
- **History:** ask explicitly whether fingers and toes started together or feet-first — patients rarely volunteer the sequence.
- **Electrodiagnostics** distinguish axonal (length-dependent) from demyelinating (conduction block, slowing) physiology and localize to nerve vs root.
