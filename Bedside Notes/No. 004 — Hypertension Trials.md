
> [!summary] TL;DR
> - We don't have much evidence to guide BP targets for frail elderly patients
> - Guidelines vary by society:
>   - **AHA/ACC:** <130/80 for adults over 65
>   - **ESC:** <140/90 for adults over 80
>   - **ACP/AAFP:** SBP <150 for adults over 60; consider <140 for high stroke/CV risk
>   - **ACO quality measure:** <140/90 for adults 18–85 — frailty can be an exclusion (see below); excludes nursing home patients, but not ALF
> - NNT ≈ 100 patients for ~3 years to prevent one major adverse cardiovascular event

## What Do We Know About Treating Hypertension?

In the past 25 years, there have been about a dozen trials evaluating the benefits of blood pressure management. In most of these trials, the very elderly are underrepresented, and none included nursing home residents or patients with dementia. The table below summarizes trials that had at least some participants over 75.

We know that treating hypertension and successfully lowering blood pressure reduces risk of stroke, MI, CHF, and all-cause mortality.

We don't know the ideal treatment target — it's probably <130/80 in ambulatory adults with elevated risk of stroke or CV disease.

The benefit of treating blood pressure takes time to show up. In SPRINT, one of the largest trials, the Kaplan-Meier curves overlapped for over a year (no benefit in year one). A 2022 *JAMA Internal Medicine* meta-analysis (182(6):660–667) found you'd need to treat 100 patients for 34.4 months to prevent one major adverse cardiovascular event (MACE).

## Summary of Hypertension Trials

| Trial | Year | Age ≥75? | Key Findings |
|---|---|---|---|
| HOPE | 2000 | Some | Ramipril vs placebo, high-risk patients, no BP target set; baseline SBP ~139. Reduced MI, stroke, death — suggests benefit of ACEi beyond BP reduction. |
| PROGRESS | 2001 | Yes | Perindopril ± indapamide vs placebo, prior stroke/TIA; BP ~147/86 → ~138/82. Reduced stroke risk, even in normotensive patients. |
| ALLHAT | 2002 | Some | Chlorthalidone vs amlodipine/lisinopril; goal SBP <140. Thiazide superior or equal for major CV outcomes. |
| ASCOT-BPLA | 2005 | Yes | Amlodipine ± perindopril vs atenolol ± thiazide; target <140/90, achieved ~136/75 (amlodipine group). CCB-based regimen reduced stroke and CV events more. |
| HYVET | 2008 | ≥80 only | Target SBP ~150 vs placebo, achieved ~144. Reduced stroke, heart failure, and all-cause mortality in very old patients. |
| ACCORD-BP | 2010 | Some | T2DM patients, SBP <120 vs <140 (achieved ~119 vs ~134). No significant reduction in composite CV events; more adverse effects in intensive group. |
| SPRINT | 2015 | Yes (SPRINT-Senior) | High-risk non-diabetics, SBP <120 vs <140 (achieved ~121 vs ~135). Reduced CV events and mortality, especially in older adults. |
| STEP | 2021 | ≥60 | Chinese adults ≥60; target SBP 110–130 vs 130–150 (achieved ~127 vs ~136). Reduced CV events without excess adverse effects. |
| BPROAD | 2024 | Included ≥75 | T2DM patients ≥50, SBP <120 vs <140 (achieved ~125 vs ~138). Reduced stroke, MI, and heart failure without increased falls or cognitive decline. |

## Thoughts and Takeaways

General theme: lower blood pressure leads to better outcomes, and there's no clear lower limit. Trials targeting SBP <120 (SPRINT, STEP, BPROAD) showed better outcomes in the treatment group, generally without increased adverse effects. (ACCORD showed no benefit of intensive treatment in diabetics, but was felt to be underpowered — BPROAD, from November 2024, fairly conclusively showed benefit of intensive treatment in diabetics.)

That said, our patient population isn't well reflected in this data. STEP and BPROAD included some frail patients, but too few to know if they benefited.

> [!tip] So What Should We Do? My Approach
> - **Does the patient "look like" trial participants?** Cognition intact, ambulatory, could come see you in an office? → Treat per the evidence, target SBP <130.
> - **Is the patient frail, demented, or non-ambulatory?** → No good evidence for tight control; target SBP <150.
> - **Life expectancy <2–3 years?** → Statistically unlikely to benefit from BP-lowering treatment. Low threshold to reduce or eliminate medications, especially ones that may reduce QOL like beta-blockers and diuretics.
> - Note: in these trials, benefit was realized even though not all patients — sometimes a minority — actually hit target. HYVET, the only trial of exclusively elderly adults, achieved an average SBP of 144 mmHg with significant benefit.

> [!note] ACO Coding
> If the patient is frail, code R54. They're excluded from the measure in the setting of:
> - Frailty + medication for dementia
> - Frailty + major illness
> - Frailty + age over 80

> [!important] Bottom Line
> Consider treating HTN even in elderly patients — but the benefit in frailty is unknown and likely low. There's no real benefit in year one and minimal benefit through year three, so weigh prognosis before treating. If you do treat, our best evidence in older adults is HYVET, with a target of <150. <140 is almost always going to be sufficient, and I'd treat to <130 in ambulatory, healthier patients at high risk for stroke/CV disease.
