> [!summary] TL;DR
> - Stop the antiplatelet when you start anticoagulation — unless the patient had a coronary stent in the past year or has a history of stent thrombosis.
> - The safest anticoagulant for our patient population is apixaban.
> - Do not reflexively stop anticoagulation for fall risk, but use apixaban.

## Antiplatelet use in patients on anticoagulation

The 2023 AHA/ACC guidelines are explicit: in patients with AF and chronic coronary disease (beyond 1 year after revascularization, or CAD not requiring revascularization) without history of stent thrombosis, oral anticoagulation monotherapy is recommended over combination OAC + antiplatelet therapy.

> [!rule] Decision rule
> Stop aspirin or clopidogrel when starting anticoagulation — unless the patient had a stent placed in the past year or has a history of stent thrombosis.

## Which anticoagulant to use?

Apixaban carries approximately 30% lower bleeding risk than rivaroxaban or warfarin.

> [!rule] Default anticoagulant
> Use apixaban unless there is a specific contraindication: mechanical heart valve, valvular AF, or antiphospholipid syndrome.

## Isn't aspirin safer? Does it work?

**Aspirin carries the same bleeding risk as apixaban**, though less than rivaroxaban. For primary prevention, aspirin reduces adverse cardiovascular events by 0.41% while increasing major bleeding by 0.47%. The math doesn't support routine use, especially in a population with limited prognosis.

## Fall risk and anticoagulation

> [!data] The falls math
> To tip the risk-benefit balance against anticoagulation:
> - Warfarin: patient would need to fall **35 times per year**
> - Rivaroxaban: **45 times per year**
> - Apixaban: ***458 times per year***

Bleeding risk calculators (HAS-BLED, ATRIA, etc.) are not useful guides here — many bleeding risk factors overlap with stroke risk factors (CHF, hypertension, age), so they can't separate the populations.

> [!tip] If a patient falls that often
> Daily falls should prompt a palliative plan of care conversation — not anticoagulation discontinuation.

## Clinical takeaways

> [!example] How I practice
> - I stop aspirin or clopidogrel quickly. I don't use either for primary prevention in this population, and I always discontinue when starting anticoagulation (absent a recent stent).
> - I use apixaban by default. I use warfarin only when cost or a specific indication requires it (mechanical valve, antiphospholipid syndrome).
> - For patients who fall frequently, anticoagulation benefits outweigh risks unless they are falling at least daily — and if they are, the conversation shifts to goals of care.
