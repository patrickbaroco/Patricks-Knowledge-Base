
*July 1, 2025*

> [!tldr] TL;DR
> Gabapentinoids (gabapentin, pregabalin) are among the top 10 most prescribed drugs in the U.S. — 40-60 million prescriptions a year. The evidence for benefit is thin, and the risks, especially for older adults, are increasingly clear.
>
> - **Modest benefit:** diabetic neuropathy, post-herpetic neuralgia, fibromyalgia (pregabalin only)
> - **No benefit:** chronic low back pain, sciatica, CRPS, pelvic pain, and more
> - **Notable harms:** increased COPD exacerbations, increased hip fracture risk, increased edema/diuretic use
>
> **These are high-risk medications, particularly in older adults.** Monitor closely, reassess often, and look for chances to taper or discontinue.

## Where They Work (at least a little)

> [!success] Modest, real benefit
> |Condition   |Effect Size                               |
> |------------|-------------------------------------------|
> |DPN / PHN   |~2-pt drop on a 10-pt pain scale; NNT ~6-7|
> |Fibromyalgia|42-50% achieve ≥30% pain reduction        |
>
> *Fibromyalgia benefit is pregabalin only — gabapentin has no proven benefit.* Gabapentin is generally less effective and more inconsistent than pregabalin overall.

## Where They Don't Work

> [!failure] No significant benefit — 40+ RCTs and systematic reviews
> - Low back pain
> - Sciatica
> - HIV neuropathy
> - Pelvic pain
> - CRPS
> - Carpal tunnel
> - Postop pain

## Safety Signals

> [!danger] COPD exacerbations
> **40% increased risk of severe exacerbation** (15.1% vs 8.3%) — propensity-matched cohort, n=28,000
> *Ann Int Med 2024;177:144*

> [!danger] Hip fractures
> **30%** increased risk overall · **80%** in patients with frailty · **140%** in patients with CKD
> Likely driven by gait instability and ataxia
> *JAMA Netw Open 2024;7:e2444488*

> [!danger] Edema and diuretics
> **23%** increase in loop diuretic use after initiation — vasodilation → edema → diuretic escalation
> *J Am Geriatr Soc 2024 (Growdon et al.)*

## Clinical Takeaways

> [!question] Is it worth it?
> We reach for gabapentinoids because we lack better tools — not because they're safe or effective.
>
> - Even in the best-evidence conditions, the NNT is ~6-7: for every patient who benefits, several don't
> - Tapering trials should be routine to confirm ongoing benefit
> - **COPD:** is modest pain relief worth a 40% jump in exacerbation risk?
> - **Older adults and patients with frailty:** gabapentinoids contribute to falls, fractures, and possibly mortality
> - **Edema risk:** may worsen heart failure or venous disease

> [!tip] Bottom Line
> - Use sparingly, and only in select indications
> - Monitor closely for adverse effects
> - Educate patients on the real risks
> - Reassess and deprescribe whenever possible
