> [!summary] TL;DR
> - There is no consensus on what agent to use for chemoprophylaxis of venous thromboembolism (VTE) after hip or pelvis fracture
>   - As of October 2025, the only medications with FDA approval are fondaparinux and enoxaparin, but DOACs and aspirin are used more commonly
> - In the PREVENT CLOT trial, aspirin was non-inferior to enoxaparin for prevention of death or PE, and apixaban added only marginal benefit for DVT
> - A 2024 meta-analysis found no benefit of anticoagulants over aspirin
> - An average 100-bed SNF can spend $13–15,000 *per month* just on apixaban

## What is the evidence?

> [!example] PREVENT CLOT trial
> - RCT involving 21 trauma centers in the US/Canada, 12,211 patients
> - Intervention: aspirin 81 mg BID vs enoxaparin 30 mg BID for a mean of 21 days
> - Primary endpoint: all-cause mortality at 90 days
> - Secondary endpoints: DVT, PE, bleeding
> - **Outcome:** no difference in all-cause mortality; small increased risk of DVT with aspirin (0.8%); no difference in PE

> [!example] 2024 meta-analysis — hip fracture
> - 1,194 patients after surgical repair of hip fracture
> - Compared aspirin vs other chemoprophylaxis agents (primarily DOACs and enoxaparin)
> - No difference in incidence of DVT or PE

> [!note] Contrast: TKA/THA literature
> Meta-analyses of studies including primarily patients s/p TKA/THA *without* prior fracture tend to favor enoxaparin over aspirin — lower DVT rates, but no difference in PE or mortality.

## Thoughts and Takeaways

My interpretation of the evidence is that we can be pretty confident that aspirin is as effective as enoxaparin at preventing PE or death after surgical repair of hip fractures. In a meta-analysis looking only at hip fractures, there was no difference in rate of DVT, PE, or death. In the PREVENT CLOT trial, there was no difference in rate of PE or death, and a less than 1% increased risk of DVT.

Many of our patients are at higher bleeding risk, and though the safety of aspirin and enoxaparin was about equal, other studies have shown higher bleeding risk with DOACs compared with aspirin. In a patient with a high risk of bleeding or a history of bleeding, I would recommend aspirin 81 mg BID for VTE prophylaxis.

In a patient who is very high risk for VTE (prior history of VTE, active malignancy, or unable to be mobilized after fracture repair), I would continue to favor apixaban 2.5 mg BID for VTE prophylaxis.

But for the "average" patient, with neither significant bleeding risk nor significant VTE risk, I recommend aspirin 81 mg BID and early mobilization (PT/OT). The PREVENT CLOT trial was large and well-done, and aspirin was as safe as enoxaparin for the "scary" outcomes: PE and death. Though there was a very small increased risk of DVT (without resultant PE), it would cost roughly $52,000 to prevent one (likely distal) DVT.

> [!tip] Bottom line
> My practice is shifting to use aspirin 81 mg BID as first-line VTE prophylaxis, reserving apixaban for patients at particular risk of VTE (prior history, malignancy, or unable to participate in PT/OT).
