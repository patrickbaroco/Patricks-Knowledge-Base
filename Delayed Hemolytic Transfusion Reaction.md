Delayed hemolytic transfusion reaction — the alloimmune branch of [[Immune-Mediated Hemolysis|immune hemolysis]].

## The mechanism and timing

- **Alloimmunization from a prior transfusion, hemolyzing later.** An antigen on red cells transfused (or from a prior pregnancy) drives alloantibody formation; the antibodies then hemolyze the transfused/self cells on re-exposure or as titers rise — often *after* the index hospitalization, so the trigger hides in a previous admission. It is predominantly **intravascular** hemolysis (hemoglobinuria). ([[RLR 030 - Dark Urine and Delayed Hemolytic Transfusion Reaction|RLR 030]])

## Diagnosis and the type-and-screen trap

- **Positive [[Direct Coombs Test|direct Coombs]] plus a positive alloantibody screen**, confirmed by matching the alloantibody to the antigens of previously transfused units. ([[RLR 030 - Dark Urine and Delayed Hemolytic Transfusion Reaction|RLR 030]])
- **Low-titer alloantibodies can be missed on type-and-screen and "wake up" on re-exposure** (antibody evanescence) — so the patient needs the specific alloantibodies flagged for all future crossmatches, and future transfusion uses antigen-matched (and leukoreduced) blood. ([[RLR 030 - Dark Urine and Delayed Hemolytic Transfusion Reaction|RLR 030]])

## Downstream and management

- **Read the cascade as one process** — hemoglobinuria (the [[Law of Proportionality|"large blood, few RBCs"]] dipstick), symptomatic anemia, [[Type 2 Myocardial Infarction|demand ischemia]], and [[Pigment Nephropathy|pigment]]/prerenal AKI — an [[Hickam's Dictum|AND, not OR]]. Treatment is steroids, antigen-matched/leukoreduced blood, and lifelong antibody flagging. ([[RLR 030 - Dark Urine and Delayed Hemolytic Transfusion Reaction|RLR 030]])
