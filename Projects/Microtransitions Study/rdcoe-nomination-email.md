Hi all,

I'm looking for two or three skilled nursing facilities from each of your regions for a research project we've agreed to host, and I could use your read on which buildings are the right fit.

The short version is that Boston University has a team studying what they call microtransitions — the small trips residents take out of the building for a clinic visit, dialysis, imaging, or just lunch with family. About half of those clinic visits come back to us with no note and no after-visit summary, and our staff are still expected to carry out whatever orders came out of the visit. They have built a sign-out form that goes out with the resident and comes back to the nursing team, and they want to run it in twelve of our facilities for a year. It is grant funded, so there is no cost to anybody, and there is no control group — every building gets it, they just stagger when the nursing teams start receiving the forms.

What I like about it is how little it asks of us. The investigators do essentially all of the work. They train the staff, about thirty minutes, and they do all of the chart review themselves. The front desk fills out the form when someone leaves the building, which takes a couple of minutes. And all our medical director has to do is make the introductions at QAPI and let them take it from there.

So the two things that make a building a good candidate are a front desk that is staffed during the day and a DON who will actually engage with it. Everything else we can work around.

If you can send me a facility name, the DON, the administrator and our medical director by Wednesday the 16th, I will pass the list along. A sentence on why you picked it is helpful. Naming a building does not commit it to anything — the study team presents at QAPI and leadership decides from there.

I have attached their slides and a FAQ if you want more detail, or something to hand to a building.

Thanks,
Patrick
