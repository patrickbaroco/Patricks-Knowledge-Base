2. **What the numbers show (10 min).** Residents 65+ with dementia on an AP, as a share of residents with dementia; August PIMS; 1,202 buildings. Target for scoring is 10%.
   - **Concentration.** 24% of residents with dementia are on an AP (5,394 of 22,333). Reaching 10% means 3,161 fewer. 50 buildings, 4% of 1,202, hold nearly a quarter of that excess (752). 40 buildings reach 20%, 54 reach 25%, 151 reach half. The same 50 hold 17% of the residents on an AP; the excess is more concentrated than the use. NC has 42% of residents with dementia, 56% of the excess, and 37 of the top 50.

The spread inside each fifth is wider than the climb across them: 5–35% in the lowest, 11–55% in the highest. The data cannot say how much of the gradient is case mix and how much is practice. ## Opening

Friday's spreadsheet is partly about antipsychotics and partly about how I'd like us to approach quality work. We choose a measure that matters, build a rate we can defend, and use it to decide where attention goes: which buildings need a closer look, which need education, and which are doing something the rest of us should learn. Antipsychotic use in dementia is the first place I've tried it. I'd like to carry it all the way through on this one, including going to see what the low-use buildings do, and then judge whether the method earns a second use. Nothing about the method needs deciding today. What I'd like from this group is where you think it breaks.

If asked whether this becomes the model: "It might. I'd rather answer that after we've seen it work once."
