## Outcome of the 9/25 call

**Sites.** ANR (Fishersville) and King's Daughters (Staunton), about 15 miles apart, so one provider can cover both in a day. Ashley has already agreed to these two. Both carry a high skilled census and high acuity, and both communicate well with Patrick if something goes wrong.

**Model.** The Monday–Friday continuity model stays as it is. The weekend provider does urgent-care-type work from a defined list: late-Friday admissions, discharge medication reconciliation, and residents flagged as high risk for rehospitalization (Steve's idea of a separate visit cadence for that group). Absorbing Monday's routine visits is out of scope; Jess and Emily raised that concern in Steve's poll. 7-on/7-off (Universal Health's model) is rejected, and Ashley agrees.

**Timing.** Arrive after the first med pass and stay into the afternoon. The building deteriorates from about 3pm, Friday through Sunday. An in-at-7:30, out-at-9:30 visit will not do the job.

**Metrics.** Return to hospital overall; return to hospital from Saturday through Tuesday, to catch the downstream effect of weekend intervention; Part A medication spend, since earlier medication review shortens the lag on changes such as apixaban to aspirin for DVT prophylaxis in a patient at low risk (roughly $30 saved per instance over three days).

**Staffing.** Recruitment has it as high priority and will accept 1099 providers to start. No job posting yet as of 9/25; Barbara is working on it. Steve polled Emily, JC, Crystal O'Connor, Rachel, Shallon and Jess Hall: all support weekend coverage, none want to do it. Consensus is a salary or a guaranteed minimum hourly rate.

**Urgency.** Rahul's is higher than Ashley's, driven by year-to-date loss of business. Ashley expects a process and was not troubled by it.

**Owners.** Rahul emails Mark (cc Steve, Kristen) on compensation. Rahul and Steve send Patrick a timeline by Tue/Wed 9/29–9/30. Patrick emails Ashley once that arrives, cc Rahul and Steve, after a heads-up to Alyssa.
