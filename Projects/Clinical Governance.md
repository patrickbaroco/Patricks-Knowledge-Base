## Context

Building a real peer review program for Eventus, as part of the new Chair of Clinical Excellence role. Peer review is the evaluation of an individual practitioner's clinical performance by other practitioners, for the purpose of assessing and improving care — the review arm that sits alongside the standard-setting work tracked in [[Clinical Quality Framework]].

This project exists because peer review stopped being an abstraction on 2026-08-20. Jeff Wells (compliance officer / in-house counsel) met on it, went well, and is **eager to expand the process and eager to be a partner in it** — which is close to the best possible starting position, since the legal architecture is exactly what this program is gated on and Jeff is the person who can unlock it.

The intellectual groundwork is already done and is substantial: [[medical-peer-review-primer]] (`Clinical Governance/medical-peer-review-primer.md`, written 8/18, edited 8/19) covers foundations, legal architecture (HCQIA, NPDB, state privilege statutes), and program design — 40+ sections, written for a physician chairing a program with no prior legal training. That note is the reference; this note is the project.

**This is the first rebuild out of the parked Chair of Clinical Excellence structure.** On 2026-08-16 five clinical-excellence drafts were pulled out of the vault into `Projects/_Parked/clinical-excellence-drafts-2026-08-14/` — not abandoned, but parked, because they rested on too many assumptions to build on. The plan was to rebuild them one at a time as real projects. Peer review picked itself: it's the one that acquired a partner, a meeting, and a 63KB reference on its own. The parked draft `Peer Review Council.md` is the source material to mine, not the plan to follow.

## Open questions

- **The gating question:** does peer review conducted by Eventus — a multi-state clinical group, not a hospital medical staff — carry statutory peer review privilege in each state we operate in? Peer review protection statutes were largely written around hospital medical staff structures; their extension to physician groups, and to APCs specifically, varies considerably by state. The realistic answer is partial or uncertain protection in at least some states, so the program should be designed to the most restrictive one. **Needs to come from Jeff Wells in writing.**
- What Jeff actually meant by "expand the process" — is there an existing peer review process at Eventus today, and if so what is it and who owns it? (His eagerness implies something exists to expand. Worth pinning down before designing from scratch.)
- Committee structure: who sits on it, how members are selected, how APCs are represented
- Case identification — how cases reach review (referral, trigger criteria, random sample, or some combination). **A concrete input appeared 2026-09-03: Sandra Jurani's ACO team keeps a standing informal list of physicians they have trouble with, and she offered it.** Decide whether that list is a referral source, a trigger-criteria input, or something the PRC should deliberately not touch until the privilege question is answered.

- How this relates formally to the [[Clinical Quality Framework]] umbrella and to the ACO quality work
- Whether the Clinical Quality Council (the other parked draft) needs to exist before or after this
