- 2026-09-03 (afternoon): **The framework has an executive sponsor and a committee architecture.** Cary Trainor endorsed the whole structure — see [[Cary Trainor Connect — Clinical Governance, 2026-09-03]]. Everything below is now settled at the COO level rather than being Patrick's private design.

    **The architecture.** Primary care and psychiatry function as **departments**: each owns its own priorities and measures and brings them up, rather than everyone hashing out anxiolytics one week and transitions of care the next. **Quality Committee** is the clearinghouse — fed by the ACO, it defines the measure (*not "high quality," but A1c under nine and what percentage should be under it*), publicizes it, builds the education, and designs the audit. Traffic is bidirectional: Quality hands down priorities and asks the departments *what would you choose as a measure for this*. Composition: department chairs, distinguished physicians as they roll out, APCs, and **someone from education**. Cary questioned whether the department layer is needed with only two disciplines, and accepted the answer — it is what brings the broader clinical community into the decisions instead of leaving them to a few distinguished roles.

    **Education pairs with Quality, hand in glove** — *"you don't have quality if you don't have good education"* — and today they are not synchronized. The concrete symptom: the ACO holds the fourth Wednesday of each month for its own education slot, and **nobody is jumping up and down to attend the ACO education meeting.** It should dissolve into specialty didactics: if heart failure is the six-month initiative because it drives return-to-hospital, the Wednesdays track heart failure, AKI, and so on. This is the same argument made to Sandra that morning, now with the COO behind it.

    **Signature standards audits go away** — confirmed with Cary, not just proposed. Easy to game at best and a very weak signal: *"if you can't manage to pass that, then we have big problems. You need to learn how to use a text expander."* Replaced by real quality audits run through the Quality Committee.

    **Other forums get named for what they are.** The experienced clinician roundtable is in practice where people ask random questions — fine, and arguably better as a monitored Teams channel, but nobody knows what it currently is.

- 2026-09-03: **The clinical pathways don't reach anyone, and there is now a number for it.** Patrick polled the Virginia APCs: **19 respondents, 3 knew where the pathways were at all, 1 had ever looked at more than one.** Cause is distribution, not content — PDFs on a wiki that is hard to reach and miserable to read on a phone, which is where clinicians actually look. Cary herself needed a moment to place what a clinical pathway was.

    **The replacement model is saturation, not a channel.** *"Right now our mission is heart failure. We should just bombard people with heart failure. It's going to be hard to not come across some education about heart failure today."* Seminars, Thursday meetings, newsletters (short, so they get read), a one-page doorway version of each handbook chapter, and an iPhone app — Will's assessment is that it isn't rocket science, and the content is just text and links. **None of this coordination exists today, and building it is a deliverable of this framework.** Handbook side tracked in [[Clinical Handbook]].

Three holds is not a procrastination pattern; it is the system reporting that a **goal was filed as a project**. Projects have a next action and get flagged for lacking one. Goals do not have next actions — they have projects, and this one already had four: [[Clinical Governance]], [[Acuity-Based SNF Scheduling]], [[Hospice Utilization]], [[Clinical Handbook]]. Two of them changed status on 9/4 without this note doing anything, which is exactly how a goal is supposed to behave.

It accumulates settled decisions continuously and produces no step — which is the definition of the type, not a failure of the note.

    **What changed mechanically:** `project:` → `goal:`, `type: goal` added, `status` and `next_action` **removed**, and a `projects:` list added. Dropping `status` is deliberate and load-bearing: the kanban board's membership test and the Dashboard's Dataview blocks both key on `status`, so removing it keeps this note off the board and out of the staleness checks without needing an exception written into either. `todoist_label: prj-quality` also removed — the label had already been retired in Todoist, and a goal should not own tasks; its projects do.
