# Paper Retention Reference

Linked from [[Whole-House Declutter & Reorganization]]. Use this during the home office/papers phase to sort into the three piles: **shred**, **scan-then-shred**, or **keep physical**. When in doubt on a specific document, default to scan-then-shred rather than shredding outright — the scanned archive costs almost nothing to keep.

## Keep physical — true originals only (fireproof box)

- Birth certificates, death certificates, marriage/divorce certificates
- Social Security cards
- Passports (current)
- Wills, trusts, powers of attorney, estate planning documents
- Property deeds and vehicle titles
- Adoption/citizenship papers
- Military discharge papers
- Life insurance policies

## Tax records — IRS guidance

- **3 years** from the filing date — the general rule for most returns (IRS's standard statute of limitations for audit).
- **6 years** — if you may have underreported income by more than 25%.
- **7 years** — records related to a bad-debt deduction or worthless-securities loss.
- **Indefinitely** — if a return was never filed, or if it was fraudulent.
- **Keep permanently, physical or scanned**: the tax return itself (not necessarily every backup receipt) and records tied to home purchase/sale, home improvements, and investment cost basis — needed until the asset is sold plus the applicable window above.

Once a year passes its window, the supporting documents (receipts, W-2s, 1099s) can be shredded; many people keep the filed return itself indefinitely as a scanned record since it's compact.

## Financial records

- **Pay stubs**: keep until reconciled against the year's W-2/tax return, then shred (or scan if needed for a loan application soon).
- **Bank/credit card statements**: 1 year, unless tied to a tax deduction or ongoing dispute (then follow the tax rule above).
- **Investment/retirement account statements**: keep annual summaries until the account closes or the asset sells; monthly statements can go once reconciled.
- **Loan documents (mortgage, auto, student)**: keep until the loan is paid off, then keep the payoff/satisfaction letter permanently.
- **Home improvement receipts**: keep for as long as you own the home plus the tax window after selling (they adjust your cost basis).
- **Warranties/receipts for major purchases**: keep until the warranty expires or the item is no longer owned.

## Medical records

- **Explanation of Benefits (EOBs) / medical bills**: keep 1 year after payment/resolution, or until reconciled with insurance and taxes if claimed as a deduction.
- **Major diagnoses, surgeries, immunization records**: keep indefinitely — these matter for future care.
- **Health insurance policies**: keep while active; discard once replaced/expired, unless a claim is pending.
- **Prescription records**: keep 1 year or as needed for reference.

## Household/vehicle records

- **Utility bills**: 1 year, unless needed for a home-office deduction (then follow tax rule).
- **Insurance policies (home, auto, umbrella)**: keep current policy; discard prior policies once the new one is active and any claims from that period are closed.
- **Vehicle maintenance records**: keep while you own the vehicle — useful for resale.
- **Appliance manuals**: keep only while you own the appliance; most are available as PDFs online (safe to shred and skip scanning).

## Building the scanned archive

- Folder: `Household/Documents`, subfoldered by category (Tax, Property, Insurance, Medical, Vehicles).
- Naming convention: `YYYY-MM-DD_Category_ShortDescription.pdf` (e.g. `2026-04-15_Tax_2025-Federal-Return.pdf`).
- Scan before shredding anything in the "scan-then-shred" pile; only the fireproof-box list above stays physical-only.
