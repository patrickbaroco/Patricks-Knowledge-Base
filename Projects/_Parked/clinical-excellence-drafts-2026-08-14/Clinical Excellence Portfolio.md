# Clinical Excellence — Project Portfolio

Companion to [[Chair of Clinical Excellence]]. Eleven candidate initiatives drawn from the role mandate and from work already in flight, scored and sequenced.

## Scoring

Three axes, 1–5 each.

- **Impact** — how directly it moves the metrics named in the spec (rehospitalization, falls with injury, behavioral health outcomes, potentially inappropriate medications, hospice utilization, SNF quality, ACO performance)
- **Feasibility** — can it produce something real inside 90 days with the people and data available now
- **Foundation** — how many other initiatives are blocked until this exists

Foundation is weighted heaviest in the first two quarters. It's the axis most often ignored, and ignoring it is why enterprise quality roles spend year one producing documents nobody implements.

## Now — first 90 days

**1. Enterprise metric pack.** Everything else is downstream. Until there is one agreed set of numbers with agreed definitions and agreed attribution rules, every variation conversation ends in methodology arguments. Tracked in [[Clinical Quality Framework]].

**2 & 3. Both councils.** These are the role's authority. Chartering them properly is the highest-leverage writing of the year — membership and decision rights determine whether the role can actually decide anything or is a standing recommendation service. See [[Clinical Quality Council]] and [[Peer Review Council]].

**4. Pathway template plus one pathway.** The template matters more than the first pathway, because it sets the format every subsequent standard inherits: what a pathway contains, who approves it, how it gets versioned, how exceptions get documented.

> [!tip] Why UTI/ASB should be the first pathway
> Four reasons, in order of weight. **You already own the intellectual content** — the UTI-as-spectrum framework (Stages 0–5) and the Loeb delirium point are yours and already written; you're not starting from a literature review. **The variation is enormous and everyone knows it**, so the pathway lands as an answer to a real problem rather than as a mandate. **It touches three of the seven named metrics at once** — antibiotic-driven rehospitalization, PIM/antimicrobial stewardship, and behavioral health via the delirium pathway. And **it's a demonstration of method**: a standard that reduces antibiotic use is a standard that visibly does *less* rather than more, which is the credibility-building version of a first standard.
>
> The alternative first choice is falls with injury. Higher facility salience, but Eventus's causal share is thinner and the data is confounded by facility reporting rates. Better as pathway two or three.

## Next — Q2 of the role (roughly Nov 2026 – Feb 2027)

**5. Deprescribing / PIM program.** Highest impact-per-effort in the portfolio and the polypharmacy/deprescribing chapter of [[Clinical Handbook]] is already drafted. Monica plus existing content plus a metric that's genuinely measurable from med lists. This is the initiative most likely to produce a publishable enterprise result inside a year.

**6. Education translation engine.** Not a course library — a *pipeline*. The standing process by which a council finding becomes a module within 30 days. Bedside Notes already exists as a distribution channel with an established audience; the question is whether it becomes the official vehicle or stays a parallel personal channel. Worth deciding deliberately rather than by drift.

**7. Acuity-based SNF scheduling.** Already an active project ([[Acuity-Based SNF Scheduling]]), on hold past its 8/10 revisit date. It belongs here rather than in "Now" — it's an operational redesign requiring Rahul's engagement and a current-state map that doesn't exist yet, and it competes for the same attention as the council stand-up. Restarting it in Q2 with council backing is stronger than restarting it now without.

**8. Acute change in condition → transfer avoidance.** The biggest single lever on rehospitalization. Sequenced after the template exists because it's a hard pathway — multi-stakeholder, facility-dependent, and the failure mode is a pathway that reads well and gets ignored at 2am.

## Later — Q3 onward

**9. Clinician performance & variation reporting.** This is the one to be disciplined about. "Reduce unwarranted clinical variation" and "strengthen physician accountability" sit in the mandate, and the instinctive first move is a provider scorecard. Doing it early fails in a specific and predictable way: clinicians dispute the risk adjustment, the conversation becomes about the data instead of the care, and the role burns the physician credibility it runs on. The order has to be — trusted data, then a shared standard to be measured against, then measurement of individuals. Anything else is measuring people against a standard that was never agreed.

**10. ACO integration.** Groundwork happens now through the monthly Sandra cadence; it becomes its own initiative once the metric pack can speak to ACO measures without a separate build.

**11. Documentation integrity.** Real, in the spec, and chronically the thing that gets started first because it feels tractable. It isn't foundational. Hold it.

## Sequencing argument in one line

Instrument, then govern, then standardize, then educate, then — only then — measure individuals.
