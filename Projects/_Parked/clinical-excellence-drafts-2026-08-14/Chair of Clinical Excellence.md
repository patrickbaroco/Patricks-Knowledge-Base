## The governing constraint

No line authority over the markets. Regional CMOs don't report here; facility operations don't report here. Behavior change in the field has to be produced through influence.

The exception, and it's a significant one: **Patrick chairs Clinical Quality.** That team is directable. So the role isn't purely influence-based — it has one lane of real authority and one function whose work can be assigned rather than negotiated.

Four levers, and everything in this project should be traceable to one of them:

1. **The councils** — the only place where "the enterprise decided this" is a true sentence. Formal decision rights are the substitute for org-chart authority over the markets.
2. **The metric pack** — a shared, trusted version of the truth. Without it, every variation conversation collapses into "my patients are sicker."
3. **Education** — the only mechanism that scales behavior change across six states and hundreds of clinicians.
4. **The Clinical Quality team** — the only execution capacity under direct control. What converts a council decision into work that actually gets tracked to closure across six states.

The corollary: meeting cadence is not administrative overhead in this role. It *is* the operating structure. A standing call with Analytics that produces a monthly metric pack is worth more than any document written alone.

> [!warning] The capacity question this raises
> Chairing Quality solves the follow-through problem and creates a different one. That team already has a workplan — survey readiness, MDS and measure reporting, incident tracking, QAPI support. Council-driven improvement work lands *on top* of it. Before the first council meeting generates its first assignment, find out what the team's actual remaining capacity is. The failure mode is a council that decides at a rate the quality team cannot absorb, which converts a functioning department into a bottleneck and makes the council look ineffective when the cause is upstream.

## Explicitly not doing in year one

Writing this down because the spec is large enough to swallow the year, and because declining work later is easier when the decline was pre-committed.

- **Provider-level performance scorecards.** Not until the metric pack is trusted and the peer review culture is established. Publishing clinician rankings off data physicians don't believe is the single fastest way to spend all the credibility this role runs on. Target: Q2 next year at the earliest.
- **Rewriting the full pathway library.** Three pathways done properly beats fifteen drafted and ignored.
- **Owning operational escalations.** Clinical standards, not clinical operations. Route to Rahul.
- **National trade organization representation.** In the spec, real, and a year-two activity. Nothing external until the internal structure holds.
