## Context

Patrick chairs the Clinical Quality Council. In a role without line authority over the markets, this council is the mechanism by which "the enterprise decided this" becomes a true statement. Chartering it well is the highest-leverage document of the first 90 days.

Draft charter below. Nothing here is settled until Sandeep signs off on decision rights.

## Draft charter

### Purpose

Set enterprise clinical priorities, review quality and utilization performance, and approve the clinical standards that govern how Eventus clinicians practice.

### Decision rights — the question that determines everything

Three possible models. **Model B is the recommendation.**

| Model | What it means | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| A — Advisory | Council recommends; CMO decides | Safe, weak. Every standard needs a second approval, and the council learns its output is provisional |
| **B — Delegated authority within scope** | Council approves clinical standards, pathways, and quality priorities. CMO retains veto and owns anything touching compensation, staffing, or contracts | Real teeth inside a bounded lane. The lane is exactly what the role spec describes |
| C — Full authority | Council decides, including operational consequences | Overreaches. Puts the council into Rahul's and the markets' territory and generates resistance the role can't absorb |

Get Model B written into the charter explicitly. A council that discovers its own authority meeting by meeting spends its first year negotiating instead of deciding.

### Membership

Keep it small enough to decide. Ten to twelve voting members is the ceiling.

**Voting**
- Chair — Patrick
- Regional CMOs (one seat each)
- Clinical Quality — one seat, filled by the senior member of the team Patrick chairs. Not a second Patrick vote; this seat exists so the team's regulatory and survey perspective is in the room when standards are set
- Pharmacy — Monica Leriger
- APC leadership — two seats, and these should be practicing APCs, not solely administrative leaders
- Behavioral health representation — one seat, given how much of the metric set is BH-adjacent

**Standing non-voting**
- Analytics — Jacob Lindbert (presents the pack; does not vote on clinical standards)
- Learning & Development — Will Sampson (owns translation of every decision)
- Clinical Operations — Rahul Kapoor (feasibility)
- ACO — Sandra

**Ex officio**
- CMO — Sandeep

> [!note] On the APC seats
> Eventus is an APC-led model. A quality council that is all physicians setting standards for APC practice will be received exactly as that, and will produce standards that don't survive contact with the actual visit. Two practicing APC seats is the minimum; consider three.

### Cadence

Monthly, 90 minutes, week 3 of the operating loop in [[Clinical Excellence Cadence Map]]. Quarterly sessions extend to two hours for the priority review.

Pre-read distributed no later than 72 hours ahead, containing the metric pack and any standard up for approval. **No pre-read, no meeting** — cancel rather than convene without one. Two anecdote-driven meetings in a row and the members stop preparing.

### Standing agenda

1. **Metric review** (25 min) — the pack. What moved, what's signal, what's noise
2. **Decision items** (35 min) — standards, pathways, and priorities up for approval. Each arrives with a written recommendation, not an open discussion prompt
3. **Improvement initiative check-in** (15 min) — status of active work, by exception only
4. **Emerging signal** (10 min) — one item surfaced from peer review, the field, or regulatory change
5. **Commitments** (5 min) — what was decided, who owns it, by when

### Outputs

Every meeting produces three things or it didn't happen:

- A decision log entry — what was decided, the rationale, effective date
- A translation assignment to Will — how the decision becomes education, within 30 days
- A communication to the markets, sent by the Regional CMO for their own market rather than centrally. Standards land better in the voice of the local physician leader.

### First-year agenda arc

- **Meeting 1 (target Oct 2026)** — charter ratification, metric pack v1 walkthrough, agree the first two enterprise priorities. No standards approved. The first meeting's job is to establish that the pack is real and the process is serious
- **Meeting 2** — pathway template approval; first pathway (UTI/ASB) introduced for comment
- **Meeting 3** — first pathway approved; first peer review theme presented
- **Meetings 4–6** — pathways two and three; first quarterly priority review
- **Meetings 7–12** — deprescribing program; first look at variation data at the *market* level, not the individual level

### Failure modes to design against

- **The council becomes a report-out.** Everyone presents, nobody decides. Guard: every agenda item has a named decision or it doesn't get the slot.
- **The council decides things the markets ignore.** Guard: Regional CMOs are voting members and communicate decisions in their own voice.
- **The pack becomes a ritual.** Guard: rotate which metric gets the deep dive; three of the five metric slots each month should be pass-over.
- **Membership creeps.** Guard: the ceiling is twelve voting members, written into the charter.
