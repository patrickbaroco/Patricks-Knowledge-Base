## Context

Senate Finance Committee minority staff released "Delivering an Affordable Home Care Guarantee for American Families," a request for information on long-term care reform, on 2026-10-16. Samantha Koehler, Senior Health Policy Advisor to Senator Kaine, circulated it on 2026-10-20 and asked recipients to share it with Virginia organizations. Allison forwarded it from VDH the same day.

The RFI comes from Ranking Member Wyden and Finance Committee Democratic staff. It grows out of a May 20, 2026 Dear Colleague letter that Kaine signed with 16 other senators; Klobuchar and Markey joined later. Staff from those offices held more than 95 listening sessions with 180 organizations, physicians among them. It is the third RFI in a series, after drug pricing (June) and health coverage (July).

**Submission.** Email to homecare@finance.senate.gov by **2026-12-18**. Name the section or sections the response addresses. The RFI asks for detailed comments, data, and specific policy proposals.

**Source.** [SFC Long-Term Care RFI, 2026-10-16](https://www.finance.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/101626_sfc_long-term_care_rfi.pdf). Local copy: [[SFC Long-Term Care RFI 2026-10-16.pdf]]. Page numbers below are the PDF's.

## The RFI

Five principles frame it: universality, simplicity, flexibility in choice of setting, workforce investment, and safeguards against fraud and corporate greed. Hospital-at-home, SNF-at-home, hospital-to-post-acute transitions, Medicare home health payment, and public LTC insurance are out of scope and will be reviewed separately later (p. 38).

**Section I. Home care affordable and accessible (pp. 8–39).** A universal benefit built on Medicare, on Medicaid through the HCBS Access Act (S. 5321), or within Medicare for All; eligibility by functional need, with the HCBS Access Act threshold of two ADLs, two IADLs, or one of each; independent federal eligibility assessment; scope of services, tiers, or a fixed weekly hour guarantee (Medicare at Home Act, 20 hours); cost-sharing by income with an out-of-pocket cap, asset limits, estate recovery, and a buy-in; appeal rights and program integrity; participant direction and paid family caregivers; MLTSS, FIDE SNPs, and scaling PACE; payment rates and provider networks; administrative simplification, Older Americans Act and disability networks, state maintenance of effort; transitions between home and institution.

**Section II. Quality of care in nursing homes (pp. 40–63).**
- *i. Taxpayer dollars to direct care (pp. 42–47).* Extend direct-care spending reporting to Medicare and set a minimum share of payment for direct care; reinstate CMS ownership reporting and Corporate Transparency Act beneficial ownership; limits on PE and corporate control; operator track record in CHOW/CHOP review; HCRIS sufficiency and consolidated cost reports; related-party benchmarks for management fees, pharmacy services administration, leases and home office costs; a private right of action under the Nursing Home Reform Act.
- *ii. Staffing standards (pp. 47–51).* The federal floor is still 18 minutes of direct care per resident day after the 2024 rule was rescinded in December 2025. The Nurses Belong in Nursing Homes Act (S. 3886) proposes a 24/7 RN, 3.5 nursing hours per resident day, evidence-based updates, mandatory survey funding, and civil monetary penalties reinvested in recruitment. Questions on update frequency, dementia populations, acuity, outcome measures, telehealth, alternatives to a federal standard, and Illinois's STRIVE-based incentive payments.
- *iii. Quality of care (pp. 52–63).* MDS and cost report accuracy, with payment reductions for inaccurate data; whistleblower support; $500,000 surety bonds; a ban on pre-dispute arbitration; state oversight dashboards (Minnesota, Ohio, New Jersey); an LGBTQ+ LTC bill of rights; cultural and language competency as a quality metric; less predictable surveys; Joint Commission deemed status; mandatory survey and ombudsman funding; CMS's risk-based streamlined survey; expanding the Special Focus Facility program to 5% of facilities; Five-Star reweighting.

**Section III. LTC workforce (pp. 64–81).** *i.* Wages and benefits: the Medicaid Access Rule's 80/20 home care pass-through, rate transparency, reversing the DoL minimum wage and contractor rules, Illinois's CNA pay scale, home care registries. *ii.* Training: state models (Massachusetts, Virginia, New York, Iowa), dementia training, CNA training hours (75, 16 clinical), the two-year training lockout, nursing faculty, a portable credential, review of requirements every 2–4 years. *iii.* Immigrant workforce: visa pathways and TPS. *iv.* Family caregivers: tax credits and paid leave. *v.* Caregivers in the care team: discharge planning, EHR access, CMMI tests of paid participant direction, the National Family Caregiver Support Program, the Village model.

## Flags

- **S. 5419 and corporate control of medical practices (II.i, p. 45).** The Stop Corporate Takeovers of Physicians Act (Warren, Wyden) would restrict private equity funds, for-profit corporations and insurers from controlling medical practices. The RFI asks about applying similar limits to nursing homes, how to define PE and corporate owners, and which ownership structures raise quality concerns. Eventus is a PE-owned physician practice, so this touches the employer in whatever capacity the response is written.
- **The physician is almost absent (pp. 40, 64).** Physician supervision of each resident's care appears once, as a Nursing Home Reform Act standard. The Medical Director appears once, in a sentence on who oversees nursing home staff. Nurse practitioners are not mentioned. None of the staffing, quality, survey, SFF or workforce proposals address attending physicians, APCs or medical directors.
- **Staffing questions (II.ii, p. 50).** Whether the evidence base for staffing standards should account for residents with dementia, how acuity should factor in, which outcome measures should judge a standard, and what evidence supports telehealth and remote monitoring alongside adequate staffing. The background ties low staffing to higher antipsychotic use (p. 48).
- **Quality measurement and Five-Star (II.iii, pp. 53–56, 62–63).** Quality measures rest on self-reported MDS data. The RFI cites homes rated one star on inspections that rated themselves four or five stars on clinical quality, and star gains made through coding and avoiding sicker residents. It asks for proposals on data accuracy, on additional measures, and on collecting feedback from residents, families and staff.
- **SFF compliance assistance (pp. 61–62).** Proposals for nonfinancial enforcement tools and a compliance assistance program with educational programming and on-site consultation. Almost two-thirds of SFF graduates received a serious deficiency within three years.
- **Survey (pp. 57–59).** Less predictable surveys, citing evidence that for-profit facilities staff up when a survey is expected; Joint Commission deemed status as a replacement or supplement for state survey agencies; CMS's 2026 streamlined survey for the top 12% with no matching scrutiny of the bottom.
- **Virginia (pp. 46, 71).** The RFI names Ohio and Virginia as the state models for reviewing an operator's track record in CHOW/CHOP. Virginia's Earn to Learn paid clinical apprenticeship for RN and LPN students is one of four state training models.
- **Assisted living and hospice (I.iii, pp. 22–23).** The HCBS Access Act's service list includes assisted living services and, under necessary medical and nursing services, hospice. The RFI asks for state experience covering assisted living as an HCBS benefit.
- **Cognitive impairment in eligibility (I.ii, p. 21).** ADLs and IADLs miss the judgment and safety risk of early dementia, and a spouse can mask IADL dependence. The RFI asks which instruments reliably capture cognitive impairment and functional need.
- **Care transitions (I.viii, p. 38).** Who owns care coordination when a person moves between nursing home and home, and whether a single assessment should serve both home care eligibility and institutional level of care.
- **Out of scope (p. 38).** SNF-at-home and post-acute transitions go to a later, separate review.
