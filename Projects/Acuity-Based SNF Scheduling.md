## Context
A quality-improvement initiative to redesign the current standard SNF visit schedule into one driven by the acuity of patients admitted to the facility, rather than a fixed standard cadence. Sits under the [[Clinical Quality Framework]] umbrella as its own initiative with a distinct deliverable.

## Decisions
- Run this as its own project (own lifecycle/deliverable) linked to [[Clinical Quality Framework]], rather than folding it into that umbrella
- Document the current-state process before designing the acuity-based model — **satisfied 2026-09-04** by FP&A's built model rather than by mapping it from outside
- **2026-09-04: Patrick is the clinical voice on someone else's model, not its builder.** FP&A owns the model; Cary owns the next steps (the combined APC + physician view with Dan, parameters on paper, the senior-physician walk-through, Stephanie on staffing). None of those are Patrick's to carry, and the project should stop being written as though he is building the schedule.
- **2026-09-04: what IS his, and what he wants as a project — input on physician coverage in high-acuity / high short-stay facilities.** That is the piece only he can supply, it is the substance of both corrections he contributed on 9/4, and it is what the existing p3 question (do physicians need their own staffing model, driven by facility acuity) was already circling.
- **2026-09-04: separate short stay from long stay.** They are different populations with different visit cadences, different staffing implications and different quality measures, and blending them into one presence-days figure per building is what hides the problem. This is the project's next step and the frame everything else hangs on

## Open questions
- ~~How the current standard schedule is actually built today (current-state map)~~ — **answered 2026-09-04.** Scheduling runs on rules of thumb (once a month for long stay, etc.) with no attention to a building's short-stay mix. The rules happen to *average* to the clinical model as designed, which is why the gap was invisible.
- ~~What acuity signal(s) should drive scheduling~~ — **answered 2026-09-04.** Short-stay / long-stay mix, applied at the facility level against the clinical model's own visit rules. Built already by FP&A.
- What the redesigned acuity-based schedule looks like — no longer a blank page; the question is now what corrections the built model needs before it can drive staffing decisions
- **How physician days get counted.** The model reads APC days only. Until the combined APC + physician view exists, every building with a physician day reads as under-covered
- **How short-stay mix changes what a physician day is worth.** High short stay: the physician is an add-on. Low short stay: interchangeable with the APC. Not yet a displayed dimension
- ~~**Where the model degrades at the extremes**~~ — **2026-09-04: not Patrick's.** Bridgewater's 142% against a 3.2-day model and Aviva's ALF/memory-care counting artifact are model-integrity questions for whoever owns the model. Same for its presentation and labelling (red below 100, the word *over-scheduled*, *"this should not be used or seen by anyone"*). Noted, not carried.
- **Whether physicians need their own staffing model, driven by facility acuity** — not a correction to the APC model but a separate one, modeling physician days on their own terms against the acuity of the building (ANR: ~15 admissions/week, arguably a 1.5–2 day/week physician building on its own terms). Neither the combined APC + physician view with Dan nor the short-stay dimension Cary is adding answers this. **Now tracked:** Todoist `prj-acuity` p3, due 2026-09-11, dependent on the short-stay/long-stay split.
- **Individual-level risk stratification as the eventual replacement for facility-level modeling** — **2026-09-04: something to work toward and keep tracked, not an action item.** It is the structural answer to two separate Cary questions (medical necessity in assisted living, and how to defend the visit gap), and the restart-with-Steve conversation is real — but it is not a task on this project and should not be surfaced as one.

    **The headline gap.** The old rule-of-thumb dashboard reports primary care APCs at **103% of model**; the new bottoms-up view reports **75%**, because it asks whether we are there enough days and whether the visits land on the right patients. For an 80-consent SNF, 10% short stay implies three presence days a week and 30% implies five — a difference the rules of thumb structurally cannot see. Actual presence falls short of scheduled nearly everywhere, *"largely a Friday issue — people are working four days a week."*

    **The two corrections Patrick contributed, both accepted.** (1) **The model counts APC days only.** ANR reads 3.75 actual against 5.5 modeled, but is covered five days — Crystal four, Patrick the fifth. Cary: *"That's important. I don't think that has been factored in."* (2) **A physician day is not a fungible APC day.** In a low-short-stay building the doc does what the NP does — regulatory visits, clearing the book, acutes — and they are interchangeable. In a high-short-stay building (Grayson, ANR at 20–30 short stay of ~100) *"you wouldn't want to be backfilling an NP with a physician. You should think of the doc as being the add-on."* Cary is adding short-stay mix as a displayed dimension for exactly this call.

    **The model appears sound where it can be checked.** ANR's visit fidelity is ~96% (81 actual against 84 modeled) under a provider who is productive without generating fake work — if the model mispriced a building like that it would be suspect. It softens at the extremes: Bridgewater's low short-stay mix pushes modeled presence to 3.2 days on a 120-bed building, producing 142% fidelity. Aviva is a counting artifact — ALF and memory care split into two units of one building, one APC rounding both in a day, reading as over-scheduled. Presentation needs work regardless: red below 100, green above, and the word *"over-scheduled."* **"This should not be used or seen by anyone."**

    **The targets are under-scheduled AND low-fidelity buildings.** Our Lady of the Valley is the archetype — 4 scheduled against 5+ modeled, 3.5 actual, 49 visits against 83 — and independently corroborated by a run of emails about tightening its scheduling. Grayson is the other, probably APC turnover in a hard-to-staff Avardis building: *"we're always going to be at risk with Avardis, so you really can't afford a whole lot of slack in the system."*

    **The finding that links this to [[Clinical Quality Framework]]: short stay is where we fall down.** Cary confirmed from the underlying memo that short-stay quality measures are poor, and worse in the more under-scheduled, lower-fidelity buildings. Correlation, not causation — but it points the direction you'd expect, and Grayson's own QM history since 2020 has the same shape. Patrick's read is that Eventus under-serves the higher-acuity SNF and long-term-care patients and especially the short stay, and that it runs into training: long-term care is part hospital medicine, part primary care, part urgent care, and **we train to the long stay.**

    **Medical necessity, Cary's question.** Low worry in SNFs — *"by definition, they're not well"* — with the real exposure running the other way (admissions arguably need daily visits for the first week or two: *"they're basically still hospital patients"*). The risk sits in **assisted living**, where in-facility acuity ranges from Brookdale buildings that are effectively SNFs to CCRCs; the live example is a Bridgewater AL patient Patrick cannot catch for an ACO visit because the man drives to his hunting lodge every day. His structural answer, and the thing this project should eventually become: **individual-level risk stratification.**

- 2026-09-04 (later): **Next step named — separate short stay from long stay.** The built model already derives its numbers from each building's short-stay mix, but it reports out as a single blended presence-days figure and a single visit-fidelity percentage per building. Pulling the two populations apart — separate cadences, separate staffing conclusions, separate measures — is what turns the dashboard from a coverage report into an acuity model, and it is the step this project owns rather than one waiting on Dan or FP&A.
