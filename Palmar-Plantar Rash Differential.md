A rash's distribution on the palms and soles is a high-yield, narrowing clue.

## How it operates

- **A palmar-plantar rash sharply narrows the differential** — prioritize secondary syphilis, alongside HSV-associated erythema multiforme and coxsackievirus (hand-foot-mouth disease), over more generic causes of rash.
- **Distribution outweighs extent.** Deliberately down-weighting a rash's truncal component and concentrating on the palms and soles is a disciplined way to avoid diluting a specific, high-yield clue with lower-yield, nonspecific findings.

## Examples from RLR episodes

- **The distributional clue that redirected the case.** A pinpoint macular rash noted throughout the body, including the palms and soles, is what sharply narrows the differential toward secondary syphilis over erythema multiforme or hand-foot-mouth disease — unifying with a preceding presumed Jarisch-Herxheimer reaction to ceftriaxone and ultimately confirming neurosyphilis.
