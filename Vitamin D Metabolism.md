Evergreen concept note. The vitamin D pathway and how to interpret its metabolites. Teaching points cited per episode.

## The three forms

- **Parent vitamin D (D2/D3), 25-hydroxy, and 1,25-dihydroxy.** The parent is unhydroxylated; the [[25-Hydroxyvitamin D|25-hydroxy]] form is made by hepatic hydroxylation (the storage/status marker); the [[Calcitriol|1,25-dihydroxy]] form is made by renal [[1-Alpha-Hydroxylation|1-α-hydroxylation]] and is the active hormone that raises calcium. ([[RLR 028 - Hypercalcemia and Vitamin D Toxicity|RLR 028]])

## Interpreting a high 1,25 — pair the metabolites

- **A 1,25 level is uninterpretable without the 25-hydroxy.** A high 1,25 is either 25-hydroxy excess spilling over (**ingestion**) or pathological conversion of normal-to-*low* 25 by a [[Granulomatous Disease|granuloma]] or [[PTHrP]] (**conversion**). The discriminator is the 25-hydroxy value: *high* → ingestion (both up); *low* → conversion (25 consumed). Sending only the 1,25, seeing it elevated, and calling it granulomatous is a classic [[Test Interpretation|interpretive trap]]. ([[RLR 028 - Hypercalcemia and Vitamin D Toxicity|RLR 028]], [[RLR 007 - Hypercalcemia|RLR 007]])
- **A prescribed activated 1,25 ([[Calcitriol|calcitriol]]) is a distinct cause of a high 1,25** — given by nephrologists to patients who cannot 1-α-hydroxylate — worth excluding by medication review. ([[RLR 028 - Hypercalcemia and Vitamin D Toxicity|RLR 028]])
