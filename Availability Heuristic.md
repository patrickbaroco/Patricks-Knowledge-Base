## Definition

The availability heuristic is judging how likely a diagnosis is by how easily an instance comes to mind. Recent, vivid, or emotionally charged cases — a patient last week, an active outbreak, a memorable miss — feel more probable than the base rate warrants. Described by Tversky and Kahneman, it is efficient (fast retrieval of a relevant short list) but systematically distorts probability toward whatever is *top of mind*.

In clinical reasoning availability genuinely cuts both ways: a fresh exposure can put the *right* rare diagnosis within reach, or a salient epidemic can anchor the whole encounter on the wrong one. (Canonical vault/tag form is **availability heuristic**, not "availability bias.")

## How it operates

- **Salience, not frequency, drives retrieval.** The dramatic and the recent are over-weighted; the common-but-dull is under-weighted.
- **It feeds [[Anchoring Bias|anchoring]] and [[Premature Closure]].** An available diagnosis becomes the anchor the search then closes around.
- **It can help — when used as a lead, not a verdict.** Availability's proper job is to *surface* the short list tied to a rare clue; it goes wrong when it also decides the answer.
- **The corrective is the schema.** For low-frequency problems, distrust gestalt and run the [[Schema-Based Reasoning|schema]] so retrieval isn't doing the reasoning.

## Counter-moves

- **Use availability to generate, the schema to adjudicate** — let a recalled case raise a hypothesis, then test it against a framework and the base rate ([[RLR 012 - Dysuria and Esophageal Perforation|RLR 012]]).
- **Name the available anchor to defuse it** — surfacing the recent/vivid case makes it something to test, not accept ([[RLR 016 - Neck Pain and Crowned Dens Syndrome|RLR 016]]).
- **Keep the differential open after the salient positive** ([[RLR 004 - Rectal Pain and Proctitis|RLR 004]]).
