Evergreen concept note. Granulomatous disease as a recurring differential across the series. Teaching points cited per episode.

## The core triad

- **Non-caseating granulomas in the US come down to three things: infection, [[Sarcoidosis|sarcoidosis]], and [[Lymphoma|lymphoma]].** Exclude infection, weigh lymphoma's features, and sarcoid often wins by exclusion. ([[RLR 011 - Fever and Chronic Consolidation|RLR 011]])
- Infectious granulomas are led by mycobacteria ([[Tuberculosis|TB]] — always endemic somewhere) and fungi; a granulomatous process "wouldn't respond to standard antibacterials," a useful discriminator when a treated infection fails to improve. ([[RLR 032 - Fever and Chronic Myelomonocytic Leukemia|RLR 032]], [[RLR 033 - Persistent Altered Mental Status and Spinal Epidural Abscess|RLR 033]])

## As a cause of hypercalcemia

- **Granulomas pathologically convert 25- to 1,25-vitamin D**, raising the active hormone and causing hypercalcemia. The tell is that the [[25-Hydroxyvitamin D|25-hydroxy]] level runs *low* (it is being consumed), distinguishing conversion from a 25-hydroxy ingestion where both are high. A negative [[PET-CT|PET]] and clean cross-sectional imaging argue against it. ([[RLR 007 - Hypercalcemia|RLR 007]], [[RLR 014 - Hypercalcemia and Immobilization|RLR 014]], [[RLR 028 - Hypercalcemia and Vitamin D Toxicity|RLR 028]])

## Where else it surfaces

- **Skin infiltration** — nodular TB and sarcoid sit on the [[Skin Nodule|deep-nodule]] differential (infiltration from afar). ([[RLR 032 - Fever and Chronic Myelomonocytic Leukemia|RLR 032]])
- **Gastric ulcers and multi-organ disease** — TB and sarcoid are among the ways hypercalcemia and unusual organ findings connect, kept alive for a patient from an endemic region. ([[RLR 028 - Hypercalcemia and Vitamin D Toxicity|RLR 028]])
- **Biopsy is often the arbiter** — tissue distinguishes granuloma from malignancy from ordinary infection when clinical data cannot. ([[RLR 032 - Fever and Chronic Myelomonocytic Leukemia|RLR 032]])
