Reading the liver chemistries as a pattern.

## The two patterns

- **Hepatocellular = AST/ALT-predominant; cholestatic = ALP/bilirubin-predominant.** The first question on abnormal liver chemistries is which pattern dominates, because it splits the differential (parenchymal injury vs obstruction/cholestasis). ([[RLR 020 - Chest Pain and Ascending Cholangitis|RLR 020]], [[RLR 021 - Chronic Pruritus and Primary Biliary Cholangitis|RLR 021]])
- **"Liver enzymes" are not liver-specific — AST especially is ubiquitous.** An AST-predominant transaminitis is most often alcohol (the 2:1 ratio need not be met), but extrahepatic sources — [[Myocardial Infarction|MI]], [[Rhabdomyolysis|rhabdomyolysis]], [[Hemolysis|hemolysis]] — mimic "liver" enzymes, so a CK belongs in the workup when the story is chest pain. ALT is more liver-specific and has a longer half-life, so as an injury ages the ALT overtakes the AST. ([[RLR 020 - Chest Pain and Ascending Cholangitis|RLR 020]], [[RLR 025 - Liver Chemistry Abnormalities and Intrahepatic Cholestasis of Pregnancy|RLR 025]])
- **Confirm a high ALP is hepatic with a GGT** (or a bone-specific ALP); a high GGT confirms the ALP is of hepatobiliary origin. ([[RLR 021 - Chronic Pruritus and Primary Biliary Cholangitis|RLR 021]])

## Fractionate the bilirubin — it redirects the workup

- **Direct-predominant → cholestasis; indirect-predominant → hemolysis or impaired conjugation** (Gilbert's, physiologic stress, and — easy to forget — the impaired conjugation of *hepatic congestion*). Fractionating even a mildly elevated bilirubin genuinely directs the workup. ([[RLR 013 - Right Upper Quadrant Pain and Heart Failure|RLR 013]], [[RLR 021 - Chronic Pruritus and Primary Biliary Cholangitis|RLR 021]])
- **An indirect-predominant bilirubin beside a pleural effusion and leg edema points away from the biliary tree and toward the [[Heart Failure|heart]]** — congestive impaired conjugation. ([[RLR 013 - Right Upper Quadrant Pain and Heart Failure|RLR 013]])

## Tempo and the obstruction caveat

- **Read the enzymes as a trajectory, not a snapshot, and let the pace localize.** Plumbing problems (a stone that lodges then pops, or an ischemic-hepatitis perfusion drop) *bounce and jump*; parenchymal injury does not. ([[RLR 025 - Liver Chemistry Abnormalities and Intrahepatic Cholestasis of Pregnancy|RLR 025]])
- **An AST/ALT rise can be the *first* marker of biliary obstruction — before the ALP and bilirubin move — and absence of ductal dilation does not exclude an obstructing stone.** In choledocholithiasis the ALT rise must never be dismissed; escalate on the enzyme trajectory, not a single negative scan (see [[MRCP]]). ([[RLR 013 - Right Upper Quadrant Pain and Heart Failure|RLR 013]], [[RLR 020 - Chest Pain and Ascending Cholangitis|RLR 020]])
- **A cholestatic/mixed pattern with a predominant ALP in a woman with chronic pruritus is [[Primary Biliary Cholangitis|PBC]] until proven otherwise** — send the [[Antimitochondrial Antibody|AMA]] before a biopsy. ([[RLR 021 - Chronic Pruritus and Primary Biliary Cholangitis|RLR 021]])
