## Definition

Base-rate reasoning is starting from prior probability — how common a diagnosis is in this population — and spending diagnostic energy in proportion to it, so that even a blind guess lands near the truth. It is the front-end discipline that keeps a workup anchored to the common while still permitting the rare: begin with the frequent causes, and let *specific* discriminating data (tempo, host, red flags) adjust the prior rather than letting a favorite diagnosis override them.

Its failure mode is [[Base-Rate Neglect|base-rate neglect]] — reaching for the zebra (or the vivid, [[Availability Heuristic|available]] case) while ignoring how rare it is.

## How it operates

- **Base rate sets the prior; specifics adjust it.** Frequency is the starting weight, then host and tempo move it — "informs but does not dictate."
- **Discordant tempo can veto the common answer.** Osteoarthritis is common, but it does not produce an acute, escalating, severe course — so the base-rate favorite is not allowed to explain what it can't.
- **The host re-weights the prior.** A doubly immunosuppressed host lowers the pyogenic prior and raises atypical/granulomatous disease.
- **Your personal base rate can be skewed.** Experience from *reading* rather than *seeing* a condition (or from an atypical practice setting) distorts the felt prior — recognizing and discounting that is itself the skill.
- **Spend energy proportionally.** Start with the common causes and let red flags — duration, failure of prior therapy — license the deeper, rarer dive.

## Examples from RLR episodes

- **Demographics steer without a test.** Primary hyperparathyroidism is down-weighted purely on an older-man demographic, and the "two I's" elevated as the causes that fit an indolent presentation. ([[RLR 007 - Hypercalcemia|RLR 007]])
- **Base rate used, then tempered.** Lung cancer (most common US malignancy) prioritizes adenocarcinoma, immediately tempered by non-smoking status and competing paraprotein data. ([[RLR 011 - Fever and Chronic Consolidation|RLR 011]])
- **Base rate before zebras.** Even chasing a rare cause, Reza re-centers the common hemolysis causes (DIC, G6PD, sickle cell, AIHA) so a guess lands near the truth. ([[RLR 015 - Hemolysis and Heinz Body Hemolytic Anemia|RLR 015]])
- **Disciplined by tempo.** Common osteoarthritis is declined as the explanation for an acute, escalating course it doesn't produce. ([[RLR 016 - Neck Pain and Crowned Dens Syndrome|RLR 016]])
- **Disciplined by host.** A 3–4-week course in a doubly immunosuppressed host lowers the pyogenic-osteomyelitis prior. ([[RLR 017 - Back Pain and Disseminated Nocardiosis|RLR 017]])
- **Compounding, not substituting.** The ~40% malignancy rate of gross hematuria anchors cancer concern, tempered by youth; when an enhancing lesion appears, base rate and imaging compound. ([[RLR 018 - Gross Hematuria in End-Stage Renal Disease|RLR 018]])
- **Proportional energy on pruritus.** Start with common causes (xerosis, atopic dermatitis, eczema, scabies); duration and prior-therapy failure license the sinister workup. ([[RLR 021 - Chronic Pruritus and Primary Biliary Cholangitis|RLR 021]])
- **A skewed personal base rate, corrected.** Rabih flags that his prior for vulvar pain comes from reading, not seeing, and consciously discounts it. ([[RLR 022 - Vulvar Pain and Behçet Disease|RLR 022]])
- **Highest-prior first.** The pneumothorax workup begins with trauma/iatrogenic before rarer secondary causes, reserving primary spontaneous as exclusion. ([[RLR 023 - Pleuritic Chest Pain and Pulmonary Langerhans Cell Histiocytosis|RLR 023]])
- **Common source, extrahepatic caveat.** Elevated AST/ALT almost always means the liver by base rate, but pause for muscle, heart, and red cells first. ([[RLR 025 - Liver Chemistry Abnormalities and Intrahepatic Cholestasis of Pregnancy|RLR 025]])
- **Base rate and tempo together.** The outpatient hypercalcemia differential is ordered by frequency (hyperparathyroidism > malignancy > milk-alkali), then corrected by tempo. ([[RLR 014 - Hypercalcemia and Immobilization|RLR 014]])
- **Test characteristics are population-specific, not just diagnosis-specific.** A "don't treat asymptomatic pyuria" heuristic learned from older-adult populations under-weights the same finding in a young patient, where pyuria with nitrites is far more likely to reflect actionable infection — the base rate that should be adjusted is the population's, not just the disease's. ([[RLR 075 - Myalgias, High Fever, and Bilateral Pyelonephritis in Anorexia Nervosa|RLR 075]])
- **Regional epidemiology sets the prior before any data returns.** Central Valley construction work drives coccidioidomycosis and paracoccidioidomycosis to the top of the list purely on geography and occupation, before any test result — and is pursued even once the pattern (centrilobular, non-eosinophilic) is atypical for it, rather than being abandoned at the first mismatch. ([[RLR 077 - Progressive Dyspnea, Pulmonary-Renal Syndrome, and Silicosis-Triggered ANCA Vasculitis|RLR 077]])
- **A remote finding recalibrates the prior for a new event.** A prior asymptomatic kidney stone does not raise the probability that current pain represents a new symptomatic stone — most stones sit quietly, exactly as with gallstone disease — so a "known stone former" history should not be over-weighted as an explanation on its own. ([[RLR 079 - Paroxysmal Flank Pain, Tumor Lysis Physiology, and an Occult Myeloproliferative Neoplasm|RLR 079]])
- **An immunocompromised host broadens the prior rather than narrowing it.** The single most reframing piece of history is not "this points to one rare disease" but "this raises the prior across an entire class of diagnoses at once" — infection, malignancy, multiplicity, and blunted symptom expression all become more likely simultaneously once a severely low CD4 count is known. ([[RLR 080 - Headache, Photophobia, and Neurosyphilis in Advanced HIV|RLR 080]])
