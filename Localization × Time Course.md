Evergreen concept note. Definition plus worked examples, drawn from the RLR / Clinical Problem Solvers series and elsewhere. Source episodes are cited per example.

## Definition

Localization × time course is Aaron Berkowitz's organizing equation for neurology — his "E=mc² of neurology": **differential diagnosis = localization × time course**. Two independent axes carry almost all of the diagnostic weight. *Localization* answers **where** the lesion sits (derived from the history and exam, often to within centimeters at the bedside); *time course* answers **how fast** it declared itself. Fixing both collapses an enormous differential to a short, testable list *before* any test is ordered. Localization is the years-long skill — it demands the anatomy — but time course is quick to learn, high-yield, and generalizes across all of medicine. It is Berkowitz's antidote to [[Neurophobia|neurophobia]]: neurology feels frightening only when the where and the how-fast are skipped.

## How it operates

- **Localize first, then time.** Establish the site (for altered mental status, the brain — see [[Altered Mental Status]]) via [[Anatomical Approach|localization]], then let the [[Illness Tempo|tempo]] select the etiologic class.
- **The two axes are independent and multiplicative.** The *same* localization with a *different* tempo is a *different* disease — which is why neither axis alone suffices.
- **Tempo sorts into buckets, each with characteristic causes.** *Instant* (vascular — "instant neurology, just add water" — seizure, toxic-metabolic, trauma); *acute*, hours-to-days (infection, inflammatory/demyelinating); *subacute*, weeks-to-months (neoplasm, antibody-mediated/paraneoplastic, indolent infection, rare rapidly-progressive prion dementia); *chronic* (neurodegenerative).
- **Toxic-metabolic fits every bucket** — it is the universal, so it is never a stopping point; name the specific toxin or metabolite.
- **The framework is not just neurology's.** The same tempo map runs through internal medicine — vascular events are sudden, infection/inflammation acute, neoplasia subacute, degeneration chronic.

## Examples from RLR episodes

- **The framework in full.** Altered mental status localizes to the brain, and re-reading the tempo as subacute-but-*intermittent* reframes the whole case toward seizure; "instant neurology, just add water" flags the vascular bucket. ([[CPS 153 - Intermittent Altered Mental Status and Non-Lesional Autoimmune Epilepsy|CPS 153]])
- **Tempo as the master variable in confusion.** The same "confusion" resolves to entirely different differentials at seconds, days, or months — localization to the brain is only the first half of the equation. ([[RLR 024 - Confusion and Ertapenem-Induced Encephalopathy|RLR 024]])
- **Localization plus tempo in persistent AMS.** A fixed, non-resolving deficit forces the search for a structural/occult driver rather than a purely metabolic one. ([[RLR 033 - Persistent Altered Mental Status and Spinal Epidural Abscess|RLR 033]])
- **Exam localizes, tempo differentiates.** The bedside exam pins a left cerebellar hemisphere; the episodic, recurrent, triggered tempo then drives (and repeatedly re-opens) the differential. ([[RLR 037 - Episodic Lightheadedness and Functional Neurological Disorder|RLR 037]])
