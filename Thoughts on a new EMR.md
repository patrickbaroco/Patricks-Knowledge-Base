> “Should you find yourself in a chronically-leaking boat, energy devoted to changing vessels is likely to be more productive than energy devoted to patching leaks.”
>- Warren Buffett, letter to shareholders, 1985

Though Warren Buffett was speaking about a failing textile company known as Berkshire Hathaway in the quote above, I find it hard to believe he was not speaking about our coming transition from ChartPath to Athena. ChartPath is the leakiest of vessels, often bobbing along just above the surface of the water. It is only the patches that keep it afloat at all: ranging from TextBlaze for Suki to the human labor of opening notes. It is time for a new vessel, one that is at last seaworthy. That said, abandoning ship can be a scary thing, even when there is a better ship just a short swim away. So my intention here is to provide some reassurance, both that the swim is not an arduous one and that it will be worth it.

Before coming to Eventus, I used Athena for years in my prior job. We used it for our outpatient palliative care practice and also for our long-term care work at three local nursing facilities. I have had the fortune (or misfortune?) to have used quite a few EMRs over the years: Athena, of course, but also Epic, CPRS, Meditech, NextGen, and Gehrimed. By far, Athena was the best of these. It is very good at the core function of an EMR: writing a clinical note. The ability to store and pull information is excellent. The workflows quickly become intuitive. And the intra-team communication functions of Athena set it apart from any other EMR. It also has a lot of integrations with other services, as demonstrated by Jamie Smith in the town hall. (If you have not watched that demo, please do. It was excellent.)

Athena has a lot of features. That means it is more complex than ChartPath. ChartPath is basically one thing--the screen we all know (and do not love). There are more places to "be" in Athena. To see it side by side with ChartPath will be intimidating to some. But I have been here before. At our hospital system, we used Meditech for our inpatient EMR. Meditech is a fairly "dumb" EMR and is not complicated. Unlike ChartPath, it did not seem intent on making my life worse, but it also had no intention of making it better. When I began using Athena as our outpatient EMR, it was a little intimidating at first. There are new things to learn. However, these are things that make your life easier. And the learning curve was fairly short. After using Athena for a short time, I hated having to use Meditech on my hospital consult days. It was a slog compared to the relative ease with which I could write a high-quality note in Athena. And in my prior experience with Athena we did not even have all the features that were demonstrated by Jamie. The Athena that I knew has evolved and improved further in the four years since I used it. 

I was not a part of selecting the new EMR. When I heard Athena was in the running, I was excited but cautious. For the front-line provider, Athena was clearly the best of all of them. But it is designed for office practices, so to make it work on the "back end" for Eventus would require a lot of work. Here, I confess my doubts as to what would be chosen: the easier product for administration, or the best product for the clinician. Eventus chose based on the needs of the clinician, and I give the leadership team a lot of credit for that. They have then worked to build the best possible version of Athena (again, witness the tools Jamie demonstrated). We need the right tools to be able to do our job well. The EMR is arguably the most important tool we have. Today, what we have is a boat riddled with leaks and kept afloat at the greatest of costs: with our time. I for one am eager to abandon ship and make the short swim to something much better.
