The vertebral venous (Batson's) plexus as an anatomic bridge.

## What it is

Batson's plexus is a network of **valveless** veins running along the vertebral column that connects the deep pelvic and renal venous drainage to the spinal epidural venous space. Because the veins lack valves, flow can move retrograde, allowing infection or tumor to spread from the pelvis/abdomen directly to the spine without passing through the systemic circulation.

## Why it matters

- **It explains how [[Pyelonephritis|pyelonephritis]] seeds a [[Spinal Epidural Abscess|spinal epidural abscess]].** Severe renal infection can track up the plexus to the epidural space — the mechanistic bridge that unified a Klebsiella pyelonephritis with a lumbar-to-thoracic epidural abscess (named at the bedside by a medical student from anatomy class). ([[RLR 033 - Persistent Altered Mental Status and Spinal Epidural Abscess|RLR 033]])
- **The same route explains the spread of pelvic malignancies (e.g., prostate) to the vertebral column** — a general anatomic principle worth carrying beyond infection.
- An example of [[Anatomical Approach|anatomical reasoning]] providing the unifying link between a GU process and a cord syndrome.
