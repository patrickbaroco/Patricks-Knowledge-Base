An approach to a low platelet count: classify by tempo and by company, reason through the accompanying cell lines, and sort mechanism into decreased production, increased destruction, or sequestration.

## Two Framing Axes
Before mechanism, place the thrombocytopenia on two axes ([[RLR 064 - Hematuria and Post-TAVR Perivalvular Leak|RLR 064]]):

- **Acute vs chronic.** Compare with prior counts. Beware the trap: a count that dropped in a **single step** and then stayed flat is not a slowly-sneaking chronic process — it is best reframed as **"[[Acute but Persistent]]"**, which relinks it to a discrete inciting event rather than to indolent causes (HIV, HCV, ITP, cirrhosis) ([[RLR 064 - Hematuria and Post-TAVR Perivalvular Leak|RLR 064]]).
- **Isolated vs accompanied.** Is only the platelet line low, or are the hemoglobin and white count also affected? Company points toward marrow processes, consumption (TMA/DIC), or sequestration.

## Reason Through the Accompanying Cell Line
Platelets are diagnostically "quiet" — they lack a differential and lack clean, routinely available destruction markers. So when platelets and another line fall together, **interpret the more informative line to infer the platelet mechanism** ([[RLR 064 - Hematuria and Post-TAVR Perivalvular Leak|RLR 064]]).

- **Read the anemia.** Its causes are legible: acute **blood loss**, **iron/EPO deficiency** (e.g., dialysis), marrow failure, and — when a prosthetic valve is in the story — **[[Valve Hemolysis|valve hemolysis]]** that consumes both hemoglobin and platelets ([[RLR 064 - Hematuria and Post-TAVR Perivalvular Leak|RLR 064]]). A hemolytic anemia with schistocytes co-timed with thrombocytopenia should trigger the microangiopathy / mechanical-hemolysis pathway.

## Mechanism: Production, Destruction, Sequestration
- **Decreased production** — marrow suppression or infiltration (chemotherapy, alcohol, B12/folate, aplasia, malignancy, **myelodysplasia**). Usually accompanied by other cytopenias.
- **Increased destruction/consumption** — immune (ITP, drug-induced, HIT), or consumptive (DIC, TTP/HUS and other thrombotic microangiopathies, mechanical valve hemolysis). Peripheral smear (schistocytes) and coagulation studies help sort these.
- **Sequestration** — splenic pooling from **portal hypertension/splenomegaly**.

## The "Truly Chronic Isolated" Rule
When thrombocytopenia is genuinely **chronic and isolated**, the differential collapses toward two answers: **splenic sequestration** and **myelodysplasia** ([[RLR 064 - Hematuria and Post-TAVR Perivalvular Leak|RLR 064]]). Checking spleen size on any prior cross-sectional imaging is a "free" high-yield step.

## Practical Points
- **Confirm it is real** — exclude pseudothrombocytopenia (EDTA clumping) with a smear or citrate tube when the value is unexpected.
- **Bleeding risk is nonlinear** — counts **>50k** are usually hemostatically sufficient absent a qualitative defect, so a modestly low count is often a minor contributor to significant bleeding ([[RLR 064 - Hematuria and Post-TAVR Perivalvular Leak|RLR 064]]; see [[Bleeding Framework]]).
