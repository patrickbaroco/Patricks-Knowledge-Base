Evergreen concept note. Stool lactoferrin is a neutrophil-derived fecal marker — the "leukocyte esterase of the gut" — that flags fecal white cells and points toward inflammatory intestinal disease. RLR / Clinical Problem Solvers teaching points are cited per episode; general clinical detail is scaffolding.

## What It Measures
Lactoferrin is an **iron-binding glycoprotein contained in the secondary granules of neutrophils**. When neutrophils enter the intestinal lumen during inflammation, lactoferrin appears in the stool.

- **Marker of fecal neutrophils** — a stool assay that flags white cells in the gut ([[RLR 063 - Diarrhea and Behçet's Disease|RLR 063]]).
- Because it reflects neutrophil presence, it behaves as a marker of **inflammatory (as opposed to functional) diarrhea**.

## The "Leukocyte Esterase of the Gut"
- The memorable teaching frame is that **stool lactoferrin is the "leukocyte esterase of the gut"** — just as urine leukocyte esterase signals white cells in the urinary tract, stool lactoferrin signals white cells in the intestine ([[RLR 063 - Diarrhea and Behçet's Disease|RLR 063]]).
- It is a modern, more stable surrogate for the older **fecal-leukocyte smear**, with the advantage of a quantitative, reproducible immunoassay.

## Clinical Use
- **Separates inflammatory from functional bowel disease** and supports the decision to pursue endoscopy in the appropriate patient (see [[Inflammatory Bowel Disease]]).
- **Elevated in infectious (invasive) colitis and IBD** — as a neutrophil marker it does **not distinguish** infection from IBD, so it complements rather than replaces stool infectious testing before immunosuppression ([[RLR 063 - Diarrhea and Behçet's Disease|RLR 063]], see [[Colitis]]).
- Can be used to **track disease activity** in known IBD.

## Interpretation
- Like **[[Fecal Calprotectin]]**, its diagnostic value depends on **pretest probability** — most useful in intermediate-probability patients and least reliable at the extremes.
- **Sensitive but not specific**: any neutrophilic luminal process (infection, IBD flare) can raise it.
- The two neutrophil-derived stool markers (lactoferrin and calprotectin) are commonly used interchangeably or in tandem for the same clinical questions ([[RLR 063 - Diarrhea and Behçet's Disease|RLR 063]]).
