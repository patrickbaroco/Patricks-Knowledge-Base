## Definition

Hickam's dictum — "a patient can have as many diseases as he damn well pleases," attributed to John Hickam — is the deliberate counterweight to [[Unifying Diagnosis|Occam's razor]]. It asserts that parsimony is a heuristic, not a law: in many patients, and especially the elderly, the immunosuppressed, the chronically ill, and those carrying several independent risk factors, **multiple simultaneous diagnoses are the norm rather than the exception.** Its job is to guard against the over-unification failure mode — forcing an outlier finding into a single elegant story it does not fit — and to license the honest, multifactorial formulation when that is the more accurate one.

Hickam does not repeal Occam; the two are prior-setting forces held in tension. A good reasoner starts parsimonious and lets residue — a finding the unifier can't explain — and the patient's population pull them toward plurality when warranted.

## How it operates

- **Invoke it when a unifier leaves residue.** If the parsimonious diagnosis explains most findings but one refuses to fit, treat that outlier as a candidate *second* disease rather than noise to be rationalized away.
- **Population sets the prior.** The higher the burden of comorbidity or immunosuppression, the more the [[Base Rate|base rate]] favors coexisting processes; Occam's prior weakens with age and risk factors.
- **Coexistence is not common cause.** Two findings arriving together may have arrived by separate routes — assume the [[Worst-Case Reasoning|worst-case identity]] of each until excluded rather than assuming one root.
- **Embrace the ambiguity.** Sometimes the truthful diagnosis is "multifactorial," and naming that explicitly beats collapsing it into one tidy label — accuracy over elegance.
- **It is a counterweight, not a license.** Defaulting to "many diseases" invites over-testing and a scattered workup; use Hickam to resist [[Premature Closure|premature unification]], then let base rate and [[Illness Tempo|tempo]] adjudicate which findings truly deserve their own diagnosis.

## Examples from RLR and CPS episodes

- **The multifactorial diagnosis, named honestly.** Weeks of visual hallucinations resolve not to one cause but to three at once — hyperglycemia-provoked occipital seizures, a possible left-occipital stroke, and diabetic-retinopathy Charles Bonnet; the hosts explicitly invoke Hickam's dictum and "embrace the ambiguity" rather than force a single label. ([[CPS 323 - Visual Hallucinations and Occipital Seizures|CPS 323]])
- **Where Occam wins anyway — the tension made visible.** Recurrent strokes through anticoagulation in an elderly man *look* like a Hickam picture (many vascular events, many risk factors) but unify under one intravascular large B-cell lymphoma — a reminder that Hickam is a check on parsimony, not a replacement for it. ([[CPS 386 - Rapidly Progressive Dementia and Intravascular Large B-Cell Lymphoma|CPS 386]])
- **The counter-case for parsimony.** Three converging obstructions (bowel, biliary, pancreatic) meet at the [[Ampulla of Vater|ampulla of Vater]] and yield to a *single* lesion — the situation where [[Unifying Diagnosis|Occam]] decisively beats Hickam, and the contrast that sharpens when each rule applies. ([[RLR 039 - Nausea, Vomiting, Constipation, and a Metastatic Ampullary Mass|RLR 039]])
