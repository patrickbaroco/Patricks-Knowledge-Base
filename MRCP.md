Magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography in the biliary workup.

## Where it sits on the sensitivity ladder

- **Imaging sensitivity for choledocholithiasis climbs US → CT → MRCP → EUS → ERCP.** Stones are missed on the lower-sensitivity modalities, so a normal ultrasound or CT does not exclude an obstructing stone; MRCP is the non-invasive step up when suspicion persists, with EUS/ERCP beyond it. ([[RLR 020 - Chest Pain and Ascending Cholangitis|RLR 020]])
- **Absence of ductal dilation does not exclude obstruction**, and an AST/ALT rise can precede the ALP and bilirubin — so escalate to MRCP on the enzyme *trajectory* and the clinical story, not on a single negative scan. ([[RLR 020 - Chest Pain and Ascending Cholangitis|RLR 020]], [[RLR 013 - Right Upper Quadrant Pain and Heart Failure|RLR 013]])

## When to reach for it

- **When you cannot exclude biliary obstruction and worry about [[Ascending Cholangitis|cholangitis]].** CT and ultrasound are insufficiently sensitive; MRCP characterizes the ducts non-invasively before committing to therapeutic [[ERCP]]. In the worked case, marked RUQ tenderness prompted the MRCP that found a common-bile-duct stone. ([[RLR 020 - Chest Pain and Ascending Cholangitis|RLR 020]], [[RLR 013 - Right Upper Quadrant Pain and Heart Failure|RLR 013]])
- **As part of stripping away extrahepatic obstruction in a cholestatic pattern** — a clean MRCP (with negative serologies and med review) helps localize to [[Intrahepatic Cholestasis|intrahepatic cholestasis]]. ([[RLR 021 - Chronic Pruritus and Primary Biliary Cholangitis|RLR 021]])
