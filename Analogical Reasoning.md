## Definition

Analogical reasoning is solving an unfamiliar problem by mapping a structure from a familiar one — porting a frame, a mechanism, or a stored case onto the situation at hand. It is how a clinician makes progress on a complaint they rarely see (borrow a schema built for something else), reaches a diagnosis they don't have scripted (extend a known class member to an unknown one), or recognizes an early, incompletely declared illness (recall a past case with the same *shape*). Its power is generativity; its danger is that a surface resemblance can mislead, so the discipline is to **name the analogy** and, where possible, **verify it** rather than let it stand as proof.

## How it operates

- **Port a frame across complaints.** A structure built for one problem can scaffold another — internal-versus-external, built for rash, transfers cleanly onto pruritus — provided you flag that you are borrowing it.
- **Extend a class member to an unknown, then confirm.** Reasoning from a known drug/disease to an unfamiliar relative *generates* the hypothesis; a confirmatory check discharges the uncertainty the analogy introduced.
- **Retrieve a stored case by shape, not diagnosis.** A remembered case with the same temporal or spatial structure (an illness caught before it fully declared) can be mapped onto a new one to predict its next move.
- **Related to [[Parallel Reasoning]].** Running the analog case alongside the current one — same-shape, different-content — is the mechanism; naming the transplant keeps it honest.

## Examples from RLR episodes

- **Frame ported across complaints.** Facing pruritus he rarely works, Reza borrows the Curbsiders' internal-versus-external *rash* frame and applies it to itch, naming the borrowing explicitly — a no-rash, refractory, decade-long itch reads *internal* and lands on PBC. ([[RLR 021 - Chronic Pruritus and Primary Biliary Cholangitis|RLR 021]])
- **Class analogy plus verification — the engine of the case.** Reza maps the unfamiliar ertapenem onto familiar [[Cefepime Neurotoxicity|cefepime]] neurotoxicity (same beta-lactam class, same phenotype) to raise antibiotic-associated encephalopathy, then runs a live search that returns a matched case report — analogy to generate, literature to confirm. ([[RLR 024 - Confusion and Ertapenem-Induced Encephalopathy|RLR 024]])
- **Stored case retrieved by shape.** Reza recalls the "Sarah Meller" case — a unilateral cerebellar ataxia that declared bilaterally only on day two — and maps its *caught-too-early* structure onto a GI bleed whose blood has not yet transited, reframing the picture before the melena appears. ([[RLR 026 - Syncope and Occult Gastrointestinal Bleeding|RLR 026]])
- **Analogies to calibrate a physical finding.** Reza reasons about a scalp nerve pain from anterior cutaneous nerve entrapment and meralgia paresthetica, and uses the chest-wall-tenderness-doesn't-exclude-ACS analogy to decide how much to trust reproducible tenderness. ([[RLR 031 - Headache and Gorham-Stout Disease|RLR 031]])
- **Borrowing urgency from another emergency.** Spinal cord compression is reasoned as time-critical by analogy to door-to-balloon in ACS and door-to-needle in stroke — "walk in with paresis, walk out." ([[RLR 033 - Persistent Altered Mental Status and Spinal Epidural Abscess|RLR 033]])

## Counter-moves

- **Name the transplant.** Saying "I'm borrowing this frame" marks its limits and prevents the analogy from hardening into fact ([[RLR 021 - Chronic Pruritus and Primary Biliary Cholangitis|RLR 021]]).
- **Verify the leap.** Pair an analogic hypothesis with a confirmatory test or literature check rather than trusting the resemblance ([[RLR 024 - Confusion and Ertapenem-Induced Encephalopathy|RLR 024]]).
