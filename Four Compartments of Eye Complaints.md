An anatomical first pass for any complaint referable to the eye, before generating a differential within the implicated compartment.

## The four compartments

- **Intraocular** — processes inside the globe itself (uveitis, glaucoma, retinal disease).
- **Orbital** — processes in the bony orbit surrounding the globe (orbital cellulitis, thyroid eye disease, mass lesions).
- **Eyelid** — processes of the lid itself (blepharitis, stye/hordeolum, chalazion).
- **Referred** — pain or symptoms referred from a headache syndrome (migraine, cluster) rather than originating in the eye at all.

## How it operates

- **Localize to a compartment before differentiating within it.** Restricting the differential to "eyelid disease" narrows quickly to blepharitis versus stye versus chalazion — a workable list only once the compartment itself is chosen correctly.
- **Bilateral involvement is atypical for focal eyelid lesions.** A stye or chalazion is usually unilateral and focal; bilateral eyelid swelling should prompt reconsideration of the compartment itself (or a systemic process) rather than doubling down on a focal eyelid diagnosis.
- **Hold the first compartment loosely.** New data (a photograph showing diffuse facial puffiness, not just eyelid swelling) can overturn the initial compartment assignment entirely.

## Examples from RLR episodes

- **From eyelid to a fifth, unlisted category.** A chief concern of bilateral eyelid edema is initially localized to the eyelid compartment (favoring blepharitis), but the discovery of a "puffy face" beyond the eyelids and posture-dependent severity ultimately moves the case outside all four compartments entirely, toward a vascular obstruction (SVC syndrome) affecting the face as a whole rather than the eye specifically.
