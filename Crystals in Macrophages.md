A crystal on the slide only proves a joint *has had* crystal disease; whether it is *active now* depends on whether the crystals are inside cells.

## The Core Distinction
- **Intracellular (phagocytosed) crystals = active disease** ([[RLR 062 - Oligoarthritis and Polyarticular Gout Flare|RLR 062]], [[RLR 061 - Knee Swelling and Calcific Tendinitis|RLR 061]]). When neutrophils and macrophages are actively engulfing crystals, the inflammatory cascade is engaged — this is the flare.
- **Extracellular crystals persist forever = a fingerprint of prior disease, not proof of a current flare** ([[RLR 062 - Oligoarthritis and Polyarticular Gout Flare|RLR 062]]). Urate and CPP crystals remain in a joint indefinitely; finding them proves only that the joint *has had* gout or pseudogout.

## Why It Matters
- **"Crystals present ≠ crystals causative"** ([[RLR 061 - Knee Swelling and Calcific Tendinitis|RLR 061]]). A positive crystal exam in a joint with a **non-inflammatory** cell count is a coincidence, not the cause — the classic trap of anchoring on the slide while the real disease is elsewhere (e.g., a periarticular [[Calcific Tendinitis|calcific tendinitis]]).
- **Diagnosing an active flare has two arms** ([[RLR 062 - Oligoarthritis and Polyarticular Gout Flare|RLR 062]]): (1) demonstrate **intracellular** crystals, and (2) **disprove infection**. A positive crystal never exonerates you from [[Septic Arthritis|sepsis]], since the two coexist.
- This guards against **anchoring** — see [[Test Interpretation]] — and pairs with the [[Law of Proportionality]]: if the cell count is too low to explain the picture, the crystals you see are likely incidental.

## Applies to Both Crystal Types
- [[Gout]] — monosodium urate crystals persist in intercritical joints; intracellular urate marks the acute attack.
- [[CPPD]] — calcium pyrophosphate crystals deposit chronically (chondrocalcinosis); intracellular CPP marks active pseudogout.
