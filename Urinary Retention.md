Urinary retention and its far-reaching downstream effects across the RLR / Clinical Problem Solvers series.

## Causes

- **Obstructive** — [[Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia|BPH]] (the commonest cause in older men), prostate cancer, urethral stricture, an obstructing stone, or a distended rectum from constipation compressing the outlet. ([[RLR 005 - Abdominal Distention, Constipation, and Urinary Retention|RLR 005]], [[RLR 034 - Polyuria and Post-Obstructive Nephrogenic Diabetes Insipidus|RLR 034]])
- **Pharmacologic** — [[Anticholinergic|anticholinergics]] (e.g., oxybutynin), opioids, and sympathomimetics; an iatrogenic trigger is easy to miss and worth tracing to root cause. ([[RLR 005 - Abdominal Distention, Constipation, and Urinary Retention|RLR 005]], [[RLR 006 - Bradycardia and Urinary Retention|RLR 006]])
- **Neurogenic** — cord compression/cauda equina, diabetic autonomic neuropathy, or other neurologic disease impairing detrusor function.

## The coupled bowel–bladder cycle

- **[[Constipation–Retention Cycle|Constipation and urinary retention drive each other]]** — a loaded rectum obstructs the bladder outlet and a distended bladder worsens constipation, a self-reinforcing loop that an anticholinergic can set off; break it by relieving both and stopping the trigger. ([[RLR 005 - Abdominal Distention, Constipation, and Urinary Retention|RLR 005]])

## Far-reaching downstream effects (why it matters)

- **Confusion / delirium.** Retention can present as [[Altered Mental Status|altered mental status]] in an elderly patient, with the mental status improving once the bladder is drained — recurrent confusion should prompt a bladder check. ([[RLR 034 - Polyuria and Post-Obstructive Nephrogenic Diabetes Insipidus|RLR 034]])
- **Reflex bradycardia.** A distended bladder can drive a vagally-mediated [[BRASH Syndrome|reflex bradycardia]] — the bladder–heart connection — reversible with decompression. ([[RLR 006 - Bradycardia and Urinary Retention|RLR 006]])
- **Post-renal AKI.** Obstruction backs pressure up the tract; an [[Law of Proportionality|AKI out of proportion]] to the patient's hemodynamics and intake should prompt a bladder scan for a post-renal cause. ([[RLR 034 - Polyuria and Post-Obstructive Nephrogenic Diabetes Insipidus|RLR 034]])
- **Catheter-associated infection and post-obstructive sequelae.** A [[Catheter-Associated UTI|catheter-associated UTI]] can obstruct the catheter and seed bacteremia; relieving obstruction resolves it fast, and the ensuing [[Post-Obstructive Uropathy|post-obstructive uropathy]] can cause a nephrogenic [[Diabetes Insipidus|diabetes insipidus]]. A Foley being *in* does not mean it is *working*. ([[RLR 034 - Polyuria and Post-Obstructive Nephrogenic Diabetes Insipidus|RLR 034]])

## Workup

- **Bladder scan / post-void residual is the fast bedside answer** — it confirms retention, gauges severity (e.g., 600–800 cc), and checks whether an existing catheter is draining; a catheter that relieves the residual is both diagnostic and therapeutic. ([[RLR 005 - Abdominal Distention, Constipation, and Urinary Retention|RLR 005]], [[RLR 034 - Polyuria and Post-Obstructive Nephrogenic Diabetes Insipidus|RLR 034]])
