Headache and photophobia worked from anatomy and base rate before any history is known.

## How it operates

- **Headache alone is often a bystander finding** — a feature of a primary headache syndrome (migraine, tension, cluster), of structures external to the face (eyes, sinuses, neck), or of an underlying systemic process, only occasionally reflecting a true intracranial process (meningitis, giant cell arteritis, venous sinus thrombosis).
- **Photophobia localizes.** Because light must pass through the eye, photophobia can arise superficially (a corneal abrasion) or from a deeper process reached through the eye as a passageway (migraine, meningitis).
- **By base rate alone, migraine is the default operating hypothesis** in a young person with headache and photophobia and no other information — the reasonable "Vegas bet."
- **The single history item most likely to overturn that default is an immunocompromising condition** — it does not just add rare diagnoses, it broadens the entire differential (infection, malignancy, multiplicity, blunted symptom expression).
- **Always examine the eyes directly** (fundoscopic and slit-lamp) rather than assuming photophobia is simply a nonspecific accompaniment of a broader diagnosis once one is found — ocular involvement can coexist with, or even explain, the presenting symptom.

## Examples from RLR episodes

- **From a Vegas-bet default to a broadened differential.** Five days of headache and photophobia in a young woman defaults to migraine by base rate alone — until her poorly controlled HIV (CD4 25) reframes the entire differential, ultimately localizing to secondary and neurosyphilis with CNS involvement.
