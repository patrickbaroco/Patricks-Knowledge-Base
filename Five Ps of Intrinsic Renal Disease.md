## Definition

A practical "day one" alternative to the traditional glomerular/tubular/vascular schema for intrinsic acute kidney injury, useful before more specific data (biopsy, detailed serologies) are available. Five categories, each starting with P: pigment, paraprotein, platelets, pharmacy, and pointy things. It trades the exhaustiveness of the classic schema for speed — a fast pattern-match to run at the bedside rather than a slower, more complete framework to work through later.

## How it operates

- **Pigment** — hemolysis or rhabdomyolysis releasing free hemoglobin or myoglobin into the tubules.
- **Paraprotein** — a monoclonal hematologic fingerprint (light-chain cast nephropathy, myeloma).
- **Platelets** — thrombotic microangiopathy (MAHA/TMA); a normal or elevated platelet count argues against this bucket.
- **Pharmacy** — drug-induced interstitial nephritis, including newer culprits like immunotherapy.
- **Pointy things** — crystalline disease, whether calcium oxalate or urate, depositing microscopically (nephrocalcinosis, urate nephropathy) even when it is also forming macroscopic stones elsewhere in the same patient.
- **Use it as a fast first pass, not a replacement.** It is explicitly offered as a substitute for the slower schema only when time and information are limited — return to the full glomerular/tubular/vascular framework once more data are in hand.

## Examples from RLR episodes

- **Ruling buckets in and out at the bedside.** In a patient with AKI, a high platelet count (not low) excludes the platelets/TMA bucket; a markedly elevated uric acid and a heavy stone burden point instead to the "pointy things" bucket, run alongside a hematologic (paraprotein-adjacent) workup once splenomegaly and marked leukocytosis are found.
