## Phase 2 — Vegetation Kill (Mid September – Mid October)

*Rescheduled 2026-08-30: the late-August round didn't happen, so both rounds shift back
about three weeks. This is fine — see the note below.*

- [ ] **Application 1: weekend of Sept 12–13** — mow short first, needs 24–48 dry hours after
- [ ] Wait 3–4 weeks
- [ ] **Application 2: weekend of Oct 3–4 (3 wks) or Oct 10–11 (4 wks)** — same protocol.
      Prefer Oct 3–4; it buys a full extra weekend of margin before frost risk

> [!note] Why September is not "too late"
> Glyphosate needs green, actively growing grass. Cool-season grass (fescue) slows in
> August heat and then *resumes vigorous growth* in early fall, translocating to the roots —
> which is exactly the movement that carries herbicide down. A mid-September application is
> at least as effective as late August, often better.

> [!warning] The real deadline is Application 2, not Application 1
> Frost is what ends the window: grass that's frost-touched or going dormant won't
> translocate, and the kill fails. Staunton-area first-frost risk begins around **Oct 19**
> (10% probability; 50% is ~Nov 1). Application 2 on Oct 3–11 clears that comfortably.
> **If Application 1 slips past ~Sept 20, the whole chain is in trouble** — Application 2
> lands on Oct 18+, into frost risk, with site prep compressed behind it.

> [!warning] Calendar check
> Protect Sept 12–13 and Oct 3–4 now. Both are weather-dependent, and each needs the
> contractor to mow short beforehand — that coordination is the piece most likely to slip.
> Keep the ~453 m² orchard out of both broadcast passes.

## Phase 4 — Dormant Seeding (Mid October – Early December)

- [ ] Monitor nighttime temps — seed when consistently below 40°F
- [ ] Broadcast seed by hand or push spreader
- [ ] Rake lightly for soil contact
- [ ] No irrigation needed — let freeze-thaw cycles work

> [!note]
> Typical window for rural Virginia: mid-to-late October. Can slide to November if October
> is warm or wet. With the 2026-08-30 reschedule, **early-to-mid November is now the
> realistic target** rather than late October.

## Phase 5 — Year One Establishment (Spring–Summer, Following Year)

- [ ] Expect weedy, sparse appearance — this is normal
- [ ] Mow whenever the stand reaches 12–18 in., cutting back to 4–6 in. — X758 at max deck (5.25 in.) is within range
- [ ] Repeat through the growing season into early fall, timing each cut before the weeds flower; expect 3–4 mowings
- [ ] Do not skip the June–August cuts — ragweed and foxtail set seed then, and each cut that beats seed set lowers Year 2 weed pressure
- [ ] Leave clippings in place unless a cut is heavy enough to mat; then rake off the thick spots
- [ ] Spot-treat perennial weeds (surviving tall fescue crowns, Canada thistle, Johnsongrass, woody seedlings) with spot-spray or wick glyphosate, or dig them
- [ ] No broadcast broadleaf herbicide — it kills the mix's legumes and composites (partridge pea, wild senna, lespedezas, asters)
- [ ] Expect meaningful flowering in Year 2

> [!tip] The patience phase
> Native seedlings stay a few inches tall in Year 1 and put their growth into roots, so a high deck passes over them and takes the weeds. Beyond mowing and spot-treating perennials, leave it alone.

> [!important] Re-scoped later the same day — this now applies to the DRAINFIELD ONLY
> The gully and front were moved to a woodland treatment (trees + no-mow fescue over-seeded

> re-scope bought.

Roughly **18 months of looking unfinished**, from mid-September 2026 to summer 2028, on the
drainfield. Kept here deliberately so it can be re-read in April 2027.

| When | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Sept 12–20** | Nothing. Glyphosate is slow — no visible change for ~a week. This is normal, not a failed application. Resist the urge to re-spray. |
| **Sept 20 – Oct 1** | Yellowing, then browning. Full straw color by 2–3 weeks after application. |
| **Oct 3–11** | Second application browns down whatever regreened. |
| **Oct – Nov** | Dead standing grass. Site prep, then seeding onto brown thatch. |
| **Dec 2026 – Mar 2027** | Flat and brown. **The least conspicuous phase** — everything in a Virginia winter is brown, so 0.26 dead acres out back blends in better than expected. |
| **April 2027** | **The hard month.** Every other lawn greens up and this one doesn't. What germinates first is annual weeds in the open ground we created; the natives are invisible seedlings putting everything into roots. Reads as *weedy and patchy*, not as *meadow*. |
| **May – Sept 2027** | Weedy and sparse, mowed to 5.25 in. each time it reaches 12–18 in., likely 3–4 times. Looks like a rough mowed field. This is the whole of year one. |
| **2028** | Meaningful flowering. Actually looks like a meadow. |

> [!tip] The single highest-leverage thing: mowed edges and the path loop
> A crisp mown border around the meadow plus the mowed path through it is what makes
> brown-then-weedy read as **deliberate** rather than **neglected**. It matters most on the
> **front zone**, which is the one people see from the road. Widen the mown frame there
> beyond what would otherwise seem necessary. This is already Phase 1 work — do not let it
> slip, it is doing more aesthetic work than anything else on the list.

> [!warning] Don't over-rake the thatch
> Roundstone's spec is to remove thatch that *prevents seed-to-soil contact* — that is a
> minimum, not a mandate to strip the ground. Standing dead grass holds soil over winter and
> keeps broadcast seed in place. Aggressive dethatching on the front zone produces bare mud,
> which looks worse **and** invites erosion on the slope.

> [!warning] The nurse crop will not rescue the appearance
> Oats or rye broadcast in November will not green up meaningfully before cold. The nurse
> crop is **erosion protection, not looks**. Green winter cover would require cereal rye
> seeded much earlier — and rye overwinters and competes with the natives in spring, which
> is a bad trade for a meadow. Per Roundstone: do not exceed nurse crop rates, and **do not
> use wheat**.

> [!note] Tell the household
> Spring 2027 is when people abandon meadow conversions and reseed to lawn. Anyone who looks
> at that front lawn should know the schedule before April, not during it.
