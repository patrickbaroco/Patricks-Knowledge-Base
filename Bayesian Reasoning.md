Evergreen concept note. Definition plus worked examples, drawn from the RLR / Clinical Problem Solvers series and elsewhere. Source episodes are cited per example.

## Definition

Bayesian reasoning is the master frame of the whole series: start from a prior probability (the [[Base Rate|base rate]], adjusted for this host), then *update* it with each new datum in proportion to how much that datum discriminates between hypotheses (its sensitivity, specificity, and predictive value), arriving at a posterior that becomes the next prior. It is not arithmetic at the bedside but a stance — every finding *moves* probability rather than proving or excluding, and how far it moves depends on the test and the disease. The "Bayesian Updates" table in each RLR episode note is this frame made explicit: data point → hypothesis raised/adjusted → direction of update.

## How it operates

- **Prior first.** Anchor the differential in prior probability before testing; a well-chosen prior means even a blind guess lands near the truth (see [[Base Rate]]), and neglecting it is [[Base-Rate Neglect|base-rate neglect]].
- **Update by discrimination, not by positivity.** A result shifts probability by how well it separates hypotheses — a "positive" with poor specificity or a "negative" with poor sensitivity barely moves the needle (see [[Test Interpretation]]).
- **Weigh predictive value against context.** A lead worth chasing can still be a false positive; hold it as *possible* while weighing its positive predictive value against contradicting data.
- **A test's meaning is conditioned on the disease.** The same result updates differently in different beds; interpret it by mechanism, not off the report.
- **Treatment is a test.** A response (or non-response) to therapy is itself a datum that updates the posterior (see [[Treatment as a Diagnostic Test]]).

## Examples from RLR episodes

- **Host-adjusted prior lowers the testing threshold.** Asplenia raises the pretest probability of encapsulated infection, justifying a lower threshold for LP even without fever — though a fully reassuring exam and labs still hold the posterior down. ([[RLR 001 - New Onset Headache|RLR 001]])
- **Predictive value weighed against contradicting data.** An elevated [[PTHrP]] is a high-morbidity lead, but it can be falsely elevated in renal failure, so a negative PET, stable weight, and chronic course argue hard against occult cancer — the hosts hold the lead as possible while explicitly weighing its [[Positive Predictive Value|PPV]]. ([[RLR 007 - Hypercalcemia|RLR 007]])
- **Base rate and imaging compound.** A ~40% malignancy rate for gross hematuria anchors cancer concern, tempered by the patient's youth; when an enhancing lesion appears, the base rate and the imaging *compound* rather than substitute. ([[RLR 018 - Gross Hematuria in End-Stage Renal Disease|RLR 018]])
- **Conditioning a negative test on mechanism.** A clean CTA argues down ACS where disease is in-situ (coronaries) but not embolic stroke (cerebral vessels) — the update from a negative test depends on the anatomy of the disease. ([[RLR 027 - Dizziness and Peripheral Vertigo|RLR 027]])
