# CQC Charter — Revision Record, 2026-08-30

*Companion to `cqc-charter-draft.md`. Records the revisions of 2026-08-30 and why each was made. Unlike the PRC and Council records, these did not come from a single external review — they came from stress-testing the charter against concrete clinical scenarios, and several were prompted by defects the Chair identified in the draft's own reasoning.*

**Summary.** Twelve substantive changes. Three were new sections filling a gap the architecture had rather than a defect in the drafting. One corrected a rule that three provisions assumed and no provision granted. Two came from the Chair rejecting language that should never have been in a charter.

---

## 1. § 5.5(a) — the correction step rewritten

**The problem.** The section offered the Practitioner the chance to identify "factual error, attribution error, or information in the record material to whether the applicable standard was met." The third limb is the one a form-strict standard largely closes, and it was listed as though it were the main event. The ground that fires most often — that the resident is not within the population the standard applies to — was not listed at all.

**Resolution.** Nine numbered provisions. Grounds are now ordered by real-world frequency: attribution, population, the departure did not occur, retrieval, timing, and other-clinician documentation last and expressly conditional. Section (a)(4) states what will *not* correct an observation, because a Practitioner who does not know that in advance will spend ten days assembling material that cannot help and will reasonably resent having been invited to. Section (a)(5) requires the notice to say all of it before the period runs: **the Committee shall not invite a response on grounds it will not accept.**

**Reframed as quality control on the audit, not a concession to the Practitioner.** § 5.4 validates the instrument; nothing validated the observation. That framing also survives the board member who asks why the process has an off-ramp in it.

**Two provisions added that the original had no equivalent of.** Section (a)(7) requires rates to be **recomputed** after attribution and population withdrawals before supporting selection or a pattern criterion — § 5.6(a) permits a crude selection threshold on the express ground that the chart, not the denominator, answers the case-mix question, and nothing previously kept that promise. Section (a)(8) feeds sustained grounds back to instrument revalidation under § 5.4.

---

## 2. § 5.5(c) — the close-out

**The problem.** An individual finding that met no referral criterion produced no communication of any kind. A Practitioner's entire contact with the audit was an allegation and then silence.

**Resolution.** Every Practitioner notified under (a) is told what became of every observation, and anyone whose observations all withdrew is told so in terms. It imposes nothing and is expressly not a Quality Intervention, so § 6.5 is unaffected.

**The reason it is mandatory rather than good practice.** The referral criteria escalate on repetition after notice. **That logic requires that notice was in fact given** — not that a standard was disseminated to everyone, but that this Practitioner was told what this audit found in these charts. Without the close-out the escalation rests on a fiction.

---

## 3. § 5.5(c)(5) — support, offered and not imposed

**The problem, and an error in the reasoning that produced it.** An earlier analysis treated individually directed education as necessarily a Quality Intervention belonging to the PRC. That put the only route to helping a Practitioner through a professional review, which is a substantial instrument to reach for where what is needed is a conversation.

**That drew the line in the wrong place.** What makes an instrument a professional review instrument is that it is **imposed**, that it follows a **finding**, and that it is **monitored against exit criteria**. An offer of open material to a named person is none of the three. Directing material at a person is not the same as imposing something on them.

**Resolution — an offer with seven constraints, each blocking a named failure.** Offered identically to everyone who receives a close-out, so it is what the process does rather than something that happens to a particular person. Taken up only on request. **Uptake never recorded, never counted in any criterion, never transmitted with a referral.** Delivered by the learning and development function under § 2.8, by someone who did not disposition the observations and holds no Employment Authority over the Practitioner. **Never assigned or tracked to completion in a learning management system** — an LMS assigns and tracks by design, and without that bar the safeguard would have been defeated by tooling rather than by anyone's decision. Any record of delivery belongs to the open education record. And expressly not a Quality Intervention.

**The offer discharges nothing.** It carries no obligation to accept it, and does not diminish the standard, the finding, or the Practitioner's own professional obligation to close a gap once aware of it. **This Charter does not tell a Practitioner that no action is required of them**, and no notice under Article V says so.

---

## 4. § 5.11 — follow-up on a closed finding

**The problem.** The audit measures by campaign. A finding closed on one standard would never be examined again, because the interval before that standard is next swept may be years. Every part of the Charter that turns on what happens after a close-out silently assumed re-audit, and nothing guaranteed it.

**Resolution.** **A sweep and a follow-up are different instruments and cannot share a schedule.** A targeted look at [120] days, on the same standard, independently of whatever is then being swept, diaried by the Coordinator so the trigger does not depend on anyone remembering across a change of topic.

**It runs once.** Clear, and the Practitioner returns to the general population with no further targeted look. Not clear, and it is the second occasion the referral criteria contemplate. **It cannot repeat or extend**, because an examination directed at a named Practitioner that recurs without limit is a monitoring status this Committee has no authority to impose.

**Disclosed at close-out**, because the Practitioner is entitled to know, and because without the disclosure the offer of support says nothing about what happens next. Section 5.11.6 extends the principle upward: a Systems disposition that produced an intervention gets re-measured, since a programme that intervenes and never measures again has no basis for reporting that it improves care.

---

## 5. § 4.7(e) — a rule three provisions assumed and none granted

**The most serious finding of the round, and it was found from the outside.** Section 5.5(a) and everything built on it referred to "a standard requiring the treating Practitioner's own element-by-element entry." **Section 4.7(b) permits nothing of the kind and never did** — it governs granularity, not locus. The assumption was load-bearing in four places, and the PRC Charter was describing a CQC rule the CQC Charter did not contain.

**What replaced it is a better rule, not a repaired one.** An authorship requirement — write it yourself although the consultant already did — is a documentation tax with no clinical content, and indefensible in exactly the forum where it would be attacked. **What the programme needs is that the treating Practitioner's note account for the element**, and an attribution satisfies that. The Practitioner is never asked to justify another clinician's decision, only to show the element was considered and record where it stands.

The rule is opt-in by designation, because a self-contained entry is the requirement most readily met by a stored phrase, and § 5.10 applies to it with particular force.

---

## 6. § 4.7(e) — settled and pending dispositions

Prompted by the entry *"anticoagulation on hold per cardiology."* **That is not a weak attribution; it is a defective pending entry**, because *on hold* asserts that something happens later and the entry says nothing about what.

The branches are now named. A **settled** disposition needs no resolving condition because nothing is pending. A **pending** disposition must carry what resolves it — an event, a date, a clinician, a threshold. *"Held per cardiology until after the epidural injection"* satisfies; *"held per cardiology"* does not.

**Failing that entry is not a harsher standard.** The fix is four words, and they are the four words that distinguish a plan from a gap — the indefinite hold nobody revisits is how a resident with atrial fibrillation spends eighteen months off anticoagulation. The ignorance case is provided for expressly, so the requirement cannot become a demand to reconstruct a specialist's reasoning.

**Subsections (b) and (c) were reconciled with (e) in the same pass.** Read strictly, (e)'s alternatives did not satisfy (b)'s requirement of a reason. Fixed on both sides. And (c) gained the distinction that explains why the two coexist: *"not a candidate"* is a reason without a referent, and *"per cardiology"* is a referent without a reason. Subsection (c) was written to catch the first and never contemplated the second.

---

## 7. § 5.10(c) — thin entries

For the case the settled branch legitimately admits: *"discontinued by cardiology"*, no reason, satisfies the letter and teaches the next reader nothing.

Failing it would rebuild the authorship rule through the audit. Ignoring it would make *"per cardiology"* the universal safe harbour within two cycles, which is what the rest of § 5.10 exists to prevent.

**Recorded as thin, always dispositioned Systems, never attributed, never referred.** Where thin entries on a measure carry real risk, the remedy is to redesignate the standard under § 4.6 to require both an attribution and a reason — not to refer anyone.

---

## 8. § 4.4(a) — what a minimum expectation is

**The problem.** It had defined a minimum expectation as *a level below which care may indicate a departure from the standard reasonably expected of a Practitioner* — which asserts the conclusion this Committee is expressly not positioned to reach, and which § 8.1.2 of the PRC Charter then had to disclaim from the other side. **Two governing documents pulling against each other on the same point is how a plaintiff comes to pick one.**

**Resolution.** Defined functionally: an individual-level requirement the Entity expects Practitioners to meet, a departure from which may support a referral. An express sentence states that the designation establishes nothing about the legal or professional standard of care.

The tension became unavoidable with § 4.7(e), which admits a standard that is a documentation and communication convention rather than a clinical rule. **What keeps a minimum expectation clinically grounded is § 5.8(a)** — every standard states its clinical basis at adoption — not the wording of the designation.

The redefinition also sharpens the two designations rather than blurring them: the operative difference is now cleanly that one may support a referral and the other may never.

---

## 9. § 5.5(b)(1) and § 6.7(c) — the clinicians in the room

**The problem, identified by the Chair.** The Charter had implied that reviewers should not form clinical impressions from audit data. That is a fiction. Everyone doing the review is a clinician; six of six residents with no accounting and no documentation produces an impression instantly and involuntarily. **The constraint is on the Committee's finding, not on anyone's perception**, and the Charter should say so rather than imply otherwise.

**Resolution.** Section 5.5(b)(1) has the Chair put the safety question aloud before every disposition against a clinical standard, with the minutes recording that it was put and answered. The Charter now states plainly that reviewers will form clinical impressions, that this is expected and not improper, and that it is most of the reason the audit is worth running.

**Section 6.7(c) was reframed from a loophole into a route.** It had been written defensively, as an anti-circumvention rule. But a § 6.7 referral is personal and carries the referrer's name, and that social cost is what would otherwise decide whether a concern is ever raised. **Asking the question in the room turns an act of individual initiative into an ordinary item of business.** A route nobody uses is the same as no route.

---

## 10. § 5.9 — education as a third route, and the onboarding case

Education added alongside standards revision and the audit question. The three routes correspond to the three ordinary causes of widespread nonconformity — the standard, the audit question, or the teaching — and the last is both the most common and the most fixable.

**And the onboarding paragraph**, on the case of a capable Practitioner newly working in a clinical area. The gap is predictable, finding it is the point of the programme, and **a predictable gap is a reason to look rather than a reason to attribute.** A gap concentrated among clinicians new to an area is a condition of onboarding, and the remedy is a programme rather than anything directed at an individual.

---

## 11. Interfaces kept aligned

**§ 6.3** now carries the evidentiary chain the escalation depends on: the close-out notice, the disclosure of the follow-up, and the fact of the follow-up conducted. **A referral resting on repetition after notice cannot be screened without them**, and PRC § 4.7 screens for exactly that. It transmits nothing about support offered. PRC § 11.3 mirrors it and the two are to be kept as a matched pair.

**§ 7.4** now reports follow-up looks and their outcomes in aggregate — the direct evidence of whether the close-out and the offer correct what they are meant to, and the answer when the Council asks whether the programme does anything.

**§ 2.8** names the learning and development function on the model of § 2.6, and distinguishes the open education agenda from the one place that function learns something attributable to an identified Practitioner.

**Naming.** The referral schedule had three names across three Charters. It is **the Audit Referral Policy** in all of them.

---

## 12. Two things removed at the Chair's direction

**Language conceding the instrument's unreliability.** The § 5.5 opening had quantified an error rate — an instrument accurate in ninety-five percent of charts is wrong about a given chart one time in twenty. Accurate as reasoning, wrong as operative text: it states a rate no validation has measured, sits in a document that circulates, and hands a Practitioner facing escalation the argument that nothing the audit finds should count. The rule now does the same work without the concession, and the illustration lives in the drafting notes.

**Narration of drafting history.** Nine passages across the three Charters described what earlier versions had said. **A charter is a governing instrument, not a changelog**, and telling a reader that an earlier draft asserted something invites the argument that the organization believed it. Rationale forward in the text; history to the notes, which is what this document is for.

---

## 13. § 2.5 and § 9.3 — the exclusion follows the act (2026-09-10)

**The problem.** Both provisions excluded members holding Employment Authority from "the disposition of an individually attributed observation under § 5.5." At drafting that was an exhaustive description of the determinations this Committee makes about an identified Practitioner. Drafting the Audit Referral Policy produced another one. Under a mandatory referral schedule most of Article VI is arithmetic — was a criterion met, do the preconditions hold, does the volume brake trip — but the Policy also carries a first-year discretion to decline to refer where a criterion is met, and **a decision not to refer a named clinician is an act of judgment about that clinician.** A member holding Employment Authority participating in it would be a supervisor voting on whether someone they supervise goes to peer review, which is the configuration the two-class rule exists to prevent.

**Resolution.** Both provisions now reach **any determination concerning an identified Practitioner**, with the § 5.5 disposition and any Article VI determination named as instances rather than as the definition. The exclusion attaches to the character of the act, so it does not need revisiting each time the Policy adds a determination.

**Not a collision with § 5.5(b)(1).** That subsection requires the Chair to put the safety question aloud at every disposition, which would need a named alternate if the Chair were excluded. **The Chair of Clinical Excellence holds no Employment Authority within the meaning of Council § 2.7**, confirmed 2026-09-10, and the Council's own composition math at § 3.2(b) has always assumed it. No alternate is required.

**No equivalent gap in the other two Charters.** The PRC excludes Employment Authority holders from the Committee and from the Reviewer Pool outright at § 2.3. At the Council, ratification of the Audit Referral Policy under § 4.2(f) is general business under § 7.4(a), where all voting members vote, which is correct — ratifying a schedule is not a determination about any Practitioner.

## 14. § 5.11.3(a) — what the closure ends (2026-09-10)

**The problem.** The subsection closed the matter where a follow-up look produced no individual finding, and added that "no further targeted examination of that Practitioner on that measure occurs." That sentence carries two readings. Narrowly it ends the look attached to that finding. Broadly it bars any later follow-up on that measure for that Practitioner, which would mean the close-out-and-look-again sequence could run **once per Practitioner per standard for the whole of a career** — and a finding two years later, on a measure the Practitioner had once been cleared on, could never be followed up at all. Criterion B in the Audit Referral Policy rests on that sequence, so the broad reading would quietly disable the escalation for anyone who had passed a follow-up once.

**Resolution.** The narrow reading was intended and is now stated. The closure ends the look attached to the finding that occasioned it; a later individual finding on the same measure is a new matter, closed out under § 5.5(c) and followed up under § 5.11.2 in the ordinary course.

**What still holds.** Section 5.11.3(c) is untouched: a look is not repeated and not extended within a matter, because an examination directed at a named Practitioner that recurs without limit is a monitoring status this Committee has no authority to impose. The distinction is between running the sequence again on a new finding and never letting it end on an old one.

---

## 15. § 5.5(b) — the disposition classifies, the schedule decides (2026-09-10)

**The problem.** The Individual disposition read: "the observation is validated as an individual finding, and **is referred** under §§ 6.1–6.5 where a criterion is met." Stated that way the Charter itself fixes the consequence, in terms admitting no exception — and the Charter already requires an exception twenty lines further on. **Section 5.9 provides that a criterion which would refer a large fraction of the Practitioners it applies to routes to standards revision, to the audit question, or to the education agenda "not to mass referral."** A schedule operationalising § 5.9 therefore had to decline a referral in a case where a criterion was met, which § 5.5(b) as drafted did not permit. The Audit Referral Policy adds a second such case, a first-period discretion that expires at the first annual review.

**Resolution.** The disposition now classifies the observation and states that referral is determined by the criteria adopted under § 6.2. A paragraph follows explaining why the consequence is stated in one instrument rather than two.

**Why this direction rather than adding exceptions here.** Section 6.2 keeps the criteria out of the Charter expressly so they can be calibrated against actual findings without amendment. Enumerating in the Charter the circumstances in which a met criterion does not refer would put the calibration back into the instrument it was taken out of, and each future adjustment would need a Charter amendment rather than a Council ratification.

**What is unchanged.** Section 5.1 continues to provide that only an individual finding may be referred through §§ 6.1–6.5, and § 4.4(a) that only a departure from a minimum expectation may support such a referral. Both are limits on what may be referred and neither states that anything must be. Section 5.11.3(b) already deferred to the criteria — a follow-up finding "is the second occasion contemplated by the criteria under § 6.2 and is dispositioned accordingly."

## 16. § 8.1.1 and § 5.6(c) — where a comparison group sits (2026-09-10)

**The problem.** Section 8.1.1 opens by stating that the two records are exhaustive and that each of the Committee's instruments is classified **expressly rather than left to inference**. It then classifies the standards register, the audit register, selection thresholds, follow-up sample sizes and the Audit Referral Policy — and does not reach the comparison groups that § 5.6(c) requires to be adopted *within* that Policy.

**Why the omission was not neutral.** The Policy is open under § 8.1.1. A construction adopted within an open instrument is open by implication, and a comparison group carries the identities of the Practitioners composing it and their rates. **The provision as drafted would have entitled any Practitioner to their colleagues' audit rates**, which no part of this Charter intends and which § 8.1(b) classifies as protected wherever it appears in any other form.

**Resolution.** A comparison group is treated as two things, classified separately and held as separate documents, which is what § 8.1's rule that no document is ever in both records requires.

- **The definition** — the characteristics by which the group is constructed, chosen before any rate is known — is **open**. It is what a Practitioner needs in order to know the terms of a comparison drawn against them, and it belongs with the standard it attaches to.
- **The constitution in a cycle** — which Practitioners fell within the group and what their rates were — is **protected**. It is derived from observations and is a record of other people's care.

**Why a group definition is open although a selection threshold is not.** The two invite different objections. A published threshold tells a Practitioner what to stay below, which is the reason § 8.1.1 protects thresholds. A published group definition tells them nothing they can act on: discipline, setting and panel size are conditions of the work rather than choices about the charts.

**Section 5.6(c) carried the same ambiguity** in the words "the construction of each group is recorded," which did not distinguish the definition from the constitution. It now says where each is recorded and how each is classified.

## 17. § 5.9, § 5.6(d) and § 7.4 — three consequential cleanups (2026-09-10)

**§ 5.9 and § 5.6(d) each preserved a criterion that does not exist.** Both carved out "severity-based referral" from the limits they impose — § 5.9 from the systems routing of widespread nonconformity, § 5.6(d) from the bar on pattern criteria where no adequate comparison can be built. **The referral criteria contain no criterion turning on the seriousness of a particular case.** A criterion of that kind would require the Committee to form a view about an identified Practitioner's care, which § 1.3 reserves to the PRC and which § 5.5(b)(1) routes to § 6.7(c) as the act of the member who holds the concern. What the criteria do contain is **referral on a single occasion**, which attaches to a standard by designation at its adoption and not to a case by assessment at referral. Both provisions now name that, so the carve-outs point to something real.

**Why this mattered rather than being a naming question.** A carve-out referring to nothing is read by whoever needs it to mean something. Section 5.6(d) is the provision that protects a Practitioner in a small discipline from a pattern referral resting on a denominator too small to say anything, and a phrase suggesting a residual severity route around it is exactly what would be argued over.

**§ 7.4 did not report the brake.** The annual report enumerated standards, education, cycles, dispositions, follow-up looks and systems conditions, and omitted the occasions on which referral on a measure was withheld under § 5.9. **That provision is what this Charter relies on to prevent a badly specified audit question from producing a wave of referrals**, and its operation was visible to no one above the Committee. The report now carries each occasion and what was done instead, which is also what allows the Council to calibrate the criteria under § 4.2(f) of its Charter.

## 18. Consistency pass against the Audit Referral Policy (2026-09-10)

Nine provisions revised so that this Charter and the schedule adopted under § 6.2 state each rule once, in one instrument. Identified on a check of the Policy draft against all three Charters.

**§ 1.3** provided that "where the audit raises a concern about an identified Practitioner, the Committee refers it under §§ 6.1–6.5." Two defects. It asserts the automatic consequence § 5.5(b) was amended to leave to the criteria — and under those criteria most measures will produce no referral at all. And it describes what the audit produces as a **concern**, which §§ 5.5(b)(1) and 6.7(c) reserve for what a member holds and refers as a person, the Committee making no referral on that basis. It now speaks of an individual finding meeting a criterion adopted under § 6.2.

**§ 5.11.3(b)** stated that a follow-up finding "is the second occasion contemplated by the criteria under § 6.2." That fixes the consequence here, as § 5.5(b) formerly did, and it is wrong for a measure whose register entry admits no criterion at all. It now defers to the criteria in the terms § 5.5(b) uses.

**§ 5.11.3(a)** closed a matter wherever the follow-up produced no individual finding. Where another examination has already made a finding on that standard for care furnished in the window, the premise fails: **a sample that misses what another examination found is evidence about the sample.** Closure now requires that no such finding has been made. Without this the Practitioner would be notified that a matter had closed while a finding capable of supporting a referral stood.

**§ 5.6(c)** was amended on 2026-09-10 to record a group definition "with the Policy." That was wrong in the same way naming a measure in the Policy would be: a definition is measure-specific, and the Policy is ratified by the Council, so adopting or changing one would have required a fresh ratification. **The definition is now recorded in the standards register with the standard it attaches to**, where it is open under § 8.1.1 by the same route as the standard itself.

**§ 5.6(d)** carved out named referral routes and by naming two implied the exclusion of the third. It now states directly that the subsection **limits pattern criteria only**, which is what it does.

**§ 2.5** closed by claiming the rule is applied at the Council "in the same terms." It is not: Council § 7.4(b) excludes Employment Authority from votes on Professional Review Actions, and this section now reaches determinations that are not dispositions. It states the relationship rather than an identity, and **§ 7.4 of the Council Charter is amended in the same pass** to describe this rule as it now stands.

**§ 2.8 and § 5.9** each cited **a § 4.2(b) that does not exist.** Section 4.2 is the derivation of standards and has no subsections; the education agenda is set at § 3.1(b). Both now cite it. This defect predates the Policy and would have sent anyone following the citation to the wrong Article.

Criteria architecture settled 2026-09-10 in `audit-referral-policy-skeleton.md`; prose draft v0.1 written the same day as `audit-referral-policy-draft.md`, prepared for counsel review. Section 6.2, the close-out escalation and PRC § 4.7 each assume it exists and says specific things, and it now does. **What remains open within it** is set out in its own "Open items in this draft": the bracketed defaults in §§ 3.4, 4.3, 5.2, 6.6, 7.4 and 9.5; the margin, floors and group denominator deliberately unset until the first cycles produce a baseline (§ 9.3); and four questions for counsel.
- **The antipsychotic standard's § 4.7(e) designation** may not survive first contact, because the behaviour documentation genuinely lives in the nursing log and requiring it twice may produce copy-paste rather than better decisions. Section 5.5(a)(8)(iii) is the provision that will surface it.

- **Whether the readmission clock resets** for the sliding-scale insulin standard, which the standard has to state and the instrument will get wrong.
- **The counsel questions** in the Charter's own list, now including privilege for the learning and development function.

## 19. Composition, read for what it costs in practicing clinicians (2026-09-18)

**Context.** Recruiting practicing clinicians is the binding constraint across all three bodies, and this Committee was read for what its composition demands. It has no fixed seats; § 2.2 lets the Chair appoint as many members as the work needs. What it fixes is the ratio at § 2.3, two-thirds in active practice, so every non-clinician member costs two practicing clinicians. The members Patrick wants on this Committee rather than on the Council, the people who know the enterprise goals, are the ones who do not practice.

**§ 2.4, psychotherapist seat to consultation.** None of the five audit measures concerns psychotherapy; the PRC cannot open a psychotherapist review until the § 6.5 addendum exists; and the counsel list already asks whether psychotherapist membership complicates the Indiana and North Carolina composition analysis. The seat was one or two practicing clinicians recruited to a body with no psychotherapy standard in front of it. A licensed psychotherapist is now consulted before any standard concerning psychotherapy is adopted, attending on the § 2.6 pattern, and the seat returns when the addendum does.

**§ 2.6.1, enterprise priorities as a non-member function; § 2.5 capped at one.** Analytics under § 2.6 and learning and development under § 2.8 already attend as functions rather than members. Enterprise priorities, accountable care and clinical operations among them, now reach the Committee the same way, with full part in standards, education and systems discussion, no vote, and no presence for any determination concerning an identified Practitioner. What they lose against membership is a vote on the education agenda and the standards, which the Chair can weigh without a count. The function comes with a duty the Committee did not have: each enterprise priority gets a stated response in the annual audit plan. Members holding Employment Authority are capped at [one], so that seating the enterprise view as a member, where that is wanted, costs two clinicians once rather than repeatedly.

**§ 3.1(f), the annual audit plan, new.** The Charter referred to an audit plan at § 5.11.6 and never said the Committee adopts one. It now does: the measures for the year, the standard each measures against, and the response to each enterprise priority, transmitted to the Council. The plan states what will be measured; authority over the standards stays here under Article IV. This is the instrument by which the Council reviews the program for alignment with enterprise priorities without touching the standards, which is the division Patrick described on 2026-09-15. **Council § 4.2 does not yet list receipt of the plan and needs a line.**

**§ 9.3, a floor for determinations concerning an identified Practitioner.** Dispositions under § 5.5 and determinations under Article VI were taken by the members eligible under § 2.5 with no stated minimum. The decision that turns an observation into a finding could have been taken by two people, or by whichever one was not away. Council § 7.3(b) sets a floor of four for the analogous vote. This Charter now requires [three] eligible members present, and the determination waits where fewer are.

**What the Committee now costs.** The Chair of Clinical Excellence and the two department chairs sit by office. With a floor of three eligible members and the department chairs eligible where they hold no Employment Authority, four appointed practicing clinicians keeps three reliably in the room. A working Committee is seven members, with analytics, learning and development, enterprise priorities and the Coordinator at the table as functions.
