# Clinical Governance at Eventus: How It Works

*Path B: authority conferred by the CEO*

---

Clinical governance at Eventus runs through two committees. The Clinical Quality Committee looks at care across the whole group. The Peer Review Committee looks at the care of individual clinicians. The Chair of Clinical Excellence chairs both and reports on their work to the Chief Medical Officer and Chief Operating Officer.

## The Clinical Quality Committee

The Clinical Quality Committee sets the standards of care the group measures itself against, builds education around those standards, and runs the audit program that measures them. Its audit priorities are set each year with the group's enterprise quality goals in view.

The Committee has no authority over any individual clinician. Most of what its audits find leads to education or to fixing a system problem. When a finding meets a criterion set out in advance in the Audit Referral Policy, the Committee refers it to the Peer Review Committee, and its involvement in that matter ends.

## The Peer Review Committee

The Peer Review Committee reviews the care furnished by individual clinicians. A concern can reach it from an audit referral, a colleague, a facility, management, or anyone else who raises one.

Two Committee members review each case and propose findings. The full Committee then votes, and its decision is final. The CEO confers this authority on the Committee once, in writing, and may withdraw it at any time. The CEO takes no part in individual cases.

The Committee can close a review with no finding. It can also agree an educational plan with the clinician, require a focused review or proctoring, or restrict what the clinician may do. Before any restriction takes effect, the clinician is told in writing and may ask for a hearing before a panel of clinicians who took no part in the review. The panel reports to the Committee, which then makes its final decision. Panel members do not vote on it.

The Chair of Clinical Excellence presides over the Committee and does not vote. After each decision, the Chair confirms the required process was followed and signs the decision. If the Chair is recused, the Vice-Chair takes that role.

No one who holds Employment Authority sits on the Committee or reviews a case. Supervisors may raise concerns and provide information, but they do not judge the care of the clinicians they supervise.

Questions of a clinician's health, impairment or fitness to practice go to Human Resources and are handled under its own policies. The Committee's only continuing concern in such a matter is whether patients are safe in the meantime.

## Reporting

The Chair of Clinical Excellence meets quarterly with the CMO and COO. The Chair reports the volume and categories of reviews and their outcomes, any reports made to the National Practitioner Data Bank or a licensing board, the system problems that reviews and audits have uncovered, how the Clinical Quality Committee's audit plan addresses the group's enterprise priorities, and each audit's results for the whole group and for each RDCOE territory. The CMO and COO decide how those results are shared. These reports identify no clinician and describe no case. The meeting keeps the executive team informed and lets operations act on system problems. It makes no decision about any clinician.

Management receives notice of any final action affecting a clinician's practice: what the action is, when it takes effect, and how long it lasts. Management does not receive the findings, the evidence, or the Committee's deliberations.

The CEO receives the same quarterly summary. The CEO is also told promptly, outside that cycle, when any of four events occurs: litigation arising from a review, a report to the Data Bank, a restriction lasting more than thirty days, or an allegation that the process was not followed.

## What stays where it is

Employment decisions remain with management and are made on the employment record. A peer review action and an employment decision are made separately, on separate records, and neither depends on the other. Billing and coding remain with compliance. Participation in facility QAPI programs is unaffected.

## Launching it

The CEO launches the structure by signing a one-page authorization. The authorization does six things:

1. It establishes the Clinical Quality Committee and the Peer Review Committee and approves their charters. The hearing procedure is written into the Peer Review Committee's charter.
2. It gives the Peer Review Committee authority to decide, on Eventus's behalf, matters concerning an individual clinician's competence or professional conduct, and states that the CEO may withdraw that authority at any time.
3. It names the Chair of Clinical Excellence as chair of both committees and authorizes the Chair to appoint their members.
4. It designates Human Resources as the pathway for health and fitness matters.
5. It commits Eventus to defend and indemnify committee members for their good-faith service, and confirms that insurance covers them.
6. It sets the reporting described above and requires the CEO's approval for any later change to the charters.

After the authorization, the Chair appoints the members of both committees. The Clinical Governance Steering Committee then dissolves into the Clinical Quality Committee, and the current informal peer review process ends when the Peer Review Committee takes its first case.
