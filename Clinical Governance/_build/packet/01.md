# Clinical Governance Framework — Overview

---

The framework creates two clinical governance committees, the Clinical Quality Committee and the Peer Review Committee, both established by a written Authorization from the Chief Executive Officer. Together they define good care, measure whether it is being delivered, address individual clinical concerns fairly, and take formal action when necessary — while keeping quality improvement, peer review, employment decisions, and operational management separate.

## The two committees

**Clinical Quality Committee — define, teach, measure.** The CQC sets evidence-based clinical standards suited to the long-term care and assisted living population, builds the education supporting them, and audits performance against them. It distinguishes minimum expectations from aspirational improvement targets, and **validates observations before any becomes a finding against an individual** — clinicians get an opportunity to show that a resident wasn't theirs, wasn't in the population the standard applies to, or that the note exists somewhere the search didn't look.

**Most of what the audit finds is never referred anywhere.** Every clinician whose charts were read gets a review report: each chart, met or not, an offer of education and support that carries no obligation and is not recorded either way, and an invitation to correct anything the review got wrong. For most standards that is the end of it. Where a repeated gap could be referred, the CQC looks once more at a sample of charts after a defined period, and only then checks the observations formally with the clinician. Only where a written referral criterion is met does the matter go to the PRC, and the CQC's involvement then ends. Any clinician may also ask the CQC for help at any time, without being audited. The CQC does not determine competence, discipline anyone, or decide whether care met the legal or professional standard of care.

**Peer Review Committee — review individual clinical care.** The PRC conducts confidential professional review where concern arises about the care furnished by an identified clinician. Matters reach it from the CQC, from clinicians, management, facilities, patients or families, from adverse events, external actions, or any other source. It determines independently whether care met the standard reasonably expected of a practitioner in that discipline and setting; **a CQC finding is evidence, not a PRC finding.**

Its dispositions are proportionate by design: close with no adverse finding; record a finding with no intervention; **agree an educational plan that closes the matter with no finding and nothing reportable**; provide feedback, education, mentoring or retrospective focused review; or propose a Professional Review Action, from mandatory consultation or proctoring to a restriction on Clinical Authorization. Two members review each case, and the Committee decides; no one holding employment authority sits on it.

**Authority and due process — establish, decide, hear.** The Chief Executive Officer establishes both committees by a one-page written Authorization, approves their charters, and must approve any amendment in writing. The Authorization gives the PRC authority to decide, on Eventus's behalf, matters concerning an individual clinician's professional competence or conduct, including action on Clinical Authorization. **Its signed decisions are Eventus's action.** The CEO may withdraw that authority in writing, for the future only, and takes no part in individual cases. Before any Professional Review Action is taken, the clinician receives written notice and may request a hearing, held within peer review before a panel of clinicians who took no part in the review and hold no employment authority; the PRC then takes final action, and no officer can review it. The Chair of Clinical Excellence chairs both committees. At the PRC the Chair presides without a vote, confirms in writing after each decision that the process was followed, and signs it. The Authorization also commits Eventus to defend and indemnify those who serve.

## How it works

Define the standard → educate → audit → report to every clinician, with an offer of education → fix the system → look again once where a repeat could be referred → validate with the clinician → refer an individual concern only on a written criterion → independent peer review → proportionate response → notice and the chance of a hearing where a Professional Review Action is proposed → the PRC's decision.

**The audit is not the only route in, and it is not the most important one.** Care raising a patient-safety or standard-of-care concern can be referred directly, by anyone, with no threshold and no written standard required. The reverse also holds: failing an internal CQC standard does not establish substandard care. The PRC starts fresh and decides that question itself.

## What management keeps

**All employment authority is retained** — hiring, termination, discipline, compensation, contracting, scheduling, assignment, and medical director nomination are untouched, and the charters say so expressly. **The committees cannot see compensation, productivity, revenue or cost, and may not consider them**, which is the primary defense against any later claim that a clinical decision was financially motivated. Management receives notice of every final action and its operational effect.

**Reporting upward is in aggregate.** The Chair meets quarterly with the Chief Medical Officer and the Chief Operating Officer and reports the committees' work without identifying any clinician or describing any case; the CEO receives the same report. The meeting makes no decision about any clinician.

## The reporting obligation

Defining clinical authorization creates a duty the organization does not have today: a restriction lasting longer than thirty days becomes reportable to the National Practitioner Data Bank.

**That duty and the legal protections arise from the same status and are not separable.** An organization that declines the obligation declines the protection with it, and goes on acting on clinician performance without either. Reports are also infrequent by design — the great majority of committee activity is education and oversight, which is not reportable.

## What the structure solves

It closes the quality loop rather than leaving quality to individual judgment exercised case by case.

It creates **intermediate responses** — feedback, education, mentoring, focused review, mandatory consultation, proctoring — between doing nothing and ending a clinician's relationship with the organization. Where those two extremes are the only options available, the first is the usual outcome, and the second is the action least protected by federal peer review immunity and most likely to be litigated.

It creates **procedural credibility.** The people who measure quality are not the people who judge competence. Two members review a case and the full Committee decides it. Clinicians holding employment authority do not decide adverse actions. Contested actions receive a hearing before clinicians who took no part in the review, and the Chair who signs each decision does not vote on it.

And it creates deliberate **information barriers** between quality improvement, peer review, employment records, facility QAPI, and patient-safety work product. That separation is what keeps clinical quality work from becoming either an informal disciplinary process or an indiscriminate record of organizational problems.

## Health, impairment and fitness

Matters raising a clinician's health, impairment or fitness to practise are **referred to the pathway the CEO designates**, in practice HR, which handles them under its own policies. Peer review does not investigate, adjudicate or manage such a matter, and neither receives nor requests any information about a Practitioner's health, evaluation, treatment or accommodation. What it keeps is the safety question: whether the Practitioner is still furnishing care while the matter is unresolved. The CEO makes the designation in the Authorization rather than the Charter naming HR, so that a change to the receiving policy needs no amendment here.
