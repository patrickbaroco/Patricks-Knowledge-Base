# Clinical Governance — what is in this packet

*The charters themselves are not included here; these are the plain-language documents that describe what they do.*

---

## The order to read them in

The first four describe the structure. The next three describe what it is like to be inside it — as someone whose charts are audited, or as someone asked to serve on a committee. The last puts the whole process on one page.

| | Document | The question it answers |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | **Framework Overview** | What are the two committees, and what does each one decide? Two pages of prose; the fastest way in. |
| 02 | **Seat Map** | Who sits where, what each committee can do, and what each committee expressly cannot do. Drawn rather than written. |
| 03 | **How a Review Unfolds** | One matter followed from the audit through to a final decision, with every point at which it can end. |
| 04 | **Where the Existing Work Goes** | Which of today's standing activities each committee absorbs. The answer to "is this another new thing on top of what we already do?" |
| 05 | **How a Quality Audit Would Work** | Written for the clinician being audited: how charts get picked, what the letter says, what happens next. |
| 06 | **Clinical Quality Committee — Member Guide** | The Quality Committee's charter in plain language, for someone deciding whether to serve on it. |
| 07 | **Peer Review Committee — Member Guide** | The same for the Peer Review Committee, in more detail, because the charter is more intricate. |
| 08 | **Review Flow, One Page** | The whole process on a single slide: where a matter starts, each step it can take, and every place it can end. |

## Three things worth knowing before reading

**The Quality Committee has no authority over any individual, at any point.** It writes standards, teaches them, measures against them, and fixes what the measurement shows is broken. Every clinician whose charts it reads gets a report on them, with an offer of education. On a standard where a repeated gap could be referred, it looks again, once, at a sample of charts after a set period; where a finding then meets a written referral rule, it hands the matter to the Peer Review Committee and stops. Any clinician can also ask it for help without being audited. It cannot restrict practice, require anything of anyone, or decide whether care met the standard of care.

**Two members review; the Committee decides; any restriction comes only after notice and the chance of a hearing.** Two members of the Peer Review Committee review each case and the full Committee decides it. Before any restriction takes effect, the clinician is told in writing and may ask for a hearing before clinicians who took no part in the review. No one holding authority over a clinician's employment takes part in any of these steps.

**The audit is not the main way a concern arrives.** Anything raising a patient-safety or standard-of-care question can be referred directly, by anyone, with no written standard required and no threshold to clear. The audit measures against standards the organization has adopted and published; everything else is measured against accepted practice, which exists whether or not anything has been written down.
