## Why the Charter says what it says

The Charter keeps its rules and leaves most of their reasons here. Where this section and the Charter differ, the Charter governs.

### Establishment and composition (Articles I to III)

The Chief Executive Officer's power to withdraw the Committee's authority is written into the Charter, so that no one has to infer it from the authorization that created the Committee (§ 1.1.2).

Nurse practitioners and physician assistants are treated as peers because their clinical responsibilities at Eventus are the same (§ 1.6(i)).

The same procedure applies to physicians and APCs by choice. The federal protections are framed around review of physicians and reach review of other clinicians less certainly, and the Charter does not tier its process by discipline to follow them (§ 1.7).

The Chair also chairs the CQC, whose audit is one source of referrals here. The Chair presides without a vote so that no one person stands at both ends of a matter (§ 2.1).

The physician floor is three so that a physician matter can still be paneled from the standing membership after one recusal (§ 2.4). The Reviewer Pool exists so that discipline and setting matching can be met as membership and case mix change, and so that review capacity can grow with volume (§ 2.5).

Department Chairs screen so that the Committee's members stay clear of a matter until a review opens, and so that the first decision is made by a clinician who knows how that service line practises (§ 2.5.1).

Minutes identify each document reviewed because Ohio courts require document-level proof that a record falls within a committee's work, and do not accept general descriptions (§ 2.11).

The rule on medical director designation is narrow because a Committee record available for a designation decision would be a record moved between the clinical and employment tracks that § 3.5 keeps apart (§ 3.6).

### Referrals and screening (Article IV)

The audit is one source of matters and not the principal one (§ 4.1). A facility's interests in a matter are not confined to the quality of the care, and a complaint may follow a clinical position the clinician was right to take, on antimicrobial stewardship or on hospitalization rates, for example (§ 4.2).

The narrow exception for mandatory referrals is for a referral naming the wrong clinician, or a licensure action on a purely administrative ground such as a lapsed registration or unmet continuing education (§ 4.3(c)).

The health pathway is designated outside the Charter so that a change to the HR policy behind it needs no amendment. The Committee does not keep a health matter because it has no standard by which to decide one. It keeps the safety question because the concern that a clinician may be furnishing care unsafely is its own, and stays open until something answers it (§ 4.3(d)).

A Department Chair does not screen a matter they helped send from the CQC; both Department Chairs withdraw from CQC referral decisions in their own service line (§ 4.4.1). A primary care clinician's prescribing of an antipsychotic is the ordinary case for adding the other Department Chair to screening (§ 4.4.2).

The safety question is asked at screening so that the moment it is first asked is fixed, and its recorded answer is what gives meaning to the statement Management receives (§§ 4.4.4, 4.5.2). A supervisor answers for the patients and facilities of a region, and may properly ask whether a clinician is safe to keep seeing patients. That can be answered without the substance of the matter (§ 4.5.2).

A referral that was unsupported is closed without notice, because telling a clinician that an unsupported allegation was made and dismissed serves no one. The optional notice of a genuine but minor concern exists because the only alternatives would be a full review or silence (§ 4.5.1).

The rule on referrals drawn from audit information keeps the protections of the CQC's route from being bypassed by using another. The CQC's notice period is the clearest case: a referral the CQC could not yet make tries to get ahead of it, and one resting on a finding the CQC's criteria would not refer bypasses the same protections less visibly. The rule polices the boundary between the two routes and closes neither (§ 4.7).

### Notice and the agreed resolution (Article V)

The opening notice states every possible outcome, including closing with no finding, because a clinician is entitled to the whole picture and not only its most serious end (§ 5.1).

What a clinician learned under an agreed resolution is not graded, because grading it would be a judgment about competence, and the agreed resolution rests on none (§ 5.7.4).

The agreed resolution exists because, between an offer of support that no one must accept and a contested review, there was nothing. A gap serious enough to reach peer review leaves Eventus obliged to know that the care now meets the standard. Completing a plan shows that the plan was done; the two later examinations show whether the care changed, and two quarters are examined because one sample of a narrow clinical area can be too small or too early to show it. The matter closes on completion so that no review stays open against a clinician about whom no finding was made (§§ 5.7.5 to 5.7.7).

### Panels and APC reviews (Articles VI and VII)

The psychotherapist addendum is written in advance, because procedure written once the person and the matter are known is procedure written about them (§ 6.5).

Federal immunity turns on a professional review body having acted in the reasonable belief that its action furthered quality care, after a reasonable effort to obtain the facts. There has to be one identifiable body that held that belief, and under the Charter it is the Committee. The Panel investigates and proposes, and the Committee decides (§ 6.7).

The sample is taken in every review so that taking it signals nothing. A sample taken only in some reviews, or announced by a second notice, would add worry without adding information. Before concurring in an agreed resolution, the Panel examines the sample because that is how it knows the gap is confined to one clinical area (§ 6.8).

The states differ in how APC practice is structured, and the assessment under Article VII is written to the arrangement that actually applies (§ 7.2).

### Findings (Article VIII)

Most of clinical practice is not reducible to written standards, which is why the absence of one is no defense (§ 8.1.2).

Care is furnished inside a system, and a departure commonly has more than one cause: the reconciliation workflow on admission, what a facility's medication administration record does on transfer, who was covering at the hour the decision was made, an interface that silently drops data. A review that skips the systems question will attribute to a clinician what belongs to the conditions of the work. An intervention aimed at a clinician will not correct a condition that was never theirs to control, and would start the six-month clock toward a failed exit for a departure the clinician could not have prevented (§ 8.1.3).

The finding-without-intervention outcome exists so that written feedback does not become the floor. A minor, isolated departure may deserve a recorded finding and nothing more (§ 8.5.1).

Teaching points are given because every review is an occasion to improve care, on the footing that the clinician shares that purpose and will want them. Labelling them keeps a list of small observations from reading as an indictment, or from being taken for a finding the Committee chose not to make (§ 8.5.2).

A clinician's supervisor cannot monitor an intervention, because oversight by the supervisor would be supervision conducted under the Charter's name. Ordinary supervision continues unchanged, and the Charter says so in order that neither the supervisor nor the Committee steps back assuming the other has it. The exit determination exists because, without it, the path of least resistance at the exit date would be extension (§ 8.6).

### Restrictions (Article IX)

A precautionary restriction can be imposed at once by the Chair and two members, and it cannot be kept in place by them. Unless the Committee ratifies it within five business days, it lapses on its own (§ 9.2).

The ordinary review timelines would run past the forty-five-day stop, so they are switched off for a matter under this Article. The Chair sets a schedule at the outset that lets the Committee decide, and give notice of any proposed action, before the forty-five days run out (§ 9.3).

A restriction may continue beyond forty-five days while a timely noticed Proposed Action is within its hearing-request period, and through the hearing process if a hearing is requested. Exercising these rights is not treated as delay attributable to the Practitioner. While a restriction continues for a hearing, the Committee looks at it again at least every thirty days, and offers the expedited schedule again each time, because a clinician's view of the ordinary schedule tends to change once a restriction is running (§ 9.5).

Tier One restricts nothing, and a precautionary restriction requires imminent danger. The investigatory restriction fills the gap between them: a short, bounded period to investigate the clinical concern, which ends on its own at fourteen days and cannot be repeated on the same facts (§ 9.8).

Management is told on the day a restriction takes effect, and told only its terms, because Management schedules the clinician and a restriction it does not know of could be breached on the next assignment. The Chair gives the notice because the Coordinator's communications about the Committee's matters are kept out of the Coordinator's own management line (§ 9.9).

### Hearings (Article X)

Article X is written to meet the notice and hearing standards of federal law, so that a clinician whose authorization is at risk can answer before clinicians who took no part in the review (§ 10.1).

The ordinary periods run for the clinician's benefit, and many clinicians will not want them. A clinician who believes a proposed action is unwarranted usually wants it resolved, and a process that made them wait four months for a vindication they expect would punish by delay. The expedited schedule lets them choose otherwise (§ 10.2.1).

A CQC member who had no part in the audit comes to a hearing as fresh as any other clinician. PRC members cannot sit, because the PRC takes the final action and the hearing panel is the independent step between its proposal and its decision. The same reason limits the Committee's review of the panel's recommendation (§§ 10.3, 10.7(b)).

Where no proctor without Employment Authority is competent to act, one with it may be named, because a rule that could not be satisfied would leave the restriction unenforceable (§ 10.8(b)).

A restriction is never rolled forward. Continuing one without process would deny the clinician the rights that attach to imposing it (§ 10.8(e)).

### The CQC interface, recusal and records (Articles XI to XIV)

The clinician's submission to the CQC travels with a referral so that the PRC starts independently, and not where the CQC ended (§ 11.3). The PRC concurs in the Audit Referral Policy because the Policy defines a route into the PRC, and the body that receives referrals by that route is the one able to say whether they can be screened and reviewed (§ 11.5).

Prior service as monitor disqualifies a later reviewer, because months of case discussion form views that are not on the record of the next matter. The supervisory trigger looks back five years, and the reasonable-person test covers older relationships, because an unlimited lookback across five states would exhaust the matched reviewers before it protected anyone (§ 12.1).

Each barrier in the Charter depends on keeping its records separate. A single combined record would put material in the hands of people the Charter bars from receiving it (§ 13.1).

The effective-date rule concerns records only. Read as covering care, it would exclude nearly every malpractice claim, settlement or licensure action, which by their nature concern earlier care (§ 13.9).

A review left open without action is not a neutral state for the clinician, which is why long-open matters are counted and reported (§ 14.5).

### Reporting and counsel (Article XV)

The quarterly meeting keeps the executive team informed and lets operations act on systems conditions (§ 15.10). The Chief Executive Officer learns only the fact of an exception event. Once a matter has become a legal or reputational event an aggregate report is not enough, and receiving the merits would put the privilege at risk and draw the Chief Executive Officer into an individual matter (§ 15.11).

Counsel's question is about Eventus's legal position, and the Committee's is about the care. Counsel may need to notify an insurer, preserve records, or answer an attorney or a regulator before a review could finish. A reviewer who knows that a finding will be read as an assessment of liability has reason to soften it, so counsel's answer is early, fixed in form, and silent on the merits (§ 15.11.1).
