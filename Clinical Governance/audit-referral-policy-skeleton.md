# Audit Referral Policy — criteria architecture

**[ENTITY LEGAL NAME]**

*Working skeleton — 2026-09-10. This is the structure and the decisions, not the draft. Nothing here is provision text.*
*The Policy is adopted by the CQC under § 6.2 of its Charter and ratified by the Council under § 4.2(f) of the Council Charter, and reviewed annually.*

---

## What the Policy has to do

Six jobs, each of which some existing provision already assumes is done somewhere.

1. **State the criteria on which an individual finding is referred** to the PRC under CQC §§ 6.1–6.5. (CQC § 6.2)
2. **Carry the escalation on repetition after notice** that CQC § 5.5(c)(3) and § 5.11.3(b) both refer to as already existing. Section 5.11.3(b) calls a follow-up finding "the second occasion contemplated by the criteria under § 6.2," which means the criteria have to contemplate occasions and count them.
3. **Hold the comparison groups.** CQC § 5.6(c) places their construction and adoption inside this Policy, not in the audit register.
4. **Operationalise the volume brake** at CQC § 5.9 — the point at which a criterion that would refer a large fraction of Practitioners routes to standards revision instead.
5. **Be the thing PRC § 4.7 measures against.** That section returns to the CQC any referral resting only on noncompliance which "the CQC's own ratified criteria would not refer." The criteria have to be specific enough that a screener can tell.
6. **Be readable by the Practitioners it applies to.** It sits in the open record under CQC § 8.1.1, expressly because a Practitioner is entitled to know what may cause a referral.

## What it may not contain

- **Selection thresholds.** They are protected under § 8.1.1 and recorded in the audit register. Section 5.6(e) has them *reviewed with* the Policy, not stated in it. The Policy is open; putting a threshold in it publishes it. The two documents move together and stay apart.
- **Anything that requires the Committee to form a view on the standard of care.** CQC § 1.3. Every criterion below is written so that it can be applied by reading the finding, the standards register and the audit record — and by nobody exercising clinical judgment about the case.
- **Any characterisation of what the PRC should do with a referral.** § 6.4, § 6.5.

## What the criteria require of the measures

**No measure and no standard is named in this Policy.** Standards are adopted, revised and retired by the Committee under Article IV on a cadence of their own, and a Policy that named them would have to be re-ratified by the Council every time one changed. This is the reason § 6.2 gives for keeping the criteria out of the Charter, applied one level down. **What the Policy states is what a criterion requires of any measure; which measures meet it is recorded per standard, in the standards register.**

**Three properties decide what a measure can support, and all three are knowable at adoption.**

- **Answerable from the record**, already a drafting requirement at § 4.7(d). Where the substance a standard asks about is habitually recorded by another discipline, the audit is reading a document the standard does not govern, and the question belongs to the standard rather than to any criterion here.
- **Denominator per Practitioner per cycle**, which decides whether a pattern criterion can ever operate on the measure. It varies by orders of magnitude across measures and is a property of the measure, not of the programme's age.
- **Attributable to one Practitioner.** Section 5.5(a)(1)(i) tests attribution chart by chart, but a measure whose care is routinely shared will sustain that ground often enough that what the correction step is reporting on under § 5.5(a)(8)(ii) is the measure.

**What the Policy asks of the standards register: a further entry at adoption.** Alongside the § 4.4 designation, any § 4.7(e) designation and the reset entry under § 10, the Committee records **which criteria under this Policy may reach a finding on the measure** — none of them; Criterion B; or Criterion B and Criterion C — and separately whether the standard carries a single-occasion designation under § 4.

**None of them is not a lesser category.** A standard may be a genuine minimum expectation under § 4.4(a) while its measurement is not yet good enough to carry a consequence, which is a different question from whether the requirement sits above the floor of acceptable practice. Section 4.4(a) is permissive on its face — a departure **may** support a referral — and this Policy is where that permission is granted or withheld.

**Most of the audit programme will consist of measures that support education and systems work and never produce a referral.** That is the expected distribution rather than a shortfall, and § 5.9 states what to do about a measure that looks otherwise.

**The entry is revisable at the § 4.6 review**, on the data § 11 requires the review to have. A measure earns its way up.

## Proposed article structure

### 1. Scope, status, and what a referral is

Restates § 6.4 in the instrument that produces the referral: meeting a criterion establishes that an individual finding was made and that a criterion was met. **Adoption of these criteria does not by itself establish that care meeting one fell below the standard of care.** The PRC decides that on its own record under its Article VIII.

**What this Policy governs, and what it does not.** It governs one route to the PRC — the CQC's own, under §§ 6.1–6.5. Every other route is open and is not conditioned on anything in this document. A Practitioner, an employee, Management, a contracted facility, a patient or family, an external event, or the Practitioner themselves may bring a matter to the PRC under § 4.1 of its Charter, and such a matter is evaluated against the standard of care under § 8.1 of that Charter and **requires no adopted standard of any kind, no criterion here, and no notice period.** Any member of this Committee holding a concern about the standard of care refers under § 6.7(c) as a person, and the question is put aloud at every disposition under § 5.5(b)(1) so that route is ordinary business rather than an act of individual initiative.

**The criteria here therefore bound a narrow channel: the one by which noncompliance with an adopted standard, with no independent concern about the standard of care or about patient safety, can reach professional review.** That is the proposition PRC § 4.7 states and polices. Narrowness in these criteria closes nothing that patient safety depends on, and the calibration question at the annual review under § 11 is whether they refer the right matters — not whether they refer enough of them.

### 2. Preconditions — the gate every criterion passes through

None of the criteria in §§ 4–6 is reached until all of the following are true of the finding. Stated once so that no criterion has to restate them, and so that the Coordinator has a checklist.

- (a) It is an **individual finding** under CQC § 5.1 — a validated observation, not an observation and not a rate.
- (b) The standard was designated a **minimum expectation** under § 4.4(a) by express vote, recorded in the standards register. (§ 4.5)
- (c) The standard was **disseminated** and the dissemination documented, and was **in effect at the time of the care reviewed**. (§ 4.5)
- (d) The **§ 5.7 period had run against the care** — not against the audit.
- (e) The **§ 5.5(a) correction step is complete**: notice issued, the response period expired or a response received and dispositioned, and the grounds sustained recorded.
- (f) The finding was **dispositioned Individual** under § 5.5(b).
- (g) Where the criterion is a rate, **the rate has been recomputed** under § 5.5(a)(7) after removal of observations withdrawn on grounds (i) or (ii).
- (h) The care was furnished **on or after the Charter's effective date**. (§ 8.7)

### 3. What can never count toward any criterion

A closed list, because the alternative is arguing it case by case.

- Observations dispositioned **Systems** or **No action**, and any observation corrected or withdrawn on a ground under § 5.5(a)(1).
- **Thin entries** under § 5.10(c) — never an individual finding, never counted.
- Observations from the **quarterly sample of charts that passed** under § 5.10(b) — dispositioned Systems by the Charter.
- Findings against a standard designated an **improvement target** under § 4.4(b), or against a standard carrying no recorded designation.
- **Rate targets** under § 4.4 — not standards, never attributed.
- **Whether an offer of support under § 5.5(c)(5) was taken up.** Expressly excluded by § 5.5(c)(5)(iii); restated here because this is the document the exclusion names.
- **Selection.** Being selected for audit, being selected again, and the thresholds that selected the Practitioner. (§ 5.6(a))
- **A rate standing alone.** A rate is selection or it is a criterion for referring findings that exist; it is never itself the thing referred.
- **Volume.** (§ 5.8(b))

### 4. Criterion A — a standard designated for single-occasion referral

**One individual finding refers, where the standard was designated at adoption as carrying that consequence.**

The designation is prospective, made at adoption by express vote alongside the § 4.4 and § 4.7(e) designations, recorded in the standards register, and open. It is the same mechanic as § 4.7(e): the Committee decides once, about the standard, in the open, rather than about a Practitioner at the point of referral.

Two available forms, so the designation has a shape rather than being a general severity vote:

- **(i) Unqualified** — any individual finding on this standard refers on a single occasion.
- **(ii) Decision-departure only** — an individual finding refers on a single occasion where § 5.5(a)(9) records the apparent departure as in the **decision** rather than only in its documentation. A documentation-only departure on the same standard goes to Criterion B.

**Why this criterion exists.** Where § 5.5(a)(9) records that the apparent departure is in the decision rather than only in its documentation, the audit has measured something that looks like a question about care, and the Charter's route for it is § 6.7(c) — a member referring as a person. Section 5.5(b)(1) says plainly why that route needs help: the social cost of raising a concern about a colleague is otherwise what determines whether it is ever raised. **A designated standard makes the referral an act of the Committee rather than of a volunteer.** It does not add a route the framework lacks; it changes who has to be the one to use it.

**Why the designation sits with the standard and not with the case.** A criterion that asks how bad this instance was asks the Committee for a clinical judgment about an identified Practitioner's care, which § 1.3 says it does not make and which § 5.5(b)(1) routes to § 6.7 as an act of an individual member. Designating the standard asks a different question — whether a single departure from *this requirement* is the kind of thing peer review should look at — and it is answered before anyone knows whose chart it will be.

**What this criterion deliberately does not catch.** The severe case on an undesignated standard. That case reaches the PRC under § 6.7, or as an adverse event under PRC § 4.1(d), or from the facility under § 4.1(e) — the routes that carry no threshold, no standard requirement and no notice period. The audit-referral process is not the route for it and the Policy should say so in terms, because someone will otherwise try to build case severity into the schedule.

### 5. Criterion B — repetition after notice

**A second individual finding on the same standard, for care furnished after the date of a close-out notice recording a first.**

This is the criterion the Charter already assumes. Its elements:

- (a) A first individual finding on the standard, **closed out under § 5.5(c) with notice actually issued to this Practitioner.** (§ 5.5(c)(3) — the escalation rests on notice having been given, not on the standard having been disseminated to everyone.)
- (b) A second individual finding **on the same standard**. Occasions are counted on one standard and are not aggregated across measures — see § 10.
- (c) **For care furnished after the date of the close-out notice.** Not audited after — furnished after. Same discipline as § 5.7.
- (d) Both findings satisfying § 2 above.
- (e) The count still running under § 10 — that is, the § 5.11 follow-up has not closed the matter, and the backstop has not expired.

A finding produced by the § 5.11 follow-up look satisfies (b) and (c) by construction — § 5.11.2 examines only care furnished in the follow-up window, which opens after the close-out. That is § 5.11.3(b) working as drafted.

### 6. Criterion C — pattern against a comparison group

**Individual findings on a measure at a rate materially above the Practitioner's comparison group, where an adequate group exists.**

The elements, and each of them is doing work:

- (a) A comparison group **adequate under § 7** exists for this Practitioner on this measure. Where it does not, **no pattern criterion applies** — § 5.6(d), which is mandatory, not a factor.
- (b) The Practitioner's rate, **recomputed under § 5.5(a)(7)**, exceeds the group rate by [a stated margin].
- (c) The Practitioner has at least **[n] cases in the denominator** and at least **[m] individual findings in absolute terms.** A rate over four charts is noise. This floor is a protection and not a volume criterion under § 5.8(b) — it can only prevent a referral, never cause one.
- (d) The instrument is validated and currently revalidated under § 5.4 and § 5.5(a)(8)(i). **There is no separate programme-maturity gate.** Whether enough data exists is what the adequacy computation under § 7 measures directly, per measure and per cycle, and a gate expressed in cycles is a proxy for it that will be wrong in both directions — too slow on a measure with a large denominator, too fast on a thin one.
- (e) The volume brake at § 8 has been applied and not tripped.

**What is referred is the findings, not the rate.** The referral under § 6.3 nominates the specific individual findings and carries them; the rate is the reason the Committee referred them. A rate describes a population and never describes a Practitioner's care in a given episode (§ 5.1), and this criterion does not change that — it makes the rate the trigger for referring findings that already exist on their own.

**This criterion is live from the effective date and will fire on very little at first.** It needs no dormancy provision and no Council activation, because § 5.6(d) and the adequacy test at § 7 are self-executing: on a measure where the comparison does not hold the criterion does not apply, and that is computed each cycle rather than assumed.

**The adequacy conditions belong in the open text of this criterion** and not only in the audit register. A Practitioner reading the Policy should be able to see for themselves that it cannot fire on a measure where the comparison cannot be constructed.

### 7. Comparison groups

Placed here because § 5.6(c) puts them here. Constructed by the analytics function, adopted by the Committee within this Policy, construction recorded.

**Two questions are commonly run together and only one of them is expensive.**

**Constructing a group requires no audit history.** Discipline, setting and panel size are roster and census data. Groups are built from the effective date, and **they are built whether or not any criterion can use them yet**, because § 2.6 has the analytics function reporting organization-wide performance and whether interventions moved their measures, and that reporting is what shows where to teach.

**Making the comparison mean something requires denominator, and denominator is a property of the measure.** That is what § 5.6(d) is about, and it is computed rather than assumed.

- Defined by the characteristics that plausibly affect the measure — ordinarily discipline, setting and panel size; where relevant acuity, length of stay or facility.
- **Adequacy, computed per measure and per cycle:** not fewer than **[ten]** Practitioners in the group; each with not fewer than **[n]** cases in the denominator; a group rate that is not degenerate, since a group at zero or at one hundred percent gives nothing to be above; and the group not constructed after the Practitioner's rate is known.
- **Where adequacy fails, Criterion C does not apply to that measure for that Practitioner**, and the failure is recorded as a fact about the measure and reported with the cycle.

**On some measures, and in some disciplines, Criterion C will never operate.** Psychiatry across five states is the clear case, and a thin-denominator measure in any discipline is another. **The asymmetry runs in the protective direction** — less exposure to pattern referral, not more — and Criterion B and every route outside this Policy are unaffected. It is stated here so that it is a known property of the design rather than something found in the second year.

**An external benchmark is not a comparison group.** Where the internal group is too small the available substitute is a payer, registry or rating-program rate, and § 5.8(c) forecloses it: such a threshold is an organizational performance target maintained by the analytics function under § 2.6 and is never attributed to an individual. Where no adequate internal group exists, the answer is that the criterion does not apply.

### 8. The volume brake

**Before any referral is made on a measure in a cycle, the Committee computes how many Practitioners the criteria would refer on that measure in that cycle.** The computation is made **after** the § 5.5(a) corrections and the § 5.5(a)(7) recomputation, on the findings as they actually stand.

**The brake trips where both thresholds are exceeded: more than [five] percent of the Practitioners to whom the measure applies, and more than [three] Practitioners.** Where it trips, **no referral is made on that measure in that cycle**, and the matter routes under § 5.9 to standards revision, to the audit question, or to the education agenda.

**Both thresholds are needed because a bare fraction misbehaves at small numbers.** In a group of eight, two referrals is twenty-five percent, and a fraction alone would call that a wave. The count floor is what stops the brake from firing on ordinary volume in a small discipline.

**Five percent rather than a larger figure**, because Criterion B requires a second occasion after notice and Criterion C requires an adequate comparison — realistic referral volume on a measure in a cycle is low single digits, and eight referrals on one measure in one quarter is already evidence about the measure.

**(a) A finding in a braked cycle is extinguished for referral purposes on that measure.** It is not held over to a quieter cycle. Referral would otherwise depend on when a Practitioner's chart happened to be swept, which is arbitrary in a way nothing else in this Policy is.

**(b) It does not count as an occasion toward Criterion B.** Section 5.9's premise is that a measure producing a wave is the thing that is wrong, and a count preserved out of a cycle the Committee has just characterised as a systems condition would carry that condition forward against individuals.

**(c) The close-out notice issues either way**, under § 5.5(c), and states the disposition as it always does.

**(d) Applies to Criteria B and C.** It does not suppress Criterion A, and § 5.9 states expressly that it does not preclude referral under § 6.7.

**(e) Each activation is recorded and reported to the Council** in the annual report under § 7.4, with what was done instead. A brake that fires silently is indistinguishable from a criterion nobody meets.

### 9. Making the referral — obligation, exceptions, contents

**(a) Referral is mandatory once a criterion is met.** Where a finding satisfies § 2 and meets a criterion under §§ 4–6, the Committee refers. **The determination is not an occasion to reconsider the disposition.**

**The judgment this programme asks of the Committee is made at the § 5.5(b) disposition** — Systems, Individual, or No action, on the record, with the safety question put aloud under § 5.5(b)(1) — and it is made once. A facility that lost its cardiology group inside a follow-up window is a staffing condition and is dispositioned Systems there; it never becomes an individual finding and no criterion reaches it. A second discretion after that point operates on the same facts with no new information, and it leaves no record anyone can review.

**What a referral costs is also what makes the obligation bearable.** Section 6.4 has a referral establish two things: that an individual finding was made, and that a criterion was met. The PRC screens every referral and § 4.6 of its Charter forbids any adverse finding at screening. Referring is close to costless; declining to refer a validated finding on a ratified criterion is the consequential act.

**(b) The schedule is a floor and a ceiling.** The Committee refers under §§ 6.1–6.5 where a criterion is met, and **does not refer under §§ 6.1–6.5 where none is.** A member who holds a concern the criteria do not reach refers under § 6.7(c) as a person, which § 5.5(b)(1) is drafted to make an ordinary item of business rather than an act of individual initiative.

**(c) The express exceptions, and they are the only ones.** The volume brake under § 8; and § 5.6(d) where no adequate comparison group exists, which bars Criterion C on that measure for that Practitioner. Both are computations recorded with the cycle.

A finding withdrawn or corrected under § 5.5(a), or resting on a measure that failed revalidation under § 5.5(a)(8)(i), is not an exception to the obligation — the precondition at § 2 fails and there is nothing to refer.

**(d) Duplication is handled at the PRC, not here.** Where the same care is already before the PRC on another route under § 4.1 of its Charter, the CQC refers in the ordinary course and the PRC dispositions the duplication at screening. **The Committee does not inquire whether a matter is open at the PRC**, and the obligation does not turn on the answer. Section 6.5 holds the wall in one direction, and a rule requiring the Committee to check would open a channel through it in the other.

**(e) Year-one discretion, which lapses.** For **[twelve] months** from the effective date, or the first **[three]** audit cycles on a measure, the Committee may decline to refer where a criterion is met. Each exercise is recorded with its reasons and reported to the Council.

**At the first annual review under § 6.2, each exercise is either written into this Policy as an express exception under (c) or discarded, and the discretion then lapses.** Section 6.2 keeps these criteria out of the Charter so that they can be calibrated against actual findings; this provision makes the first year's exercise of judgment the input to that calibration rather than a standing latitude. It is dated on its face and does not renew.

**(f) The determination is a Committee act**, taken in executive session under § 9.5, recorded in the minutes under § 9.4.
- The referral **names the criterion met** — § 6.4 makes that one of the two things a referral establishes.
- **Sequence and diary:** the close-out notice under § 5.5(c) issues first and states the fact of referral under § 5.5(c)(4); the Coordinator diaries the referral and the § 5.11 follow-up where one is owed.
- **Contents are § 6.3 and nothing more.** Not the Committee's deliberations, and not any member's clinical impression recorded under § 5.5(b)(1) — that impression travels under § 6.7 as an act of the person who holds it, or it does not travel.

### 10. Counting rules

The mechanical questions the criteria raise, answered once.

- **Occasion.** One individual finding is one occasion, whatever number of charts it rests on within a single audit cycle on a single measure. Otherwise a sweep that pulls three of a Practitioner's charts produces a second and third occasion on the same day, and the notice logic in Criterion B collapses.
- **The count ends when the follow-up look closes the matter.** Where the § 5.11 follow-up produces no individual finding, or where there was no eligible chart to examine, the matter closes under § 5.11.3(a) or § 5.11.5 and **the first finding no longer counts toward Criterion B.**

  **Extinguishment rather than duration.** Section 5.11.3(a) returns the Practitioner to the general population of the audit programme. A count that survived that closure would let a finding three years old pair with a new one and reopen a matter the Charter closed, which is the standing count § 5.11.3(c) denies this Committee the authority to impose. **Every referral under Criterion B therefore rests on a designed second look rather than on accumulated history.**

  **A later finding on the same measure runs the sequence again as a new matter** — close-out under § 5.5(c), follow-up under § 5.11.2 — which is what § 5.11.3(a) provides. What does not happen is a second look within one matter.

- **Backstop window of [twelve] months**, for the residual case where a follow-up has not yet come due or has not been conducted. It exists so that a count is never open indefinitely for administrative reasons.

- **A finding later corrected or withdrawn** in a subsequent cycle is removed from every count, and any referral resting on it is withdrawn to the PRC with the reason. (Consequence of § 5.5(a)(7); worth stating.)
- **Standard revised.** **The Committee records at adoption of the revision whether the revision resets the count**, entered in the standards register with the effective date and the § 5.7 period. **Absent an entry, the revision resets.**

  **The determination is made at adoption rather than at referral**, for the reason § 4.4 gives about the designation it governs: a test applied at the point of consequence is argued at the point of consequence, by a Practitioner with every reason to say the revision changed the requirement and a Committee with every reason to say it did not. Deciding it in the register, prospectively and in the open, takes the question away from the case.

  **The default runs toward resetting** because a revision is an adoption under § 4.3, carrying dissemination under § 4.5 and a fresh § 5.7 period against the care. Preserving a count is then a deliberate act costing one line in the register.

- **A reset requires a fresh close-out before the escalation runs again.** Criterion B(a) rests on this Practitioner having been told what the audit found in these charts, which § 5.5(c)(3) distinguishes from the standard having been disseminated to everyone. A close-out issued on the pre-revision standard does not discharge it for the revised one.

- **Redesignation and retirement.** Redesignation to § 4.4(b), and retirement, end the count — an improvement target may never support a referral. Redesignation from § 4.4(b) to § 4.4(a) begins a count from that date, and nothing from the improvement-target period carries into it.

- **Where a revision removes or narrows the requirement the finding rested on, the finding does not count toward any criterion**, whether or not the revision resets. This is a correction and not a clock, and it is stated separately so that the reset rule is not asked to do it.
- **A Practitioner's findings do not aggregate across different standards.** Criterion B counts occasions on one standard, and no criterion in this Policy counts findings across measures.

  **Why the count is confined to one standard.** A cross-standard tally adds findings from measures carrying different § 5.4 validations, different grounds-sustained rates under § 5.5(a)(8) and different thin-entry rates, which are not commensurable quantities. It also rises with the number of denominators a Practitioner appears in, so a large and complex panel accumulates findings across measures while performing at group on every one of them — and § 5.8(b) forecloses a criterion that operates that way. There is no comparison group for such a count, which is the condition § 5.6(d) treats as disqualifying.

  **Where the breadth signal goes instead.** Findings across several measures are a sound reason to examine more charts, and § 5.6(a) makes selection free of every requirement a criterion carries — no comparison group, no risk adjustment, no notice period, and no consequence. Section 5.11.2 states it directly: a look directed by a recorded finding is selection under § 5.6(a) and nothing more. **A breadth rule is therefore a selection rule and belongs in the audit register, which is protected, rather than in this Policy, which is open.** Whatever such a look produces runs through § 5.5(a) correction and Criteria A and B on the ordinary terms.

  **The constraint on any such rule**, noted here because this Policy is where the reasoning is recorded: it is applied to the whole population each cycle and selects whoever meets it. **An examination directed at a named Practitioner by decision, recurring, is the monitoring status § 5.11.3(c) denies this Committee the authority to impose.** The difference between a rule and a status is that nobody decides to look at a particular person again.

### 11. Annual review and calibration

Section 6.2 keeps the criteria out of the Charter expressly so they can be calibrated against actual findings without amendment. The review should therefore be told what to look at:

referrals made by criterion; PRC dispositions in the de-identified aggregate under § 7.2; grounds sustained by measure under § 5.5(a)(8); thin-entry rates under § 5.10(c); volume-brake activations; measures on which no adequate comparison group could be constructed; any exercise of the discretion under § 9(e), with what it is converted into; and the follow-up outcomes under § 5.11 reported to the Council under § 7.4.

**And the distribution of individual findings per Practitioner across measures**, which no criterion in this Policy acts on. It is reported because the decision to confine the count to a single standard should be revisited against what the programme actually sees, and the review cannot revisit it without the number in front of it.

### 12. Record and status

Open under CQC § 8.1.1(a); a copy in the Council's governance record under Council § 10.2(a); ratification recorded there. Amendment by the Committee, ratified by the Council in the same way.

# Settled

**The Criterion B count is extinguished by the § 5.11 closure rather than expiring on a calendar** — § 10 — with a [twelve]-month backstop for the residual case. **The volume brake trips on both a [five] percent fraction and a floor of [three] Practitioners**, computed after correction; braked findings are extinguished for referral on that measure and do not count as occasions — § 8. Decided 2026-09-10.

**CQC § 5.11.3(a) fixed in the draft**: the closure ends the look attached to that finding, not the Committee's ability to run the sequence again on a later one. The broad reading would have disabled Criterion B for anyone who had once passed a follow-up. Decided 2026-09-10.

**The § 2.5 exclusion now follows the act, fixed in the CQC draft rather than deferred.** Sections 2.5 and 9.3 reach any determination concerning an identified Practitioner, naming the § 5.5 disposition and Article VI determinations as instances. The Policy restates the rule rather than creating it. The Chair of Clinical Excellence holds no Employment Authority, so § 5.5(b)(1) needs no alternate. Decided 2026-09-10.

**No measure or standard is named in this Policy.** Standards change on their own cadence and the Policy is ratified by the Council; naming a measure would put a re-ratification in the way of every revision. The Policy states what a criterion requires of a measure, and the standards register records which measures meet it. The § 5.10(a) divergence work is therefore a **standard**, drafted in the standards workstream — see `audit-standards-queue.md`. Decided 2026-09-10.

**Criterion C is live from the effective date with no maturity gate and no dormancy switch** — § 6(d), § 7. Adequacy is computed per measure per cycle, § 5.6(d) is self-executing, and on thin-denominator measures and in small disciplines the criterion will never operate — stated rather than discovered. External benchmarks are not comparison groups. Decided 2026-09-10.

**What a measure can support is recorded in the standards register at adoption** — education and systems only, Criterion B, or Criterion B and C. Decided 2026-09-10.

**Referral is mandatory once a criterion is met** — § 9(a) — with only the two express exceptions at § 9(c), and a year-one discretion at § 9(e) that converts to written exceptions at the first annual review and then lapses. **The schedule is a floor and a ceiling on the Committee's own route** — § 9(b). Decided 2026-09-10.

**A revision resets the count unless the Committee records otherwise at adoption**, with the determination entered in the standards register; redesignation to § 4.4(b) and retirement end the count; and a revision that removes or narrows the requirement voids the finding outright. A reset requires a fresh close-out before Criterion B runs again. Decided 2026-09-10.

**Repetition is counted on a single standard and findings do not aggregate across measures** — § 10. The breadth signal is handled as selection in the audit register rather than as a criterion here, and the findings-per-Practitioner distribution is reported to the annual review so the confinement can be revisited with data. Decided 2026-09-10.

**The Policy governs one route among several** and its narrowness closes nothing that patient safety depends on — § 1. The calibration question at the annual review is whether the criteria refer the right matters, not whether they refer enough of them. Decided 2026-09-10.

# Ready to draft

Every architectural decision is settled. What remains is calibration, and the Charter's own Open items give the reason it cannot be done here: the numbers cannot be set before there is a baseline.

**Set by the Committee when it first adopts the Policy, against the first cycles' actual distributions, and reviewed annually under § 11:** the margin above the group rate in Criterion C(b); the denominator floor [n] and findings floor [m] in C(c); the per-Practitioner denominator in the § 7 adequacy test. These are the three that cannot be chosen honestly in advance, and each of them can only narrow the criterion.

**Bracketed values proposed:** non-degenerate group-rate floor in § 7; margin above group rate in Criterion C(b); denominator floor [n] and findings floor [m] in C(c); [ten] Practitioners in the comparison group in § 7; [twenty percent] volume brake in § 8; [twenty-four]-month lookback in § 10; [twelve] months and [three] cycles for the sunrise in § 9(e);

**For counsel.** Questions for counsel are kept outside this document, in `counsel-questions.md`.

**Exposure worth naming now.** The risk in this document runs toward breadth, not narrowness. Every criterion here operates on a Practitioner against whom nobody has raised a concern about care — that is what distinguishes this channel from every other route to the PRC. A criterion that fires easily converts ordinary documentation nonconformity into professional review, which is the outcome § 5.9, § 4.4(b), § 5.7 and the close-out and follow-up machinery at § 5.5(c) and § 5.11 are all built to avoid. **Where a criterion and the systems reading of the same facts are both available, § 5.9 is the Charter's instruction about which one is ordinarily right.** The floors in Criterion C, the maturity gate, the lookback window and the volume brake are the drafting expression of that, and each of them should be set on the assumption that erring toward not referring costs the programme very little, because every matter that genuinely concerns the standard of care has a route that does not pass through here.
