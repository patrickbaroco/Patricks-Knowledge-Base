# AP use: Lindbert Top 201 report, reviewed 2026-09-25

The raw PDF stays on the email. It lists 201 buildings (10,941 census, 5,577 on AP, 51.0%), ranked on Total AP − (0.167 × census), split into four priority groups, with a separate column for residents 65+ with dementia on AP (1,710 across the list). Arithmetic checks out: every row's score matches its inputs and the totals match the summary page.

## Findings

1. **The ranking uses all AP use, not the dementia 65+ cohort.** The 2026-09-17 request was to rank on the formula starting with AP limited to dementia over 65. The cohort appears only as a side column.
2. **The top of the list is the population the filter was meant to exclude.** Group 1 (38 buildings) holds 1,734 residents on AP at a 72% AP rate, of whom 242 (14%) are in the dementia cohort. It is dominated by Kentucky personal care homes and North Carolina adult care and support-services homes, several with a cohort count of 0. These are the MH-only and shared-PCP buildings of Rucci's objection.
3. **The selection itself is on the wrong variable.** The report's last page states that 3,684 of the 5,394 organization-wide cohort (68%) sit in buildings outside these 201. Re-sorting this list will not find them; the ranking has to be rerun across all buildings.
4. **A zero in the cohort column is ambiguous.** The report says buildings not matched in the cohort dataset are shown as 0. Twenty buildings (624 residents on AP) show 0; true zero and failed match cannot be told apart.
5. **Re-sorted on the cohort within these 201**, using cohort − (0.167 × census), the leaders are Willow Ridge, Durham Ridge, Sanford Manor, Gardens of Hendersonville, Springs of Ballentine, Gabriel Manor, Memory Care of the Triad, The Blair, Brookdale South Park and Lantern at Morning Pointe of Danville. Only Durham Ridge is in the current top ten. 80 of the 201 score above zero. Provisional only, given finding 3.
6. **The narrative pages overreach.** The impact analysis assumes every resident in the cohort is deprescribed and says this closes "nearly half the gap without touching a single non-dementia resident's care plan." The 16.7% goal is applied to whole census, including residents with schizophrenia and other indications the CMS long-stay measure excludes. The figures should not be quoted as projections.
7. Four South Carolina buildings appear. They belong: the 9/10 Top 20% report lists 18 SC buildings in the portfolio of 1,202.

## Ask of Lindbert

Rerun across all buildings with the numerator limited to residents 65+ with dementia on AP; show the cohort's own count and census alongside; distinguish unmatched buildings from true zeros.

## What can be reconstructed without a rerun

Every building off the list scores below about 10.5 on total AP, and its dementia 65+ count cannot exceed its total AP, so no building outside the 201 can score 10.5 or more on the cohort. The top 19 of the cohort ranking are therefore complete as listed in finding 5 (through Haven at Carolina Place, 10.8). Below that, the rerun is required. Neither 9/10 report (organization-wide AP-AA report, section 5; Top 20% report) carries building-level dementia counts, only system totals.

One exception: the 20 buildings showing a cohort of 0 could, if unmatched rather than truly zero, belong in the top group. Large ALFs showing zero (North Roanoke ALF, census 150; Town Creek ALF; Meadow Hills ALF) are the likeliest candidates.
