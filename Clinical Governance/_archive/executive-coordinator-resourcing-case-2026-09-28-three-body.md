# Resourcing the Clinical Excellence Program Support Function

*Companion to `peer-review-project-brief.md`. Purpose: move the coordinator role from a personnel question into a program-resourcing requirement.*

---

## The ask

Fill the **Executive Coordinator, Clinical Excellence** role (spec: `Executive_Coordinator_Clinical_Excellence_role_spec.docx`) and fund the 30% Clinical Excellence allocation as part of the clinical governance build.

The role is already funded today in a different cost center. The decision is where the position sits and what it is chartered to do, not whether to add headcount.

---

## The program work that has no owner

The clinical governance build creates a standing administrative function that does not currently exist anywhere in the organization. It is separable from the analytic and drafting work, which belongs to the Chair.

**What the Chair does:** the PSES activity inventory and its analysis, charter drafting, the privilege taxonomy, orientation content, the reportability framework, and everything requiring clinical or legal judgment.

**What the coordinator does:**

| Function | Detail |
|---|---|
| Council administration | Agendas, pre-reads, packet assembly and distribution, minutes, action-item tracking to closure for the Clinical Quality Council and Peer Review Council |
| Case logistics | Scheduling, panel logistics, recusal screening against the charter's trigger list, notice distribution |
| Record custody | The peer review file, access log, segregation from HR and credentialing systems, confidentiality legends, legal hold when one attaches |
| PSES boundary execution | Applying the policy's rule document by document, at the moment each document is created |
| Statutory calendar | Hearing request windows, the day-14 process trigger, the day-30 reportability trigger, exit-criteria review dates, the biennial attestation date |
| PRC–credentialing interface | Transmission of final actions on a defined form, and the transmission log |
| Research and content support | Literature retrieval and source library for pathway and education work |

**Sizing (estimate).** At current volume: PRC meeting cycle 6–7 hours a month, case logistics 2–4, custody and interface log 2, calendar tracking under 1. Adding CQC administration brings council work to roughly 16–19 hours a month — about 10–12% of a full FTE. The remainder of the allocation covers research, content, and liaison support.

The build period adds packet volume and version control during charter review cycles. It is an increment, not a step change, because the drafting itself is Chair work.

## Why this is a program requirement rather than administrative support

**Records discipline is the privilege architecture.** The state analysis in the brief makes this the whole ballgame:

- **Ohio** — courts require document-by-document proof of scope; generalities lose the privilege. Rewards meticulous segregation and precise minutes.
- **PSES design** — a committee that sometimes operates inside and sometimes outside produces a record no court will honor. The boundary is drawn correctly or not at the moment each document is created.
- **Kentucky, post-*Kitchen*** — protection turns on whether work is retrospective analytic product or contemporaneous documentation created for an independent obligation. A records-handling distinction.
- **Recusal** — recused members leave the room, and the minutes record that they left.
- **Indiana** — a provider under investigation may see accumulated records pertaining to their own practice. Assume the subject reads the file.

Each is a coordinator function with a legal consequence attached. A charter that satisfies the union of five states' requirements and is then administered by whoever has time that week does not survive its first discovery request. The program does not fail on legal theory. It fails on minutes.

**The privileges construct was already made contingent on this.** The brief's resourcing decision: *"pursue it, contingent on resourcing and a board resolution. Do not win the principle and then implement alone."* This names the resource.

## Where the adjacent functions sit

Two functions are frequently bundled into this role and should not be. Naming their placement here prevents the question from reopening later.

**Credentialing stays in the credentialing department.** The department is established and owns the function. This role is the administrative interface between the PRC and credentialing, nothing more. The interface is defined in three rules:

1. **Outbound (PRC → credentialing): final actions only, on a defined form.** Never deliberations, drafts, minutes, panel composition, or reasoning. The coordinator transmits what the chartered body has marked for transmission and does not determine what qualifies.
2. **Inbound (credentialing → PRC): open.** License actions, malpractice history, NPDB query results, payer sanctions, and coverage gaps are independently obtained and discoverable regardless. Nothing is waived by bringing them in.
3. **A written transmission log.** Every item that crosses, dated, with what it was, who authorized it, and who received it. This is what satisfies Ohio's document-by-document standard and what demonstrates the boundary held if a credentialing file is ever produced.

*Open item for the credentialing conversation:* ownership is settled, capacity is not. A privilege taxonomy and a peer-review-informed reappointment cycle is new work on a different rhythm from payer-driven recredentialing. That is their assessment to make.

**NPDB operations split three ways and none of them land here.**

- **The reportability determination** stays with the CGC, made with counsel, recorded as a required field in the final action document. The obligation attaches to the final action, so it comes off the deciding body. It does not go to the CMO — that would put a decision flowing directly from the peer review record on the management side of the firewall, in the program's highest-consequence output.
- **The authorized agent and signature** is an entity function and belongs with compliance or general counsel. The agent attests on the entity's behalf, including the biennial attestation, which is a compliance instrument. The CMO role is interim, and an interim officeholder as the entity's NPDB agent is a continuity risk on a function with statutory deadlines.
- **Querying** belongs with credentialing, where the queries actually occur and the workflow already exists.

Only the calendar — the 15-day submission window, the subject's dispute period, the attestation date — is coordinator work.

## Why Lindsey

She already runs the operational spine of the rounding week: facility rounding lists, acuity triage, the notes-to-do queue, follow-up tracking across ANR, Summit, Bridgewater, and Kendal. The 70% of the role is a formalization of work she is doing now, which means the 30% is the only genuinely new scope and the ramp is short.

The role spec's background section was written permissively and deliberately: *"no specific degree is required where capability and track record demonstrate readiness for the role."* Track record is the argument available, and it is a strong one.

**Pair it with a development commitment.** The Certified Provider Credentialing Specialist credential through NAMSS is the recognized qualification for this function. Funding it establishes the role as a professional function rather than administrative support, and gives a defensible answer to a later question about what qualified the person holding the record.

## Allocation

**Hold 70/30**, consistent with the spec as drafted and delivered.

The spec justifies the asymmetry — heavier on the clinical side than the Chair's own 60/40, because coordinator preparation runs ahead of the Chair's rounding and clinical wrap-up continues after the Chair has moved into Clinical Excellence work. That rationale still holds.

**State the flex explicitly:** the split may move toward 60/40 once the role has run long enough to assess actual demand, at which point the spec's rationale paragraph is rewritten to match. Committing to reassess rather than to a number avoids proposing one figure and quietly running another, which is what undermines the next resourcing ask.

At steady state the coordinator's allocation tracks the Chair's, because the support load is derivative of the Chair's own split. That coupling is the durable design principle and it is what makes the role stable across whoever holds the Chair position.

**One exception to the flex:** records custody and statutory deadline tracking are driven by the program's calendar, not the Chair's attention. Notice deadlines do not pause when the Chair is on pathway or ACO work. Small in hours, non-negotiable in timing.

## Three things to resolve before proposing

**1. Immunity coverage.** HCQIA damages immunity runs to the professional review body, its members, **staff, and anyone assisting** — favorable and worth stating. State privilege coverage of non-clinician staff has not been analyzed in any of the current work. Added to the counsel question list; it sits alongside the existing open question on discipline coverage, which already flags that the statutes were written around physicians.

**2. The dual-role conflict needs a written answer.** She would support rounding at facilities where clinicians subject to PRC review practice, while holding PRC materials about those clinicians. This is more than a confidentiality question: underlying facts are never protected in any of the five states, so she is deposable on what she knows — and she would have to distinguish what she knows from rounding from what she knows from the file, under oath.

The answer is structural. Screen her out of case handling for clinicians at the facilities she supports operationally; the schedule makes that a short, defined list. Write it as a staff-facing recusal provision in the PRC charter, which also closes a real gap — the charter's recusal provisions are written for members, not staff.

**3. Two trackers, not one.** The spec currently reads "track outstanding action items across both councils to closure." A single tracker spanning CQC and PRC is exactly the artifact that defeats the PSES boundary: one discoverable document showing that patient-safety-track and peer-review-track work were administered as one workflow. Separate trackers, separate systems, separate access lists, and the same discipline for minutes, the source library, and calendar invites. Fix this in the spec before it becomes practice.

## Sequence and framing

**Get the follow-up date first.** The thread has held since 8/18 on the absence of a date, not the absence of an argument. That request does not depend on this document.

**Bundle the counsel conversation.** The privilege answer, the engagement direction for the current-state assessment, and the structural design questions gating charter drafting are one conversation, not three.

**Then the resourcing conversation**, with Ben and Cary per the original thread.

**One caution on framing.** Do not let this read as a program justification retrofitted onto a staffing decision already made. The honest version is that the program genuinely needs the function and she is genuinely the right person for it. Both are true. The order they are presented in is what makes that credible — the work first, the candidate second.

## Note on the role spec

It never names her, and states that the position is *"designed to remain stable across whoever holds the Chair role, not specific to any one individual."*

Keep that. It is the right way to charter a role, it survives the current Chair's own turnover, and it is the same separation of *role* from *person* the governance program applies everywhere else. The proposal to fill it is a separate document and a separate conversation.
