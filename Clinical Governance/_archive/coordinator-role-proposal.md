## The ask

Fill the **Executive Coordinator, Clinical Excellence** role (spec: `Executive_Coordinator_Clinical_Excellence_role_spec.docx`) with Lindsey Marks, and fund it as part of the clinical governance build rather than as administrative headcount.

## Why this belongs in the peer review project

The brief already made the resourcing decision and named the failure mode. §3, privileges construct: *"pursue it, contingent on resourcing and a board resolution. Do not win the principle and then implement alone."* §3, implementation sequence: *"Credentialing operations needs an owner who is not Patrick."*

The role spec's 30% allocation maps onto that gap almost line for line:

| Role spec duty | Program requirement it satisfies |
|---|---|
| Agendas, pre-reads, minutes, action-item tracking for CQC and PRC | Two co-equal committees, both chartering to the CGC (§3) |
| Peer review case-selection logistics under strict confidentiality | PRC case identification (§5, open questions) |
| Tracking outstanding action items across both councils to closure | CGC authority to accept/reject/modify/**remand** — remand is meaningless without tracking |
| Literature review and source library for pathway work | Drafting queue items 9–11 (§6) |
| Liaison with Regional CMOs and cross-functional partners | CGC composition spans three chairs, four regional CMOs, the RDCOEs |

## The argument that actually carries weight

**Records discipline is not administrative support here — it is the privilege architecture.**

The brief's own state analysis makes this the whole ballgame:

- **Ohio** — *"courts require document-by-document proof of scope; generalities lose the privilege. Rewards meticulous segregation and precise minutes."*
- **PSES design** — *"a committee that sometimes operates inside and sometimes outside produces a record no court will honor."*
- **Recusal** — *"Recused members leave the room; minutes record that they left."*
- **Indiana** — *"assume the subject reads the file."*
- **Kentucky, post-*Kitchen*** — protection turns on whether the work is retrospective analytic work product versus contemporaneous documentation created for an independent obligation. That is a records-handling distinction, drawn correctly or not at the moment each document is created.

Every one of those is a coordinator function with a legal consequence attached. A charter that satisfies the union of five states' requirements and is then administered by whoever has time that week does not survive its first discovery request. The program does not fail on legal theory; it fails on minutes.

**Second argument, for the resourcing conversation:** the PSES activity inventory alone is 20–40 hours plus counsel review over roughly two months (§4), and it precedes the policy, which precedes almost everything else in the drafting queue. That work has no owner today.

## Why Lindsey specifically

She already runs the operational spine of the rounding week — the facility rounding lists, acuity triage, the notes-to-do queue, follow-up tracking across ANR, Summit, Bridgewater, and Kendal. The 70% of this role is a formalization of work she is doing now, which means the 30% is the only genuinely new scope and the ramp is short.

The role spec's background section was written permissively and deliberately: *"no specific degree is required where capability and track record demonstrate readiness for the role."* Track record is the argument available here, and it is a strong one.

## What to resolve before proposing

Three things, flagged rather than buried — each is a question the room will ask.

**1. Does immunity extend to her?** Yes, and this is favorable. HCQIA damages immunity runs to the professional review body, *its members, staff, and anyone assisting* (§4). The separate provision immunizing anyone who provides information to a review body is thinner comfort but also applies. State privilege coverage of non-clinician staff is not analyzed anywhere in the current work — **add to the counsel question list for Jeff Wells.** It sits naturally alongside open question 8, which already flags that discipline coverage was never analyzed beyond physicians.

**2. The dual-role conflict is real and needs a written answer.** She would simultaneously support your rounding at facilities where clinicians subject to PRC review practice, and hold PRC materials about those clinicians. That is not disqualifying, but the charter's recusal provisions are written for committee members, not staff. Either write a staff-facing confidentiality and screening provision into the PRC charter, or accept it explicitly with counsel's sign-off. Do not leave it undefined — undefined is how *drift* happens, which the brief already identifies as a trap in a different context.

**3. The 70/30 split will not hold during the build.** Twelve to eighteen months of charter drafting, PSES inventory, credentialing policy, and a reappointment cycle standing up is not 30% of one person. Either the split is temporary and says so, or the program build gets its own time allocation. Proposing 70/30 and then quietly running 50/50 undermines the next resourcing ask.

## Framing for the conversation

The migration has been a personnel question since 7/24 and has produced one non-answer in five weeks. As a personnel question it competes with every other headcount request and has no natural advocate.

As program resourcing it has a different channel and a willing partner. Jeff Wells is *"eager to expand the process and eager to be a partner in it"* — and the resourcing argument is one he can make in compliance terms rather than staffing terms. The privilege construct was already made contingent on resourcing; this names the resource.

**Sequence:** get Jeff's written privilege answer first (already the gating action, `prj-peer`, due 8/24). A charter obligation on the record makes the coordinator role a compliance requirement rather than a preference. Then take it to Ben — who has the open question — with Cary, per the original 7/24 thread.

One caution on sequence: do not let this read as retrofitting a program justification onto a staffing decision already made. The honest version is that the program genuinely needs the function and she is genuinely the right person for it — both are true, and the order they are presented in matters.

## Note on the role spec itself

It never names her. It states the position is *"designed to remain stable across whoever holds the Chair role, not specific to any one individual."*

Keep that. It is the right way to charter a role and it survives your own turnover. The proposal to fill it with Lindsey is a separate document and a separate conversation — which is the same separation of *role* from *person* the program applies everywhere else.
