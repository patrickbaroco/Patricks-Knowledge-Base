# CQC Article V — Redraft of the correction step, the close-out, support, follow-up, and the practitioner notice

*Draft v0.2 — 2026-08-30. Not adopted. Replaces § 5.5(a) of cqc-charter-draft.md and adds a new § 5.5(c). v0.2 rewrites every reference to the former authorship rule against the new § 4.7(e), under which the treating Practitioner's note must account for each element and an attribution to another clinician satisfies it.*

**What changed and why.** Three problems in the current § 5.5(a).

1. **The grounds are listed in the wrong order and one is missing.** The section offers "factual error, attribution error, or information in the record material to whether the applicable standard was met." The third limb is the one a standard designated under § 4.7(e) mostly closes, and it is listed as though it were the main event. The ground that will actually fire most often — that the resident is not within the population the standard applies to — is not listed at all.
2. **The section reads as a concession to the Practitioner.** It is better defended, and more accurate, as quality control on the audit. That framing survives the board member who asks why the process has an off-ramp in it, and it is the honest reason the section exists.
3. **Nothing is done with what the correction step learns.** Corrections sustained on attribution, population, or retrieval are evidence about the instrument, and they are also evidence that the rate which selected the Practitioner was wrong. Neither consequence is stated anywhere.

Plus the gap identified separately: an Individual finding that meets no referral criterion produces no communication of any kind. New § 5.5(c) closes it.

---

## § 5.5 Validating an observation, and disposition

**5.5 Validating an observation, and disposition.** Section 5.4 validates the instrument. This section validates the observation, which is a different question: an instrument that performs well across a population can still be wrong about a particular chart, and observations feed referrals.

*(Opening revised 2026-08-30 and applied to cqc-charter-draft.md. The ninety-five-percent illustration it replaces was moved to that draft's Open items notes.)*

### (a) Opportunity to correct

**No observation may be dispositioned as Individual until the Practitioner has been given the opportunity to correct it.** The Practitioner has [ten] business days from the date of the notice under (a)(5). The Committee may correct or withdraw the observation accordingly, and records what it did and on which ground.

**This subsection is quality control on the audit.** Section 5.4 validates the instrument and nothing else validates the observation. The errors this subsection catches are disproportionately the errors that produced the rate which selected the Practitioner in the first place, and a referral resting on a chart the audit got wrong is worth less than no referral at all. That the subsection is also owed to the Practitioner is true, and is not the reason it is here.

This is a record-correction step, not a proceeding. **It is not professional review. It determines nothing about competence, imposes nothing on anyone, and does not limit the PRC's independent review of any matter later referred.** It asks one question — did the audit accurately identify a departure from the standard — and it does not ask whether the care was appropriate, which is the PRC's question under Article VIII of its Charter. Nothing in this subsection entitles a Practitioner to argue the clinical merits before this Committee, and the Committee does not hear them.

**(a)(1) Grounds.** An observation is corrected or withdrawn on any of the following. They are stated in the order in which they arise in practice, and the notice under (a)(5) states them in the same order, so that the Practitioner spends the response period where it can do something.

- **(i) Attribution.** The care was not furnished by the Practitioner; the resident was not on the Practitioner's panel at the material time; the Practitioner was covering and the decision belonged to the treating clinician; or the condition to which the standard applies was managed by another clinician under an arrangement documented in the record.

- **(ii) Population.** The resident is not within the population to which the standard applies. The diagnosis is absent, coded in error, superseded by later testing, or established by data the standard does not accept. **A resident wrongly included is removed from the numerator and from the denominator, and (a)(7) applies.**

- **(iii) The departure did not occur.** The medication was ordered, the assessment was performed, the communication was made. A medication administration record, an active order, a transmitted document, or an equivalent record establishes it.

- **(iv) Retrieval.** The Practitioner's own entry satisfying the standard exists in the record and the instrument did not reach it — a note held under a different encounter, a scanned or misfiled document, an entry in a part of the note the instrument does not parse, or a proper addendum made before notification. **This ground is available whatever the standard requires, because what it produces is exactly what is required.** It is expected to fire often against standards designated under § 4.7(e), because an accounting entry is frequently a short attributing phrase — *"held per cardiology"* — of the kind an instrument searching for a structured field or a stated reason will pass over.

- **(v) Timing.** The care was furnished before the date on which the standard may support an individual finding under § 5.7; or the record was examined before the point at which the standard required the entry.

- **(vi) Other information in the record satisfying the standard as written,** including an entry by another clinician, **where the standard is not designated under § 4.7(e).** Where it is designated, this ground is not available and (a)(4)(i) governs.

**(a)(2) The state of the record governs.** The observation states the date on which the record was examined. What corrects an observation is information that existed in the record before the Practitioner was notified. **The test is whether the required substance existed in the record in a form sufficient to satisfy the applicable standard, not whether the instrument retrieved it from the expected location.**

**(a)(3) Grounds (i), (ii) and (iii) are not affected by the form of the standard.** Whether a resident is the Practitioner's, whether the resident has the condition, and whether the drug was in fact prescribed are questions of fact about the audit. However a standard is drafted under § 4.7, it cannot make an observation correct that was attached to the wrong clinician or the wrong resident.

**(a)(4) What does not correct an observation.** Stated expressly, because a Practitioner who does not know it in advance will spend the response period assembling material that cannot help, and will reasonably resent having been invited to.

- **(i) Where the standard is designated under § 4.7(e), a clinical fact documented only by another clinician does not satisfy it.** A consultant's note, a laboratory value standing alone, or a conversation recorded by another discipline may establish that a reason existed somewhere. It does not show that the treating Practitioner accounted for the element, which is what the standard asks.

  **But the accounting is a low bar and the Practitioner should be told what it is.** An entry in the Practitioner's own note attributing the decision — *"SGLT2 inhibitor discontinued by cardiology, recurrent mycotic infections"* — or recording a defined pending step satisfies the standard and withdraws the observation under ground (iv) or (vi). **The Practitioner is not asked to justify another clinician's decision or to repeat its reasoning.** What does not satisfy the standard is a note that is silent on the element. **This subsection corrects the audit. It does not relax the standard.**

- **(ii) Reasoning supplied after notification.** Where a Practitioner states that the care was furnished and the reasoning existed but was not written down, the departure from the standard stands. **The explanation is nonetheless recorded, and is transmitted with any referral under § 6.3.** It is relevant to what the departure was, and it travels.

- **(iii) An amendment to the record made after notification.** See (a)(6).

- **(iv) An argument that the care was appropriate.** That question belongs to the PRC under Article VIII of its Charter, and this Committee neither asks nor answers it.

**(a)(5) The notice states all of this before the response period runs.** The notice sent under this subsection shall state, for each observation, the resident and date of service, what the standard required, what the audit found, whether the apparent departure is in the decision or in its documentation or in both, the grounds available under (a)(1) in the order there stated, and the matters in (a)(4) that will not correct it. **The Committee shall not invite a response on grounds it will not accept.**

**(a)(6) Amending a record that should be amended.** **Nothing in this subsection discourages it.** Where care was furnished and the record does not reflect it, completing the record is owed to the resident and to the facility, and that obligation exists independently of any audit. An amendment shall be a proper addendum — separately dated, attributed, with the original entry intact — and never an alteration of an existing entry. **Improper alteration of an existing entry may independently warrant referral under § 6.7.** An amendment made after notification is disclosed in the Practitioner's response and recorded. It does not change the observation, and it does satisfy the standard in subsequent audit cycles.

**(a)(7) Rates are recomputed before they are used.** **No rate supports selection under § 5.6(a) or a pattern criterion under § 6.2 until observations withdrawn on grounds (a)(1)(i) or (ii) have been removed from both numerator and denominator and the rate recomputed.** Section 5.6(a) permits a crude selection threshold on the express ground that the chart, not the denominator, answers the case-mix question. This subsection is where the chart answers it. A pattern criterion applied to an uncorrected rate rests on the very error the chart review exists to find.

**(a)(8) What the correction step teaches about the instrument.** Every ground sustained is recorded against the measure and reported to the analytics function under § 2.6.

- (i) Where grounds (i), (ii) or (iv) are sustained at or above a rate set in the audit register, the measure is revalidated under § 5.4 before it supports any further individual finding.
- (ii) **Where the same ground is sustained across multiple Practitioners on the same measure, the measure or the instrument is the problem and not the Practitioners.** It routes to standards revision or to the audit question under § 4.7(d) and § 5.9.
- (iii) Where ground (vi) is repeatedly raised and repeatedly unavailable because the standard is designated under § 4.7(e), the Committee shall consider at the next standards review whether the designation is earning its place on that measure. Designation is opt-in under § 4.7(e) and is not a fixed rule, and this is the evidence on which it is revisited. **A pattern of Practitioners producing a consultant's documented reason and nothing of their own is evidence about the education, not only about the standard** — the accounting entry is a small thing to write, and a widespread failure to write it usually means nobody explained what it is.

**(a)(9) The observation states whether the apparent departure is in the decision, in its documentation, or in both.** A decision that was made and not documented is a departure from a standard written under § 4.7, and it is a different departure from a documented decision that did not satisfy the standard. The PRC needs that distinction at screening, and this Committee is the one positioned to record it.

### (c) Close-out

**(c)(1) Every Practitioner notified under (a) is told what became of every observation.** The close-out notice states, for each observation, its disposition under (b), which grounds under (a)(1) were sustained, and where the applicable standard and its examples of conforming documentation are found in the open record under § 8.1(a). **A Practitioner all of whose observations were withdrawn is told so in terms, and told that nothing further follows.**

**(c)(2) A close-out notice imposes nothing.** It states no obligation, requires no response, sets no date, names no monitor, and creates no record of remediation. **It is not a Quality Intervention under § 8.2 of the PRC Charter, is not education directed at an individual, and is not a disposition of any kind concerning the Practitioner.** It reports the outcome of an audit and points to material that is open to everyone. Section 6.5 is unaffected: the Committee still imposes nothing on any Practitioner at any point.

**(c)(3) Why this is required.** Without it, a Practitioner's entire contact with the audit program is an allegation followed by silence, which is the least defensible version of the process the Committee could operate. And the referral schedule under § 6.2 escalates on repetition after notice. **That logic requires that notice was in fact given** — not that a standard was disseminated to everyone, but that this Practitioner was told what this audit found in these charts. The close-out notice is that record, and without it the escalation rests on a fiction.

**(c)(4) The close-out notice is issued whether or not a referral criterion is met,** and where a referral is made under §§ 6.1–6.5 it states that fact and nothing about what the PRC may do.

**(c)(5) Support, offered and not imposed.** The close-out notice carries an offer of support on the standard to which the observations related: the material in the open record, and **an opportunity, on request, for a discussion with a clinician who practises in the relevant area, a targeted teaching session, or case discussion.**

The offer is subject to all of the following, and is not the Committee's to make on any other terms.

- **(i) It is made identically to every Practitioner who receives a close-out**, whatever the disposition and whether or not any observation stood. It is what this process does, not something that happens to a particular person.
- **(ii) It is taken up only on request.** Nothing occurs unless the Practitioner asks, and there is no consequence of any kind for not asking.
- **(iii) Whether it is taken up is not recorded against the Practitioner, is not a factor in any disposition or in any criterion under § 6.2, and is not transmitted under § 6.3.** The moment uptake is counted, the offer has become an imposition in substance.
- **(iv) It is delivered by the education function under § 2.8**, by a person who did not participate in the disposition of that Practitioner's observations and who holds no Employment Authority over that Practitioner. That person executes the undertaking required by § 8.6 before receiving any individually attributed information, and **does not disclose to any person in their management reporting line that a particular Practitioner was offered or received support.** The rule bites on the attribution and not on the activity.
- **(v) It is not assigned, is not tracked to completion, and is not recorded in any system that reports individual completion to Management or feeds a performance process.** Where the same material exists as a general offering in a learning management system, a Practitioner may take it there as anyone may, and nothing connects that to the audit. **Uptake would otherwise be counted by the tooling rather than by anyone's decision, and the safeguard at (iii) would be defeated without a decision being made.**
- **(vi) Any record of delivery belongs to the open education record under § 8.1(a).** It is not a record of any observation or disposition, and is not individually attributed in anything this Committee holds.
- **(vii) It is not a Quality Intervention** under § 8.2 of the PRC Charter. It rests on no finding about the Practitioner's competence, imposes nothing, has no duration, no exit criteria and no monitor, and creates no record of remediation.

**The offer discharges nothing.** **It carries no obligation to accept it. It does not diminish the standard, the finding, or the Practitioner's own professional obligation to practise competently and to close a gap once aware of it**, and it does not affect any person's ability to refer under § 6.7 or the operation of § 5.11. **This Charter does not tell a Practitioner that no action is required of them**, and no notice issued under this Article shall say so.

**Why this is consistent with § 6.5.** What makes an instrument a professional review instrument is that it is **imposed**, that it follows a **finding** that care fell below standard, and that it is **monitored against exit criteria**. An offer of open material to a named person is none of those. **Directing material at a person is not the same as imposing something on them**, and that distinction is what lets a quality programme help someone without first determining anything about them.

**Why it needs to exist.** A Practitioner newly working in a clinical area will predictably have gaps, and **finding them is the purpose of this programme rather than an unfortunate by-product of it.** Without this subsection the only route to individual support runs through a professional review, which is a substantial instrument to reach for where what is needed is a conversation.

## § 5.11 Follow-up on a closed individual finding

*New section. Sits in Article V after § 5.10.*

**5.11.1 Why this section exists.** The audit programme measures by campaign: a measure is swept across the organization, and the next sweep addresses a different measure. **A finding closed under § 5.5(c) would therefore never be examined again**, because the interval before that standard is next swept may be years. Every part of this Charter that turns on what happens after a close-out — the escalation of the criteria under § 6.2 on repetition after notice, the offer of support under § 5.5(c)(5), and the reporting under § 2.6 and § 7.4 on whether an intervention moved its measure — depends on somebody looking again.

**5.11.2 The follow-up look.** Where an individual finding is closed out under § 5.5(c) without referral, the Committee shall examine a sample of that Practitioner's charts against the same standard **at [one hundred twenty] days from the close-out, independently of the audit plan and of whatever measure is then being swept.** The sample size is set in the audit register, and **the Coordinator diaries the follow-up under § 2.7 and brings it to the Committee when it falls due.** The trigger cannot depend on anyone remembering it across a change of audit topic, which is the failure this section exists to prevent.

**A follow-up look is selection under § 5.6(a) and nothing more.** A recorded finding is a permissible basis for deciding whose charts are examined; the examination carries no consequence of its own; and any observation it produces is corrected under § 5.5(a) and dispositioned under § 5.5(b) exactly as any other is.

**5.11.3 It runs once.**

- (a) **Where the follow-up produces no individual finding the matter closes**, the Practitioner is told so, and no further targeted examination of that Practitioner on that measure occurs. They return to the general population of the audit programme.
- (b) Where it produces an individual finding, that finding is the second occasion contemplated by the criteria under § 6.2 and is dispositioned accordingly.
- (c) **A follow-up look is not repeated and is not extended.** An examination directed at a named Practitioner that recurs without limit is a monitoring status. This Committee has no authority to impose one — see § 6.5 — and it would accomplish by administration what §§ 8.2 and 8.6 of the PRC Charter require process to accomplish.

**5.11.4 It is disclosed when the finding is closed.** The close-out notice states that a follow-up look will occur, on which standard, and approximately when. **A Practitioner is entitled to know that their charts will be examined again and why.** The disclosure is also what makes the offer under § 5.5(c)(5) coherent rather than empty: what was found, what help is available, and when the Committee will look again.

**5.11.5 Where there is nothing to examine.** Where the Practitioner has no chart within the population to which the standard applies during the follow-up window, there is nothing to look at and the matter closes under § 5.11.3(a). **A finding is not held open awaiting a case.**

**5.11.6 Systems dispositions are re-measured too.** Where a Systems disposition led to a standards revision, an education intervention, or a change in tooling, the audit plan shall provide for re-measurement of that measure at an interval the Committee sets. **A programme that intervenes and does not measure again has no basis for reporting that it improves care**, which is what § 2.6 and § 7.4 require it to report.

# Practitioner notice — template

*Plain-language notice issued under § 5.5(a)(5). The register entry and the enclosure carry the detail; this page carries the framing. Written to be read once, in five minutes, by someone who is annoyed.*

---

**QUALITY AUDIT — OBSERVATIONS FOR YOUR REVIEW**

**Standard:** [name and section] · **Audit cycle:** [ ] · **Record examined:** [date] · **Response due:** [date, 10 business days]

**What this is.** Our routine audit of [standard] examined [n] of your charts and flagged [m] where the record does not appear to meet it. This is not a complaint and it is not peer review. **No finding has been made about your care, and nothing here counts for anything until it has been checked with you.** That check is what this notice is for. Charts get attributed to the wrong clinician, diagnoses get miscoded, and notes sit in places an automated search does not reach — you are the person best positioned to catch that.

**What was flagged.** See the enclosed list. For each chart it gives the resident, the date of service, what the standard requires, what the audit found, and whether the gap appears to be in **the decision** or in **the documentation of it**.

**What we are asking you to do.** Look at the list and tell us where the audit is wrong. Please respond by [date].

**These will correct a flag** — in roughly the order they turn out to matter:

1. **It wasn't your patient.** You were covering, the resident wasn't on your panel then, or another clinician was managing that condition.
2. **The resident doesn't have the condition.** Wrong code, an old echo, a diagnosis that later testing ruled out, or a diagnosis established by data the standard doesn't accept.
3. **It didn't happen.** You did order it, you did do the assessment, you did make the call — and there's a record showing it.
4. **You wrote it and our tool missed it.** Your own note is there, but under a different encounter, in a scanned document, in a section our tool doesn't read, or in an addendum you made before this notice. **Tell us where to look.** This one holds no matter how the standard is worded — and it's the most common correction on standards that ask you to account for each element, because *"held per cardiology"* is easy to write and easy for a search to miss.
5. **The timing is wrong.** The care predates the date this standard started counting, or predates the point where the standard asked for the entry.
6. **Someone else documented what the standard accepts** — where this standard doesn't ask you to account for the element in your own note. The enclosure says whether it does.

**These will not correct a flag.** We would rather tell you now than have you spend a day on it:

- **A reason that appears only in someone else's note.** If cardiology stopped it, or ID advised against it, or palliative documented the goals of care, and your note says nothing about that element — the gap stands. The enclosure tells you which standards work this way.

  **To be clear about how small this ask is: you do not have to justify their decision, or repeat their reasoning, or take it on as yours.** *"SGLT2i discontinued by cardiology, recurrent mycotic infections"* meets the standard. So does *"anticoagulation on hold per cardiology, pending EGD 9/12."* What we need is that your note shows you knew where the element stood. A note that is silent on it reads the same whether you considered it or never saw it, and for a resident on six therapies managed by four clinicians, that difference is the whole point.

  One caution on the pending version: it holds for this cycle, not forever. If the same element is still pending next cycle with the follow-up long past and nothing new written, that's a new flag.
- **Telling us your reasoning now.** If you considered it and decided against it and didn't write it down, the gap stands. **Tell us anyway** — we write it down, it stays with the record, and if this ever goes further it goes with it.
- **Amending the chart now.** It won't change this cycle's flag. **But if care was given and the chart doesn't show it, amend it** — you owe that to the resident and the facility regardless of any audit. Use a proper dated addendum, never an edit to an existing entry, and tell us you did it. It counts from the next cycle on.
- **Arguing that the care was right.** We're not the committee that asks that question, and we won't be deciding it.

**What happens after.** Every flag ends up in one of three places: **withdrawn** (the audit was wrong, or you showed us where to look); **a systems issue** (this is about the order set, the workflow, or how we wrote the standard — it comes off your record entirely and goes to fixing the thing); or **a confirmed gap**.

**A confirmed documentation gap, on its own, is not a referral to anybody.** It sits in the audit record. What we do next is fix the standard, the tooling, or the teaching around it, and audit again.

**You will hear back either way**, with what happened to each item and where to find the standard and examples of what meets it.

**Help, if you want it.** Everyone who gets one of these letters gets the same offer: if it would be useful, we can set up a conversation with a clinician who works in this area, a short teaching session, or a case discussion. **Ask and we'll arrange it.** We don't record who takes this up, it isn't counted anywhere, and it plays no part in anything that follows — so there's no downside to asking and no mark against you for not.

**To be clear about what this offer does and doesn't change.** You're under no obligation to take it up. **You are still held to the standard, the items above still stand, and your own professional obligation to close a gap once you know about it is your own** — we're offering help with it, not excusing it.

**We'll look again in about [four months].** A small sample of your charts on this same standard, whatever the audit is focused on by then. If it's clear, that's the end of it and you go back into the general pool — we don't keep looking. If it isn't, we'll tell you that too. We're telling you now because you should know it's coming, and because otherwise the offer above would be hollow.

**Questions:** [coordinator, contact]. You are welcome to call before you respond.
