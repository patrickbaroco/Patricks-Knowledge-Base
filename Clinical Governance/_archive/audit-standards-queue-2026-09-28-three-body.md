# Audit standards — queue and register entries

**[ENTITY LEGAL NAME]**

*Working file — 2026-09-10. The standards workstream, kept separate from `audit-referral-policy-skeleton.md` on purpose.*

**Why this is a separate document.** Standards are adopted, revised and retired by the Committee under Article IV on a cadence of their own. The Audit Referral Policy is adopted by the Committee and **ratified by the Council** under § 4.2(f) of the Council Charter. A Policy that named measures would put a Council ratification in the way of every standards revision. The Policy therefore states what a criterion requires of any measure; **this file is where measures are worked out, and the standards register is where the answers are recorded.**

## The register entry — what the Committee decides when it adopts a standard

Ten determinations, all made at adoption, all recorded, and all open under § 8.1.1(a). Four of them decide what the standard can do to a Practitioner, and they are the reason the register rather than the Policy is the document a clinician would actually consult.

1. **The standard itself**, satisfying § 4.7(a)–(d), with examples of conforming documentation.
2. **Clinical basis** under § 5.8(a), stated without reference to any payment arrangement.
3. **§ 4.4 designation** — minimum expectation or improvement target. **By express vote; there is no default.**
4. **§ 4.7(e) designation** — whether the treating Practitioner's own entry must account for each element. Express, reasoned, not the default.
5. **Single-occasion designation** under the Policy — none, unqualified, or decision-departure-only where § 5.5(a)(9) records the departure as in the decision.
6. **Which criteria may reach a finding on this measure** — none of them; Criterion B; or Criterion B and Criterion C.
7. **§ 5.7 notice period** — [sixty] days by default, longer where the standard asks a change in practice rather than a change in documentation.
8. **Follow-up parameters** under § 5.11.2, where longer than the default opening period and window.
9. **The audit question and its map to this standard** under § 5.3, recorded in the audit register.
10. **Effective date.**

A **reset entry** is added at each later revision, stating whether the revision resets the Criterion B count. Absent an entry, it resets.

## First question in this workstream: does divergence need a standard at all?

The § 5.10(a) cross-check — an entry asserting a fact recorded elsewhere is tested against the record — is a **standing requirement of the audit program imposed by the Charter itself**, not by any adopted standard. That creates a fork worth settling before anything is drafted.

**If it operates only under § 5.10(a)**, divergence produces observations, and those observations are dispositioned. But § 5.1 defines an individual finding as a validated observation indicating a departure **from an applicable minimum expectation**. With no standard there is no minimum expectation, so **divergence could never be an individual finding and could never be referred.** It would work as a control, feeding Systems dispositions, the education agenda and instrument revalidation, and nothing more.

**If it is to be referable, it needs an adopted standard.** A candidate form: *an entry asserting a fact recorded elsewhere in the record shall be consistent with the record as it stood at the time the entry was made.*

Three things to check against Article IV before adopting anything like it:

- **§ 4.7(a)** requires a standard to state what must be decided and documented and never what must be prescribed. A consistency requirement prescribes no treatment, so it appears to sit inside (a) rather than against it — but this is the first standard in the programme whose subject is not a clinical decision, and it should be tested rather than assumed.
- **"As it stood at the time the entry was made"** is the qualifier that disposes of ordinary lag at the level of the standard rather than leaving every instance to the § 5.5(a) correction step. Without it the standard is unmeetable, because the record moves after the note is written.
- **§ 4.7(d)** is satisfied comfortably. It is answerable from the record and from nothing else.

**If it is adopted:** minimum expectation; § 4.7(e) not applicable; no single-occasion designation; **Criterion B and Criterion C**, this being the measure whose denominator supports a rate earliest. The string-identity and invariance detectors are **selection** under § 5.6(a) and belong in the audit register, not here and not in the Policy.

## The four clinical measures — proposed entries and what is unsettled

Proposals only. Each needs its § 4.7 drafting pass and a clinical basis statement.

### Urine culture ordered without a documented sign or symptom

Minimum expectation · § 4.7(e) designated · no single-occasion designation · **Criterion B**.

Attribution is clean, because an order has an orderer, and the population is definable. Denominator per Practitioner per cycle is modest, so Criterion C is not available at adoption and is revisited at the § 4.6 review.

**Unsettled.** The exclusions have to be in the standard rather than in the audit question — mental status change alone, and cloudy or malodorous urine alone, do not count. And the § 4.7(e) designation bites hard here: **the sign or symptom is frequently the nursing report**, which is where the clinical fact often lives. If the designation stands, a nursing-documented symptom does not satisfy the standard and the ordering clinician's own note must carry it. That is a small entry to write and a large change in habit, and § 5.7 should carry a period longer than the default for it.

### Antipsychotic in dementia — target behaviour, non-pharmacological measures, gradual dose reduction

Minimum expectation · § 4.7(e) **designation unresolved** · no single-occasion designation · **no criterion reaches it at adoption**.

**Education and systems only, on purpose.** The target behaviour and the non-pharmacological measures tried are habitually documented in the nursing record, and a standard designated under § 4.7(e) would ask for an entry the workflow may not produce. Run the measure, teach to it, watch how often ground (vi) and § 5.5(a)(4)(i) fire, and let § 5.5(a)(8)(iii) do the work it was written for. Revisit the designation and the criteria entry together at the § 4.6 review.

**Unsettled.** Which diagnoses fall outside the population, and the interaction with § 5.8(d): a case a rating program counts but which satisfies this standard produces no individual finding. How a gradual dose reduction attempt and a documented contraindication to attempting one are each expressed. Whether "target behaviour" is a documented behaviour or a documented *treatment target*, which are different requirements.

### Sliding scale insulin beyond thirty days from admission or readmission

Minimum expectation · § 4.7(e) designated · no single-occasion designation · **Criterion B**.

Attribution is sound: whoever started it at discharge, by day thirty-one it is the treating Practitioner's to account for. Denominator is small, so Criterion C is not available.

**Unsettled, and it is the one that will bite.** **Whether the clock resets on readmission.** The standard has to state it and the instrument will get it wrong either way. The thirty days is a **population exclusion and not a defence** — a resident inside the window is out of the denominator, and a resident outside it is not thereby in breach.

### Atrial fibrillation — anticoagulation accounting

Minimum expectation · § 4.7(e) designated **to require both an attribution and a reason**, which § 4.7(c) recommends by name for this measure · no single-occasion designation · **Criterion B**.

This is the measure where § 5.5(b)(1) will earn its keep. A resident with atrial fibrillation, no anticoagulant, nothing anywhere in the record and a silent note is a case where a member may hold a genuine concern — and that concern travels under § 6.7(c) as an act of the person holding it, not through any criterion in the Policy.

**Unsettled.** Valvular and non-valvular treated alike or not. Whether any risk-score threshold appears at all — **§ 4.7(a) says a standard states what must be decided and documented and never what must be prescribed**, so a threshold that requires treatment is out; a threshold that defines the population may be defensible. How goals of care and hospice enrolment express as a settled disposition rather than as an exclusion.

## Candidates for a single-occasion designation, none of them yet drafted

The four measures above are over-response and omission measures, and none of them carries a single-occasion designation. **The CQC's own open items name the under-response handles** — return to hospital shortly after a decision not to transfer; death in facility without documented goals of care; a change in condition with no documented disposition — and record that they are a matter for the referral schedule and the Committee's judgment rather than a charter duty. Two of them are the natural first single-occasion standards:

- **A documented response to a critical laboratory value.** Unqualified designation. One chart where the value returned and nothing in the record shows anyone acted on it does not need a second occasion to be worth a look.
- **A documented goals-of-care conversation before a decision not to transfer on an acute change in condition.** Decision-departure-only designation under § 5.5(a)(9). Where the conversation happened and was not written up, the ordinary ladder applies; where the decision was made with nothing to support it, that is different in kind.

**The programme is asymmetric without them.** Over-response produces an event the audit can find; under-response produces an absence. These two measures are how an under-response question gets a route through the audit rather than depending entirely on § 6.7.
