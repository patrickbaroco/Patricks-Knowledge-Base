## Article I — Establishment, Purpose, and Status

**1.1 Establishment.** The Chief Executive Officer of the Entity, by written authorization dated [DATE] ("the Authorization"), establishes the Clinical Quality Committee ("the Committee") as a committee of the Entity and approves this Charter.

**1.2 Purpose.** The Committee sets clinical standards, drives the education agenda that supports them, and audits ongoing quality against them. Its subject is the quality of care furnished across the organization.

**1.3 What the Committee is not.** The Committee does not conduct professional review of individual Practitioners, does not make findings about individual competence, and **imposes no consequence of any kind on any individual.** Where the audit produces an individual finding meeting a criterion adopted under § 6.2, the Committee refers it under §§ 6.1–6.5 and its involvement ends there. Individual accountability is the Peer Review Committee's ("PRC") subject and no part of this one.

**1.4 Status.** The Committee is a peer review, medical review, or quality assurance committee within the meaning of Va. Code § 8.01-581.17; Ohio Rev. Code § 2305.25 et seq.; N.C. Gen. Stat. § 90-21.22A; Ind. Code § 34-30-15; and Ky. Rev. Stat. § 311.377. It adopts the peer review guidelines published by the Medical Society of Virginia, as amended, by reference, for purposes of Va. Code § 8.01-581.17(B)(iii).

**1.5 Position relative to the patient safety evaluation system.** The Committee operates entirely **outside** any patient safety evaluation system the Entity may establish. **Standards-setting and education are prospective and normative work. The audit is retrospective quality review.** Neither is incident capture assembled for reporting to a patient safety organization, and the retrospective character of the audit record is part of why it is classified as it is under § 8.1(b).

**1.6 Facility QAPI.** The Committee's work is organizational. It is not the quality assurance and performance improvement program any facility maintains under 42 C.F.R. § 483.75, does not conduct or participate in facility QAPI as a committee, and does not take facility QAPI material into its record. Documentation a facility is independently required to create is not protected by anything in this Charter and is not made so by being placed in a Committee file.

**1.7 Definitions.** Capitalized terms not defined here have the meanings given in § 1.6 of the PRC Charter.

## Article II — Composition

**2.1 Chair.** The Chair of Clinical Excellence chairs the Committee ex officio, for so long as that position is held. The chairmanship is not termed.

**2.2 Members.** Appointed by the Chair. Terms of [two] years, staggered, renewable. The Chairs of the Primary Care Committee and the Psychiatry Committee sit as members.

**2.3 Composition floor.** Not less than [two-thirds] of members shall be licensed clinicians in active clinical practice. This exceeds the fifty percent required by Ind. Code § 34-30-15 and shall not be reduced by amendment below fifty percent.

**2.4 Disciplines.** Physicians and APCs. **Where a standard concerning psychotherapy is proposed, a licensed psychotherapist is consulted before adoption** and may attend for that purpose on the terms of § 2.6. A psychotherapist seat is not created until the addendum required by § 6.5 of the PRC Charter is in effect, since no standard concerning psychotherapy can produce a referral before then.

**2.5 Members holding Employment Authority.** Not more than [one] member may hold Employment Authority. Members holding Employment Authority participate fully in standards, education, and systems work. **They do not participate in any determination concerning an identified Practitioner** — including the disposition of an individually attributed observation under § 5.5 and any determination under Article VI — and withdraw before it is discussed. The PRC excludes such persons from its membership altogether under § 2.3 of its Charter. This Committee seats one because the enterprise view is useful in standards and systems work, and confines that member for the same reason the PRC excludes them.

**2.6 Analytics function.** The Entity's data and analytics program assigns a function to the Committee, which tracks organization-wide performance, reports it to the Committee on the cadence the Committee sets, and reports whether interventions moved the measures they targeted. Analytics staff attend as non-members.

**2.6.1 Enterprise priorities function.** The Entity designates a person or persons to bring its enterprise clinical priorities to the Committee, including the priorities of any accountable care arrangement in which the Entity participates and those of clinical operations. They attend as non-members on the terms of § 2.6, take part in the discussion of standards, the education agenda and systems work, and take no part in and are not present for any determination concerning an identified Practitioner. **The Committee owes each enterprise priority a stated response in the annual audit plan under § 3.1(f)**, adopting a standard that carries it, recording why none does, or recording that it is carried by a standard already in effect. The plan is presented under that section to the Chief Medical Officer and the Chief Operating Officer, who see the response there.

**2.7 Coordinator.** The Executive Coordinator, Clinical Excellence serves the Committee, is custodian of its records under Article VIII, maintains the standards register and the audit register, and administers notice and dissemination under § 4.5. Sections 2.7.1 through 2.7.5 of the PRC Charter apply in full, and references there to that Committee are read as references to this one.

**2.8 Education function.** The Entity's learning and development program assigns a function to the Committee, which delivers the education agenda adopted under § 3.1(b), maintains the education material in the open record under § 8.1(a), delivers support offered under § 5.5(c)(5), and conducts the inquiry into shared practice under § 7.6(b). Its staff attend as non-members.

**The two things it does for this Committee carry different rules.** The education agenda is open material circulated to everyone and carries no sensitivity. **Support under § 5.5(c)(5) is the only place this function learns anything the audit attributes to an identified Practitioner**, and § 5.5(c)(5) governs what may be done with it.

## Article III — Scope

**3.1 The Committee:**

- (a) adopts, amends, and retires clinical standards under Article IV;
- (b) sets the education agenda that supports them;
- (c) owns the quality audit program under Article V;
- (d) refers individual concerns to the PRC under §§ 6.1–6.5;
- (e) receives systems findings and de-identified aggregate reporting from the PRC;
- (f) adopts an annual audit plan stating the measures to be audited in the year, the standard each measures against, and the response to each enterprise priority brought under § 2.6.1, and presents it through the Chair to the Chief Medical Officer and the Chief Operating Officer at the quarterly meeting under § 7.4. **The plan states what will be measured; authority over the standards themselves stays with this Committee under Article IV.** Those officers may say that the plan is not measuring the right things. They do not say what a standard requires;
- (g) records commendations under § 5.5(d), and recognizes facilities and teams under § 7.6.

**3.2 Ownership and execution.** The Chair owns the audit program. Execution is shared among members, the analytics function, and the Coordinator. Ownership is not delegable; execution is.

## Article IV — Standards

**4.1 Two sources.** A standard adopted by the Committee rests on either:

- (a) applicable guidance published by a recognized professional body, from which the standard is derived; or
- (b) the best available evidence, where no applicable guidance exists.

**4.2 Derivation, and why internal standards are legitimate.** Where applicable guidance from a recognized professional body exists, the Committee uses it as the preferred basis for its standard and identifies in the standard any adaptation made for the population or the setting.

**The Committee does not adopt a published guideline wholesale.** A society guideline carries many recommendations, a great number of them untested in this population and some unsuited to it, and adopting one in its entirety would import all of them as expectations. It would also imply that a professional society recommendation and the standard of care are the same thing. They are not — see § 6.7.

Where no applicable guidance exists, the Committee writes a standard from the evidence. **The populations the Entity serves — long-term care and assisted living residents, frequently of advanced age, with multimorbidity and limited life expectancy — are underrepresented in or excluded from the trials on which most society guidelines rest.** A standard applicable to those residents frequently does not exist to be derived from. Where the Committee authors one it shall state, in the standard, the evidence it rests on and the reasoning that carried it from that evidence to that population.

**4.3 Adoption is the Committee's act.** A standard may be drafted anywhere. It becomes a standard of the Entity only on adoption by the Committee, by vote, with a stated effective date, recorded in the standards register.

The record of adoption states the designation required by § 4.4, **whether it reaches the periodic review entry under § 4.7(f)**, and **the education and implementation that will precede measurement** — which may include dissemination of the standard and its clinical rationale, examples of conforming documentation, and implementation tools. This is recorded at adoption rather than required separately, so that it attaches to an event that already produces a record. Whether education or another intervention moved performance is reported by the analytics function under § 2.6 through subsequent audit cycles.

**4.4 Two designations.** On adoption the Committee designates each standard as one of:

- (a) a **minimum expectation** — an individual-level requirement the Entity expects Practitioners to meet, an individual finding of a departure from which may support an audit referral under §§ 6.1–6.5; or
- (b) an **improvement target** — a level the organization is working toward, which may exceed what the standard of care requires. **A departure from an improvement target generates systems data and may never support an audit referral under §§ 6.1–6.5.** It does not limit § 6.7.

**Designation as a minimum expectation does not establish that the requirement forms part of the legal or professional standard of care.** It states what the Entity expects of a Practitioner and what may be referred. What the standard of care requires is determined by the PRC under Article VIII of its Charter, on its own record, and § 6.7 governs the relationship between the two.

The distinction carries practical weight where a standard is designated under § 4.7(e), because such a standard may be an Entity documentation and communication convention rather than a clinical rule, and designating it a minimum expectation is not an assertion that competent practice requires it. **What keeps a minimum expectation clinically grounded is § 5.8(a)**, which requires every standard to state its clinical basis at adoption — not the wording of this designation.

A quality committee will naturally want to set ambitious standards. This designation is what keeps ambition from becoming a disciplinary trap. It is made at adoption, on the record, rather than argued about at the point of referral.

**The designation is made by express vote. There is no default.** A standard adopted without a designation recorded in the standards register is in effect for education and measurement, but may not support an audit referral under §§ 6.1–6.5 until the Committee designates it. A default of minimum expectation would let an aspirational standard become disciplinary through inattention. A default of improvement target would leave gaps in what can be referred. Neither default is acceptable, so the Charter provides none.

**A subject does not require one of each.** The designation attaches to a standard, not to a clinical topic. Most subjects will carry a minimum expectation and nothing else. An improvement target is adopted only where there is a reason to adopt one, and may be added years later or never.

**Rate targets are not standards under this Article.** A goal expressed as a proportion of a population — a share of residents on a given therapy, a rate of completion — is an organizational measure, not something an individual Practitioner meets or fails. It belongs to the analytics function under § 2.6 and to the systems work of this Committee. It is not adopted under § 4.3, carries no designation, and is never attributed to an individual. Most of what would informally be called a reach goal belongs here rather than at § 4.4(b). Section 4.4(b) is for the narrower case: a standard written at the level of the individual Practitioner but above the floor of acceptable practice.

**4.5 Dissemination and notice.** Every adopted standard is disseminated to the Practitioners it applies to, and the dissemination is documented. **No individual finding against a standard may support an audit referral under §§ 6.1–6.5 unless the standard was adopted as a minimum expectation, disseminated, and in effect at the time of the care reviewed**, and unless the period under § 5.7 has run.

This governs referrals arising from the audit program, which is one route among several. It places no limit on what may be referred or reviewed on standard-of-care grounds under § 6.7.

**4.5.1 Review guide.** **Each standard is disseminated with a review guide**: its requirements stated element by element, as a clinician reading a chart would apply them, with the standard's examples of conforming documentation. The review guide is part of the open record under § 8.1(a).

**The review guide is shared with every assigned supervising physician, in the expectation that it will be used in routine review of the charts of the APCs they supervise.** That review is part of supervision and is conducted outside this Committee. The Committee does not select the charts reviewed, receives nothing of what is found, and takes no part in what follows from it.

A supervising physician who reads an APC's charts against the same standard the audit applies will see what the audit sees, from the chart itself, and is the person best placed to teach from it.

**4.6 Review.** Each standard is reviewed at least every [three] years, or sooner on new evidence or a change in accepted practice. Redesignation between § 4.4(a) and § 4.4(b) is made like adoption. Retirement is recorded like adoption.

**4.7 How standards are written.** Every standard adopted under this Article shall satisfy the following. These are drafting requirements, not guidance.

- (a) **A standard states what must be decided and documented, never what must be prescribed.** The residents the Entity serves are frequently frail, multimorbid, and of limited life expectancy, and for many of them the correct decision is to withhold a therapy that a guideline would otherwise indicate. A standard written as a treatment requirement creates pressure to treat against the resident's interest. A standard written as a decision requirement does not.

- (b) **Where a standard covers multiple elements, it requires documentation element by element, not a single attestation covering all of them.** **Except as subsection (e) provides for a standard designated under it, and subsection (f) for a periodic review entry,** for each element: the action taken, or its absence together with a reason specific to that element. A global statement that a subject was considered does not satisfy a standard written under this subsection.

  By way of illustration, a standard on guideline-directed therapy in heart failure with reduced ejection fraction is satisfied by an entry in the form *"Beta-blocker: carvedilol 3.125 mg bid; ACE/ARB/ARNI: none, secondary to recurrent hyperkalemia; MRA: none, secondary to recurrent hyperkalemia; SGLT2: dapagliflozin 10 mg daily"* — and is not satisfied by *"GDMT reviewed, not a candidate."*

- (c) **A reason given for withholding shall be of a verifiable kind** — a laboratory value, a documented event, an allergy or intolerance, a documented conversation, or a stated goal of care. A conclusion offered without a referent, such as that a resident is not a candidate, does not satisfy a standard requiring a reason.

  **An attribution is not a reason under this subsection.** *"Per cardiology"* records who decided and not why. It is a referent without a reason, where *"not a candidate"* is a reason without a referent, and neither tells the next reader what to do. An attribution satisfies a standard designated under subsection (e) by a different route, and a standard may be designated to require both — which is what the Committee should ordinarily do where an untreated condition carries a well-defined risk, as with anticoagulation in atrial fibrillation.

- (d) **A standard shall be answerable from the record.** If satisfying it requires information that exists nowhere in the chart, it cannot be audited and shall not be adopted.

- (e) **The treating Practitioner's note stands on its own.** Unlike (a) through (d), this subsection applies only where a standard is designated under it at adoption.

  **Where a standard is so designated, each element it covers shall be accounted for in the treating Practitioner's own entry. To account for an element is not to justify it.** The entry satisfies the standard where, for each element, it records:

  - **the action taken** — *"apixaban 5 mg bid"*;
  - **a settled disposition** — the element is not in place and that is the current decision, recorded with a reason of a verifiable kind under (c), with the decision attributed to an identified clinician or service, or with both — *"SGLT2 inhibitor discontinued by cardiology, recurrent mycotic infections"*; or
  - **a pending disposition** — the element is not in place for now, recorded together with **what resolves it**: an event, a date, an identified clinician, or a threshold — *"anticoagulation on hold per cardiology until after epidural injection by pain management"*.

  **These alternatives satisfy the documentation requirement of subsection (b) for a standard designated under this subsection, and for an entry examined under subsection (f), notwithstanding (b)'s otherwise applicable requirement that an absent element be accompanied by a reason under (c).**

  **A pending disposition without a resolving condition does not satisfy the standard.** An entry describing an element as held, paused, deferred, on hold, temporary, or pending asserts that something is to happen later, and is measured as a pending disposition whatever else it contains. *"Anticoagulation on hold per cardiology"* records who decided and nothing about what ends it; it does not satisfy a standard designated under this subsection. The difference between that entry and the example above is four words, and they are the four words that distinguish a plan from a gap — **the indefinite hold that no one revisits is the characteristic mechanism by which a resident with atrial fibrillation spends eighteen months off anticoagulation and then has a stroke.**

  **Where the treating Practitioner does not know the reason or the resolving condition, recording that, together with the step that will establish it, satisfies this subsection** — *"anticoagulation on hold per cardiology, reason not in the records available to me, will clarify at next visit."* The requirement is dischargeable under ignorance, and it is not a requirement to reconstruct another clinician's reasoning.

  **What does not satisfy it is silence.** A consultant's note establishing the clinical fact, standing alone elsewhere in the record, does not satisfy a standard designated under this subsection and does not withdraw an observation under § 5.5(a).

  **What this subsection asks, and what it does not.** It does not ask the treating Practitioner to repeat a specialist's reasoning, to re-justify a decision another clinician made, or to assume responsibility for it. It asks that the note show the element was considered and record where it stands. The residents this Entity serves commonly carry several guideline-relevant therapies managed across several clinicians, and **a note silent on an element is indistinguishable from a note written by someone who did not know it was there.** A standard is designated under this subsection in order to distinguish those two cases, and no entry by another clinician distinguishes them.

  **A pending disposition satisfies the standard for the cycle in which it is entered, and not indefinitely.** Where a subsequent audit finds the same element still pending, the resolving condition past due or passed, and no intervening entry, that is an observation. A deferral that never resolves is the characteristic failure of this branch, and it is not cured by having been accurate when written.

  **An attribution is subject to § 5.10(a).** Where an entry attributes a decision to a clinician or service, the audit tests that assertion against the record, and divergence between what the note asserts and what the record shows is itself an observation.

  **Designation is express, reasoned, and not the default.** A standard is subject to this subsection only where the record of adoption says so and states why. **Absent designation, documentation by any clinician in the record may satisfy the applicable documentation requirement.** The default is the looser rule because a self-contained entry is the requirement most readily met by a stored phrase, and § 5.10 applies with particular force to a standard designated under this subsection.

- (f) **The periodic review entry.** A physician responsible for a resident's care in a service line reviews that care periodically and enters a note of the review in the resident's record. In primary care in a skilled nursing facility the review is the visit required by 42 C.F.R. § 483.30, at which the physician reviews the resident's total program of care, and in assisted living the Entity provides a periodic physician visit for the same purpose. In psychiatry it is the supervising physician's periodic visit to, or review of the care of, a resident whose psychiatric care an APC furnishes. **Each is a periodic review entry where it is entered in the resident's record.** A record of supervision kept outside the resident's record is not.

  **Where a standard reaches the periodic review entry, that entry is examined as the reviewing physician's own entry, and accounts for each element the standard covers as subsection (e) provides**, whether or not the standard is otherwise designated under (e). An entry by another clinician does not satisfy the standard for the periodic review entry. A note concurring in another clinician's plan satisfies it where it states, for each element, what is concurred in.

  **A standard reaches the periodic review entries of each service line it applies to, unless the record of adoption states that it does not, and why.** Where periodic review entries begin to be made in a service line after a standard applying to it was adopted, the standard reaches them only after the notice period under § 5.7, running from notice to the physicians who make them.

  The review exists to examine the plan of care, so a standard that asks the entry to account for an element asks it to show what the review is for. **The audit then reads the APC's entries and the reviewing physician's entry together, and reports on each to its author.** The physician's part in the resident's care is examined on the physician's own entry, and nothing about the APC's results passes to the physician by this route.

## Article V — The Audit Program

**5.1 Purpose and terms.** The audit program measures ongoing quality against adopted standards, at the organization and, where a standard permits, at the level of the individual Practitioner.

Three terms are used precisely throughout this Article and Article VI and are not interchangeable:

- **Observation** — a chart-level apparent departure, identified by the audit instrument or by a person executing an audit. An observation is unvalidated. It states nothing about the quality of care and **carries no conclusion and no consequence** until dispositioned under § 5.5. An observation reported under § 5.4.1 and not corrected stands as reported. It remains unvalidated, and what it may open is the follow-up look under § 5.11.2.
- **Rate** — aggregate performance against a standard across a defined population. A rate describes a population. It never describes a Practitioner's care in a given episode.
- **Individual finding** — a validated observation that the Committee has determined indicates a departure from an applicable minimum expectation. **Only an individual finding may be referred by the Committee through the audit-referral process at §§ 6.1–6.5.** Sections 6.6 and 6.7 are a separate route and are not limited by this definition.

**An individual finding under this Charter is not a finding of the PRC.** The Committee determines one thing: whether its audit accurately identified a departure from a standard the Committee adopted. Whether the care fell below the standard of care, and what that means about the Practitioner, is determined by the PRC on its own record under Article VIII of its Charter.

**5.2 What is measured.** Three classes, treated differently:

- (a) **Process and decision measures** — the assessment made, the decision reached, the medication chosen, the response to a change in condition, the goals-of-care conversation, the communication to the facility. These are what a Practitioner controls, and they are the substance of the program.
- (b) **Treatment-gap prevalence measures** — a documented state that should be rare, such as a resident with atrial fibrillation and no anticoagulation and no documented reason. Particularly suitable for chart-level attribution and validation. Suitable is not the same as unambiguous: anticoagulation may be managed by another clinician, medication reconciliation may be incomplete, and the rationale may live in a document the instrument cannot see. That is what § 5.5(a) is for.
- (c) **Outcome measures** — used to find signal, not to attribute it. An outcome alone does not establish that care fell below standard, and care may fall below standard without an adverse outcome. This is the same rule the PRC applies at § 8.1 of its Charter, deliberately.

**5.3 Audit questions map to standards.** **No audit question is live unless it maps to a standard adopted under Article IV and in effect.** The map is recorded in the audit register. A question that does not map is either retired, or its standard is adopted first.

This governs the audit instrument, which scores every Practitioner against a stated criterion and must therefore state it. **It does not govern what may be reviewed. See § 6.7.**

**5.4 Validation of the instrument.** Before observations from any automated or assisted audit instrument may support an individual finding, the instrument shall be validated against clinician review, and the validation shall be recorded and repeated on any material change to the instrument. This section validates the instrument. It does not validate any particular observation, which is the subject of § 5.5(a).

**5.4.1 The review report.** **Every Practitioner whose charts are examined in a cycle receives a review report**, whether or not any observation was made. The review report is the ordinary product of the audit. It is generated from the examining clinician's record of each chart and issues without any disposition under § 5.5.

**(a) What it carries.** For each chart examined, the resident, the date of service, and whether the chart met the standard. For each observation, what the standard required, what the examination found, and whether the apparent departure is in the decision, in its documentation, or in both, as § 5.5(a)(9) provides. With these: the standard and its review guide under § 4.5.1; any commendation under § 5.5(d); any clinical comment under § 5.5(e); the offer of support under § 5.5(c)(5); and, where the Practitioner is an APC, the statement under § 5.5(c)(6). **Where § 5.4.2(b) or (c) applies to an observation, the report says so, and the notice under § 5.5(a)(5) issues with it.** **Where a follow-up look will follow under § 5.11.2, the report makes the disclosure § 5.11.4 requires**, and states that an observation on the same standard in the follow-up window will be checked with the Practitioner under § 5.5 and may lead to a referral under the Audit Referral Policy.

**(b) Corrections are invited, with no period set.** The report invites the Practitioner to identify any observation the examination got wrong, on the grounds in § 5.5(a)(1), and states the matters in § 5.5(a)(4) that do not correct an observation. **Except where § 5.4.2(b) or (c) applies, no date is set for a response.** A correction received before a follow-up look is conducted removes the observation from it, and where none stands the look does not run (§ 5.11.2). Nothing else turns on whether a response is made. A clinician executing the audit applies a correction received, records the ground sustained under § 5.5(a)(8), and recomputes the rate under § 5.5(a)(7). An observation corrected or withdrawn no longer stands, and the Practitioner is told so.

**(c) When the correction step runs.** The correction step under § 5.5(a) exists so that no referral rests on an observation the audit got wrong. **An observation in a review report supports no referral unless § 5.4.2(b) or (c) applies to it.** Otherwise it is not an individual finding and is not dispositioned. It may open a follow-up look under § 5.11.2, and it becomes part of a referral only if the correction step later validates it under § 5.4.2(a). The step therefore runs where its result could support a referral, under § 5.4.2, and there it reaches the observations in the earlier report as well. **Most measures carry no referral criterion under the Audit Referral Policy, and on those the review report is the whole of the individual process.**

**(d) A review report imposes nothing.** Section 5.5(c)(2) applies to it as it applies to a close-out notice.

**(e) The cycle summary.** Before the review reports for a cycle issue, the Committee receives a summary of the cycle: by measure, the charts examined, the observations made, and the commendations and clinical comments recorded; the charts flagged under § 5.5(b)(1.1); and any condition of process, staffing, tooling, workflow or the standard itself that the examination suggests. **The Chair puts the question under § 5.5(b)(1) over the charts carrying observations and the charts flagged**, and the minutes record that it was put and answered.

The Committee may then record any observation, or any class of observations, as a systems condition. An observation so recorded does not stand in the review report, the report says why, and § 7.5 applies to the condition. **The Committee otherwise receives the summary as a whole and determines nothing about any Practitioner in it.** **Recording an individual observation as a systems condition is a determination concerning an identified Practitioner**, and §§ 2.5, 9.3 and 9.6 apply to it. The question under § 5.5(b)(1) is put Practitioner by Practitioner, so that § 9.6 can operate.

**5.4.2 When an observation is checked with the Practitioner.** **The correction step and disposition under § 5.5 run only where their result could support a referral under §§ 6.1–6.5.** That is so in three cases.

- (a) **Repetition.** An observation made at a follow-up look under § 5.11.2, on a standard whose entry in the standards register names Criterion B. **The correction step then reaches the observations standing in the review report that opened the follow-up as well as the new ones**, in one notice and one response period, and each is dispositioned under § 5.5(b). Where no observation in the earlier report survives as an individual finding, there is no first occasion: the new observations, so far as they are dispositioned Individual, are closed out under § 5.5(c), and § 5.11.2 applies to them as a new matter. **An observation on that standard made by any other examination, on care furnished in the follow-up window, is checked with the follow-up look and treated as made at it.** Where the look has already been conducted or does not run, such an observation is reported under § 5.4.1 and, where § 5.11.2(a) applies to it, opens a look as a new matter. An observation on care furnished in the opening period before the window is reported under § 5.4.1 and opens no follow-up look of its own.
- (b) **Single occasion.** An observation on a standard carrying a single-occasion designation under the Audit Referral Policy, of the kind the designation reaches.
- (c) **Pattern.** Observations on a measure whose entry names Criterion C, where the Practitioner's observations on that measure in the cycle, on the figures as reported, would meet elements (a) through (c) of that criterion. The step runs on that Practitioner's observations on that measure in that cycle, and the criterion is evaluated on what survives.

**In every other case the observation is reported under § 5.4.1 and goes no further.** This section governs the audit route only, and nothing in it limits § 6.7.

The correction step asks something of the Practitioner and of the Committee, and the Committee spends that time where a referral could follow. Elsewhere a report, an offer of help, and an open invitation to correct the record serve the Practitioner better than a response period does.

**5.5 Validating an observation, and disposition.** Section 5.4 validates the instrument. This section validates the observation, which is a different question: an instrument that performs well across a population can still be wrong about a particular chart, and observations feed referrals.

**This section applies where § 5.4.2 requires it.** An observation reported under § 5.4.1 in none of the cases stated there is not dispositioned under this section.

**No observation may be dispositioned as Individual until the Practitioner has been given the opportunity to correct it.** The Practitioner has [ten] business days from the date of the notice under (a)(5). The Committee may correct or withdraw the observation accordingly, and records what it did and on which ground.

**This subsection is quality control on the audit.** Section 5.4 validates the instrument and nothing else validates the observation. The errors this subsection catches are disproportionately the errors that produced the rate which selected the Practitioner in the first place, and a referral resting on a chart the audit got wrong is worth less than no referral at all.

This is a record-correction step, not a proceeding. **It is not professional review. It determines nothing about competence, imposes nothing on anyone, and does not limit the PRC's independent review of any matter later referred.** It asks one question — did the audit accurately identify a departure from the standard — and it does not ask whether the care was appropriate, which is the PRC's question under Article VIII of its Charter. Nothing in this subsection entitles a Practitioner to argue the clinical merits before this Committee, and the Committee does not hear them.

**(a)(1) Grounds.** An observation is corrected or withdrawn on any of the following. They are stated in the order in which they arise in practice, and the notice under (a)(5) states them in the same order, so that the Practitioner spends the response period where it can do something.

- **(i) Attribution.** The care was not furnished by the Practitioner; the resident was not on the Practitioner's panel at the material time; the Practitioner was covering and the decision belonged to the treating clinician; or the condition to which the standard applies was managed by another clinician under an arrangement documented in the record.

- **(ii) Population.** The resident is not within the population to which the standard applies. The diagnosis is absent, coded in error, superseded by later testing, or established by data the standard does not accept. **A resident wrongly included is removed from the numerator and from the denominator, and (a)(7) applies.**

- **(iii) The departure did not occur.** The medication was ordered, the assessment was performed, the communication was made. A medication administration record, an active order, a transmitted document, or an equivalent record establishes it.

- **(iv) Retrieval.** The Practitioner's own entry satisfying the standard exists in the record and the instrument did not reach it — a note held under a different encounter, a scanned or misfiled document, an entry in a part of the note the instrument does not parse, or a proper addendum made before notification. **This ground is available whatever the standard requires, because what it produces is exactly what is required.** It is expected to fire often against standards designated under § 4.7(e), because an accounting entry is frequently a short phrase — *"discontinued per cardiology, prior GI bleed"*, *"held per cardiology pending EGD 9/12"* — of the kind an instrument searching for a structured field or a stated reason will pass over.

- **(v) Timing.** The care was furnished before the date on which the standard may support an individual finding under § 5.7; or the record was examined before the point at which the standard required the entry.

- **(vi) Other information in the record satisfying the standard as written,** including an entry by another clinician, **where the standard is not designated under § 4.7(e).** Where it is designated, or where the entry examined is a periodic review entry under § 4.7(f), this ground is not available and (a)(4)(i) governs.

**(a)(2) The state of the record governs.** The observation states the date on which the record was examined. What corrects an observation is information that existed in the record before the Practitioner was notified. **For an observation first listed in a review report, the Practitioner was notified on the date of that report.** **The test is whether the required substance existed in the record in a form sufficient to satisfy the applicable standard, not whether the instrument retrieved it from the expected location.**

**(a)(3) Grounds (i), (ii) and (iii) are not affected by the form of the standard.** Whether a resident is the Practitioner's, whether the resident has the condition, and whether the drug was in fact prescribed are questions of fact about the audit. However a standard is drafted under § 4.7, it cannot make an observation correct that was attached to the wrong clinician or the wrong resident.

**(a)(4) What does not correct an observation.** Stated expressly, because a Practitioner who does not know it in advance will spend the response period assembling material that cannot help, and will reasonably resent having been invited to.

- **(i) Where the standard is designated under § 4.7(e), or the entry examined is a periodic review entry under § 4.7(f), a clinical fact documented only by another clinician does not satisfy it.** A consultant's note, a laboratory value standing alone, or a conversation recorded by another discipline may establish that a reason existed somewhere. It does not show that the treating Practitioner accounted for the element, which is what the standard asks.

  **But the accounting is a low bar and the Practitioner should be told what it is.** An entry in the Practitioner's own note attributing the decision — *"SGLT2 inhibitor discontinued by cardiology, recurrent mycotic infections"* — or recording a pending disposition with what resolves it, satisfies the standard and withdraws the observation under ground (iv). **The Practitioner is not asked to justify another clinician's decision or to repeat its reasoning.** What does not satisfy the standard is a note that is silent on the element. **This subsection corrects the audit. It does not relax the standard.**

- **(ii) Reasoning supplied after notification.** Where a Practitioner states that the care was furnished and the reasoning existed but was not written down, the departure from the standard stands. **The explanation is nonetheless recorded, and is transmitted with any referral under § 6.3.** It is relevant to what the departure was, and it travels.

- **(iii) An amendment to the record made after notification.** See (a)(6).

- **(iv) An argument that the care was appropriate.** That question belongs to the PRC under Article VIII of its Charter, and this Committee neither asks nor answers it.

**(a)(5) The notice states all of this before the response period runs.** The notice sent under this subsection shall state, for each observation, the resident and date of service, what the standard required, what the audit found, whether the apparent departure is in the decision or in its documentation or in both, the grounds available under (a)(1) in the order there stated, and the matters in (a)(4) that will not correct it. **The Committee shall not invite a response on grounds it will not accept.** Where § 5.4.2(a) applies, the notice also lists the observations standing in the review report that opened the follow-up, and the response reaches them.

**(a)(6) Amending a record that should be amended.** **Nothing in this subsection discourages it.** Where care was furnished and the record does not reflect it, completing the record is owed to the resident and to the facility, and that obligation exists independently of any audit. An amendment shall be a proper addendum — separately dated, attributed, with the original entry intact — and never an alteration of an existing entry. **Improper alteration of an existing entry may independently warrant referral under § 6.7.** An amendment made after notification is disclosed in the Practitioner's response and recorded. It does not change the observation, and it does satisfy the standard in subsequent audit cycles.

**(a)(7) Rates are recomputed before they are used.** **No rate supports selection under § 5.6(a) or a pattern criterion under § 6.2 until observations withdrawn on grounds (a)(1)(i) or (ii) have been removed from both numerator and denominator and the rate recomputed.** Section 5.6(a) permits a crude selection threshold on the express ground that the chart, not the denominator, answers the case-mix question. This subsection is where the chart answers it. A pattern criterion applied to an uncorrected rate rests on the very error the chart review exists to find.

**(a)(8) What the correction step teaches about the instrument.** Every ground sustained is recorded against the measure and reported to the analytics function under § 2.6.

- (i) Where grounds (i), (ii) or (iv) are sustained at or above a rate set in the audit register, the measure is revalidated under § 5.4 before it supports any further individual finding.
- (ii) **Where the same ground is sustained across multiple Practitioners on the same measure, the measure or the instrument is the problem and not the Practitioners.** It routes to standards revision or to the audit question under § 4.7(d) and § 5.9.
- (iii) Where ground (vi) is repeatedly raised and repeatedly unavailable because the standard is designated under § 4.7(e), the Committee shall consider at the next standards review whether the designation is earning its place on that measure. Designation is opt-in under § 4.7(e) and is not a fixed rule, and this is the evidence on which it is revisited. **A pattern of Practitioners producing a consultant's documented reason and nothing of their own is evidence about the education, not only about the standard** — the accounting entry is a small thing to write, and a widespread failure to write it usually means nobody explained what it is.

**(a)(9) The observation states whether the apparent departure is in the decision, in its documentation, or in both.** A decision that was made and not documented is a departure from a standard written under § 4.7, and it is a different departure from a documented decision that did not satisfy the standard. The PRC needs that distinction at screening, and this Committee is the one positioned to record it.

**(b) Disposition.** Every observation to which this section applies receives one of three dispositions, recorded:

- **Systems** — attributable to process, staffing, tooling, workflow, or the standard itself. Retained by the Committee, feeds standards revision and the education agenda, individual attribution not carried forward.
- **Individual** — the observation is validated as an individual finding, and **referral under §§ 6.1–6.5 is determined by the referral criteria adopted under § 6.2.**
- **No action** — the observation does not indicate a departure from an applicable standard, or has been corrected or withdrawn under (a).

An observation dispositioned Systems or No action does not become an individual finding and is not referred.

**This subsection classifies the observation. It does not itself state what follows from an Individual disposition.** What follows is determined by the criteria in effect under § 6.2, which are where the conditions of a referral belong and where any qualification on it belongs with them — including the one this Charter itself requires at § 5.9, that a criterion which would refer a large fraction of the Practitioners it applies to routes to standards revision, to the audit question, or to the education agenda rather than to referral. **Stating the consequence here as well would put two instruments in a position to contradict each other**, and the one that can be calibrated without amendment is the schedule.

**(b)(1) The safety question is put at every disposition.** Before dispositioning observations against a clinical standard, and at every cycle summary under § 5.4.1(e), **the Chair shall ask whether any member holds a concern that the observations reflect a departure from the standard of care rather than from documentation**, and the minutes record that the question was put and that it was answered.

**The people executing this audit are clinicians and will form clinical impressions from what they see. That is expected and it is not improper** — it is why the audit is worth running. What this Committee may not do is convert such an impression into its own finding, because testing it requires a discipline-matched panel reading the charts against the standard of care, which is the PRC's function and needs the PRC's process.

**A member who holds such a concern refers under § 6.7(c), as a person.** The Committee makes no referral on that basis and takes no view on whether one should be made. **Asking the question aloud is what makes that route usable**: it turns an act of individual initiative into an ordinary item of business, and the social cost of raising a concern about a colleague is otherwise what determines whether it is ever raised.

**Where § 5.5(a)(9) records that the apparent departure is in the decision rather than in its documentation, the question under this subsection is put with that on the record.**

**(b)(1.1) A chart that meets the standard may still raise a concern.** The audit instrument carries a flag with which the clinician examining a chart may mark it for the Chair's attention where the chart meets the standard but the examiner holds a concern about the care it records. **Flagged charts are brought before the members when the question under (b)(1) is put, whether or not they produced an observation.**

A flag is not an observation. It is not dispositioned under (b), is not notified to the Practitioner under (a), counts toward no criterion under § 6.2, and supports no referral under §§ 6.1–6.5. What may follow from it is a referral under § 6.7(c) by a member who holds the concern, as a person, or nothing. Because the chart met the standard, any such referral rests on a concern independent of the standard, as § 6.7(c) requires. A flag forms part of the protected record under § 8.1(b), and § 9.6 applies to it as to any determination concerning an identified Practitioner.

**A standard measures whether a decision was documented, and a note can meet it while recording a decision no clinician would defend.** Without this subsection, the question under (b)(1) would reach only the charts that failed, and the chart that passed on paper would never be discussed.

**(c)(1) Every Practitioner notified under (a) is told what became of every observation.** The close-out notice states, for each observation, its disposition under (b), which grounds under (a)(1) were sustained, and where the applicable standard and its examples of conforming documentation are found in the open record under § 8.1(a). **A Practitioner all of whose observations were withdrawn is told so in terms, and told that nothing further follows.**

**(c)(2) A close-out notice imposes nothing.** It states no obligation, requires no response, sets no date, names no monitor, and creates no record of remediation. **It is not a Quality Intervention under § 8.2 of the PRC Charter, is not education directed at an individual, and is not a disposition of any kind concerning the Practitioner.** It reports the outcome of an audit and points to material that is open to everyone. Section 6.5 is unaffected: the Committee still imposes nothing on any Practitioner at any point.

**(c)(3) Why this is required.** Without it, a Practitioner's entire contact with the audit program is an allegation followed by silence. And the referral schedule under § 6.2 escalates on repetition after notice. **That logic requires that notice was in fact given** — not that a standard was disseminated to everyone, but that this Practitioner was told what this audit found in these charts. The review report under § 5.4.1 is the first such record and the close-out notice the second, and without them the escalation rests on a fiction.

**(c)(4) The close-out notice is issued whether or not a referral criterion is met,** and where a referral is made under §§ 6.1–6.5 it states that fact and nothing about what the PRC may do.

**(c)(5) Support, offered and not imposed.** The review report under § 5.4.1 and the close-out notice each carry an offer of support on the standard to which the observations related: the material in the open record, and **an opportunity, on request, for a discussion with a clinician who practises in the relevant area, a targeted teaching session, or case discussion.**

The offer is subject to all of the following, and is not the Committee's to make on any other terms.

- **(i) It is made identically to every Practitioner who receives a review report or a close-out**, whatever the disposition and whether or not any observation stood. Its presence in a notice therefore says nothing about the Practitioner who receives it.
- **(ii) It is taken up only on request.** Nothing occurs unless the Practitioner asks, and there is no consequence of any kind for not asking.
- **(iii) Whether it is taken up is not recorded against the Practitioner, is not a factor in any disposition or in any criterion under § 6.2, and is not transmitted under § 6.3.** The moment uptake is counted, the offer has become an imposition in substance.
- **(iv) It is delivered by the education function under § 2.8**, by a person who did not participate in the examination of that Practitioner's charts or the disposition of their observations and who holds no Employment Authority over that Practitioner. That person executes the undertaking required by § 8.6 before receiving any individually attributed information, and **does not disclose to any person in their management reporting line that a particular Practitioner was offered or received support.** The rule bites on the attribution and not on the activity.
- **(v) It is not assigned, is not tracked to completion, and is not recorded in any system that reports individual completion to Management or feeds a performance process.** Where the same material exists as a general offering in a learning management system, a Practitioner may take it there as anyone may, and nothing connects that to the audit. **Uptake would otherwise be counted by the tooling rather than by anyone's decision, and the safeguard at (iii) would be defeated without a decision being made.**
- **(vi) Any record of delivery belongs to the open education record under § 8.1(a).** It is not a record of any observation or disposition, and is not individually attributed in anything this Committee holds.
- **(vii) It is not a Quality Intervention** under § 8.2 of the PRC Charter. It rests on no finding about the Practitioner's competence, imposes nothing, has no duration, no exit criteria and no monitor, and creates no record of remediation.

**The offer discharges nothing.** **It carries no obligation to accept it. It does not diminish the standard, the finding, or the Practitioner's own professional obligation to practise competently and to close a gap once aware of it**, and it does not affect any person's ability to refer under § 6.7 or the operation of § 5.11. **This Charter does not tell a Practitioner that no action is required of them**, and no notice issued under this Article shall say so.

**Why this is consistent with § 6.5.** What makes an instrument a professional review instrument is that it is **imposed**, that it follows a **finding** that care fell below standard, and that it is **monitored against exit criteria**. An offer of open material to a named person is none of those. **Directing material at a person is not the same as imposing something on them**, and that distinction is what lets a quality programme help someone without first determining anything about them.

**Why it needs to exist.** A Practitioner newly working in a clinical area will predictably have gaps, and **the audit is designed to find them.** Without this subsection the only route to individual support runs through a professional review, which is a substantial instrument to reach for where what is needed is a conversation.

**(c)(6) The APC decides what, if anything, reaches their supervising physician.** Where the Practitioner is an APC, the review report and the close-out notice each state that the APC may share them with their assigned supervising physician, or ask that physician for help without sharing it. **For the second, each includes a separate page the APC may forward**: a request for help naming only the clinical area of the standard, with the review guide under § 4.5.1, and nothing from the protected record.

**The APC shares, and the Committee does not.** The Committee transmits nothing under this subsection and neither learns nor records whether the APC shared anything or what. Not sharing carries nothing.

**(d) Commendation.**

**(d)(1) What it is.** Where the audit examines a chart and finds care that meets an improvement target designated under § 4.4(b), or documentation the Committee would adopt as an example of conforming documentation under § 4.3, the Committee may record a commendation attributed to the Practitioner. **A commendation records what the chart shows. It is not a disposition under (b), and it determines nothing about the Practitioner's competence**, favorably or otherwise.

**(d)(2) Selection.** Charts may be selected for this purpose under § 5.6(a), including by selecting facilities or Practitioners whose rate on a measure falls well below that of their comparison group. The thresholds are recorded in the audit register. Selection for this purpose carries no consequence, as selection under § 5.6(a) carries none.

**(d)(3) Notice.** The Practitioner is told of the commendation in the review report, stating the chart, the date of service, and what the chart showed. Nothing is required in response.

**(d)(4) It offsets nothing.** A commendation is not a factor in any disposition under (b), in any criterion under § 6.2, or in follow-up under § 5.11, and is not transmitted under § 6.3. **A finding on one chart is not answered by excellence on another.** The Practitioner may supply a commendation to the PRC.

**(d)(5) Record.** A commendation is part of the protected record under § 8.1(b). The Committee enters it in no employment or performance record. A commendation is a determination concerning an identified Practitioner, and § 2.5 applies to it.

**(d)(6) Use as an example.** Where a commended chart is to become an example of conforming documentation, a clean version prepared for that purpose enters the open record under § 8.1(a), de-identified as to the resident and, unless the Practitioner consents in writing to be named, as to the Practitioner. The commendation and the chart remain protected.

**(d)(7) The Committee does not publish a commendation.** Public recognition is governed by § 7.6 and draws on nothing in the protected record.

**(e) Clinical comment.**

**(e)(1) What it is.** A clinician who examines a chart may write a short comment to the Practitioner on the management of that resident's care, where the chart raises a clinical question the standard does not reach: whether a medication's indication warrants it, whether an alternative was tried, whether a dose reduction was given up early. **A comment is advice from a colleague, and it is the view of the clinician who signs it.** It is not an act of the Committee, is not an observation, is not dispositioned, and determines nothing about the Practitioner's competence. It travels in the review report.

**(e)(2) Written on request by a physician member.** The examining clinician may ask that the comment be written instead by a physician member of the Committee, stating the question that prompted the request. **Where the comment would concern a physician's entry and the examining clinician is not a physician, the request is required.** The comment is then written by a physician member practising in the Practitioner's service line, primary care or psychiatry, who is not excluded under § 2.5 and not recused under § 9.6. The member reads the chart and may decline to write a comment. The request is part of the protected record and is not sent to the Practitioner. The review report waits up to [ten] business days from the cycle summary for a requested comment, and issues without it after that; a comment written later is sent on its own.

**(e)(3) It counts toward nothing.** A comment is not a factor in any disposition under (b), in any criterion under § 6.2, or in follow-up under § 5.11, and is not transmitted under § 6.3. A comment made again at a follow-up look adds nothing to any count. **Where the clinician holds a concern that the care falls below the standard of care, the instrument is the flag under (b)(1.1), and not a comment.**

**(e)(4) Record.** A comment and any request for one are part of the protected record under § 8.1(b). The Committee enters a comment in no employment or performance record. Section 8.4 applies to it, and §§ 2.5 and 9.6 apply to the person who writes it.

**(e)(5) Why it exists.** A standard measures whether a decision was documented. Some of what most needs teaching lies in the decision itself: an indication that does not warrant the drug, an alternative never tried, a reduction reversed at the first sign of difficulty. Such a decision is neither a departure from a written standard nor, ordinarily, a departure from the standard of care. **Without this subsection the program could answer it only with silence or with a referral to the PRC**, and neither is what a colleague would do.

**5.6 Selection, denominators, and comparison.** Numbers are used in this program in two distinct ways, and what is required of them differs.

- (a) **Selection.** A rate, a threshold, or an event may be used to decide which Practitioners or which charts are examined. **Selection is not a finding.** It carries no consequence, records nothing about the care in question, and requires no comparison group, no risk adjustment, and no notice period. A threshold used for selection may be absolute and crude. **The case-mix question is answered by examining the chart, not by constructing the denominator** — which is the better place to answer it, because a chart shows why a rate is what it is and a denominator only shows that it is.
- (b) **A rate** is expressed over the population to which the standard applied. **Raw counts are not used**, and a rate is never itself an individual finding.
- (c) Comparison groups are constructed by the analytics function and adopted by the Committee on the terms provided in the Audit Referral Policy. A comparison group is defined by the characteristics that plausibly affect the measure, ordinarily including discipline, setting, and panel size, and where relevant acuity, length of stay, or facility. **The definition of each group is recorded in the standards register with the standard it attaches to, and is open under § 8.1.1. Its constitution in a given cycle — which Practitioners composed it and what their rates were — is recorded in the audit register and is protected.**
- (d) **Where an adequate comparison cannot be constructed, no pattern criterion applies to that measure for that Practitioner.** Small disciplines, uncommon settings, and small panels produce numbers that cannot distinguish a real difference from noise. This subsection limits pattern criteria only. Referral on a single occasion, referral on repetition after notice, and referral under § 6.7 are unaffected.
- (e) Selection thresholds are adopted by the Committee, recorded in the audit register, and reviewed with the Audit Referral Policy.

**5.7 Notice before a standard counts.** A standard takes effect on the date stated at adoption.

**Only care furnished on or after [sixty] days following documented dissemination may support an individual finding that counts toward a referral criterion.** The period runs against the care, not against the audit. A standard disseminated on 1 January does not reach care furnished on 10 January merely because the audit ran in April.

The Committee may set a longer period at adoption, and ordinarily should where a standard asks for a change in practice rather than a change in documentation. **The record of adoption states the period for each standard**, so that it is chosen for that standard rather than inherited.

Sixty days is the default because it is what a documentation standard needs: long enough for a Practitioner to encounter the residents the standard applies to at least once in the ordinary visit cycle, and for the entry to be made at that visit. **A standard asking for a change in practice needs longer**, because the decision opportunity has to arise as well.

**5.8 The criterion is clinical, not economic.** Nearly every clinical measure carries financial consequence for the Entity under one arrangement or another — shared savings, capitation, bundled payment, facility rating. A rule excluding measures with financial linkage would exclude avoidable hospitalization, which is at once the most clinically important measure in this population and the most financially consequential, and would defeat the program. The protection is therefore stated as a principle rather than as a list of prohibited measures.

- (a) **Every standard states its clinical basis at adoption, without reference to any payment arrangement.** A measure whose justification is that it produces revenue or avoids cost is not a clinical standard and is not adopted.
- (b) **Volume is never itself the criterion for an individual finding.** The audit may select the Practitioners or the charts it examines by rate, threshold, or event under § 5.6(a), but an individual finding rests on whether the documented decision satisfied the applicable minimum expectation. A Practitioner whose care satisfied the standard generates no finding, whatever the count. Whether the decision was clinically appropriate is not this Committee's question — it belongs to the PRC under Article VIII of its Charter.
- (c) **A measure drawn from a payer or rating program is adopted by its clinical content, not by the program's threshold.** A threshold, where the Committee wants one, is an organizational performance target maintained by the analytics function under § 2.6. It is not a standard under Article IV and is never attributed to an individual.
- (d) **A program's denominator and its exclusions are likewise selection artifacts and not clinical criteria.** A case that such a program counts, but which satisfies the applicable Committee standard, produces no individual finding. An antipsychotic prescribed for well-supported bipolar disorder with the rationale documented satisfies a standard drafted under § 4.7, whether or not the rating program's denominator happens to exclude that diagnosis. Where a standard cannot distinguish that case, the standard is the problem — see § 4.7(d) and § 5.9.

**5.9 If a large fraction would be referred.** Where a criterion would refer a large fraction of the Practitioners it applies to, that is a systems condition. **It routes to standards revision, to the audit question, or to the education agenda under § 3.1(b), not to mass referral.** Widespread nonconformity ordinarily means the standard asks for the wrong thing, the audit question does not measure what the standard requires, or the standard was never adequately taught — and the remedy is whichever of those it turns out to be.

**A gap concentrated among Practitioners newly working in a clinical area is a condition of onboarding and preparation, and the remedy is a programme rather than anything directed at an individual.** That such a gap is predictable is a reason to look for it, which is the purpose of this Committee's work, and not a reason to attribute it.

**The same holds at the review report.** Where the review reports in a cycle record observations on a measure for more than the fraction and number of Practitioners stated in the Audit Referral Policy, **the condition is a systems condition under this section.** No follow-up look under § 5.11.2 opens on those reports, § 5.4.2(c) does not run on that measure in that cycle, the reports say so, and the measure is re-measured under § 5.11.6 after whichever remedy the Committee chooses. The ordinary remedy for a measure that most Practitioners do not yet meet is the teaching.

**This subsection does not preclude referral of an individual case that independently satisfies a criterion under which a single individual finding refers, or referral under § 6.7.** That a practice is widespread does not make any particular instance of it acceptable.

**5.10 Controls against satisfying a standard without meeting it.** A standard requiring documentation can be satisfied by documentation. A stored phrase inserted into every note will pass an audit indefinitely and mean nothing, and neither the audit instrument nor a reviewer reading the note alone can detect it. Three controls are therefore standing requirements of the audit program.

- (a) **Cross-check against the record.** Where a documented entry asserts a fact recorded elsewhere — an active medication and dose, a laboratory value, an order — the audit shall test agreement between the two. Divergence between what a note asserts and what the record shows is itself an observation, and is the principal defense against a stale stored phrase.

- (b) **Sample the charts that passed.** Not fewer than [once each quarter] the Committee shall review a sample of charts that satisfied a standard, selected at random, for whether the documentation reflects a decision that was actually made. Auditing only the failures is how a standard quietly becomes a macro. Observations under this subsection are recorded as systems conditions, open no follow-up look, and are not referred.

- (c) **Thin entries.** An entry may satisfy a standard and still leave the element's status unresolvable by the next clinician to read the chart. *"SGLT2 inhibitor discontinued by cardiology"* is a settled disposition and satisfies a standard designated under § 4.7(e); it records who decided and nothing else. The audit records such an entry as **thin**.

  **A thin entry is recorded as a systems condition and is never an individual finding.** It opens no follow-up look. It is not referred, is not attributed to a Practitioner in any report, and no criterion under § 6.2 counts it. To record it as a departure would rebuild through the audit the very requirement — that the treating Practitioner supply the reasoning behind another clinician's decision — that § 4.7(e) deliberately declines to impose.

  **What it is for is the education agenda and the review under § 4.6.** A measure generating mostly thin entries is evidence about the standard and about the teaching, not about the Practitioners: either the designation asks for something the workflow cannot produce, or no one has shown people what a useful entry looks like. Thin-entry rates are reported to the Committee with each cycle and in the report under § 7.4. Where the Committee concludes that thin entries on a measure carry real risk, the remedy is to designate the standard to require both an attribution and a reason under § 4.7(c) at the next review — not to refer anyone.

**5.11 Follow-up on a review report or a closed individual finding.**

**5.11.1 Why this section exists.** The audit programme measures by campaign: a measure is swept across the organization, and the next sweep addresses a different measure. **An observation reported under § 5.4.1, or a finding closed under § 5.5(c), would therefore never be examined again**, because the interval before that standard is next swept may be years. Every part of this Charter that turns on what happens after a review report or a close-out — the escalation of the criteria under § 6.2 on repetition after notice, the offer of support under § 5.5(c)(5), and the reporting under § 2.6 and § 7.4 on whether an intervention moved its measure — depends on somebody looking again.

**5.11.2 The follow-up look.** The Committee shall examine a sample of a Practitioner's charts against the same standard, **for care furnished in the follow-up window**, independently of the audit plan and of whatever measure is then being swept, where:

- (a) a review report under § 5.4.1 records an observation standing, not checked under § 5.4.2, on a standard designated under § 4.4(a) whose entry in the standards register names Criterion B, on care that §§ 4.5, 5.7 and 8.7 permit to support an individual finding, in a cycle to which the review-report provision of § 5.9 has not applied, and where no observation on that standard in the same report was referred; or
- (b) an individual finding on a standard whose entry in the standards register names Criterion B is closed out under § 5.5(c) without referral, other than a finding validated under § 5.4.2(a) from the review report that opened a follow-up look.

**An observation corrected or withdrawn before the look is conducted does not stand for the purpose of (a).** Where none stands, the look does not run and the Practitioner is told so.

**The follow-up window opens [forty-five] days after the review report or the close-out and runs for [ninety] days.** The look is conducted promptly after the window closes.

**The period before the window opens is time given to the Practitioner to act, and no care furnished in it is examined.** It is set so that a Practitioner who takes up the offer of support under § 5.5(c)(5) has time to request it, to have it arranged, to attend it, and to begin applying it before any of their care is measured. **It is a fixed period and does not depend on whether the offer was taken up** — § 5.5(c)(5)(iii) forbids uptake being recorded or counted, so the period is set for the Practitioner who uses the offer, and the Practitioner who does not is neither advantaged nor disadvantaged by its length.

**The window is ninety days because that is two to three chronic care visits for each resident the standard applies to.** What the follow-up asks is whether practice changed, and a shorter window answers only whether a note was written.

The Committee may set a longer opening period or a longer window for a standard at adoption, and ordinarily should where the standard asks a change in practice rather than a change in documentation. Both are recorded with the standard.

The sample size is set in the audit register, and **the Coordinator diaries the follow-up under § 2.7 and brings it to the Committee when it falls due.** The trigger cannot depend on anyone remembering it across a change of audit topic, which is the failure this section exists to prevent.

**A follow-up look is selection under § 5.6(a) and nothing more.** A recorded observation or finding is a permissible basis for deciding whose charts are examined; the examination carries no consequence of its own; and any observation it produces is corrected and dispositioned under § 5.5 where § 5.4.2 applies to it, and is otherwise reported under § 5.4.1.

**5.11.3 It runs once.**

- (a) **Where the follow-up produces no individual finding, and no individual finding on that standard has been made for care furnished in the follow-up window by any examination, the matter closes**, the Practitioner is told so, and no further targeted examination of that Practitioner on that measure occurs **in respect of the report or finding that occasioned it.** They return to the general population of the audit programme. **A later observation or finding on the same measure is a new matter**, reported under § 5.4.1 or closed out under § 5.5(c), and followed up under § 5.11.2 in the ordinary course. What this subsection ends is the look attached to the report or finding that occasioned it, and not the Committee's ability to run the same sequence again on a different one.
- (b) Where it produces an individual finding, that finding is dispositioned under § 5.5(b), and what follows from it is determined by the criteria adopted under § 6.2. **It is the occasion those criteria contemplate where a first occasion survives under § 5.4.2(a), or where the follow-up was opened by a close-out**, and this Charter does not state the consequence for it any more than § 5.5(b) does for any other finding.
- (c) **A follow-up look is not repeated and is not extended.** An examination directed at a named Practitioner that recurs without limit is a monitoring status. This Committee has no authority to impose one — see § 6.5 — and it would accomplish by administration what §§ 8.2 and 8.6 of the PRC Charter require process to accomplish.

**5.11.4 It is disclosed in advance.** The review report or the close-out notice that opens the follow-up states that a follow-up look will occur, on which standard, and approximately when. **A Practitioner is entitled to know that their charts will be examined again and why.** The disclosure is also what makes the offer under § 5.5(c)(5) coherent rather than empty: what was found, what help is available, and when the Committee will look again.

**5.11.5 Where there is nothing to examine.** Where the Practitioner has no chart within the population to which the standard applies during the follow-up window, there is nothing to look at and the matter closes under § 5.11.3(a). **A matter is not held open awaiting a case.**

**5.11.6 Systems dispositions are re-measured too.** Where a Systems disposition, a systems condition recorded under § 5.4.1(e) or § 5.9, or the review of a measure whose entry in the standards register is None led to a standards revision, an education intervention, or a change in tooling, the audit plan shall provide for re-measurement of that measure at an interval the Committee sets. **A programme that intervenes and does not measure again has no basis for reporting that it improves care**, which is what § 2.6 and § 7.4 require it to report.

## Article VI — Referral to the Peer Review Committee

**6.1 What this Committee refers.** An individual finding under § 5.1, concerning the care furnished by an identified Practitioner. This Article governs the Committee's own route to the PRC. It is one of several routes, it is not the principal one, and it does not describe the limits of what the PRC may review — see § 6.7.

**6.2 Criteria.** Referral criteria are maintained in a written schedule — **the Audit Referral Policy** — adopted by the Committee, in effect on the PRC's concurrence under § 11.5 of its Charter, and reviewed annually. Each revision takes effect in the same way. That name is used for it in both Charters. They are not set out in this Charter, so that they can be calibrated against actual findings without amendment.

**6.3 What accompanies a referral.** The individual finding, the underlying data, any information the Practitioner submitted under § 5.5(a) and which grounds were sustained, what the Committee did with the submission, and the disposition history. **Not the Committee's deliberations.**

**Where the referral follows a second finding on the same standard, it carries the chain the escalation rests on:** the review report under § 5.4.1 or the close-out notice under § 5.5(c) that opened the follow-up, with the offer of support, any commendation and any clinical comment removed, the disclosure of the follow-up look made under § 5.11.4, the fact and date of the follow-up conducted under § 5.11.2, and, where a review report opened it, the disposition under § 5.5(b) of the observations that report recorded. **A referral resting on repetition after notice is not capable of being screened without them**, and the PRC screens for exactly that under § 4.4 of its Charter.

**Nothing concerning support under § 5.5(c)(5) is transmitted**, including whether it was offered, taken up, or declined, **and no clinical comment under § 5.5(e) is transmitted.**

**6.4 A referral is not a PRC finding.** A referral establishes only that an individual finding was made under § 5.1 and that a referral criterion was met. It does not establish that care fell below the standard of care. The PRC screens every referral, makes its own findings on its own record, and is not bound by this Committee's view.

**6.5 The Committee's involvement ends at referral.** A referral the PRC returns under § 4.4 of its Charter for want of the record § 6.3 requires is completed and sent again. Otherwise the Committee takes no part in the review, receives no report of its outcome beyond the de-identified aggregate under § 7.2, and imposes nothing on the Practitioner at any point.

**6.6 Referral is not the only route.** Any Practitioner or employee may refer a matter directly to the PRC under § 4.1(b) of its Charter. A referral from this Committee carries no special weight and is one source among several.

**6.7 The standard of care is not displaced.** Except as § 7.7 provides for a person helping a Practitioner who asked for help, nothing in this Charter limits what may be referred to, or reviewed by, the PRC.

- (a) **A departure from the standard of care is reviewable whether or not any standard adopted under Article IV addresses it.** Most of clinical practice cannot be reduced to written standards, and no organization writes a policy for every scenario. The standard reasonably expected of a Practitioner of that discipline in that setting is the measure, and it exists independently of this Committee.
- (b) **The absence of an adopted standard is not a defense and does not imply that conduct is acceptable.** Nothing in the Entity's standards program shall be read to make permissible whatever it does not address.
- (c) **Any person — including any member of this Committee, any person executing an audit, and any member of the analytics function — may refer a matter directly to the PRC as a person**, subject to § 7.7. Such a referral is not an individual finding, and §§ 4.5, 5.3, 5.5, 5.6, and 5.7 do not apply to it.

  **This route carries no threshold, no adopted standard, and no notice period.** A concern about the standard of care or about patient safety reaches the PRC directly, from a colleague, a facility, a family, or from anyone who sees it in the course of an audit. The audit is asymmetric by nature: over-response leaves an event that can be found, and under-response leaves an absence that cannot, and an unconditioned route is what answers that asymmetry. **The route works only where the question is actually put**, which is why § 5.5(b)(1) requires the Chair to put it at every disposition rather than leaving it to be volunteered.

  **A direct referral shall not be used to circumvent the requirements applicable to referrals arising from the audit.** Where the matter came to the referrer's attention through an audit, the referral must rest on a basis independent of the Practitioner's failure to satisfy a Committee standard or audit criterion — that is, on a concern about the standard of care or about patient safety. **The same facts may support both. What must be independent is the basis, not the facts.** A chart showing atrial fibrillation with no anticoagulation and no rationale may fail a Committee standard and independently raise a standard-of-care concern, and the second is a proper ground for direct referral even where the first is not yet available. The PRC screens for this under § 4.7 of its Charter.
- (d) Compliance with a standard adopted under Article IV does not establish that care met the standard of care.

## Article VII — Interfaces

**7.1 Primary care and psychiatry committees.** Both contribute to standards development and to the education agenda, on material circulated to them for comment, which is open under § 8.1(a). **Neither receives the Committee's internal deliberative material, nor any observation or individual finding attributed to a Practitioner, nor participates in any disposition under § 5.5, nor takes any part in peer review.** Neither is chartered as a peer review body, and the boundary in this section is what makes that unnecessary.

**7.2 From the PRC.** The Committee receives de-identified aggregate reporting [quarterly], and systems findings referred under Article XI of the PRC Charter. Neither identifies a Practitioner.

**7.3 Systems conditions found late.** A systems condition the PRC identifies at any stage of a matter, including after a hearing, comes to the Committee under Article XI of the PRC Charter on the same terms as one identified in review, and without individual attribution.

**7.4 To executive leadership.** The Chair reports the Committee's work quarterly, at the meeting with the Chief Medical Officer and the Chief Operating Officer under § 15.10 of the PRC Charter: standards adopted, education delivered, audit cycles run, review reports issued and corrections received by ground, dispositions by category, **follow-up looks conducted under § 5.11 and their outcomes in aggregate**, **systems conditions transmitted under § 7.5 and whether they were acted on**, **each occasion on which referral on a measure was withheld under § 5.9, or follow-up looks were withheld at the review report, and what was done instead**, each determination under § 6.6 of the Audit Referral Policy, what the analytics function shows about whether interventions moved their measures, the results of each audit cycle under § 7.4.1, and, when adopted, the annual audit plan under § 3.1(f). The Chief Executive Officer receives the same report. **The follow-up figures are the direct evidence of whether the close-out and the support offered under § 5.5(c)(5) correct what they are meant to correct**, and are the answer when anyone asks whether the programme does anything. No Practitioner is identifiable in the report.

**7.4.1 Audit results, for the enterprise and by territory.** **The results of each audit cycle are reported for the Entity as a whole and for the territory of each Regional Director of Clinical Operations and Excellence.** Each result is a single figure per measure for all Practitioners the measure applies to, physicians and APCs together.

A territory's result on a measure is not reported where fewer than [thirty] charts or fewer than [ten] Practitioners stand behind it, or where one Practitioner accounts for more than [forty] percent of its charts, and it is shown as too few to report. No territory is designated an outlier; the results are reported as they are.

**Distribution of the results within the Entity is for the Chief Medical Officer and the Chief Operating Officer to decide**, and the Committee does not distribute them. Because they identify no Practitioner and are intended to circulate, they are part of the open record under § 8.1(a) and are not represented as confidential.

**7.5 To operations.** Where an observation is dispositioned Systems under § 5.5(b), where a systems condition is recorded under § 5.4.1(e) or § 5.10 or identified under § 5.9, or where one is referred to the Committee under § 7.2 or § 7.3, **the Committee transmits the condition to the person or function able to act on it.**

**What travels is the condition and not the case.** The transmittal describes the process, staffing, tooling, workflow, or standard condition identified, and the measure and setting in which it was found. **It identifies no Practitioner, carries no observation, no individual finding and no disposition, and is accompanied by no part of the protected record under § 8.1(b).**

**A transmittal under this section is open-record material under § 8.1(a) and shall not be represented as confidential.** A description of a standing order that produces unnecessary testing is operational information. Treating it as protected would be inaccurate, and would keep it from the people able to act on it.

**The Committee directs no operational change.** It has no authority over Management or over a contracted facility, and nothing in this Charter confers any. What it does is state the condition, say what it believes the condition is producing, and record the transmittal and its date.

**Where a condition transmitted under this section has not been acted on, the Committee records that and reports the fact under § 7.4**, at a meeting attended by the officers responsible for operations. The report is of the fact. The Committee directs nothing, and the decision to act remains Management's. **A systems finding that reaches nobody able to act on it changes nothing**, and this reporting is what makes an unactioned condition visible rather than silent.

**7.6 Recognition and shared practice.**

**(a) Recognition is of facilities and teams.** The Committee may recognize a facility, or the clinical team serving it, for performance on an organizational measure. **Recognition rests only on information outside the Committee's records**, meaning operational reports the Entity produces in the ordinary course, and never on an observation, a finding, a commendation, or the constitution of a comparison group. A rate is not attributed to an individual Practitioner under § 4.4, and recognition is subject to that rule as any other use of a rate is.

**(b) Shared practice is learned openly.** Where the Committee wishes to learn how a facility achieves its performance, the inquiry is conducted by the education function under § 2.8, by interview and observation of process and not by chart audit, and its product is written for the open record under § 8.1(a). A practice description states what the facility does, names the facility only with the consent of its leadership, identifies no resident, and **makes no comparison naming any other facility or Practitioner.**

**(c) Nothing public refers to the audit.** No recognition or practice description states or implies that the audit examined, selected, or found anything.

**7.7 A request for help.** **Any Practitioner may ask for help in any aspect of their clinical practice, documentation included**, at any time, whether or not their charts have been examined, and without stating a reason. The request is made to the education function under § 2.8, directly or through the Chair or the Coordinator, and the education function decides how it is answered.

**A request opens nothing.** It is not an observation. It does not select the Practitioner's charts under § 5.6(a), open a follow-up look under § 5.11, count toward any criterion under § 6.2, or travel with a referral under § 6.3, and it is not a Quality Intervention under § 8.2 of the PRC Charter.

**It is never recorded against the Practitioner.** The Chair or the Coordinator passes it on and keeps no record of it. It is not counted or transmitted, and is entered in no employment or performance record. Section 5.5(c)(5)(iv) through (vi) applies to the help given as it applies to the offer of support.

**A request shields nothing.** It does not affect the audit, the operation of § 5.11, or any route to the PRC under § 6.6 or § 6.7.

**A present risk disclosed by a request.** Where the help discloses care that leaves a resident at present risk, including a change in a plan of care that has not been documented, the first object of the help is to remove that risk, and the Practitioner's acting on it answers the concern. The person helping does not refer the Practitioner to the PRC on what the request disclosed unless the failure to act may result in imminent danger to the health of any individual, or the Practitioner does not act on it within [ten] business days. In either case the person helping tells the Practitioner at the time of referring. Nothing in this paragraph limits a duty to refer under § 4.3(a) of the PRC Charter, or a referral by any person on information obtained otherwise than through the request.

**Why it exists.** Without this section every route to individual help begins with an examination of the Practitioner's charts. A clinician who knows where their practice is thin should be able to say so before anyone looks, and will say so only if saying it costs nothing.

## Article VIII — Records

**8.1 Two records, maintained separately.**

- (a) **The open record** — adopted standards, education material, anything disseminated, and **any draft circulated outside the Committee for comment.** This record is intended to circulate and **is not confidential.** A document distributed to every Practitioner in the organization cannot be privileged, and the Committee shall not represent that it is.

  **A draft becomes open when it is circulated.** A circulated draft shall not therefore carry the Committee's candid assessment of current performance, of any facility, or of any Practitioner. That material belongs in (b) and stays there.

- (b) **The protected record** — observations, review reports, clinical comments and requests for them, individual findings, dispositions, referrals, minutes, the Committee's deliberations, and **the internal drafting record**: working drafts not circulated outside the Committee, evidence tables, and members' comments.

  **On adoption, a clean final version of the standard is entered into the open record.** The drafting record that produced it is not moved and remains protected permanently.

No document is moved between the two records, and none is ever in both. The distinction exists because Ohio requires document-by-document proof of scope, and a file mixing published standards with case-level findings invites the argument that the whole file is operational.

**8.1.1 Where the Committee's instruments sit.** The two records are exhaustive, and each of the following is classified expressly rather than left to inference.

- **Open, under (a):** every adopted standard and its examples of conforming documentation; the standards register; the **Audit Referral Policy** as in effect under § 6.2, a copy of which is also held in the PRC's governance record under § 13.1(a) of its Charter; the **definition of each comparison group** — the characteristics by which it is constructed under § 5.6(c), recorded in the standards register with the standard it attaches to; **recognitions under § 7.6(a) and practice descriptions under § 7.6(b)**; **each review guide under § 4.5.1**; **audit results reported under § 7.4.1**; and **any record of help given under § 7.7**, which names no Practitioner.
- **Not kept by the Committee:** a request for help under § 7.7, which is passed to the education function and entered in neither record.
- **Protected, under (b):** the **audit register** — the map of audit questions to standards under § 5.3, the record of instrument validation under § 5.4, selection thresholds under § 5.6(e), and follow-up sample sizes under § 5.11.2 — the **constitution of each comparison group in a cycle**: which Practitioners composed it and what their rates were; **commendations under § 5.5(d)** and the selection thresholds that produced them; and **clinical comments under § 5.5(e)** and requests for them.

**The review guide states what the standard requires; the audit register states how the Committee examines charts for it.** The first is published so that every clinician and supervising physician can apply the standard. The second is not, because search logic and selection published in advance invite the audit to be managed rather than met.

The audit register is protected because it is the Committee's working instrument for examining charts, created by and for the Committee, and it sits closer to the observations it produces than to the standards it measures against.

**The Audit Referral Policy is open because a Practitioner is entitled to know what may cause a referral**, and because it is a governance instrument in which both committees have joined. **Selection thresholds are not open.** Selection carries no consequence under § 5.6(a), so nothing turns on a Practitioner knowing them, and publishing them would invite the audit to be managed rather than met.

**A comparison group is two things, and they are classified separately and held as separate documents.** Its **definition** is the set of characteristics by which it is constructed, chosen before any rate is known, and it is what a Practitioner needs in order to know the terms of a comparison drawn against them. Its **constitution** is which Practitioners fell within it in a given cycle and what their rates were, which is derived from observations and is a record of other people's care. **A Practitioner is entitled to the first and to no part of the second.**

**A group definition is open although a selection threshold is not**, because the two are open to different objections. A published threshold tells a Practitioner what to stay below; **a published group definition tells them nothing they can act on**, discipline, setting and panel size being conditions of their work rather than choices about their charts.

**8.2 Segregation from other records.** Both are maintained separately from the PRC's record, from employment records, from facility QAPI material, and from any record designated patient safety work product.

**8.3 Retrospective posture of the audit record.** The audit component of the protected record — observations, review reports, clinical comments, individual findings, dispositions, and the deliberations concerning them — is created by and for the Committee, retrospectively, for the purpose of quality review. It does not duplicate, replace, or serve any documentation the Entity or any facility is independently required to create, and such documentation is not made a Committee record by being filed with one.

This section describes the audit record. It does not describe the internal drafting record under § 8.1(b).

**Classification is not privilege.** The internal drafting record is maintained in the protected record because it is the Committee's own deliberative material rather than retrospective audit material. Whether that material is privileged or otherwise protected from disclosure in any jurisdiction is a question for counsel. **Its classification under this Charter does not itself establish legal protection, and nothing in this Charter should be read as an assertion that it does.**

**8.4 Write as though the subject will read it.** Individually attributed observations and findings shall be written on the assumption that the Practitioner will read them: what was observed stated plainly, the evidence identified, characterization avoided.

**8.5 Underlying facts are not protected.** Medical records, the observations of witnesses with independent knowledge, and information otherwise available remain discoverable in every jurisdiction in which the Entity operates. Nothing in this Charter makes a fact confidential that would not otherwise be.

**8.6 Confidentiality, retention, hold.** Every member and person assisting executes a written undertaking before receiving any part of the protected record. Retention is [seven] years, or as counsel directs. No record is altered or destroyed once litigation is filed or reasonably foreseeable.

**8.7 Effective-date boundary.** Observations and findings generated before the effective date of this Charter are not carried into the Committee's record and do not count toward any referral criterion.

## Article IX — Meetings and Voting

**9.1 Cadence.** [Monthly].

**9.2 Quorum.** A majority of members, of whom not less than [two-thirds] are licensed clinicians in active clinical practice.

**9.3 Voting.** Majority of members present and voting. Dispositions under § 5.5 and determinations under Article VI are decided by the members eligible under § 2.5 and not recused under § 9.6, **and are not taken with fewer than [three] such members present.** Where fewer are present the determination waits.

- (a) is the Practitioner's assigned supervising physician, or the collaborating or supervising physician under the law of the jurisdiction;
- (b) participated in the care of the resident at issue;
- (c) holds, or held within the preceding [five] years, a supervisory relationship with the Practitioner;
- (d) holds a personal, familial, or financial relationship a reasonable person would regard as bearing on impartiality; or
- (e) is in direct economic competition with the Practitioner.

**A recused member receives none of the material concerning that Practitioner**, leaves before the matter is taken up, and does not return until it is concluded. Recusal is determined by the Chair, or where the Chair is affected, by the members who are not, and is recorded.

**A supervising physician's responsibility for an APC is unaffected by membership.** A member reviews the charts of the APCs they supervise under § 4.5.1 as any supervising physician does. What this section prevents is the member learning, inside the Committee, what the Committee found about a Practitioner they supervise, so that nothing crosses from the Committee's record into the supervisory relationship.

**9.6.1 Where the Chair is recused**, a member designated in advance by the Chair presides in that matter, puts the question under § 5.5(b)(1), and exercises the Chair's other functions in it.

## Article X — Immunity, Amendment, and Effective Date

**10.1 Immunity.** The Committee, its members, and persons assisting are entitled, **to the fullest extent provided by applicable law**, to the protections of the state statutes cited in § 1.4 and, where applicable, of 42 U.S.C. § 11111. **To the extent 42 U.S.C. § 11111(a)(2) applies, a person providing information is immune unless the information is false and the person knew it was false.**

**A caution, deliberately stated.** That provision protects information given to a *professional review body*, and this Committee is by design not one — see § 1.3. Whether it reaches information given to this Committee has not been determined and is a question for counsel. **A person with a concern about an identified Practitioner should therefore bring it to the PRC**, under § 4.1(b) of its Charter, where the statutory fit is clearer. That is better practice in any event, and it means the question never has to be answered in a case that matters.

**10.2 Non-retaliation.** No adverse action shall be taken against any person **because** that person provided information to the Committee in good faith, participated in the Committee's work, was selected for audit, or became the subject of an observation or an individual finding.

**Nothing in this section limits action taken on other grounds.** It does not limit action on conduct independently established through the Peer Review Committee, an employment process, or any other lawful process, and it does not limit ordinary employment or operational action unrelated to the person's participation in, or subjection to, the Committee's work. The protection is against retaliation for participation or status. It is not immunity from consequence.

**10.3 Annual review.** The Committee reviews this Charter annually and reports the review under § 7.4.

**10.4 Amendment.** By the Committee, with the written approval of the Chief Executive Officer. An amendment takes effect on approval.

**10.5 Effective date.** [DATE].

# Open items in this draft

**Deliberately kept lean.** The threshold apparatus, calibration period, asymmetric-discretion rule, and disposition tiers developed for this Committee during design have been moved out of the Charter and into the referral schedule under § 6.2, or dropped. The Charter states that criteria exist, that they are written, that they take effect with the PRC's concurrence, and that observations are dispositioned. It does not attempt to specify them. Two reasons: the numbers cannot be set before there is a baseline, and a quality committee that reads like a disciplinary apparatus will not be accepted by the people whose participation it depends on.

**What survives from that design, and why each earns its place:**

- **§ 5.3, questions map to adopted standards.** Closes the loop with § 4.5. Without it the audit measures against something nobody adopted and no one was on notice of. **Bounded by § 6.7** — it constrains the audit instrument, not the universe of reviewable conduct.
- **§ 4.4, minimum expectation versus improvement target.** Added 2026-08-26. Only departures from the former can refer.
- **§ 6.7, the standard of care is not displaced.** Added 2026-08-26, and the most important paragraph in the Charter. Without (b) in particular, a written standards program supplies the argument that anything unwritten is permitted.
- **§ 5.6, selection separated from findings.** Added 2026-08-26 after stress-testing an antipsychotic-in-dementia scenario against the Charter. The program had no concept of how a Practitioner or a chart gets chosen for examination, which left rate-based selection permitted only by omission. Subsection (a) makes it explicit and states the reason a crude selection threshold is defensible: the chart answers the case-mix question, and it answers it better than any denominator could. Subsection (d) is the part that matters most for fairness — where an adequate comparison cannot be built, the pattern criterion simply does not apply, which prevents a referral resting on a denominator of eleven.
- **§ 5.8, clinical basis rather than a prohibited-measure list.** Rewritten 2026-08-26. The earlier version barred any measure with financial linkage to the Entity. Under an ACO, an institutional special needs plan, capitation, or bundles, essentially every clinical measure has financial linkage — the rule would have excluded avoidable hospitalization and would have been argued around in its first inconvenient meeting.
- **§ 5.9, mass referral is a systems finding.** One sentence, and it is the only thing preventing a badly specified audit question from generating a wave of referrals. **Education added as a third route 2026-08-30**, with the diagnostic that goes with it: the three routes correspond to the three ordinary causes of widespread nonconformity — the standard, the audit question, or the teaching — and the last is both the most common and the most fixable.
- **§ 5.1, three terms.** Added 2026-08-26 after external review. Observation, rate, and individual finding had all been called "finding," which put § 5.5 and § 5.6 in direct conflict — one disposed findings at the chart level, the other defined a finding as a rate. Dispositions now attach to observations; an individual finding is what an observation becomes when validated. The last paragraph keeps "finding" from becoming ambiguous between this Committee and the PRC.
- **§ 5.5, observations are dispositioned, not findings.** The section is no longer titled "every finding is dispositioned," because the point of the revision is that only some observations become findings.
- **§ 5.5(a), opportunity to correct.** Added 2026-08-26, and the most substantive addition in this round. § 5.4 validates the instrument; nothing validated the observation. Observations feed referrals. Deliberately narrow: factual error, missing documentation, attribution error, record context. Not an appeal, not a hearing, no argument on the clinical merits — that would rebuild peer review inside the quality committee and destroy the separation § 1.3 and § 6.5 exist to create.
- **§ 6.7(c), second paragraph.** Added 2026-08-26. Without it the notice protections were optional: anyone could relabel an audit failure as a personal referral. The test is an independent *basis*, not independent facts, because the same chart routinely supports both. Enforced at PRC screening under § 4.7 of that Charter rather than by prohibition alone.
- **Route-specific citations.** "Under Article VI" was wrong in eight places, because Article VI contains both the audit-referral route and the § 6.7 direct route. Audit-route provisions now cite §§ 6.1–6.5, which keeps § 6.7 unrestricted.
- **§ 8.3, classification distinguished from privilege.** The Charter says how it files documents. It does not assert that filing them that way makes them privileged.
- **§ 4.7, how standards are written.** Added 2026-08-26. Decisions documented rather than treatments prescribed; element-by-element rather than global attestation; reasons of a verifiable kind; answerable from the record.
- **§ 7.5, the route to operations.** Added 2026-08-30, on a read of the three Charters from the Chief Operating Officer's seat. The Committee could identify a systems condition and had no chartered route to anyone able to fix it: § 5.5(b) retained it, and Article VII's interfaces ran to the other clinical committees, from the PRC, on remand, and to the Council. **The most valuable single output of an audit — finding the standing order that manufactures the problem — reached the person who could change it only informally, and nobody was obliged to act.**

  Three things make the section work. What travels is the condition and never the case, so nothing individually attributed leaves the Committee. The transmittal is **open-record material and is not represented as confidential**, because a description of a broken protocol is operational information and pretending otherwise would keep it from the people who need it. And a condition not acted on is recorded and reported under § 7.4 as a fact — this Committee may not direct Management, so **the only available mechanism is visibility, and without it a systems disposition is indistinguishable from doing nothing.**

- **Final proofing pass, 2026-08-30.** § 1.5 had said standards-setting, education *and audit* are prospective work, while § 8.3 correctly treats the audit record as created retrospectively — a contradiction that survived every earlier round and that matters, because the retrospective character of that record is part of why it is classified as it is. § 5.5(a)(1)(iv) used *"held per cardiology"* as its example of an entry the instrument missed, which is the precise entry § 4.7(e) now says does **not** satisfy a designated standard; and § 5.5(a)(4)(i) offered ground (vi) as a route to withdrawal in a § 4.7(e) matter, where (vi) is expressly unavailable. § 5.11 used the term "follow-up window" without defining it, which would have left analytics to invent the one variable that decides whether a follow-up happens at all.

- **§ 5.11.2, rebuilt around the window rather than a look date.** Revised 2026-08-30, on the same reasoning as § 5.7: **count visit cycles, not calendar convenience.** The section had set a single interval and sampled backwards from it, which meant that shortening the interval would start examining care furnished before the Practitioner had realistically acted on the close-out.

  It now has two parameters, and they are additive. **A [forty-five]-day opening period, which is time given rather than time elapsed** — set so that a Practitioner who takes up the offer under § 5.5(c)(5) can request it, have it arranged, attend it, and begin applying it before any of her care is measured. **It cannot be made conditional on uptake**, because § 5.5(c)(5)(iii) forbids uptake being recorded, so it is set for the Practitioner who uses the offer. Then a **[ninety]-day window**, which is two to three chronic care visits for each eligible resident: the follow-up asks whether practice changed, and a shorter window answers only whether a note was written.

  The look therefore falls around day one hundred thirty-five, later than the hundred and twenty first drafted — **the extra time buys a three-month window instead of a two-month one**, which is the thing the follow-up exists to measure. A ninety-day window also makes closure for want of an eligible case under § 5.11.5 substantially less likely than a sixty-day one would have.

- **§ 5.7, sixty days rather than ninety.** Revised 2026-08-30. The period measures how long before practice can fairly be expected to have changed across a panel, not how long it takes to read a module. **For a documentation standard that is one ordinary visit cycle**, which sixty days covers. The power to set a longer period was already there for standards asking a change in practice; what is new is that the record of adoption now states the period for each standard, so it is chosen rather than inherited.

- **Consistency pass against the Audit Referral Policy, 2026-09-10.** Nine provisions revised so that this Charter and the schedule adopted under § 6.2 state the same rule once. Section 1.3 no longer asserts an automatic referral that § 5.5(b) leaves to the criteria, and no longer describes what the audit produces as a "concern," a word § 5.5(b)(1) and § 6.7(c) reserve for what a member holds and refers as a person. Section 5.11.3(b) defers to the criteria in the terms § 5.5(b) now uses. Section 5.11.3(a) closes a matter only where no finding on care in the window has been made by any examination, a sample that misses what another examination found being evidence about the sample. Section 5.6(c) records a group definition in the standards register rather than with the Policy, so that adopting one does not require the Policy to be revised. Section 5.6(d) states that it limits pattern criteria only. Section 2.5's closing sentence no longer claims identity with a rule that is narrower. **And two citations to a § 4.2(b) that does not exist** — at § 2.8 and § 5.9 — now cite § 3.1(b), where the education agenda is in fact set. Recorded in full in `cqc-charter-revisions.md`.

- **§ 5.9, § 5.6(d) and § 7.4, three consequential cleanups.** Revised 2026-09-10. Sections 5.9 and 5.6(d) each preserved "severity-based referral" as something the referral criteria contain. **The criteria adopted under § 6.2 contain no criterion turning on the seriousness of a particular case**, because a criterion of that kind would ask this Committee for a judgment about an identified Practitioner's care, which § 1.3 reserves to the PRC and which § 5.5(b)(1) routes to § 6.7(c) as the act of the member holding it. What the criteria do contain is referral on a single occasion, designated at the adoption of a standard. Both carve-outs now name that, so each points to something that exists. Section 7.4 gains the occasions on which referral was withheld under § 5.9, without which the provision this Charter relies on to prevent mass referral operates unreported.

- **§ 8.1.1 and § 5.6(c), where a comparison group sits.** Revised 2026-09-10. Section 8.1.1 classified the Committee's instruments on the stated premise that the two records are exhaustive and that each instrument is classified expressly rather than left to inference, and it did not reach the comparison groups § 5.6(c) places inside the Audit Referral Policy. Left there, the Policy's own classification would have governed by implication — and because the Policy is open, **a Practitioner would have been entitled to their colleagues' rates.** The two provisions now distinguish the definition of a group from its constitution in a cycle, classify each, and require them to be held as separate documents, which § 8.1 requires of anything that would otherwise sit in both records.

- **§ 5.5(b), the Individual disposition.** Revised 2026-09-10. The subsection stated that a validated finding "is referred under §§ 6.1–6.5 where a criterion is met," which admits of no exception — and this Charter requires one at § 5.9, where a criterion that would refer a large fraction of Practitioners routes to a systems remedy instead. **The disposition now classifies the observation and refers the consequence to the criteria adopted under § 6.2**, so that every condition and every qualification on a referral sits in the one instrument that can be calibrated against actual findings without amending this Charter.

- **§ 5.11.3(a), what the closure ends.** Revised 2026-09-10. The subsection barred "further targeted examination of that Practitioner on that measure," which on a broad reading would let the close-out-and-look-again sequence run **once per Practitioner per standard for the whole of a career** — a finding two years later could never be followed up, and the escalation the criteria rest on would be unavailable for the rest of that person's service. The narrow reading was intended and the subsection now states it: the closure ends the look attached to that finding, and a later finding on the same measure runs the ordinary sequence as a new matter.

- **§ 2.5 and § 9.3, the exclusion follows the act.** Revised 2026-09-10, in the course of drafting the Audit Referral Policy. Both provisions named the disposition under § 5.5 and nothing else, which was every determination this Committee made about an identified Practitioner when they were written. The referral schedule adds another: a determination under Article VI, and in particular a determination not to refer where a criterion is met. **Both provisions now reach any determination concerning an identified Practitioner**, so the exclusion attaches to the character of the act rather than to a list of the acts that existed at drafting. The principle is unchanged.

- **§ 8.1.1, where the Committee's instruments sit.** Added 2026-08-30. The two-record classification was exhaustive on its face and left the standards register, the audit register and the Audit Referral Policy unplaced. **The Policy is open, because a Practitioner is entitled to know what may cause a referral. The audit register is protected, and selection thresholds with it** — selection carries no consequence under § 5.6(a), so nothing turns on knowing them, and publishing them would invite the audit to be managed rather than met.

- **The revisions of 2026-08-30.** Twelve substantive changes across Articles II, IV, V, VI and VII, recorded in full in `cqc-charter-revisions.md` rather than here: the correction step at § 5.5(a) rewritten; the close-out at § 5.5(c) and the support offer at § 5.5(c)(5); the follow-up look at § 5.11; § 4.7(e) and its settled and pending dispositions, replacing an authorship rule that three provisions assumed and none granted; thin entries at § 5.10(c); the definition of a minimum expectation at § 4.4(a); the safety question at § 5.5(b)(1) and § 6.7(c) reframed as a route; education and onboarding at § 5.9; the education function at § 2.8; and the interfaces at § 6.3 and § 7.4. **That record carries the before-and-after; this Charter does not.**

- **§ 5.10, controls against a standard satisfied by a stored phrase.** Added 2026-08-26. Cross-check against the record, and a quarterly sample of charts that passed. Auditing only the failures is how a standard becomes a macro. **Thin entries were added as a third control 2026-08-30** — see the revision record.

**Why § 5.8 is short.** A longer version was drafted and discarded. It would have required at least one audit measure directed at under-response, and named the events through which under-response is findable — return to hospital shortly after a decision not to transfer, death in facility without documented goals of care, a change in condition with no documented disposition. The requirement was dropped because a charter obligation that can be unmet by ordinary inattention is worse than no obligation: the protection this program depends on turns on the process having been followed, so every added specific is another way to be found in breach of our own document in complete good faith.

**The design intent behind it, recorded here rather than imposed there.** An audit program directed only at overuse is vulnerable to the argument that it serves cost rather than care. The under-response events above are the practical handles for balancing it, and the Committee should use them. But this is a matter for the referral schedule and the Committee's judgment, not a charter duty.

**And the honest limitation.** The audit is asymmetric by nature. Over-response produces an event that can be found; under-response produces an absence that cannot. What makes that tolerable is § 6.7 — under-response reaches peer review from a colleague, a facility, or a family, on a route carrying no threshold, no standard requirement, and no notice period. Which means the asymmetry is survivable only if that route is genuinely usable and people know it exists. That is a communications obligation rather than a drafting one, and it belongs in the clinician-facing explainer.

**Structural notes:**
