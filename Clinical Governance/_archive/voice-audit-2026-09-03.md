# Voice audit — the three charters, 2026-09-03

Swept `cqc-charter-draft.md`, `prc-charter-draft.md` and `cgc-charter-draft.md` in full against the rule articulated today: **no aphoristic paradox.** Three shapes — the clever inversion used as a lead, the superlative that ranks instead of informing, and the X-not-Y whose negated half carries no fact.

The test applied to each candidate: *does the sentence give the reader a fact, a rule, or a test they did not already have?* If yes it stayed. Every quote below was checked character-for-character against the file; line numbers are current as of this sweep.

**No settled decision is reopened by any of this.** § 6.7(c) is still a route rather than a loophole; Council § 8.6(e) still never extends a restriction. Only the sentences carrying those decisions change.

---

## CQC Charter

**1. § 5.5, line 173 — the concessive inversion**
> That the subsection is also owed to the Practitioner is true, and is not the reason it is here.

The reader gets a drafting motive that cannot be applied to any case. It also puts in writing that the Practitioner's interest is not why the validation step exists, which is a sentence a plaintiff's lawyer would enjoy.

**Instead:** The subsection is also owed to the Practitioner. **Its scope is nonetheless set by its quality-control purpose** — it asks only whether the audit accurately identified a departure, and not whether the care was appropriate.

**2. § 7.5, line 387 — an unsourced claim about quality programmes, in open-record material**
> **A systems finding that reaches nobody able to act on it is the most common way a quality programme comes to have no effect**, and this reporting exists so that the failure is visible rather than silent.

"The most common way" ranks against nothing measured. Worth more than the usual attention because § 7.5 transmittals are **open-record material under § 8.1(a)** — this sentence circulates unprivileged, and it asserts a general failure rate for quality programmes.

**Instead:** **A systems finding that reaches nobody able to act on it changes nothing.** Neither this Committee nor the Council may direct Management, so the record of non-action and the report to the Council are the only mechanism by which an unactioned condition becomes visible at all.

**3. § 5.5(c)(3), line 241 — conceding the process could be indefensible**
> Without it, a Practitioner's entire contact with the audit program is an allegation followed by silence, which is the least defensible version of the process the Committee could operate.

Same family as the audit-unreliability rule from 8/30: it writes a concession about the programme's own defensibility into the instrument.

**Instead:** …an allegation followed by silence, and the Committee holds no record that the Practitioner was ever told what became of it.

**4. § 6.7(c), line 362 — two in one paragraph**
> **This is a route and not a concession.**
> A route nobody uses is the same as no route, and § 5.5(b)(1) exists so that the question is asked rather than left to be volunteered.

The heading negates a characterisation nobody made; the three "no threshold, no adopted standard, no notice period" clauses two sentences later are the content it was standing in for. The second sentence is a pure epigram with the operative material trailing behind it.

**Instead:** promote the clauses — **Nothing in this Charter conditions this route.** And: **The route works only where the question is actually put**, which is why § 5.5(b)(1) requires the Chair to put it at every disposition rather than leaving it to be volunteered.

**5. § 5.5(c)(5), line 261 — negating a reading nobody offered**
> A Practitioner newly working in a clinical area will predictably have gaps, and **finding them is the purpose of this programme rather than an unfortunate by-product of it.**

**Instead:** …will predictably have gaps, and **the audit is designed to find them.**

**6. § 5.5(c)(5)(i), line 249**
> It is what this process does, not something that happens to a particular person.

Restates the bolded "made identically to every Practitioner" immediately before it. The rewrite says the thing the Practitioner actually needs to hear.

**Instead:** **The offer accompanies every close-out notice issued under (c)(1)**, and its presence in a notice therefore says nothing about the Practitioner who receives it.

**7. § 4.4, line 93**
> Neither is acceptable, so neither exists — the Committee decides what a standard is for at the moment it writes it.

Both halves restate "There is no default" and "made by express vote," already bolded at the head of the paragraph.

**Instead:** Neither default is acceptable, so the Charter provides none. **A standard carries the designation recorded for it in the standards register at adoption, and no other.**

**8. § 4.7(e), line 135 · § 7.5, line 383 — "the entire purpose" / "the entire object"**
> Telling those two apart is the entire purpose of designating a standard under this subsection…
> …would keep it from the people who need it — which is the entire object of dispositioning an observation Systems in the first place.

Both rank a provision instead of stating it. In each case only the surrounding clause is operative.

## PRC Charter

**9. § 5.1, line 181 — the paradox proper** *(duplicated verbatim at line 585 — change both)*
> **A notice describing only what a review might do at its worst is accurate and misleading at the same time**, and a Practitioner receiving one is entitled to the complete picture of what is and is not in prospect.

**Instead:** **A notice naming only the recommendation for a Professional Review Action omits § 8.5 and § 8.5.1**, and the Practitioner is entitled to the complete range of what is and is not in prospect.

**10. § 8.1.3, line 304 — a consequence the reader cannot check**
> …imposing one that cannot work starts a clock under § 8.3 that ends somewhere worse than where it began.

The first clause is a genuine reason. "Somewhere worse" names no outcome anyone can locate in Article VIII — and the real mechanism is more damning than the flourish.

**Instead:** …imposing one starts the [six]-month clock under § 8.3 and produces a failed exit determination under § 8.6(g) for a departure the Practitioner could not have prevented.

**11. § 5.7.7, line 236**
> What makes this section proportionate is that **it requires and confirms one thing while determining nothing** — and that the Practitioner may always refuse it and take the process instead.

Ranks the provision's virtues, and both halves restate § 5.7.4 and § 5.7.2.

**Instead:** **The section requires completion of a defined plan and confirms completion as a fact, and makes no finding about the care furnished**, and the Practitioner may decline it and take the process under Articles VI and VIII instead.

**12. § 4.3(d)(2), line 127**
> It asks after a status, not after a person, and the two facts it may establish are the only two it needs.

The two facts are enumerated in bold four lines earlier; "the only two it needs" restates "two facts and no others."

**Instead:** The inquiry is directed to the status of the referred matter, and the two facts stated above are the only two the Committee may establish under this subsection.

**13. § 4.3(d), line 115 · § 4.7, line 173 — announcing that the rationale is the rationale**
> …**a question it has no standard to decide**, which is what this subsection exists to prevent.
> This section exists so that the protections attaching to audit referrals cannot be avoided by relabelling, and screening is where that is caught.

The first: close with the fact instead — "and Article VIII supplies none." The second repeats the bolded rule at line 161 and can either go, or become operative: **Screening applies this section to every referral within it, whatever route it arrived by, and records the determination with its reasons.**

## Council Charter

**14. § 8.6(e), line 277 — posture in place of a rule**
> **That is uncomfortable and it is deliberate.**

Announces in advance that the drafter meant it. The sentence that follows carries the entire justification; this one only asks the reader to be impressed by the nerve.

**Instead:** **The action terminates whether or not the exit criteria have been met.** Then the existing sentence about § 8.3 of the PRC Charter, unchanged.

**15. § 10.3, line 339 — and PRC § 13.6, line 448: the same superlative twice**
> **Repetition of findings outside the process is neither privileged nor immunized and is the most likely source of individual liability.**

An unsourced litigation ranking in the provision that governs the confidentiality undertaking members sign. The fact a member needs is that the exposure is *personal* and reaches outside every protection in Article XII.

**Instead:** **Repetition of findings outside the process is neither privileged nor immunized**, and neither 42 U.S.C. § 11111(a)(1) nor the state statutes cited in § 1.4 reaches the person who repeats them.

**16. § 10.2, line 333 — two in one paragraph**
> **The separation is what makes the information barriers in these Charters real rather than declared.**
> **A record kept as one would undo in the filing what each of those provisions establishes in the process.**

"Real rather than declared" names no barrier. The filing/process antithesis does the persuading while leaving the mechanism for the reader to supply.

**Instead:** **Each of the barriers stated elsewhere in these Charters depends on this separation.** … **A single combined record would place material in the hands of a body these provisions bar from receiving it**, whatever the process rule says.

**17. § 1.2.1, line 18 · § 4.5, line 160 — minor**
> The intermediate responses are the substance of what these charters make available.
> …and a designation decision is where the pressure to move it will be greatest.

The first re-labels a list given two clauses earlier as "the point." The second ranks a risk without describing it; the sentence is complete without the clause.

## Defended — flagged by the sweep, and they should stay

- **PRC § 4.1** — "**The audit is one source and not the principal one.**" States an operative rule: no source has priority. Repeated deliberately across all three documents.
- **CQC § 4.7(c)** — "It is a referent without a reason, where *'not a candidate'* is a reason without a referent." Chiasmus, but it hands the reader a two-part test for classifying a deficient entry.
- **Council § 3.9** — "Growth should change who fills a seat, not how many seats there are." Accurate one-line statement of what the cap plus automatic conversion actually do.
- **Council § 12.4** heading — "Immunity is a presumption, not a shield." The body delivers the test, so the heading is a label rather than a substitute.
- **Council § 2.2** — "A tier the PRC may impose directly would therefore be a tier it is prohibited from imposing." Reads as paradox; it is the actual conclusion of the § 2.1 / § 2.3 / PRC § 3.2(a) chain.
- **CQC § 5.8** — "at once the most clinically important measure in this population and the most financially consequential." The double superlative is the point: one measure sits at both extremes, which is why the exclusion rule cannot be a list.

## Separate: three potshots that survived the 8/30 sweep

Same skill, different rule — never characterise another function's policy, instrument or work.

**A. PRC § 4.2, line 99 — the most exposed sentence in the three charters**
> Facilities have interests other than quality of care, and a Practitioner may draw a complaint precisely for holding a clinical line — on antimicrobial stewardship, on hospitalization rates, or on documenting what an administrator would prefer undocumented.

This attributes non-clinical motive to contracted facilities, and bad faith to an unnamed administrator, **in operative text, in a document a facility may one day read in discovery.** The rule that follows stands entirely without it: *a complaint under § 4.1(e) is screened on its clinical merits and its source is recorded.*

**B. PRC § 14.2, line 464**
> …has a nexus to patient welfare and is within this section, whatever an administrative policy calls it.

The closing clause characterises Management's written policy as potentially mislabelling the matter. The following sentence about conforming revisions already covers it.

**C. PRC § 11.3, line 410**
> A referral arriving with only the adverse half of the CQC's record would invite the Committee to begin where the CQC ended, which is the opposite of what § 11.4 requires.

Milder, and internal — but it is the one place the CQC's own record is described as incomplete. Judgment call.

# Applied, 2026-09-03

**25 edits across the three charters.** All applied; each replacement string matched exactly once, and every original was verified verbatim before the change. A dated "Voice pass" entry was added to the Open items of each Charter. **No rule changed anywhere.**

CQC 9 · PRC 10 · Council 6.

**Three findings were applied differently than proposed above, and one was dropped.**

**Dropped — PRC § 11.3.** The sentence about a referral arriving with only the adverse half of the CQC's record is the stated rationale for a mirrored provision, and the sentence immediately after it says so: *"This provision mirrors § 6.3 of the CQC Charter and is to be kept aligned with it."* Cutting it removes the reason § 6.3 was amended to transmit the Practitioner's correction-step submission. It describes what a referral package contains, not the CQC's competence. **Left as written.**

**Seven rewrites became plain deletions.** The rewrites proposed above for CQC § 7.5 (383), CQC § 4.4, CQC § 5.5, CQC § 5.5(c)(3), PRC § 4.3(d), PRC § 4.7 and Council § 1.2.1 each introduced rule content that was not in the original. A voice edit in a governing instrument must not add a rule nobody voted on, so in each case the flourish was cut and the sentence already carrying the rule left standing.

**CQC § 6.7(c) was restructured, not spot-edited.** The two proposed replacements would have collided with the bolded sentence between them. The paragraph now opens on the no-threshold clause, which is the content the old heading was standing in for. The settled reframing from loophole to route is untouched.

**PRC § 4.2 was cut surgically.** Deleting the whole sentence would have left § 4.2 with a rule and no reason, against the rationale-forward convention. The clause attributing bad faith to an administrator is gone; a neutral statement that a facility's interests are not confined to the quality of the care remains, with the antimicrobial stewardship and hospitalization-rate examples.

# Left alone, and one you should look at

**The Open items back matter of all three Charters was not swept.** Those are notes to counsel and rewriting them disturbs the change record. The superlative habit is there if you want it uniform — "the most important paragraph in the Charter," "the most substantive addition in this round," "the part that matters most for fairness," "the last is both the most common and the most fixable," "the most valuable single output of an audit," "the single most consequential line in the document," "this is the provision the whole records architecture rests on."

**One line in that back matter is not a superlative problem.** CQC Open items, the § 5.5(a) entry:

> An instrument accurate in ninety-five percent of charts is wrong about a given chart one time in twenty, and observations feed referrals.

That is the sentence rejected on 2026-08-30 under the rule against conceding the audit instrument's unreliability. It was cut from operative text and survives here, where it states an error rate no § 5.4 validation has measured, in a section that goes to counsel. **Cut, 2026-09-03.** The entry now reads: *§ 5.4 validates the instrument; nothing validated the observation. Observations feed referrals.* Recorded in the CQC Voice pass note.

**Also outstanding:** `cqc-charter-revisions.md` still carries *"A route nobody uses is the same as no route"* in its record of the § 6.7(c) reframing. Companion document, so the rule reaches it, but it is a change record rather than an instrument. Left for you to decide.
