# Questions for counsel

Kept outside the charter and policy drafts so that the drafts carry only their own text and drafting notes. Section numbers refer to the drafts as revised 2026-09-28. Each question keeps the date it was added.

## Peer Review Committee Charter

- **The Authorization and state privilege.** Whether committees established by the Chief Executive Officer's written authorization, rather than by the governing body, are protected committees in each of the five states, and whether the Chief Executive Officer can, under the Entity's governing documents, create a committee whose decisions are the Entity's own action. **Indiana's statute protects committees "organized by the governing body"**, and Indiana is where the Authorization may need more than a signature. Added 2026-09-28.
- **Indemnification and insurance for members, panelists, Reviewer Pool members, external reviewers, any presiding officer, and the Coordinator.** Committed in the Authorization (§ 15.3.1). Whether the commitment and the Entity's D&O and malpractice coverage reach each of these people, including non-employees. Added 2026-08-30; revised 2026-09-28.
- **§§ 2.1, 2.1.1 — the Chair's certification.** Whether a presiding officer who certifies process and signs, without voting, is consistent with the Committee being the body that held the reasonable belief under § 11112(a), and whether the Chair's role as chair of the CQC affects that in a matter the CQC referred. Added 2026-09-28.
- **§ 10.3 — CQC members on hearing panels.** Whether seating a member of the CQC who took no part in the audit affects the panel's standing under § 11112(b)(3)(A). Added 2026-09-28.
- **Article X, carried from the draft hearing procedure** (added 2026-09-28): whether the burden allocation in § 10.5(e) creates any disadvantage in defending an action later, given that HCQIA requires a reasonable belief rather than proof; whether the limited review in § 10.7(b) is consistent with the Committee being the entity taking the action under § 11112(a)(4); whether continuing a precautionary restriction through a requested hearing (§ 9.5) is sound in each of the five states; whether running one procedure for every discipline creates exposure a tiered procedure would not; whether § 10.4's undertaking to make contracted personnel available creates an obligation the Entity cannot meet; and the standard for when a Proposed Action is "pending" on resignation (§§ 10.9, 15.9).
- **§ 9.5 — whether continuing a precautionary restriction through the period to request a hearing** is consistent with 42 U.S.C. § 11112(c)(2), which permits an immediate restriction for imminent danger subject to subsequent notice and hearing. Added 2026-09-28.
- **§ 10.2.1 — whether a Practitioner's written waiver of the thirty-day minimums preserves the § 11112(b) safe harbor.** The statute says "not less than 30 days," and the expedited schedule depends on the answer. **To be answered before the offer is made to anyone.** Added 2026-09-28.

- **§ 5.7 — whether an agreed resolution entered under the alternative of a full review could be characterised as coerced**, and whether the notice under § 5.7.3, the unconditional right to decline, and the absence of any consequence for declining are sufficient answer. Added 2026-08-30.
- § 5.7 — confirmation that an agreed, non-restrictive educational plan resting on no finding sits outside NPDB and state reporting, including where it is entered while a matter is open.

- § 8.2(d) — whether retrospective focused review is reliably non-reportable, and precisely where the line sits between it and § 8.4(a) mandatory consultation. This is the single most consequential line in the document.
- § 9.4 — whether the thirty-day analysis runs from imposition of the precautionary restriction or from final action. Drafted as imposition, which is the conservative reading.
- § 9.1 — whether imposition by the Chair and two members, subject to automatic lapse, is a professional review action at the moment of imposition or only on ratification, and what that means for § 11112(c)(2).
- **§ 9.8 — the investigatory restriction.** (a) The statute permits "a suspension of clinical privileges of not longer than 14 days" during an investigation. Whether a narrower restriction, such as mandatory consultation for a defined set of decisions, falls within § 11112(c)(1)(B), or whether § 9.8 should be confined to suspension. (b) Whether a review opened under § 4.4(d) is "an investigation being conducted to determine the need for a professional review action" for this purpose. (c) Confirmation that a restriction ending within fourteen days is not reportable to the Data Bank, and that counting a following § 9.1 restriction from the first imposition under § 9.8(f) is right rather than over-inclusive. (d) Whether any of the five states treats a short investigatory restriction differently for licensing-board reporting. Added 2026-08-30 as a question whether to add the instrument; rewritten 2026-09-28 now that it is drafted.
- § 2.3 — whether categorical exclusion of Employment Authority creates any argument that the deciding body lacked the knowledge to form a reasonable belief under § 11112(a)(1).
- § 7.4 — whether opening a physician review on the basis of an oversight assessment made in another Practitioner's review creates any notice defect for the physician.
- § 6.5 — psychotherapy notes: whether professional review of a psychotherapist is within the health care operations exception, and what a compliant procedure looks like.
- § 13.3 — confirmation that the retrospective-posture language tracks the *Charles* and *Kitchen* line as counsel reads it.
- Whether Ind. Code § 34-30-15-6(b), which confines statutory hearing and appeal rights to independent practitioners with privileges and excludes employees, creates any inconsistency with running full HCQIA process for everyone. The intent is to exceed the state floor deliberately.
- § 4.3 — whether the mandatory referral list should include a malpractice claim at filing or only at settlement or judgment.
- § 8.1.1 — whether the standard-of-care formulation should be stated by reference to state law in each jurisdiction, which differs on locality rules and on expert qualification, or left as drafted at the professional standard.
- **§ 8.1.2(d) — whether a charter provision stating that the Entity's adoption of a documentation requirement does not by itself establish the standard of care has any effect against a third party**, or whether it binds only the Entity's own bodies. If it does not travel, the exposure is managed in how CQC § 4.7(e) standards are drafted and designated rather than here. Worth putting to counsel as one question spanning both Charters. Added 2026-08-30.
- § 3.2(a) as revised — whether classifying a § 9.1 restriction as not a final Professional Review Action "under this Charter" is compatible with however § 11112(c)(2) is answered, or whether the two characterisations need to be reconciled expressly.
- § 4.3(c) — whether the single permitted No action ground is drawn narrowly enough that the mandatory-referral list retains its effect, and whether a malpractice claim at filing belongs on that list (see the existing question above).
- **§ 4.3(a) and § 1.5 — whether the qualifier added to the duty to refer is sufficient**, or whether the interaction between a broad mandatory-referral duty and the patient safety work product exclusion needs more than a savings clause. Added 2026-08-30.
- **§ 4.3(d) — whether the law of any jurisdiction in which the Entity operates requires a report concerning an impaired or unfit practitioner**; on whom that duty falls — the Entity, this Committee, or the individual who holds the information; and whether referral under an internal policy satisfies the duty or merely postpones it. The Charter is drafted to comply without stating any jurisdiction's rule, and this is the question that tells the Entity what complying requires. Added 2026-08-30.
- § 4.3(d) — whether routing health, impairment and fitness matters out of professional review affects HCQIA analysis where a restriction is later imposed on health-related grounds, and whether the Practitioner Health pathway should itself be chartered as a protected committee in any of the five states.
- § 6.7 as revised — whether locating the § 11112(a) reasonable belief in the Committee rather than the Panel is the right allocation where the Panel includes external reviewers and non-member panelists who do not vote.

- **§ 2.7 and § 2.9 — whether state peer review privilege in each of the five states covers a non-clinician employee serving as records custodian, and whether her dual operational role affects that.** Not analyzed anywhere in the current work.
- **§ 5.7.6 — whether the two follow-up examinations after an agreed resolution are an investigation for Data Bank purposes.** The matter closes when the plan is completed, and the examinations run afterwards as disclosed follow-up of a closed matter. If they are treated as an investigation, a physician who resigns during the two quarters may be resigning while under investigation.
- **§§ 2.5.1 and 4.4 — whether state peer review privilege in each of the five states covers a Department Chair who screens referrals for the Committee without being a member of it.** The Charter treats the Department Chair as a person assisting a professional review body and requires the § 13.5 undertaking; the same question is already asked for the Coordinator under §§ 2.7 and 2.9.

- **§ 4.3(a) — whether repeated short removals from duties on performance-related grounds (none longer than [seven] days, three or more within [ninety] days) could be characterised as a restriction of clinical privileges for Data Bank or state reporting purposes.** Added 2026-10-15 with the cumulative removal trigger.

- **§ 15.11.1 — notice to counsel.** (a) Whether telling counsel, in a fixed form, that a matter does or does not present a circumstance requiring counsel's attention affects peer review privilege over the Committee's record in any of the five states, given that counsel's purpose is the Entity's legal position. (b) Whether a written request by counsel for more, and the Committee's answer to it, stays within the protected process. (c) Whether answering within the [ten]-business-day screening period meets the notice-of-circumstances terms of the Entity's malpractice coverage, or whether some circumstances need notice sooner. (d) Whether the disclosure-of-error circumstance should refer to any state's disclosure or apology statute. Added 2026-11-10.
- **§ 12.1 — recusal after a review for counsel.** Whether a reviewer who assessed the care at counsel's request and is then recused here is exposed to any argument that the later peer review drew on counsel's assessment, and whether the separation in § 15.11.1 is enough to keep counsel's file and the Committee's record apart. Added 2026-11-10.

- **§ 4.5.2 — the answer to Management.** (a) Whether telling Management that no action affecting a Practitioner's practice is required, while a review is open, could support a claim of negligent supervision or retention if a patient is later harmed, and whether the defined meaning in § 4.5.2 is enough answer. (b) Whether giving that answer to Management, a person holding Employment Authority, affects privilege over the screening record. (c) Whether the notice of an interim restriction to Management under § 9.9, which states its terms, affects privilege over the matter, and whether terms specific enough to schedule by can be stated without disclosing the substance. Added 2026-11-10.

- **§ 8.5.2 — teaching points.** (a) Whether teaching points recorded in a closed review could be used to show that the Entity knew of a Practitioner's knowledge gap, in a later claim of negligent supervision or retention, and whether that is managed by how they are recorded. (b) Whether the provision that a teaching point does not by itself establish notice in a later matter binds anything beyond the Entity's own bodies. Added 2026-11-10.

## Clinical Quality Committee Charter

- **Indemnification and insurance for members and persons assisting.** Committed in the Authorization, and asked in full at the PRC Charter. Noted here because this Committee's members and the persons executing its audits are exposed to the same question, and because its own status under § 1.3 makes the answer less certain for them than for the PRC. Added 2026-08-30.

- **§ 5.5(c)(5) — whether an employee of the learning and development function who learns individually attributed audit information is covered by state peer review privilege in each of the five states.** This is the same question already asked about the Coordinator as records custodian, and should be put in the same breath. Added 2026-08-30.
- § 5.5(c)(5) and § 5.11 — whether an offer of support that is expressly not required, paired with a scheduled follow-up look, is characterised as a response to a known deficiency or as a failure to act on one, if an adverse outcome follows. This is the scenario in which the unresolved question about the Committee's protected record becomes expensive, and it is an argument for answering that question before adoption rather than after.

- Whether a committee whose principal output is prospective standards and education qualifies as a protected committee under each of the five statutes, given the statutes contemplate retrospective review. This is a different question from the one asked about the PRC and it has not been analyzed.
- Whether § 8.1(a) — an open, expressly non-confidential record held by a privileged committee — creates any argument that the Committee's protected record is also operational. The intent is the opposite: that a clean split protects the audit record.
- Whether adoption of an internal standard under § 4.2 creates any exposure distinct from adopting a published guideline, and whether the required statement of evidence and reasoning is sufficient answer.
- Whether psychotherapist membership affects the composition analysis under Ind. Code § 34-30-15 or N.C. Gen. Stat. § 90-21.22A, both written around a narrower set of disciplines.
- § 1.6 — confirmation that the stated separation from facility QAPI is sufficient, given Entity clinicians participate in facility QAPI individually.
- **The Authorization and state privilege.** The same question asked at the PRC Charter: whether a committee established by the Chief Executive Officer's authorization rather than by the governing body is protected in each of the five states, Indiana in particular. Added 2026-09-28.
- **§ 5.5(d)(4) — whether a Practitioner's own disclosure of a commendation letter affects protection of the record it came from.** Added 2026-09-28.
- **§ 7.6(b) — whether a practice description prepared by the education function after the audit selected the facility is safely outside the protected record**, given the document-by-document approach Ohio takes under § 8.1. Added 2026-09-28.
- **Proposed (not yet in the Charter) — whether an APC's own sharing of their close-out notice with their assigned supervising physician affects privilege over the Committee's record.** The proposal lets the APC choose to share nothing, only a request for help naming the clinical area, or the full close-out; the APC sends it, and the Committee neither transmits nor records the choice.

- **§ 7.8 — whether the Committee's approval of clinical policies affects the protection of its record**, given that policies are operational documents surveyors read and Ohio's document-by-document approach; and whether approval by the quality committee bears on a policy's use as evidence of the standard of care, beyond what § 7.8.5 states about the Entity's approval. Added 2026-11-13.

## Audit Referral Policy

- **Whether an adopted, open referral schedule assists or exposes the Entity** where a Practitioner referred under it contends the criteria were applied unevenly. The schedule is what makes even application demonstrable, and it is equally what would make uneven application demonstrable. The obligation in § 6.1 was drafted on the view that the first consideration dominates.
- **Whether § 6.1 creates any duty running to a patient or a contracted facility** where a criterion was met and the volume brake under Article V suppressed the referral. The brake is the systems response § 5.9 of the Charter requires, and every activation is recorded and reported, but the question should be answered before adoption.
- **Whether a comparison group's definition, classified open under § 8.1.1 of the Charter and disseminated with the standard, carries any risk to the privilege of its constitution**, which is derived from observations and protected under § 8.1(b). Section 4.1 provides that a group is not constructed after the Practitioner's rate is known, so the open half is not itself derived from audit data.
- **Whether the first-period discretion under § 6.6** — a recorded, reported and expiring latitude to decline referral — is better or worse, in an immunity analysis under 42 U.S.C. § 11112 and the five state statutes, than either a fixed obligation or a standing discretion.

### From the Policy skeleton

Earlier wording of three of the questions above, kept as written.

- Whether an open, adopted referral schedule strengthens or weakens the Entity's position where a Practitioner referred under it later argues the criteria were applied unevenly. The schedule is what makes even application demonstrable; it is also what makes uneven application demonstrable, and Decision 1 turns partly on that answer.
- Whether the Policy's status in the open record under § 8.1.1 affects the privilege analysis for the comparison-group constructions it will contain, which are derived from protected audit data.
- Whether a mandatory schedule creates any duty running to a patient or facility where a criterion was met and the volume brake at § 8 suppressed the referral. The brake is a systems response required by § 5.9, and the Policy records every activation, but the question should be asked before adoption rather than after.
