# Clinical Governance — what is in this packet

*The charters themselves are not included here; these are the plain-language documents that describe what they do.*

---

## The order to read them in

The first four describe the structure. The last three describe what it is like to be inside it — as someone whose charts are audited, or as someone asked to serve on a committee.

| | Document | The question it answers |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | **Framework Overview** | What are the three bodies, and what does each one decide? Two pages of prose; the fastest way in. |
| 02 | **Seat Map** | Who sits where, what each body can do, and what each body expressly cannot do. Drawn rather than written. |
| 03 | **What Happens to a Clinician** | One matter followed from the audit through to a final decision, with every point at which it can end. |
| 04 | **Where the Existing Work Goes** | Which of today's standing activities each body absorbs. The answer to "is this another new thing on top of what we already do?" |
| 05 | **How a Quality Audit Would Work** | Written for the clinician being audited: how charts get picked, what the letter says, what happens next. |
| 06 | **Clinical Quality Committee — Member Guide** | The Quality Committee's charter in plain language, for someone deciding whether to serve on it. |
| 07 | **Peer Review Committee — Member Guide** | The same for the Peer Review Committee, in more detail, because the charter is more intricate. |

---

## Three things worth knowing before reading

**The Quality Committee has no authority over any individual, at any point.** It writes standards, teaches them, measures against them, and fixes what the measurement shows is broken. Where a standard is not met, it offers education and looks again at a sample of charts after a set period; where the finding also meets a written referral rule, it hands the matter to the Peer Review Committee and stops. It cannot restrict practice, require anything of anyone, or decide whether care met the standard of care.

**The panel investigates; the Committee decides; only the Council restricts.** Peer review is deliberately split across three steps held by different people, and no one holding authority over a clinician's employment sits in any of them.

**The audit is not the main way a concern arrives.** Anything raising a patient-safety or standard-of-care question can be referred directly, by anyone, with no written standard required and no threshold to clear. The audit measures against standards the organization has adopted and published; everything else is measured against accepted practice, which exists whether or not anything has been written down.
