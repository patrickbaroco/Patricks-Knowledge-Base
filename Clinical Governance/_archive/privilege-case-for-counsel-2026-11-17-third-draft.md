# The Peer Review Committee: Privilege, and What the Charter Requires

**Memo to Jeff Wells, from Patrick Baroco · 2026-11-17**
*These are my preliminary working conclusions for your review. Section numbers refer to the Peer Review Committee Charter draft of 2026-11-17, which accompanies this memo.*

---

## Purpose and requested review

Jeff,

The attached Charter gives Eventus a consistent, clinician-led process for reviewing individual care, supporting improvement, and making decisions about a clinician's authorization to practice fairly. It keeps that work separate from employment and practitioner-health decisions, and it sets out the reporting and procedural safeguards we intend to follow.

I would like your help confirming the simplest legally sound way to establish and operate it across our five states. My reading finds plausible statutory routes in Virginia and Ohio, questions of membership and litigation scope in North Carolina, and questions about the organizing entity in Indiana and Kentucky. Those are questions for you to confirm; the Charter cannot resolve them on its own.

Much of the procedure is an intentional Eventus choice. Please identify any legal requirement we have missed, any provision that overstates the protection available, and any step we could simplify without giving up the clinical independence and practitioner protections we want to keep.

The Charter takes effect only on your written confirmation of the Committee's formation, the privilege bases it relies on and their limits, and the safeguards they require (§ 16.3).

**I'm asking for your guidance on three decisions:**

1. **Formation and protection.** What instruments, qualifications and state-specific safeguards must be in place?
2. **Reporting and process.** What needs correcting before the Committee handles cases under this Charter?
3. **Proportionality.** Which procedures are necessary, and which could be simplified while keeping the design?

Parts 1 and 2 set out my reading. Part 3 is a checklist of the specific questions behind the three decisions.

# Supporting analysis and questions

## Part 1. Privilege, state by state

### Virginia

Va. Code § 8.01-581.17(B)(iii) protects a committee established under guidelines adopted by a statewide association of Virginia-licensed providers. The Medical Society of Virginia's guidelines apply to committees of an entity "that employs or contracts with physicians," which describes Eventus. Organization by the governing body is not part of the test. The Charter adopts the guidelines by reference (§ 1.4).

**To strengthen it,** Eventus can execute the Medical Society's adoption form, and the Committee's scope can be checked against the guidelines' own description of what a committee reviews. **Limits that stay:** facts and medical records are not privileged; a court may order disclosure on good cause in extraordinary circumstances; and an oral communication about a specific incident, made to a committee formed under the guidelines route, as ours is, is privileged only if made more than 24 hours after the incident.

### Ohio

R.C. 2305.25 defines a health care entity and a peer review committee by function, and includes for-profit corporations and their committees and hearing processes. Nothing requires a governing body to create the committee. The party claiming the privilege must prove that a qualifying committee exists and that each withheld record falls within it (*Smith v. Cleveland Clinic*, 2011-Ohio-6648). A weak affidavit does not by itself defeat the claim, and a court may review records in camera (*Stull v. Summa Health Sys.*, 2024-Ohio-5718).

**To strengthen it:** an affidavit template drafted now, and a document register kept from the first case under the Charter (§ 13.2).

### North Carolina

N.C. Gen. Stat. § 90-21.22A defines a medical review committee by composition and purpose: providers licensed under Chapter 90, evaluating the quality, cost or necessity of care. There is no organizer requirement, and nurse practitioners and physician assistants are Chapter 90 licensees.

**Litigation scope.** The discovery bar in subsection (c) applies in actions against listed defendants: a Chapter 90 provider, a hospital, an ambulatory surgical facility, or a provider-sponsored organization. Where the reviewed clinician is a defendant, my reading is that the records are protected in that action. An action naming Eventus alone is open. **Membership.** A member licensed only in another state may not count as a Chapter 90 licensee on a North Carolina matter.

### Indiana: uncertain, with several possible routes

Ind. Code § 34-30-15 protects a committee organized in one of the ways listed in § 34-6-2.1-145(a), at least half of whose members are professional health care providers. The question is whether Eventus can organize its own committee under one of those routes while keeping the clinical authority and independence the Charter gives it.

| Route | What would have to be true |
|---|---|
| **Eventus as a "professional health care organization"** (§§ 34-6-2.1-164, -165(14), -165(25)) | Under (14), Eventus's members, shareholders, subsidiaries, **affiliates**, or partners are listed providers or professional corporations of health professionals. Under (25), Eventus wholly or partly owns or controls a listed entity |
| **Eventus as a regional or local organization of professional health care providers** (§ 34-6-2.1-145(a)) | Eventus's actual clinician organization fits this separate route, which most naturally describes an association. Untested |

**The affiliate question.** Licensed health facilities are among the listed providers. Eventus supplies the attending clinicians, and often the medical director, for nursing facilities in Indiana, under contracts that tie its clinical work to theirs. The statute does not define "affiliate." On the usual corporate reading, an affiliate is an entity under common control, and a services contract would not qualify. On a broader reading, an organization whose clinicians are integrated into a facility's care, and whose physicians direct its medical care under 42 C.F.R. § 483.70, may be affiliated with it. Our Indiana facility and medical-director contracts may be relevant to whether Eventus has a qualifying affiliation. Please assess the statutory meaning of "affiliate" against those agreements, any ownership, control or shared-governance relationships, and Eventus's relationship with LTC ACO. We have not identified authority establishing that a services contract alone qualifies.

**If a route applies,** the remaining question is the constituting instrument. A board resolution can document valid organization, but it cannot cure ineligibility on its own. **If none applies,** an external organization with its own authority (a facility's professional staff, a medical society) could establish a committee, but that committee would not be Eventus's PRC under another name, and its protection would not automatically reach Eventus's decisions. Eventus could also create a qualifying relationship, such as an affiliated professional corporation, though that is a corporate decision that would make sense only for other reasons. If no Indiana privilege route is available, we would need to decide whether to proceed without relying on that privilege. HCQIA may still provide damages protection for qualifying physician-review actions that meet its requirements.

**Separate from the route:** physician assistants are not among the listed providers, so a PA's review may sit outside the statute and PAs may not count toward the composition floor. A provider under investigation may see the records about their own practice and may appear and rebut (§ 34-30-15-4); the Charter yields to that right (§ 13.4.1), and *Savage v. Indiana University Health* (S.D. Ind. Apr. 27, 2026, magistrate judge's order) read it broadly.

### Kentucky

KRS 311.377 now applies in any civil action, and *Jewish Hospital v. Perry* (Ky. 2021) protected a root cause analysis under it. Subsection (1) protects review bodies constituted by listed organizations: hospitals, hospices and home health agencies, insurers and HMOs, organized medical staffs, medical societies, and governmental agencies. A physician group is not plainly on that list, and whether Eventus or a related organization fits a category is the question.

Subsection (2) also requires an attestation of participation in a patient safety and quality improvement initiative. The CQC's program is one, and Eventus's participation in LTC ACO's quality program may support it. The attestation does not answer the organizer question.

### The federal layer

- **HCQIA gives immunity from damages, not privilege.** It does not keep the record out of discovery.
- **There is no federal peer review privilege** in our circuits (*Virmani v. Novant Health*, 4th Cir. 2001; *Memorial Hospital v. Shadur*, 7th Cir. 1981). The Committee cannot rely on state privilege alone to prevent disclosure in federal employment litigation, which is one reason its records are written on the assumption that the clinician will read them (§ 13.4).
- **PSQIA provides separate federal protection** for qualifying patient safety work product, including information assembled or developed for reporting and actually reported to a listed PSO, and qualifying deliberations or analysis within a patient safety evaluation system. It never covers records made to meet an independent obligation (*Baptist Healthcare System v. Kitchen*, Ky. 2025). The Charter keeps the Committee outside any patient safety evaluation system (§ 1.5). A PSO is an option for the CQC's audit work, especially in Indiana and Kentucky. It is not needed to launch the PRC.

## Part 2. What the Charter requires

### How the rules are sorted

- **Legal duty.** Binding whenever its conditions are met, whatever the Charter says.
- **Condition of a protection.** Required only because Eventus relies on a specific protection, or has chosen a specific safe harbor.
- **Eventus choice.** Our design. Many of these support a protection without being required for it.
- **Statement of law.** A sentence describing the law. It needs your check, and it is not itself a rule.

### Legal duties

- Data Bank reporting for physicians: an adverse action on Clinical Authorization lasting more than thirty days, a restriction in force more than thirty days while a review runs, and a resignation or surrender during an investigation. With these come the copy to the state licensing board, registration, and the attestation (§§ 9.4, 15.5 to 15.9).
- Any state duty to report an impaired practitioner. The Charter leaves it with whoever owns it (§ 4.3(d)).
- Indiana's access right (§ 13.4.1).
- Preserving records once litigation is reasonably foreseeable (§ 13.8).

### Conditions of a protection

**Federal immunity under HCQIA.**

- An immediate restriction for imminent danger, followed by notice and a hearing or other adequate procedure (§§ 9.1, 9.3).
- The fourteen-day investigatory restriction (§ 9.8).
- The notice-and-hearing safe harbor: no restriction without Article X; what the notice must say; thirty days to request a hearing and thirty days' notice of it; a panel not in direct economic competition with the clinician; the rights at the hearing; a written recommendation and decision (§§ 3.2(a), 5.5, 8.4, 10.1 to 10.7, 10.10). This is the route we chose to meet the statute's fairness standard. It is one route, and missing a single condition does not by itself lose the immunity.

**State privilege.** A lawfully formed committee of an eligible organizer (§ 1.1); in Virginia, adoption of the guidelines (§ 1.4); in North Carolina, Chapter 90 licensees; in Indiana, at least half the members licensed providers (§ 2.2).

### Statements of law to check

- What the Committee is established to serve as in each state (§ 1.3).
- Clinical Authorization as clinical privileges under 42 U.S.C. § 11151(3) (§ 1.6(b)).
- The description of the federal protections and their limits (§§ 15.1 to 15.4).
- That underlying facts and medical records are not protected (§ 13.7). This describes a limit of the privilege. It does not create a duty to disclose.

### Eventus choices you should know about

The following are the principal Eventus design choices I would like you to assess:

- **The Committee carries the burden of proof at a hearing, by a preponderance** (§ 10.5(e)). The safe harbor does not require this; it gives the clinician more than the statute does.
- **The Chief Executive Officer establishes the Committee** (§ 1.1). Lawful formation is the condition, and the CEO is our chosen way of meeting it.
- **Deliberations are passed to no one** (§ 3.3). This supports privilege without being required for it, and it gives way to access the law grants.
- **Your role** (§ 2.12). Counsel advises the Committee on its authority, the law, fair process, confidentiality, disclosure and reporting, and attends at the Chair's invitation for a stated legal purpose. Whether a legal duty applies is decided on your advice; the Committee finds the clinical facts and makes the clinical decisions. Routine review does not need your attendance or approval.
- **The Panel investigates and the Committee decides** (§ 6.7). The law needs one identifiable, authorized review body. How the work is divided inside it is ours.
- **Minutes, separated records and a case register** (§§ 2.11, 13.1, 13.2). Built to give an Ohio court document-level proof. No statute prescribes this system.
- **The Committee works outside any patient safety evaluation system** (§ 1.5).
- **The agreed resolution** (§ 5.7). A clinician with a bounded knowledge gap may accept an education plan with no finding; the Committee then examines a sample of the clinician's charts in two later quarters.
- **Reporting APCs.** Federal reporting for APCs is permissive. The Charter applies one consistent rule, to be set with you (§ 15.9).

## Part 3. Checklist

**1. Formation and protection**

- **Indiana, the organizing route.** Do Eventus's ownership, membership, professional-corporation, affiliate or control relationships make it a professional health care organization? In particular, do its contracts to staff and medically direct Indiana nursing facilities make those facilities affiliates under § 34-6-2.1-165(14)?
- **Indiana, the regional-organization route.** Does Eventus's clinician organization qualify as a regional or local organization of professional health care providers?
- **Indiana, the instrument.** If a route applies, what board action or other constituting instrument should we use?
- **Indiana, physician assistants.** How should a PA's review be handled, and do PAs count toward the composition floor?
- **Indiana, access.** When does § 34-30-15-4 apply to our reviews, what redaction does it permit, and does it reach records of deliberation?
- **Kentucky.** Does Eventus or a related organization fit a category in KRS 311.377(1)? What attestation language satisfies subsection (2), and does LTC ACO participation support it?
- **North Carolina.** Does the § 90-21.22A(c) bar protect Eventus in an action naming Eventus alone? Must every member acting on a North Carolina matter hold a North Carolina license?
- **Virginia.** Should Eventus execute the Medical Society's adoption form?
- **Ohio.** Will you draft the affidavit template and approve the document register?
- **Optional: a patient safety organization.** Is it worth running the CQC's audit work, at least in Indiana and Kentucky, inside a patient safety evaluation system? This is not needed to launch the PRC.

**2. Reporting and process**

- **After an agreed resolution.** If a clinician resigns during the two later chart examinations, is that a resignation during an investigation for Data Bank purposes, and is that exposure acceptable (§§ 5.7.6, 15.9)?
- **APC reporting.** What consistent rule should govern reporting events concerning APCs (§ 15.9)?
- **Information about APCs.** Federal protection for a person who provides information covers only information about physicians. What do the state statutes give someone reporting on an APC (§ 15.2)?
- **Chair recusal.** The Chair chairs both committees, screens, and joins in imposing restrictions. Are the recusal triggers in § 12.1 adequate?
- **Health-only restrictions.** Where a restriction stands on a matter referred to the practitioner health pathway, can that pathway reach a decision within the forty-five days § 9.5 allows (§ 9.7)?
- **Employment consequences.** Where a restriction leaves a clinician unable to do the job, how is the employment decision kept separate from the Committee's (§§ 3.5, 15.3)?

**3. Proportionality**

- Confirm or correct the sorting in Part 2, above all anything I have called a choice that the law in fact requires.
- Check the statements of law listed in Part 2.
- Tell me which procedures you would simplify.
