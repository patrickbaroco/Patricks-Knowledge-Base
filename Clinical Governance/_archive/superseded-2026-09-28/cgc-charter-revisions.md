# Council Charter — Revision Record, 2026-08-30

*Companion to `cgc-charter-draft.md`. Records an external governance review of the Council Charter, read against the revised CQC and PRC Charters. All changes described here have been applied, § 8.6 included — it was held back from the main pass and settled separately, at item 12.*

**Context.** This was the first review of the Council Charter since the CQC and PRC were revised through three rounds. As expected, most of what it found were **seams between documents** rather than defects inside one: provisions that were correct when written and became wrong when the Committee charters moved. Eleven numbered issues, several cleanups, and one counsel item. Nine confirmed as stated, two confirmed with a wider remedy, five further defects found in verification.

---

## 1. Retrospective focused review could not be both an Autonomy Tier and a Quality Intervention

**Confirmed, and it is an internal contradiction rather than a conceptual clash.** § 2.1 makes the Autonomy Tier part of Clinical Authorization. § 2.3 makes any action that may adversely affect Clinical Authorization a Professional Review Action. § 3.2(a) of the PRC Charter forbids the PRC to take a Professional Review Action. And PRC § 8.2(d) has the PRC imposing retrospective focused review directly, on the express footing that it does not restrict authority.

On the earlier drafting the PRC, exercising the power its Charter gives it, was doing the single thing that Charter prohibits.

**Resolution.** Removed from the ladder, which now runs Unrestricted / mandatory consultation / proctoring or prospective approval / any other restriction on scope. Every tier other than Unrestricted restricts authority. Focused review is stated to be **not an Autonomy Tier and not part of Clinical Authorization**, and lives only as a Quality Intervention under PRC § 8.2(d). The reasoning is recorded in the section itself, since the earlier arrangement will look deliberate to anyone who reads an old draft.

**Consequence for counsel.** The reportability question is re-pointed and now asked once across the two Charters — where the line sits between focused review and mandatory consultation — rather than twice in different terms.

---

## 2. The records architecture needed the work the CQC's record already had

**Confirmed.** § 10.2 had a single bucket: "the Professional Review record (Council, PRC, hearing panels)." That undoes in the filing what three provisions establish in the process — PRC § 8.1 (deliberations are not transmitted), § 9.6 (no participant in the PRC review sits on the hearing panel), and the governance/case distinction implicit throughout Article VII. It also filed an individual's adverse action alongside charter adoption, elections, and aggregate reporting.

**Resolution — the proposed five buckets, plus two sentences the split makes necessary.**

- (a) the Council's **governance record**; (b) the **PRC's protected record**, retained there, with only the § 8.1 transmittal reaching the Council; (c) the Council's **case record**; (d) each **hearing panel's record**; then the CQC's open and protected records, employment records, and patient safety work product as before.
- **Custody is not access.** § 10.1 makes the Coordinator custodian of (a) through (d) — the same person holds the PRC record, the Council case record, and the hearing panel record that § 9.6 says panelists must not have seen. Without this sentence the separation reads as theatre. The rule is that separation governs **who receives** material, not who files it.
- **Classification is not privilege.** Borrowed from CQC § 8.3, so the architecture is not mistaken for an assertion about what any court will protect.

---

## 3. The systems remand went to the wrong committee

**Confirmed.** § 8.3 permitted a remand from the Council directly to the CQC. PRC § 10.3, PRC § 11.1, and CQC § 7.3 are all written on the opposite assumption: the remand goes to the PRC, which closes the individual review, notifies the Practitioner that it closed without adverse finding, and refers the systems finding onward **without Practitioner attribution**.

**Resolution — accepted.** The Council remands to the PRC on systems grounds; the PRC proceeds under § 10.3 and Article XI of its Charter. The section now says why the intermediate step is not ceremony: **it strips the attribution before the finding reaches a committee that has no jurisdiction over individuals and maintains an open, expressly non-confidential record.**

---

## 4. Precautionary restrictions were not synchronised with the PRC

**Confirmed on all four counts.**

- **No voting threshold.** § 7.3(c) set a reduced quorum of three Action-Eligible Members; nothing said how the determination is carried. § 7.4(c)'s threshold applies to a Professional Review Action, and these documents deliberately decline to classify precautionary ratification as one. Resolved with a **route-specific rule** — majority of Action-Eligible Members present and voting at a § 7.3(c) meeting — drafted expressly so that it gives the determination a threshold **without deciding the HCQIA classification**, which remains with counsel.
- **"The Secretary" does not exist** in this Charter. It is the Coordinator.
- **The forty-five-day hard stop, the requested-hearing exception, and the thirty-day continuing review** were added to PRC §§ 9.4 and 9.5 and appeared nowhere here — although the Council is the body that has to carry them out. All three are now stated at § 8.7, with a cross-reference at § 7.1.2 so the convening obligation follows.
- **New § 11.2A** makes the precautionary reportability analysis explicit: not a final Professional Review Action, nonetheless capable of being reportable, and the question is asked in every case running past thirty days from imposition, whether or not the review has concluded.

Also added: where the underlying matter is dispositioned under PRC § 4.4(e), **PRC § 9.7 governs** and no recommendation from the Committee is to be awaited.

---

## 5. The immunity language contradicted the CQC Charter

**Confirmed, and this is the item most likely to be found by someone looking for it.** § 12.1 extended § 11111(a)(1) to "the Committees," and § 12.2 gave § 11111(a)(2) immunity to anyone providing information to "the Council or a Committee." The CQC Charter says the CQC is **by design not a professional review body**, and warns in terms that whether § 11111(a)(2) reaches information given to it is undetermined and a question for counsel.

Two governing documents giving opposite answers about whether a referring clinician is protected is worse than either answer alone.

**Resolution — split.** § 12.1 covers the Council, the PRC and hearing panels. New **§ 12.1.1** states that the CQC and persons participating in its work have such protections, if any, as applicable law provides, that its own Charter governs the question, and that this Charter makes no assertion on it. § 12.2 is limited to the Council, the PRC, and hearing panels, and repeats the CQC Charter's practical direction: **bring a concern about an identified Practitioner to the PRC**, where the statutory fit is clearer.

---

## 6. Psychotherapist scope, and the Council's missing powers

**Confirmed on both.** § 1.6 admitted psychotherapists only at the hearing stage under § 9.2(d); the revised PRC § 6.5 contemplates PRC review under a standing discipline-specific addendum adopted by the Council. And § 4.2 did not contain three jobs the PRC Charter assigns to the Council — appointing PRC members (PRC § 2.2), maintaining the Reviewer Pool (§ 2.5), and adopting that addendum (§ 6.5).

**Resolution.** § 1.6 recognises the addendum route. § 4.2 gains (j) for those three, and — **beyond what the review identified, from the same day's PRC work** — (k) designation by resolution of the pathway to which health, impairment and fitness matters are referred under PRC § 4.3(d), and (l) receipt of the reporting § 4.3(d)(2) requires.

---

## 7. Composition mechanics — three problems, and a fourth

**All confirmed.** The Chair was elected "annually" and served a two-year term. § 3.4 said At-Large Members are the only class eligible for the chairmanship while § 3.5 stated three conditions that did not in fact exclude every other class — a Primary Care or Psychiatry chair who is a practising clinician without Employment Authority and sits on neither Committee satisfies all three. And §§ 3.2 and 3.9 did not work together: § 3.9's mechanism addresses organizational growth, and nothing handled an At-Large Member promoted into Employment Authority mid-term, or a vacancy.

**Resolution.** Two-year term, election on expiry, removal, or vacancy. § 3.5 now names the At-Large class and states the conditions **in addition to** it. New **§ 3.11** covers loss of eligibility — acquiring Employment Authority ends the seat that day, with no determination required or available — and vacancies in At-Large seats and in the two offices. § 3.9 is scoped to size and growth.

**The fourth, added beyond the review:** § 3.2 said the floors apply at all times and § 3.9 restored them "at or before the next regular meeting," leaving a window in which the Council could act while out of compliance. **A floor failure now suspends the action for which that floor is a condition until it is restored**, with action validly taken beforehand unaffected. An action taken by an improperly composed body is precisely what gets challenged.

---

## 8. Recusal aligned with the PRC's

**Confirmed.** § 6.1 recused permanently on a supervisory relationship of any age, had no direct-economic-competition trigger — **although § 9.2(e) imposes one on hearing panels in the same document** — and its residual clause reached only personal, familial, and financial relationships.

**Resolution.** The PRC's § 12.1 list adopted wholesale: five-year lookback on supervision, economic competition, prior service as clinical monitor under PRC § 8.6, and a general reasonable-person residual that expressly carries older supervisory relationships. The two lists are stated to be kept aligned.

**§ 6.3 — confirmed and correct.** A Committee chair presenting under § 6.2 holds the underlying material by virtue of the office, so "does not receive the case materials" could not be true of them. **The rule is that they receive nothing further as a Council member, take no part in deliberation, and cast no vote** — not that prior knowledge ceases to exist.

---

## 9. § 4.5 was wider than everything around it

**Confirmed, and it conflicted with three provisions rather than with the spirit of them.** § 5.2 separates the tracks, § 5.3 says documents do not move between them, § 10.6 permits no disclosure of a professional review record to a third party without a recorded counsel analysis. § 4.5 then allowed the Council to "transmit quality findings," and said its record "shall not be the *sole* basis" for a designation — **conceding that it may be a partial one.**

**Resolution.** No professional review record is transmitted for the purpose of a designation. The Council may confirm only the fact and terms of a final action of which Management already has notice under § 5.1, plus facts independently available from outside the record. Findings, evidence, deliberations, and the record of any matter that did not result in final action do not travel.

---

## 10. Two defects already fixed in the PRC Charter had been recreated here

**Both confirmed.** § 10.7 drew the effective-date boundary around *matters* rather than records, which on a literal reading makes pre-effective-date care unreviewable. § 13.4 *assigned* every serving Practitioner Unrestricted Clinical Authorization, which would erase a pre-existing restriction, supervision requirement, or contractual scope limit in the very act of adopting the Framework.

**Resolution.** § 10.7 takes the PRC's formulation — pre-existing records are not imported, historical care remains reviewable. § 13.4 becomes a transition provision: implementation alters nobody's authority in either direction, existing conditions continue according to their own terms, and **it grants no authority that did not exist the day before.**

---

## 11. The amendment rules contradicted each other

**Confirmed.** § 4.3 reserves material amendment of the Charter to the Board; § 13.2 required Board ratification only for Articles I, III, IV, V, VI and IX — **omitting Article VII**, which carries the quorum and voting rules for Professional Review Actions, and also Articles XI and XII.

**Resolution.** § 4.3's rule governs, with an illustrative and expressly non-exhaustive list, and doubt as to materiality resolved in favour of ratification.

---

## Cleanups

- **"The Committee Charter" is undefined** — § 2.5 defines *the Committees* as both the CQC and the PRC. The review caught the § 3.7.5 instance; §§ 7.1.2 and 8.7 had the same defect. All now name the PRC Charter.
- **"The Secretary" at § 8.7** is the Coordinator.
- **The audit referral schedule had three names across three Charters** — "audit referral threshold schedule" here, "a written schedule" at CQC § 6.2, "the Audit Referral Policy" at PRC § 4.1(a). It is **the Audit Referral Policy** in all three, and CQC § 6.2 now says so.
- **The composition math assumed a recusal no provision required.** The drafting note has always reasoned that the Primary Care and Psychiatry chairs recuse on their own service lines; nothing made them. § 6.2 now does, on the same terms as the Committee chairs and without further finding.
- **§ 7.4(a)** now states that a remand neither takes nor declines a Professional Review Action, and that a systems remand's disposition is made by the PRC. The general-business vote includes the members § 7.4(b) excludes from case dispositions, and a remand is the one general-business item that touches an individual matter.

---

## 12. § 8.6 — resolved separately, and not as a parallel to PRC § 8.6

Held back from the main pass and settled on its own, because the PRC's answer does not transfer. A Quality Intervention is educational and retrospective, and one person can conduct it and report on it. **A restriction on authority is not observed; it is performed** — by a proctor, a required consultant, an approver, prospectively and in real time. "Who monitors compliance" collapsed three functions into one noun.

**Four parts.**

- **The performer is named and is not a monitor.** Discipline-appropriate on § 9.2(d) terms, reports facts only — that a required consultation or approval was sought and given or refused — makes no assessment of exit criteria, and is recused from every Council determination in the matter, including the one that ends the restriction. § 6.1(h) carries the trigger.
- **Employment Authority is not an automatic bar here**, departing deliberately from PRC § 8.6(d). The pool competent to proctor a particular kind of decision may be very small; this role decides nothing about the Practitioner; and **a rule that cannot be satisfied leaves the restriction unenforceable**, which is worse than the risk the exclusion guards against. A clinician without Employment Authority is preferred, and where none is available the Council records that.
- **The Coordinator administers** — terms, reports, duration, bringing the matter back on the termination date. From the record, no contact with the Practitioner, as at PRC § 8.6(b).
- **The Council determines termination**, on an Action-Eligible vote under § 7.4(b). Ending a restriction on Clinical Authorization is a decision about Clinical Authorization, and cannot belong to whoever was watching.

**§ 8.6(e) is the substantive addition, and it fills a gap neither the review nor the original drafting had reached.** PRC § 8.3 protects Tier One against indefinite extension. Nothing protected Tier Two, where the stakes are higher and the action is capable of being reportable. **A restriction now terminates on its stated date and is continued only by a new Professional Review Action with the full Article IX process.** Where the exit criteria are unmet on that date the action still terminates, and the Council takes a fresh action if warranted. That is uncomfortable by design: a restriction rolled forward without process is one the Practitioner never again received the rights that attach to imposing.

**§ 8.6(f)** states that a restriction operates on the Clinical Authorization rather than on the roster — the Practitioner is simply not authorized to act otherwise, and any obligation of Management to schedule consistently follows from the authorization, not from a Council directive. Without it, the operational necessity of implementing a restriction reads as the Council directing Management contrary to § 4.4.

---

## Where the three Charters now stand

The pattern of this review is worth recording: **most of what it found were seams, not defects** — provisions correct when written that became wrong when the other two Charters moved. That is the predictable cost of revising documents that cross-reference each other, and it argues for a combined read after any future round rather than sequential single-charter reviews.

What remains across all three is not drafting:

- the **Audit Referral Policy**, undrafted, which CQC § 6.2, PRC § 4.7 and the CQC close-out all depend on;
- the **Practitioner Health pathway**, now designated by Council resolution under § 4.2(k);
- the **psychotherapist addendum**, a precondition to opening any such review;
- and the counsel questions in all three "For counsel" lists, now including the single re-pointed reportability question on focused review.

# Composition revision, 2026-09-18

*Applied to `cgc-charter-draft.md`. Article III, § 6.2, § 7.3(b) and § 13.1, with the open items and composition math brought current. Recorded as item 23 in the Charter's own list.*

**Context.** The seventeen-seat Council seated every regional leader by office and carried § 3.9 to manage what growth would do to that. The binding constraint turned out to be recruitment, not growth: six at-large seats, PRC standing membership, and hearing panels together asked for more practicing clinicians than the organization can supply at once. The Council is now nine voting members and a non-voting chair.

## 1. Two voting classes

Clinical members are the Chair of the Primary Care Committee, the Chair of the Psychiatry Committee, and five At-Large Practitioner Members. Enterprise members are the Chief Medical Officer and the Chief Operating Officer, neither of whom sat before. Regional Chief Medical Officers and Regional Directors of Clinical Operations and Excellence no longer sit; § 3.9 now says so, and says what they do instead.

**The enterprise exclusion attaches to the seat.** § 3.1 states that enterprise members hold Employment Authority by virtue of office, are not Action-Eligible for any purpose, and are neither present for nor party to any determination concerning an identified Practitioner, whether or not the officeholder in fact exercises the authority § 2.7 describes. That leaves § 2.9 and § 7.4(b) as the operating mechanism and removes the question of whether a particular CMO or COO "really" holds Employment Authority. § 2.7 continues to govern the CQC and any future seat.

## 2. The Chair of Clinical Excellence does not vote

New § 3.1.1. As drafted before, § 6.2 recused the Chair of Clinical Excellence from every matter arising from a Committee that member chairs, which was every Adverse Recommendation and every standard, audit plan or policy the CQC sent up. The seat voted on the Council's own Charter and Framework and on nothing else, while presenting the recommendations under decision. **A seat recused by rule from the body's core function is a non-voting seat with extra steps**, and the extra steps were the thing a Practitioner's counsel would point at. § 9.6 already conceded the point for the chairmanship. The seat is now ex officio non-voting, not counted toward quorum, floors or any vote, and presents every Committee matter. § 6.2 is rewritten to refer to § 3.1.1 rather than to recuse a member who has no vote to withhold.

The cost is that the Charter and the Clinical Authorization Framework are adopted by a body the person who drafts them does not vote on. That is the design.

## 3. Five at-large members

Down from six. With the Chair of Clinical Excellence out of the count, five holds the clinical class at seven. Five is also the smallest number at which §§ 3.5, 3.6 and 3.11(c) have a pool to draw on for the chairmanship, the vice-chairmanship and a vacancy; at two, which was proposed in an earlier pass, the entire pool for both offices was two people and a vacancy had no runner-up. § 3.4 now gives three reasons for the class rather than two, the third being that at-large members are the members the Council's determinations reach in their own practice.

## 4. Quorum and thresholds

§ 7.3(b) floor from five to four. § 7.4 is unchanged: (b) already excludes members holding Employment Authority, which now describes the enterprise class by definition, and (c) already requires a majority of all Action-Eligible Members not recused, matching PRC § 2.8.

**Composition math.** Nine voting members, seven clinicians, two holding Employment Authority. Action pool seven; six after a service-line recusal under § 6.2; floor four; an adverse action needs four of six. The floor survives two absences. Where two recusals and two absences coincide the matter waits.

## 5. Consequential edits

§ 3.2 restoration now points to § 3.11 alone. § 3.3 and § 3.11(b) renumbered to the new classes and extended to the Chair of Clinical Excellence. § 3.9 size cap and automatic conversion replaced by a fixed size, with seat changes routed through §§ 4.3 and 13.2. § 3.10 reports against §§ 3.1 and 3.2. § 13.1 no longer refers to a § 3.9 limit. Bracketed defaults updated; the Ind. Code § 34-30-15 clinician floor is flagged for a re-check at the smaller size, since one departure moves the ratio further at nine than at seventeen.

## What this revision does not do

The post-closed-session notice to Management, stating the action, its terms and its effective date, is not yet drafted and belongs in Article V or § 8.5. The PRC Charter, the Fair Hearing Procedure, the seat map, the framework overview, both member guides and the Jen packet all state or draw on the old composition and are next. Indemnification remains the binding constraint on recruiting and is unchanged by any of this.

**Addendum, 2026-09-20.** § 4.2(m) added: the Council receives the CQC's annual audit plan under CQC § 3.1(f) and records whether it answers each enterprise priority brought under CQC § 2.6.1. The section states that the plan is what will be measured and that the standards remain the CQC's under its Article IV, which is the division decided on 2026-09-15: the Council reviews the program for alignment with enterprise priorities, the CQC keeps authority over its standards.
