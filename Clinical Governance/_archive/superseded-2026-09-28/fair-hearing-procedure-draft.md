# Fair Hearing Procedure

**[ENTITY LEGAL NAME]**

*Draft v0.1 — 2026-08-26. Not adopted. Prepared for counsel review.*
*Adopted by the Clinical Governance Council under § 4.2(i) of its Charter. Referenced at Council Charter §§ 8.4 and 9.4.*
*Bracketed values are drafting defaults; see "Open items in this draft" at the end.*

---

## Article I — Scope and Purpose

**1.1 Authority.** This Procedure is adopted by the Clinical Governance Council ("the Council") and governs every hearing constituted under Article IX of the Council Charter.

**1.2 When a hearing arises.** Where the Council proposes to take a Professional Review Action, the Practitioner is entitled to notice and a hearing under this Procedure before the action becomes final.

**1.3 Purpose.** To satisfy the adequate notice and hearing requirements of 42 U.S.C. § 11112(b), and to give a Practitioner whose Clinical Authorization is at risk a fair opportunity to answer before a body that took no part in the review that produced the recommendation.

**1.4 Uniform application.** This Procedure applies identically to physicians, nurse practitioners, physician assistants, and psychiatric nurse practitioners. The federal safe harbor is written around physicians; running one procedure for everyone is a deliberate choice, not an oversight.

**1.5 What this is not.** A hearing under this Procedure is not a court proceeding, is not governed by rules of evidence, and determines nothing about employment. **The subject of the hearing is the Practitioner's Clinical Authorization and nothing else.** Employment decisions are made by Management on its own record, under its own process, and are not before the panel.

**1.6 Substantial compliance.** Failure to meet a particular condition of 42 U.S.C. § 11112(b) does not, in itself, establish that the standards of § 11112(a)(3) were not met. This Procedure is nevertheless drafted to meet them, and departures from it shall be recorded with the reason.

## Article II — Notice of Proposed Action

**2.1 Contents.** Written notice to the Practitioner stating:

- (a) that a Professional Review Action has been proposed against the Practitioner;
- (b) the reasons for the proposed action, in terms sufficient to permit a response, including the conduct or performance at issue and the period concerned;
- (c) the action proposed, and its proposed duration or the criteria on which it would terminate;
- (d) that the Practitioner has the right to request a hearing;
- (e) that the request must be made within **[thirty] days** of the Practitioner's receipt of the notice;
- (f) a summary of the rights available at the hearing under Article V; and
- (g) that failure to request a hearing within the time allowed waives the hearing and permits the Council to take final action.

**2.2 Delivery.** By a method producing proof of receipt. The period in § 2.1(e) runs from receipt.

**2.3 The material relied on.** With the notice, or within [five] business days of it, the Practitioner receives the material the Council relied on in proposing the action — the findings, the evidence considered, and the record of any response the Practitioner has already given. **The Practitioner does not receive the deliberations of the Peer Review Committee or of the Council.**

**2.4 Waiver.** A Practitioner who does not request a hearing within the time allowed waives it. The Council may then take final action on the existing record. **Waiver does not affect reportability**, which turns on the action taken and not on whether it was contested.

**2.5 Offer of an expedited schedule.** **Every notice of proposed action shall be accompanied by an offer of an expedited schedule**, stating the shortened periods and the effect of accepting them.

- (a) **Acceptance is in writing** and operates as a waiver of the periods it names. A Practitioner who accepts may still obtain a continuance for good cause under § 4.3.
- (b) **Declining carries no inference.** A Practitioner who declines, or who does not respond, proceeds on the ordinary schedule. **The choice shall not be recorded as delay, characterized adversely, or considered by the panel or the Council on any question before them.**
- (c) The offer and the response are recorded by the Coordinator.

The default expedited schedule is: **[ten] days to request a hearing; notice of hearing not less than [ten] days before it; [ten] days for the panel's recommendation; [ten] days for the Council's decision.** A matter on that schedule concludes in roughly **[forty-five] days** rather than roughly one hundred and twenty.

The offer exists because the ordinary periods run for the Practitioner's benefit, and many Practitioners will not want them. **Someone who believes a proposed action is unwarranted usually wants it resolved rather than extended.** The statutory minimums are a floor for their protection, not an obligation imposed on them, and a process that makes them wait four months for a vindication they expect is a process that punishes by delay.

## Article III — The Hearing Panel

**3.1 Request.** A request for hearing is made in writing to the Coordinator and requires no statement of grounds.

**3.2 Constitution.** The Council constitutes the panel within **[fifteen] days** of the request. Composition is governed by Article IX of the Council Charter: not fewer than [three] licensed clinicians, none of whom is a member of the Council, the Peer Review Committee, or the Clinical Quality Committee, none of whom is subject to a recusal trigger, none of whom is in direct economic competition with the Practitioner, and providing discipline-matched representation.

**3.3 No prior participation.** No person who participated in the Peer Review Committee's review of the matter, in the audit that produced it, or in the Council's decision to propose the action may sit on the panel, advise it, or be present for its deliberations.

**3.4 Presiding officer.** The Council may appoint a presiding officer, who need not be a clinician and who may be an attorney. **The presiding officer rules on procedure and evidence and does not vote.** Where no presiding officer is appointed, the panel elects a chair from its members, who performs those functions and does vote.

**3.5 Objection to a panel member.** The Practitioner may object in writing within [five] business days of being notified of the panel's composition, stating the ground. Objections are decided by the presiding officer, or where there is none, by the Council. The decision and its basis are recorded.

**3.6 Counsel to the panel.** The panel may have counsel, who advises on procedure and does not participate in deliberation on the merits. Counsel to the panel shall not be the person presenting the case under § 5.4.

---

## Article IV — Notice of Hearing and Pre-Hearing Exchange

**4.1 Notice of hearing.** Written notice stating:

- (a) the place, time, and date of the hearing, which shall be **not less than [thirty] days** after the date of the notice; and
- (b) a list of the witnesses expected to testify in support of the proposed action.

**4.2 Exchange.** Not later than **[ten] business days** before the hearing, each side provides the other with the documents it intends to present and the witnesses it intends to call. Material not exchanged may be excluded by the presiding officer, who may instead grant a continuance.

**4.3 Continuance.** The presiding officer may continue the hearing for good cause, on request or on the panel's own motion. A continuance granted at the Practitioner's request extends every subsequent period accordingly, and the reason is recorded.

**4.4 Access to witnesses.** The Entity shall make its employees and contracted personnel available to testify. **Attendance is not conditioned on which side calls them.**

---

## Article V — Conduct of the Hearing

**5.1 Attendance and forfeiture.** The Practitioner shall appear. **The right to a hearing is forfeited if the Practitioner fails to appear without good cause**, and the Council may then take final action on the existing record.

**5.2 Representation.** The Practitioner may be represented by an attorney or by any other person of the Practitioner's choosing. Where the Practitioner is represented by an attorney, the Council may be as well.

**5.3 Record.** A record of the proceedings is made, by transcript or recording. **The Practitioner may obtain a copy**, on payment of any reasonable cost of preparation. The record is part of the protected record under Article X of the Council Charter.

**5.4 Presentation.** A representative designated by the Council presents the basis for the proposed action. The Practitioner then responds. Each may call witnesses, examine and cross-examine, and present evidence.

**5.5 Burden.** **The Council's representative bears the burden of establishing, by a preponderance of the evidence, the factual basis for the proposed action.** The Practitioner is not required to prove that the action is unwarranted.

**5.6 Evidence.** The Practitioner may present evidence determined by the presiding officer to be relevant, **irrespective of whether it would be admissible in a court of law.** The panel is not bound by rules of evidence.

**5.7 Questions from the panel.** Panel members may question any witness and may request additional information, in which case the presiding officer allows both sides a fair opportunity to address it.

**5.8 Closing statement.** The Practitioner may submit a written statement at the close of the hearing, within [five] business days.

**5.9 Closed proceeding.** The hearing is closed. Present are the panel, the presiding officer, counsel, the parties and their representatives, the Coordinator, and any witness while testifying.

---

## Article VI — Recommendation of the Panel

**6.1 Deliberation.** The panel deliberates in private. The presiding officer may be present to advise on procedure and does not vote. No person excluded by § 3.3 is present.

**6.2 Decision.** By majority of the panel.

**6.3 Written recommendation.** Within **[thirty] days** of the close of the hearing, the panel issues a written recommendation **including a statement of the basis for it**, identifying the evidence relied on. The panel may recommend that the proposed action be taken, not taken, or modified.

**6.4 Delivery.** To the Council and to the Practitioner simultaneously.

---

## Article VII — Final Action by the Council

**7.1 The Council decides.** Final action is taken by the Council, by the Action-Eligible Members under § 7.4(b) of its Charter, at the quorum set by § 7.3(b), and at the threshold set by § 7.4(c).

**7.2 Limited standard of review.** The Council proposed the action and the Council takes the final action, and the hearing panel is what stands between those two facts. **The Council shall therefore accept the panel's recommendation unless it finds, and states in writing, that** —

- (a) the recommendation is unsupported by the record of the hearing;
- (b) the hearing was affected by a material procedural defect; or
- (c) the recommendation rests on an error as to the Council's authority or the applicable standard.

**The Council does not substitute its own judgment for the panel's on a matter the panel decided on the evidence before it.** Where the Council rejects or modifies a recommendation, the ground under this section is recorded and given to the Practitioner.

**7.3 Written decision.** Within **[fifteen] days** of receiving the recommendation, or at its next convening under § 7.1.1 of its Charter, whichever is sooner, the Council issues a written decision to the Practitioner **including a statement of the basis for it**, and stating the action, its effective date, and its duration or the criteria on which it terminates.

**7.4 No further appeal.** The decision of the Council is final. **There is no appeal to the Board**, and the Board has no authority to review, modify, or reverse it.

**7.5 Reporting.** Reportability is determined by the Council with counsel under Article XI of its Charter. The thirty-day reporting clock runs from the date of final action.

## Article VIII — Where a Precautionary Restriction Is in Effect

**8.1 The arithmetic does not work without this Article.** A restriction imposed under Article IX of the Peer Review Committee Charter hard-stops at forty-five days. The minimum periods in this Procedure — thirty days to request a hearing, thirty days' notice before it — exceed that before a hearing has even been held. **A precautionary restriction therefore cannot survive a contested hearing unless this Article says how.**

**8.2 Continuation during the hearing process.** Where the Practitioner requests a hearing, a precautionary restriction in effect continues through the conclusion of the process under this Procedure, notwithstanding the forty-five-day limit. The delay is at the Practitioner's instance and the restriction is not extended by the Entity's inaction.

**8.3 Council review at intervals.** The Council reviews a restriction continued under § 8.2 **not less often than every [thirty] days**, on the record then available, and terminates or modifies it where it is no longer warranted. Each review is recorded.

**8.4 Reportability is already determined.** A restriction continuing past thirty days is assessed for reportability under Article XI of the Council Charter at that point, without waiting for the hearing to conclude. **The label does not control and the pendency of a hearing does not suspend the clock.**

**8.5 Waiver of time periods.** The Practitioner may waive or shorten any period in this Procedure in writing. Where a precautionary restriction is in effect, **the offer under § 2.5 is made again at each review under § 8.3** — a Practitioner's view of the ordinary schedule changes when a restriction is running against them, and the offer should be available at the moment that view changes rather than only at the outset.

---

## Article IX — Waiver, Abandonment, and Resignation

**9.1 Failure to request.** § 2.4 applies.

**9.2 Failure to appear.** § 5.1 applies.

**9.3 Withdrawal.** A Practitioner may withdraw a hearing request in writing at any time. The Council may then take final action on the existing record.

**9.4 Resignation.** A Practitioner who resigns during the pendency of a proposed action, or in return for the action not being taken, does not thereby extinguish any obligation. The Council records the circumstances and determines reportability with counsel under § 11.6 of its Charter. **Resignation while a proposed action is pending may itself be reportable.**

---

## Article X — Records and Timelines

**10.1 Records.** The hearing record, the panel's recommendation, the Council's decision, and all notices are part of the professional review record under Article X of the Council Charter, held by the Coordinator, and segregated from employment records.

**10.2 The Coordinator administers this Procedure** — notices, receipts, the panel's constitution, the exchange, the record, and every period below.

**10.3 Summary of periods.**

| Step | Runs from | Period |
|---|---|---|
| Request a hearing | Receipt of notice of proposed action | [30] days |
| Material relied on provided | Notice of proposed action | [5] business days |
| Panel constituted | Request | [15] days |
| Objection to a panel member | Notice of composition | [5] business days |
| Notice of hearing to hearing date | Notice of hearing | not less than [30] days |
| Pre-hearing exchange | Before the hearing | [10] business days |
| Written closing statement | Close of hearing | [5] business days |
| Panel recommendation | Close of hearing | [30] days |
| Council decision | Receipt of recommendation | [15] days or next convening |
| Data Bank report | Final action | 30 days |
| Council review of a continued precautionary restriction | Each review | every [30] days |

**A contested matter on the ordinary schedule runs roughly one hundred and twenty days from notice of proposed action to final decision, and that is the floor rather than the expectation.** Two of those periods are statutory minimums and cannot be shortened except by the Practitioner's waiver under § 2.5.

**10.4 The expedited schedule offered under § 2.5.**

| Step | Ordinary | Expedited |
|---|---|---|
| Request a hearing | [30] days | [10] days |
| Notice of hearing to hearing date | not less than [30] days | not less than [10] days |
| Panel recommendation | [30] days | [10] days |
| Council decision | [15] days | [10] days |
| **Approximate total** | **~120 days** | **~45 days** |

Every other period is unchanged. The expedited schedule shortens waiting; it removes no right.

# Open items in this draft

**Decisions made in drafting that the Council should make deliberately.**

1. **The burden is on the Council, not the Practitioner (§ 5.5).** The common hospital model puts an initial burden of coming forward on the review body and then places the burden of persuasion on the practitioner to show the recommendation lacks a factual basis. This draft does not. The Council's representative bears the burden throughout, by a preponderance.

   The reason is situational rather than legal. This is a new program, in a group owned by a financial sponsor, asking clinicians to accept that a committee can restrict how they practice. **The allocation of the burden is the single most visible signal of whether the process is real.** It also costs little: a recommendation that cannot be supported by a preponderance of the evidence is a recommendation that should not have been made. Counsel may prefer the conventional allocation, and that is a legitimate view — but it should be chosen, not inherited.

2. **The Council's review of the panel is limited (§ 7.2).** The Council proposes the action and takes the final action. In a hospital the medical executive committee recommends and a separate board decides after the hearing; here one body does both, because the board is deliberately out of the case flow. The panel is the only independent step, and a Council free to substitute its own judgment would make the panel advisory in fact.

   So the Council may reject or modify only on stated grounds: unsupported by the record, material procedural defect, or error as to authority or standard. This is a real constraint on the Council and it is the provision most likely to be softened in review. **Softening it removes the independence that the whole hearing exists to supply.**

3. **Article VIII was necessary because the numbers did not work.** The precautionary restriction hard-stops at forty-five days. Thirty days to request a hearing plus thirty days' notice exceeds that before anyone sits down. Without Article VIII, either the restriction lapses in every contested case or the hard stop is violated in every contested case. The chosen answer — the restriction continues where the Practitioner requests a hearing, with Council review every thirty days and reportability assessed at thirty days regardless — preserves the anti-drift purpose while allowing due process to take the time it takes.

   **This requires a conforming change to § 9.5 of the Peer Review Committee Charter**, which currently states the forty-five-day stop without the exception. That change has not yet been made.

4. **The expedited schedule is offered as a matter of course (§ 2.5).** Not merely available on request. The ordinary periods exist to protect the Practitioner, and a Practitioner who expects to be vindicated is harmed rather than helped by four months of waiting — a long process is itself a punishment, and it falls hardest on the people who are eventually cleared. Two provisions keep the offer honest: declining carries no inference and is never characterized as delay, and the offer is repeated at every review where a precautionary restriction is running, because that is when a Practitioner's view of the schedule changes.

   **This is also the answer to the strongest operational objection to the whole hearing apparatus.** If formal process takes four months and a Tier One intervention takes a conversation, pressure builds to keep matters in Tier One that do not belong there — the same drift the anti-drift clock at PRC § 8.3 exists to prevent, arriving through the front door. A forty-five-day path for a Practitioner who wants one relieves most of that pressure.

5. **A presiding officer is optional (§ 3.4).** A panel of three clinicians running a contested hearing with counsel present is a difficult thing to ask of them. An appointed presiding officer who rules on procedure and does not vote makes the hearing survivable and the record cleaner. The cost is that it looks more like litigation, which is the thing clinicians dislike about the whole apparatus.

**Not built, and worth considering.** 42 U.S.C. § 11112(c)(1)(B) permits a restriction of not longer than **fourteen days** while an investigation is conducted to determine whether a professional review action is needed, without the procedures of subsection (b). That is a distinct and useful tool: it does not require imminent danger, and at fourteen days it is well inside the thirty-day reporting threshold. Neither the Peer Review Committee Charter nor this Procedure provides for it. It would be a third instrument alongside the precautionary restriction and the full process, and it may be worth having.

**Bracketed defaults to confirm:** the expedited periods in § 2.5 and the table at § 10.4; thirty days to request; thirty days' notice; fifteen days to constitute the panel; ten business days for exchange; thirty days for the panel's recommendation; fifteen days for the Council's decision; thirty-day intervals for review of a continued restriction; panel size.

**For counsel:**

- Whether the burden allocation in § 5.5 creates any disadvantage in defending an action later, given that HCQIA requires a *reasonable belief* rather than proof.
- Whether § 7.2's limited standard of review is consistent with the Council being the entity taking the action for purposes of § 11112(a)(4).
- Whether Article VIII's continuation of a precautionary restriction through the hearing is sound in each of the five states, and whether any state requires a shorter outer limit.
- Whether running the identical procedure for non-physician Practitioners creates any exposure that a tiered procedure would not, given that the federal safe harbor is written around physicians and the state provisions differ.
- Whether § 4.4's undertaking to make employees available to testify creates any obligation the Entity cannot meet as to contracted personnel.
- § 9.4 — the standard for when a proposed action is "pending" for reporting purposes on resignation.
- Whether the fourteen-day investigatory restriction under § 11112(c)(1)(B) should be added, and if so, to which document.
- **Whether a Practitioner's written waiver of the thirty-day minimums under § 2.5 preserves the § 11112(b) safe harbor.** The statute says "not less than 30 days," and the expedited path depends on the answer. This should be confirmed before the offer is made to anyone.
