## Article I — Establishment, Purpose, and Status

**1.1 Establishment.** The Board of Directors of [Entity] ("the Board") establishes the Clinical Governance Council ("the Council") by resolution adopted [DATE], and delegates to it the authority set out in Article IV.

**1.2 Purpose.** The Council exists to conduct and oversee a formal peer review process for the purpose of furthering quality health care furnished by or on behalf of the Entity. All Council activity is undertaken in furtherance of that purpose.

**1.2.1 What this Charter provides.** Federal and state law extend protection to a health care entity that reviews clinician performance through a formal peer review process, and to the individuals who take part in it. **That protection is conditional on the process having in fact been followed**, which is why this Charter and the charters under it set the process out in this detail.

The structure also creates a range of intermediate responses — education, oversight, a defined period of review, a requirement of consultation — **between taking no action and ending a clinician's relationship with the Entity.** An organization whose available responses are only those two extremes has a narrower range than patient safety requires and than the law contemplates.

**The obligations this Charter creates and the protections it makes available arise from the same status and are not separable.** See Article XI.

**1.3 Status as a health care entity.** The Entity provides health care services and, through the Council and the Committees, follows a formal peer review process for the purpose of furthering quality health care, within the meaning of 42 U.S.C. § 11151(4)(A)(ii).

**1.4 Status as a committee.** The Council, each Committee reporting to it, and each hearing panel constituted under Article IX is a peer review, medical review, quality assurance, or professional review committee within the meaning of Va. Code § 8.01-581.17; Ohio Rev. Code § 2305.25 et seq.; N.C. Gen. Stat. § 90-21.22A; Ind. Code § 34-30-15; and Ky. Rev. Stat. § 311.377. The designation "Council" is internal nomenclature and does not alter that status.

**1.5 Adoption of guidelines.** The Council adopts the peer review guidelines published by the Medical Society of Virginia, as amended, by reference, for purposes of Va. Code § 8.01-581.17(B)(iii).

**1.6 Practitioners within scope.** Physicians, nurse practitioners, physician assistants, psychiatric nurse practitioners, and such other licensed clinicians as the Council designates ("Practitioners"). **Licensed psychotherapists are within PRC review only under the standing discipline-specific addendum required by § 6.5 of the PRC Charter and adopted by the Council under § 4.2(j), and within hearing proceedings only under a panel constituted under § 9.2(d).** No other provision of this Charter applies to them by default.

**1.7 Uniform procedure.** The procedures of this Charter apply identically to all Practitioners regardless of discipline. Federal immunity is thinner for non-physician Practitioners than for physicians; uniform procedure is adopted deliberately rather than tiering process by discipline.

## Article II — Definitions

**2.1 Clinical Authorization.** The terms and conditions under which a Practitioner is permitted to furnish care on behalf of the Entity, including the Autonomy Tier assigned under § 2.2 and any other condition the Council adopts in the Clinical Authorization Framework. **Clinical Authorization constitutes clinical privileges within the meaning of 42 U.S.C. § 11151(3)**, which defines clinical privileges to include "other circumstances pertaining to the furnishing of medical care under which a physician or other licensed practitioner is permitted to furnish such care by a health care entity." The term "Clinical Authorization" is used for internal clarity and does not alter that status.

**2.2 Autonomy Tier.** One of:

- (a) **Unrestricted.** No restriction imposed by the Council under this Framework attaches. **A Practitioner is Unrestricted for the purposes of this Charter while remaining subject to the ordinary requirements of licensure, statutory scope of practice, any collaborative or supervisory arrangement the law of the jurisdiction requires, and any scope limit in their contract or credentialing.** Those are not Autonomy Tiers, are not imposed by the Council, and their existence does not make a Practitioner anything other than Unrestricted here.
- (b) **Mandatory consultation.** The Practitioner must obtain consultation before acting in defined circumstances.
- (c) **Proctoring / prospective approval.** The Practitioner must obtain approval before acting in defined circumstances.
- (d) **Any other restriction on the scope of Clinical Authorization** adopted in the Clinical Authorization Framework.

**Every tier other than (a) restricts the Practitioner's authority to furnish care, and each is capable of being reportable under Article XI.** The Council documents the basis for the tier assigned in every case.

**Retrospective focused review is not an Autonomy Tier and is not part of Clinical Authorization.** Review after the fact of defined categories of a Practitioner's completed care leaves the authority to furnish care unchanged. It is a Quality Intervention under § 8.2(d) of the PRC Charter, imposed by that Committee directly, and it is neither a Professional Review Action nor reportable.

**The reason the ladder is confined to authority is structural.** Section 2.1 makes the Autonomy Tier part of Clinical Authorization; § 2.3 makes any action that may adversely affect Clinical Authorization a Professional Review Action; and § 3.2(a) of the PRC Charter forbids that Committee to take one. **A tier the PRC may impose directly would therefore be a tier it is prohibited from imposing.** This ladder describes authority to furnish care and nothing else.

**2.3 Professional Review Action.** An action or recommendation of the Council, based on a Practitioner's competence or professional conduct that affects or could affect adversely the health or welfare of a patient, which adversely affects or may adversely affect the Practitioner's Clinical Authorization.

**2.4 Professional Review Activity.** Activity undertaken to determine whether a Practitioner may furnish care, or the conditions under which care may be furnished, or to change or modify those conditions.

**2.5 The Committees.** The Clinical Quality Committee ("CQC") and the Peer Review Committee ("PRC"), each chartered by the Council under § 4.2(a).

**2.6 Management.** The Chief Executive Officer, Chief Operating Officer, Chief Medical Officer, Regional Chief Medical Officers, Regional Directors of Clinical Operations and Excellence, and any other person holding authority over the employment, compensation, contracting, scheduling, or assignment of Practitioners.

**2.7 Employment Authority.** Authority to hire, terminate, discipline, set compensation for, evaluate for compensation purposes, or direct the assignment of a Practitioner.

**2.8 Adverse Recommendation.** A recommendation of the PRC that, if adopted, would constitute a Professional Review Action.

**2.9 Action-Eligible Member.** A voting member who holds no Employment Authority. Under § 7.4(b), only Action-Eligible Members vote on a Professional Review Action.

## Article III — Composition

**3.1 Members.** The Council consists of nine voting members in two classes, and one non-voting member.

Clinical members:

- (a) the Chair of the Primary Care Committee;
- (b) the Chair of the Psychiatry Committee;
- (c) [five] At-Large Practitioner Members elected under § 3.4.

Enterprise members:

- (d) the Chief Medical Officer;
- (e) the Chief Operating Officer.

**Enterprise members hold Employment Authority by virtue of office and are not Action-Eligible Members for any purpose of this Charter.** They participate in the governance business of the Council under § 7.4(a). They do not deliberate or vote on, and are not present for the deliberation of, any determination concerning an identified Practitioner, whether or not the holder of the office in fact exercises the authority described in § 2.7. The exclusion attaches to the seat and survives any change in who holds it.

**3.1.1 Chair of Clinical Excellence.** The Chair of Clinical Excellence, who chairs the CQC and the PRC, sits ex officio and does not vote. The Chair of Clinical Excellence presents every matter arising from either Committee, responds to questions, provides information, and withdraws before deliberation begins on any determination concerning an identified Practitioner. **The Chair of Clinical Excellence is not counted toward quorum under § 7.3, toward either floor under § 3.2, or toward any vote under § 7.4.** Chairing the bodies that find and recommend, and voting on what they recommend, are not held by the same person; § 9.6 states the same rule for the hearing stage.

**3.2 Composition floors.** At all times:

- (a) not less than [two-thirds] of voting members shall be licensed clinicians in active clinical practice. This exceeds the fifty percent required by Ind. Code § 34-30-15 and contemplated by N.C. Gen. Stat. § 90-21.22A, and shall not be reduced by amendment below fifty percent; and
- (b) voting members holding no Employment Authority shall constitute a majority of voting members.

**Where either floor is not met, the Council shall not take any action for which that floor is a condition until it is restored.** Restoration is under § 3.11, at or before the next regular meeting. Action validly taken while a floor was met is unaffected.

**3.3 Terms.** Members under § 3.1(a), (b), (d) and (e), and the Chair of Clinical Excellence under § 3.1.1, serve ex officio for so long as they hold the underlying position. At-Large Members serve [two]-year terms, staggered, renewable [once consecutively].

**3.4 At-Large Practitioner Members.** [Five] members, elected by and from Practitioners in active clinical practice who hold no Employment Authority. At least [two] shall be physicians and at least [two] shall be nurse practitioners or physician assistants. Eligibility requires [twelve] months of continuous service. Elections are administered by the Coordinator, who maintains the eligible roll, gives notice, and records the result; every Practitioner in active clinical practice may vote. Terms are staggered so that no more than half expire in any year.

At-Large Members exist for three reasons. They are the pool from which the Chair and Vice-Chair are elected under §§ 3.5 and 3.6, no other class being eligible. Under § 7.4(b) they carry the vote on Professional Review Actions together with whichever committee chair is not recused under § 6.2, and § 6.2 recuses one chair in most matters. And they are the members the Council's determinations reach in their own practice, which is the standing a body of chairs and officers cannot supply on its own. Five is the number at which the action vote survives a service-line recusal and one absence, and at which the offices under §§ 3.5 and 3.6 and a vacancy under § 3.11(c) have a pool to draw on.

**3.5 Chair.** The Chair is elected by the voting members **from among the At-Large Practitioner Members**, who shall additionally (i) be licensed clinicians in active clinical practice, (ii) hold no Employment Authority, and (iii) not sit on the CQC or the PRC. **No member of any other class is eligible**, and the eligibility conditions are stated in addition to that requirement rather than in place of it. Election is subject to ratification by the Board, which may decline ratification only for cause stated in writing. **The Chair serves a [two]-year term**, renewable [once consecutively], and may be removed only by [two-thirds] vote of the voting members. An election is held on the expiry of a term, on removal, and on a vacancy under § 3.11(d).

**3.6 Vice-Chair.** Elected on the same basis and eligibility as the Chair. The Vice-Chair presides whenever the Chair is recused, absent, or the office is vacant.

**3.7 Executive Coordinator, Clinical Excellence.** The Council is served by the Executive Coordinator, Clinical Excellence ("the Coordinator"), a non-voting employed position of the Entity. The Coordinator is custodian of the records of the Council and of each body reporting to it under Article X; maintains minutes to the standard of § 7.6; administers notice and the timelines of this Charter; administers the election of At-Large Members under § 3.4; reports composition under § 3.10; and holds the reporting duties assigned under § 11.4. The Coordinator need not be a clinician.

**3.7.1 The duty runs to the Council.** In performing the functions of this section the Coordinator acts for the Council and not for Management. **The Coordinator shall not disclose any matter of the Council, a Committee, or a hearing panel to any person in the Coordinator's management reporting line, including the Coordinator's own supervisor.** The ordinary reporting obligations of the Coordinator's employment do not reach this material. Where an instruction from Management conflicts with this section, the Coordinator refers it to the Chair and the Chair records it.

**3.7.2 Evaluation.** Performance of the functions of this section is assessed by the Chair. The content, subject, or outcome of any matter is not an input to any performance evaluation, compensation decision, or employment decision concerning the Coordinator.

**3.7.3 Protection.** No adverse employment action of any kind may be taken against the Coordinator by reason of performing these functions, of information the Coordinator provides to the Council or a Committee, or of the Coordinator's refusal to disclose protected material. Any such action is itself a matter for the Council and is reported to the Board under § 5.4.1, promptly and outside the annual cycle.

**3.7.4 Immunity and confidentiality.** The Coordinator is staff of a professional review body and a person assisting it for purposes of 42 U.S.C. § 11111(a)(1). The Coordinator executes the undertaking required by § 10.3, which survives termination of the Coordinator's employment.

**3.7.5 Alternate.** The Council designates an alternate to perform these functions where the Coordinator is unavailable or is screened under § 2.9 of the PRC Charter. The alternate is subject to this section in full.

**3.8 Attendance by non-members.** Counsel may attend any meeting. Any other person may attend only for a stated purpose, by invitation of the Chair, and only for so much of a meeting as that purpose requires. Attendance and departure are recorded in the minutes.

**3.9 Size.** The Council consists of the nine voting seats stated in § 3.1. A change to the number or class of seats is an amendment within § 4.3 and § 13.2. **This Charter does not seat regional leadership by office.** Regional Chief Medical Officers and Regional Directors of Clinical Operations and Excellence refer matters, are interviewed, and provide information on the same terms as any other holder of Employment Authority, and attend under § 3.8.

**3.10 Annual composition check.** At the first regular meeting of each year the Coordinator reports the Council's composition against § 3.1 and § 3.2. The report is recorded in the minutes.

**3.11 Loss of eligibility, and vacancies.**

- (a) **An At-Large Member who acquires Employment Authority ceases to hold the seat on the day of acquisition.** The seat becomes vacant and the member does not vote in the interim. No determination by the Council is required, and none is available.
- (b) A member under § 3.1(a), (b), (d) or (e), or the Chair of Clinical Excellence under § 3.1.1, ceases to sit on ceasing to hold the underlying position.
- (c) **A vacancy in an At-Large seat is filled for the unexpired term** by the eligible candidate next highest in the most recent election who is willing to serve, and otherwise by a special election administered under § 3.4 within [sixty] days.
- (d) A vacancy in the chairmanship or vice-chairmanship is filled by election under § 3.5 or § 3.6 within [sixty] days. The Vice-Chair presides in the interim, and where both offices are vacant the voting members elect a member to preside until the election is held.
- (e) **Where a vacancy or a loss of eligibility causes a floor under § 3.2 to fail, § 3.2 governs what the Council may do until the floor is restored.**

## Article IV — Authority

**4.1 Source.** The Council exercises authority delegated by the Board under the resolution referenced in § 1.1. The Board retains no case-level role.

**4.2 Powers.** The Council may:

- (a) charter, amend, and dissolve the CQC and the PRC, and approve their charters;
- (b) receive Adverse Recommendations and other recommendations from the PRC and, as to each, accept, reject, modify, or remand;
- (c) take final Professional Review Actions affecting Clinical Authorization;
- (d) constitute hearing panels under Article IX;
- (e) adopt and amend the Clinical Authorization Framework, including the Autonomy Tiers and any additional conditions;
- (f) ratify the Audit Referral Policy adopted by the CQC under § 6.2 of its Charter, and review it annually;
- (g) determine reportability under Article XI and cause reports to be filed;
- (h) ratify, modify, or terminate a precautionary restriction under § 8.7;
- (i) adopt procedures necessary to its function, not inconsistent with this Charter;
- (j) **appoint the members of the PRC** on the recommendation of its Chair under § 2.2 of its Charter, **maintain any Reviewer Pool** under § 2.5 of that Charter, and **adopt the standing discipline-specific addendum** required by § 6.5 of that Charter;
- (k) **designate the pathway to which health, impairment, and fitness matters are referred** under § 4.3(d) of the PRC Charter, by resolution recorded in the governance record, and amend the designation in the same way;
- (l) receive the reporting required by § 4.3(d)(2) of the PRC Charter and act on it; and
- (m) **receive the annual audit plan adopted by the CQC under § 3.1(f) of its Charter**, and record whether the plan answers each enterprise priority brought under § 2.6.1 of that Charter. The plan states what the CQC will measure; the standards it measures against are the CQC's under Article IV of its Charter, and this Council does not amend them.

**4.3 Reserved to the Board.** Adoption of and material amendment to this Charter; ratification of the election of the Chair; receipt of the annual report under § 5.4; and revocation of the delegation under § 4.3.1.

**4.3.1 Revocation of the delegation.** **The delegation under § 4.1 is made by Board resolution and may be revoked by Board resolution, in part or in whole, at any time.** The route out is stated in the instrument rather than left to be inferred from the one that created it.

**Revocation is prospective.** It does not vacate a Professional Review Action already final, does not terminate a restriction in force otherwise than on that restriction's own terms, and does not extinguish a reporting obligation that has already attached — none of which is within the Board's gift in any event. Matters open on the date of revocation are concluded or closed under this Charter, or under whatever the Board adopts in its place.

**The Board takes no case-level role on revocation, and the power to revoke carries no power to direct any matter while the delegation stands.** A delegation that could be withdrawn in order to reach a particular outcome in a particular case would not be a delegation, and the protections this Charter exists to secure would not attach to a body operating under that condition.

**4.4 Excluded from the Council's authority.** The Council has no authority over the employment, compensation, contracting, scheduling, facility assignment, or medical director designation of any Practitioner, and may not direct Management as to any of them. **The Council shall not receive or consider information about a Practitioner's compensation, productivity, revenue, or cost in connection with any Professional Review Activity.** Economic consequences that follow mechanically from a change in Clinical Authorization are not within this exclusion; reaching for an economic instrument is.

**4.5 Medical director designation.** Designation of a medical director under 42 C.F.R. § 483.70 is made by the facility, and nomination or withdrawal of nomination by the Entity is an administrative decision of Management, taken on an independently documented record. **The Council takes no action on designation, and no Professional Review record is transmitted for the purpose of one.** Where Management asks, the Council may confirm only the fact and terms of a final Professional Review Action of which Management already has notice under § 5.1, together with facts independently available from sources outside the Professional Review record. **Findings, evidence, deliberations, and the record of any matter that did not result in final action are not transmitted.** Any disclosure beyond this section is made only on a permissible-disclosure analysis performed by counsel and recorded under § 10.6.

**Stated this narrowly because a wider channel would defeat §§ 5.2, 5.3 and 10.6.** A record available as a partial basis for an administrative designation is a record that has moved between the tracks those sections keep apart.

## Article V — Relationship to Management and to the Board

**5.1 Notice, not authority.** Management receives notice of final Professional Review Actions. Management holds no vote, no veto, and no role in the deliberations of the Council, the Committees, or any hearing panel.

**5.2 Separate tracks.** Employment decisions are made by Management, on the employment record, under the Entity's employment policies. A Professional Review Action is not an employment decision and an employment decision is not a Professional Review Action. Neither is a condition of the other.

**5.3 Separate records.** The record of a Professional Review Action and the record of any employment decision concerning the same Practitioner are maintained separately under § 10.2. Documents do not move between them.

**5.4 Report to the Board.** The Council reports to the Board annually in aggregate: volume, categories, dispositions, reports filed, and program-level observations. The report contains no Practitioner identity and no case-level detail.

**5.4.1 Exception reporting.** The Board is informed promptly, and outside the annual cycle, of **the fact** of each of the following: litigation filed or threatened arising from a Professional Review Activity; a report submitted under Article XI; a precautionary restriction that has remained in effect longer than thirty days; and any allegation that the process required by these Charters was not followed.

**The report is of the fact and not of the substance.** It identifies no Practitioner, describes no clinical matter, states no finding, and conveys nothing of any deliberation.

**A Board cannot discharge its duty on an annual aggregate alone once a matter has become a legal or reputational event.** Nor can it receive the merits, which would forfeit the privilege the Entity is relying on and draw the Board into a process § 4.1 reserves to the Council. This section occupies the narrow space between those two, and is confined to it. Where the Board requires more than the fact, it is provided through counsel on a permissible-disclosure analysis recorded under § 10.6.

## Article VI — Recusal

**6.1 Automatic.** Recusal under this Article is automatic, not discretionary, and is not waivable by the affected Practitioner or by the Council. A member shall recuse from any matter in which the member:

- (a) participated in the care of the patient at issue;
- (b) holds, or held within the preceding [five] years, a supervisory relationship with the Practitioner under review;
- (c) participated in any employment decision concerning that Practitioner, or is likely to;
- (d) is the collaborating or supervising physician for the Practitioner under review;
- (e) holds a personal, familial, or financial relationship with the Practitioner that a reasonable person would regard as bearing on impartiality;
- (f) is the subject of the matter;
- (g) is in direct economic competition with the Practitioner under review;
- (h) served as clinical monitor of a Quality Intervention concerning that Practitioner under § 8.6 of the PRC Charter, or is named under § 8.6(b) of this Charter in respect of a restriction concerning that Practitioner; or
- (i) **stands in any other relationship or circumstance — including a supervisory relationship older than the period stated in (b) — that a reasonable person would regard as bearing on impartiality.**

**This list is aligned with § 12.1 of the PRC Charter and is kept aligned with it**, with the addition at (h) of a Council-specific trigger for a person performing a restriction under § 8.6(b). A person disqualified from reviewing a matter at the Committee should not decide it here.

**6.2 Chairs of the Committees.** The Chair of Clinical Excellence does not vote under § 3.1.1. Any other member who chairs a Committee may present a matter arising from that Committee, respond to questions, and provide information, but shall not deliberate or vote on it, and shall withdraw before deliberation begins. This applies to every matter arising from a Committee that member chairs, without further finding.

**The Chair of the Primary Care Committee and the Chair of the Psychiatry Committee recuse on the same terms from any matter concerning a Practitioner within their own service line**, without further finding. The composition of this Council assumes that recusal, and the floor at § 7.3(b) is set so that the action vote survives it.

**6.3 Effect of recusal.** Recusal means exclusion from the Council's decisional materials and from its deliberation, not abstention from the vote alone. **A recused member receives no Council decisional materials in the matter and no material in any capacity other than the one giving rise to the recusal**, leaves the room before deliberation begins, and does not return until the matter concludes. The minutes record the departure and the return.

**A member recused under § 6.2 will already hold the underlying material by virtue of the office from which the matter arises, and this section does not purport to unknow it.** What it requires of such a member is that they receive nothing further as a member of this Council, take no part in its deliberation, and cast no vote.

**6.4 Determination.** The Chair determines recusal. A member may recuse voluntarily. A disputed question of recusal is decided by majority vote of members not themselves affected by it, and the determination is recorded.

**6.5 Quorum.** Recused members are not counted toward quorum for the matter from which they are recused.

## Article VII — Meetings, Quorum, and Voting

**7.1 Regular meetings.** [Quarterly], on a schedule adopted annually. Regular meetings carry the Council's governance business: charters, the Clinical Authorization Framework, the Audit Referral Policy, composition, reporting, and the annual report.

**7.1.1 Convening on a recommendation.** The Council shall convene within [fifteen] calendar days of receiving an Adverse Recommendation, whether or not a regular meeting falls within that period. Case business is event-driven and does not wait for the quarterly cycle.

**7.1.2 Convening on a precautionary restriction.** The Council shall convene to ratify, modify, or terminate a precautionary restriction imposed under Article IX of the PRC Charter within [five] business days of its imposition, on not less than [twenty-four] hours' notice. It shall convene again as § 8.7(c) requires where a restriction continues through a requested hearing.

**7.2 Special meetings.** Called by the Chair, or on the written request of [three] voting members, on [five] days' notice, or on shorter notice with the consent of a majority of voting members.

**7.3 Quorum.**

- (a) *General business.* A majority of voting members not recused from the matter, of whom not less than [two-thirds] are licensed clinicians in active clinical practice.
- (b) *Professional Review Actions.* A majority of Action-Eligible Members not recused from the matter, and in no event fewer than [four].
- (c) *Precautionary ratification.* Not fewer than [three] Action-Eligible Members not recused. This reduced floor applies to a determination under § 8.7 and to no other business.

**7.4 Voting.**

- (a) *General business.* Charters, the Clinical Authorization Framework, policy and procedure, ratification of the Audit Referral Policy, and remand under § 8.3 are decided by majority of members present and voting. All voting members vote. **A remand neither takes nor declines a Professional Review Action.** Where a remand on systems grounds disposes of an individual matter, the disposition is made by the PRC under § 10.3 of its Charter and not here.
- (b) *Professional Review Actions.* **Only Action-Eligible Members vote on whether to take, to modify, or to decline to take a Professional Review Action.** Members holding Employment Authority may be present for the presentation of the matter and may provide information, but withdraw before deliberation begins and do not vote. The minutes record the withdrawal.
- (c) *Threshold.* A Professional Review Action adverse to a Practitioner requires the affirmative vote of a majority of all Action-Eligible Members not recused from the matter, whether or not present.

The distinction between (a) and (b) is the rule the CQC applies to its own membership under § 2.5 of its Charter: members holding Employment Authority participate in standards, policy, and systems work, and not in determinations concerning an identified Practitioner. It is applied here for the same reason, and the class of determinations it reaches at each body is stated in that body's own Charter.

**7.5 Executive session.** All Professional Review Activity is conducted in executive session by default.

**7.6 Minutes.** Minutes state, for each matter: the matter considered and its identifier; the documents reviewed, identified individually; the members present, the members recused, and the times of departure and return; the action taken; and the vote. Deliberative content is summarized in substance and is not transcribed. The specificity required by this section is set by Ohio Rev. Code § 2305.252 case law, which requires document-level proof of scope and does not accept generalities.

**7.7 Electronic participation.** Permitted where every participant can hear and be heard simultaneously and the confidentiality of the session can be maintained. Recording of any Professional Review Activity is prohibited.

## Article VIII — Case Flow

**8.1 Receipt.** The PRC transmits to the Council its recommendation, the findings supporting it, the evidence considered, the notice given to the Practitioner, and the Practitioner's response if any. **The PRC does not transmit its deliberations.**

**8.2 Council options.** As to each recommendation the Council shall accept, reject, modify, or remand it. The basis is recorded.

**8.3 Remand.** The Council may remand to the PRC for further fact-gathering, for reconsideration on stated grounds, or for a defined procedural step it finds was not completed.

**Where the Council concludes that the matter reflects a systems condition rather than individual performance, it remands to the PRC on that ground.** The PRC then proceeds under § 10.3 and Article XI of its Charter: it closes the individual review, notifies the Practitioner in writing that the review closed without adverse finding, and refers the systems finding to the CQC without Practitioner attribution.

**The Council does not remand an individual matter to the CQC directly.** The intermediate step is not ceremony — it is what strips the attribution before the finding reaches a committee that has no jurisdiction over individuals and maintains an open, expressly non-confidential record. Sections 10.3 and 11.1 of the PRC Charter and § 7.3 of the CQC Charter are all written to that sequence.

**8.4 Adverse recommendations.** Where the Council proposes to take a Professional Review Action, the Practitioner shall first receive notice and the opportunity for a hearing under Article IX and the Fair Hearing Procedure. No Professional Review Action becomes final before those rights are exhausted or waived.

**8.5 Final action.** Final Professional Review Action is taken by the Council. The written decision states the action, its effective date, its duration or the criteria for its termination, and the basis for it.

**8.6 Exit criteria, and what a restriction of defined duration requires.**

**(a) Stated at the time.** Every Professional Review Action of defined duration shall state in writing, at the time it is taken, its terms, its duration, the criteria on which it terminates, and each person named under (b) and (c). Recorded then, and not afterward.

**(b) The person who performs the restriction is not a monitor.** Where an action requires a proctor, a consultant whose consultation must be obtained, or a person whose approval must be sought, **that person performs the restriction rather than observes it**, and is named in the action.

- (i) The person shall be a licensed clinician competent in the matter to which the restriction relates, discipline-appropriate on the terms of § 9.2(d).
- (ii) **A clinician holding no Employment Authority shall be named where one competent to act is available.** Where none is, the Council may name a clinician who holds Employment Authority, and records that no qualified alternative was available. The pool competent to proctor a particular kind of decision may be very small, and **a rule that cannot be satisfied would leave the restriction unenforceable** — a worse outcome than the one the exclusion guards against, because this role decides nothing about the Practitioner.
- (iii) **A person named under this subsection reports facts and nothing else** — that a required consultation or approval was sought, and whether it was given or refused. They make no assessment of the exit criteria and no recommendation as to disposition.
- (iv) **They are recused under § 6.1 from every determination of this Council in the matter**, including the determination under (d).

**(c) Administration.** The Coordinator records the terms, receives the reports required by (b)(iii), tracks the duration and any review date, and brings the matter to the Council on the date the action terminates. This is administration from the record and requires no contact with the Practitioner.

**(d) Termination is determined by the Council.** **Whether the exit criteria have been met is a determination of the Council, taken by the Action-Eligible Members under § 7.4(b), and not by any person named under (b) or (c).** Ending a restriction on Clinical Authorization is itself a decision about Clinical Authorization. On the determination the Council terminates the action and records that it has; the Practitioner is notified in writing.

**(e) A restriction is not extended.** **A Professional Review Action of defined duration terminates on its stated date, and is continued beyond that date only by a new Professional Review Action taken with the notice and hearing process that Article IX and the Fair Hearing Procedure require.**

Where the exit criteria have not been met on the stated date, the action nonetheless terminates, and the Council takes a fresh action if it concludes one is warranted. A restriction rolled forward without process is the defect § 8.3 of the PRC Charter guards against in its own tier, appearing here in the more serious form — a restriction on authority, capable of being reportable, continued without the Practitioner ever again receiving the rights that attach to imposing one.

**(f) Implementation is not direction of Management.** **A restriction operates on the Practitioner's Clinical Authorization and not on the roster.** A Practitioner subject to one is not authorized to act otherwise than as it permits, and any obligation of Management to schedule, assign, or credential consistently with that follows from the authorization itself. Management learns of the action through the notice under § 5.1. **Nothing in this section directs Management as to employment, scheduling, or assignment**, which § 4.4 forbids — and nothing in § 4.4 relieves anyone of the effect of a restriction on Clinical Authorization.

**8.7 Precautionary restrictions.** A precautionary restriction imposed by the PRC under Article IX of its Charter **lapses automatically at [five] business days from its imposition unless the Council ratifies it within that period.** Lapse requires no action by anyone.

**Ratification is a determination of the Action-Eligible Members, at the quorum set by § 7.3(c), by majority of those present and voting.** This is a route-specific rule stated so that the determination has a threshold. **It states how the determination is made without deciding whether the determination is itself a Professional Review Action**, which is a question reserved to counsel and is not resolved by this subsection or by § 2.2.

On ratification the Council states the restriction's terms and the date by which review shall conclude. The Council may modify or terminate a precautionary restriction at any time, on its own motion or on the Practitioner's request.

**The Council carries the obligations that §§ 9.4 and 9.5 of the PRC Charter attach to the restriction, because the Council is the body able to discharge them:**

- (a) **The Coordinator tracks the thirty-day reportability clock from the date of imposition, not from ratification**, and notifies the Chair and the Council at day [twenty]. Reportability is determined under § 11.2A.
- (b) **The restriction terminates at [forty-five] days from imposition** unless, before that day, the Council has taken a Professional Review Action with the process Article IX and the Fair Hearing Procedure require.
- (c) **The single exception is a hearing the Practitioner has requested.** The statutory minimum periods — thirty days to request a hearing and thirty days' notice before it — exceed forty-five days, so without this exception no contested matter could be reached. A restriction so continued runs through the conclusion of that process, and **the Council reviews it not less often than every [thirty] days** and terminates or modifies it where it is no longer warranted. The reportability assessment at thirty days from imposition is unaffected by the pendency of a hearing.
- (d) Where the matter underlying the restriction is dispositioned under § 4.4(e) of the PRC Charter and no question about care furnished remains, **§ 9.7 of that Charter governs**: no review follows, no recommendation from the PRC is to be awaited, and the Council determines what to do on the basis of the process to which the matter was referred.

## Article IX — Hearing Panels

**9.1 Constitution.** Where the Council proposes to take a Professional Review Action, it constitutes a hearing panel.

**9.2 Composition.** Not fewer than [three] licensed clinicians, each of whom:

- (a) is not a member of the Council;
- (b) is not a member of the PRC or the CQC;
- (c) is subject to no recusal trigger under § 6.1;
- (d) collectively provide discipline-matched representation: care furnished by a nurse practitioner is reviewed with meaningful nurse practitioner representation; by a physician assistant with physician assistant or equivalent representation; by a psychiatric nurse practitioner with psychiatric-trained representation; by a licensed psychotherapist with representation from that discipline;
- (e) is not in direct economic competition with the Practitioner.

**9.3 Panel chair.** Elected by the panel from among its members.

**9.4 Procedure.** Governed by the Fair Hearing Procedure adopted under § 4.2(i), which shall satisfy the notice and hearing requirements of 42 U.S.C. § 11112(b). That Procedure also governs the continuation of a precautionary restriction through a contested hearing, and limits this Council's review of a panel recommendation.

**9.5 Report.** The panel reports its findings and recommendation to the Council. The Council takes final action under § 8.5. Members of the panel do not vote on that action.

**9.6 Separation from initial review.** No person who participated in the PRC's review of a matter shall sit on the hearing panel for that matter or vote on its outcome. This provision is the reason the Chair of Clinical Excellence does not chair the Council.

## Article X — Records, Confidentiality, and Privilege

**10.1 Custodian.** The Coordinator is custodian of the records of the Council, the PRC, and each hearing panel.

**10.2 Segregation.** The following are maintained as separate records, in separate systems or separately secured locations, and documents are not moved between them:

- (a) **the Council's governance record** — this Charter and the Committee charters, the Clinical Authorization Framework, the Audit Referral Policy and its ratification, adopted procedures, composition and elections, aggregate reporting under § 5.4, and the minutes of governance business under § 7.1;
- (b) **the PRC's protected record**, retained by that Committee under Article XIII of its Charter. **Only material that Charter expressly authorises to be transmitted reaches the Council** — the transmittal under § 8.1, and the status, notification and reporting expressly provided for elsewhere in that Charter. **Its deliberations do not.**
- (c) **the Council's case record** — what the Council receives under § 8.1 and what it generates in deciding a particular matter, including the minutes of that matter;
- (d) **each hearing panel's record**, maintained separately from both (b) and (c);
- (e) the CQC's standards and education record, which is intended for circulation and is not confidential;
- (f) the CQC's protected audit record;
- (g) employment and personnel records;
- (h) any record designated patient safety work product.

**Each of the barriers stated elsewhere in these Charters depends on this separation.** The PRC does not transmit its deliberations (§ 8.1); no person who participated in the PRC's review sits on the hearing panel for that matter (§ 9.6); and an individual's adverse action is not filed with the business of adopting charters and ratifying policy. **A single combined record would place material in the hands of a body these provisions bar from receiving it**, whatever the process rule says.

**Custody is not access.** The Coordinator is custodian of (a) through (d) under § 10.1. Holding a record does not make it available to a body that may not receive it: the separation governs who receives material, not who files it. The Coordinator's undertaking under § 10.3 and the duties under §§ 3.7.1 to 3.7.3 apply to every record in the Coordinator's custody.

**Classification is not privilege.** How this Charter files a document does not establish that the document is privileged or otherwise protected in any jurisdiction, and nothing in this Article shall be read as asserting that it does.

**10.3 Confidentiality.** Every member, panelist, and person assisting shall execute a written confidentiality undertaking before receiving any Professional Review record, and shall not disclose its contents outside the process. **Repetition of findings outside the process is neither privileged nor immunized**, and neither 42 U.S.C. § 11111(a)(1) nor the state statutes cited in § 1.4 reaches the person who repeats them.

**10.4 Underlying facts are not protected.** Medical records, the observations of witnesses with independent knowledge, and information otherwise available remain discoverable in every jurisdiction in which the Entity operates. Nothing in this Charter makes a fact confidential that would not otherwise be.

**10.5 Retention and legal hold.** Case and hearing-panel records under § 10.2(c) and (d) are retained [seven] years from closure or from final action, as applicable, or as counsel directs, whichever is longer. **Not every matter ends in final action** — the Council may reject a recommendation, remand, or close without a Professional Review Action. The governance record under § 10.2(a) has no closure event and is retained as counsel directs. No record is altered or destroyed once litigation is filed or reasonably foreseeable.

**10.6 Disclosure to facilities and third parties.** No Professional Review record is disclosed to a contracted facility or other third party except on a permissible-disclosure analysis performed by counsel and recorded.

**10.7 Effective-date boundary.** **Records created before the effective date of this Charter, under whatever arrangements preceded it, are not imported into the Council's record.** Where a matter is pending on the effective date, the prior record is closed and a new record opened under this Charter.

**This section is about records. It is not about care.** Nothing in it limits review of care furnished, or of an event occurring, before the effective date.

## Article XI — Reporting Obligations

**The obligation in this Article and the protections in Article XII arise from the same status and are not separable.** The protections are available to a health care entity that follows a formal peer review process — see § 1.3 — and that status is what creates the duty stated here. **An entity that declines the duty declines the protection with it**, and continues to act on clinician performance without either.

This is stated at the head of the Article because the duty appears some distance from the status that creates it, and read on its own it presents as a new exposure rather than as the price of removing a larger one.

**11.1 Registration.** The Entity shall register with the National Practitioner Data Bank as a reporting entity before the Clinical Authorization Framework takes effect.

**11.2 Reports.** Where a Professional Review Action adversely affects the Clinical Authorization of a physician for a period longer than thirty days on the basis of professional competence or conduct affecting or potentially affecting patient health or welfare, the Entity shall submit an Initial Action Report within thirty days of the action, and shall report to the applicable state licensing board as that state requires. Reportability is determined by the character of the action and not by its label.

**11.2A Precautionary restrictions.** A precautionary restriction is not a final Professional Review Action under this Charter. **It is nonetheless capable of being reportable, and the question is asked in every case in which one remains in effect longer than thirty days from imposition.** The Council determines reportability with counsel and records the determination and its basis, whether or not a report is filed and whether or not the underlying review has concluded. Neither the label given to the restriction, nor its characterisation in § 8.7, controls.

**11.3 Attestation.** The Entity shall complete the biennial attestation that all reportable actions have been submitted.

**11.4 Ownership.** The Coordinator maintains the reporting calendar, tracks the thirty-day clock on each action, and prepares the attestation. Determination of reportability in any case is made by the Council with counsel and is recorded.

**11.5 Query.** The Entity may query the Data Bank on entering an employment or affiliation relationship and at each reappointment. [Recommended as standing practice.]

**11.6 Resignation during review.** Where a Practitioner resigns while under investigation or in return for not conducting an investigation, the Council shall determine with counsel whether a report is required. Resignation does not extinguish the obligation.

## Article XII — Immunity and Protection of Participants

**12.1 The Council, the PRC, and hearing panels.** The Council, the PRC, hearing panels, their members, and any person assisting them are entitled to the protections of 42 U.S.C. § 11111(a)(1) where the standards of § 11112(a) are met, and to the protections of the state statutes cited in § 1.4.

**12.1.1 The CQC.** The CQC is by design not a professional review body — see § 1.3 of its Charter. **The CQC and persons participating in its work are entitled to such federal and state protections, if any, as applicable law provides, and its Charter governs the question.** **This Charter makes no assertion on the point**, which is unresolved and reserved to counsel, and shall not be read as asserting here what the CQC Charter declines to assert about itself.

**12.2 Persons providing information.** **Any person who provides information to the Council, the PRC, or a hearing panel is immune under 42 U.S.C. § 11111(a)(2) unless the information is false and the person knew it was false.** This protection is not conditioned on the outcome of any review and does not depend on the standards of § 11112(a) being met.

**Whether that protection reaches information provided to the CQC has not been determined**, for the reason given in § 12.1.1 and stated in the CQC's own Charter. **A person with a concern about an identified Practitioner should bring it to the PRC** under § 4.1(b) of its Charter, where the statutory fit is clearer. That is better practice in any event, and it means the question need not be answered in a case that matters.

**12.3 Non-retaliation.** No adverse action shall be taken against a Practitioner or employee **because** that person participated in, provided information in good faith to, or became the subject of a Professional Review Activity. Retaliation is itself a matter for review.

**This section does not limit action taken on other grounds.** It does not limit a Professional Review Action taken under this Charter, action on conduct established through an employment process or any other lawful process, or ordinary employment and operational action unrelated to the person's participation in or subjection to Professional Review Activity. **Read otherwise, this section would forbid the Council from acting on the review it just conducted, which is not its purpose.** The protection is against retaliation for participation or status. It is not immunity from the outcome of the process.

**12.4 Immunity is a presumption, not a shield.** The protections of § 11112(a) are rebuttable and available only where the process set out in this Charter was in fact followed. They cover damages; they do not cover injunctive or declaratory relief, and they do not extend to civil rights claims.

## Article XIII — Amendment, Review, and Effective Date

**13.1 Annual review.** The Council reviews this Charter annually and records the review. The review includes the composition check under § 3.10 and, where a floor of § 3.2 is not met, the restoration § 3.11 requires.

**13.2 Amendment.** By majority of all voting members. **Material amendment additionally requires Board ratification, as § 4.3 reserves.**

A material amendment is one that alters the Council's authority or its relationship to the Board or to Management; the composition floors or the two-class voting rule; the recusal rules; the quorum or voting thresholds applicable to Professional Review Actions; the hearing rights of a Practitioner; the reporting obligations; or the immunity provisions. **The enumeration is illustrative and not exhaustive, and a doubt as to materiality is resolved in favour of ratification.**

**13.3 Effective date.** [DATE].

**13.4 Transition.** **Implementation of the Clinical Authorization Framework does not by itself reduce, enlarge, or otherwise alter any Practitioner's existing authority to furnish care.** Every Practitioner then in service is brought within the Framework without individual review, at the Autonomy Tier corresponding to the terms on which that Practitioner is then authorised to furnish care.

**Any restriction, supervision requirement, contractual scope limit, or other condition in effect on the effective date continues in effect** according to its own terms, and is recorded in the Framework.

This is a transition provision. **It grants no authority that did not exist the day before.** A Practitioner subject to a condition on the day before the effective date remains subject to it on the day after, and the Framework records the condition rather than replacing it.

**Composition math as drafted.** Nine voting members: two committee chairs, five at-large, two enterprise, with the Chair of Clinical Excellence sitting non-voting. Two voting members hold Employment Authority, seven do not, and the § 3.2(b) majority holds; seven of nine are clinicians in active practice against the two-thirds floor at § 3.2(a). The action-voting pool is seven, less the primary care or psychiatry chair on a matter in their own service line, so six in a typical case against a § 7.3(b) floor of four. An adverse action in that case requires four affirmative votes of six under § 7.4(c). The floor survives two absences.

**Bracketed defaults to confirm:** the Coordinator's title, if "Executive Coordinator, Clinical Excellence" is not final; two-thirds clinician floor, which at nine voting members is seven and is worth confirming against Ind. Code § 34-30-15 at the smaller size; five at-large members and their discipline mix; twelve-month eligibility for at-large service; four-member action quorum; two-year at-large and Chair terms; quarterly meetings; five days' notice for special meetings; three-member hearing panels; seven-year retention.

**For counsel:**

- **Indemnification and insurance — and this Charter's silence on it is deliberate pending advice.** Whether the Entity indemnifies members of the Council, the Committees, hearing panels, the Reviewer Pool, external reviewers, and the Coordinator against claims arising from participation; whether existing directors' and officers' and professional liability coverage responds; and whether any of it requires Board action or an amendment to the bylaws. **The statutory immunity in Article XII is rebuttable, does not reach injunctive or declaratory relief, and does not extend to civil rights claims**, so it is not a complete answer to a clinician asking what happens if they are sued for serving. In practice this is a condition of recruiting anyone onto these bodies, and it is the question a director is most likely to ask first. Added 2026-08-30.

- Whether the Board will adopt § 1.1 and § 4.1 as drafted, and whether delegation in these terms causes the Board to assume a clinical duty it does not now have.
- Whether § 1.4's definitional statement is effective in each of the five states, or whether any state requires the body to be denominated a committee in fact.
- Whether § 1.5's adoption by reference satisfies Va. Code § 8.01-581.17(B)(iii).
- Whether the Entity qualifies as a professional health care organization under Ind. Code § 34-6-2-99(a)(2)(A), and if not, which organizer route is available.
- Whether the § 90-21.22A(c) discovery bar reaches the Entity as a corporate defendant in North Carolina.
- Whether retrospective focused review under § 8.2(d) of the PRC Charter is reliably non-reportable, and where precisely the line sits between it and mandatory consultation under § 2.2(b) of this Charter. Now that focused review has been removed from the Autonomy Tier ladder, this is the same question the PRC Charter asks about its §§ 8.2(d) and 8.4(a), and it should be put once rather than twice.
- Whether existing employment agreements or Management authority conflict with §§ 4.4, 5.1, and 5.2.
- § 11.6 — the standard for "under investigation" and when it attaches.
- **§ 3.7 — whether state peer review privilege in each of the five states covers a non-clinician employee serving as records custodian.** HCQIA § 11111(a)(1) reaches staff and persons assisting; the state statutes were written around clinicians and this has not been analyzed. This is the provision the whole records architecture rests on.
- Whether §§ 3.7.1–3.7.3 are enforceable against the Entity as the Coordinator's employer, or whether they need mirroring in the Coordinator's employment agreement to bind.
- Whether § 7.4(b) creates any exposure in reverse — a Practitioner arguing the deciding body excluded the members with the most direct knowledge of their practice. The answer is presumably that those members provide information under § 7.4(b) and the exclusion goes only to the vote, but it should be tested rather than assumed.
