# Clinical Governance Program

## Executive Summary

*Office of the Chair of Clinical Excellence — [DATE]*
*Reconciled with the charters as revised 2026-09-18.*


---

## Purpose

To establish a formal structure for setting clinical standards and reviewing individual clinician performance, and to obtain Board adoption of the governing charter.

---

## Current State

Review of individual clinician performance already occurs at Eventus. Concerns have been raised, performance has been examined, and clinicians have been asked to work under conditions as a result. No structure supports those decisions.

- **No chartered committee, written procedure, or notice requirements.** Decisions are made case by case, without a defined process.
- **No legal protection.** Federal law provides protection to organizations that follow a formal peer review process. Eventus does not qualify, because the process does not exist.
- **No defined scope of practice.** What a clinician is permitted to do on their own has never been documented — not in credentialing, not in employment agreements. Because it was never defined, it cannot be adjusted.
- **Consequently, only two options exist:** take no action, or terminate. There is no intermediate step such as added oversight, a required consultation, or a defined period of review. Where the only alternative to no action is termination, no action is the usual result.
- **Chart auditing is now underway.** No policy governs what happens to the findings. Once running at scale, this produces a dated record of what was known, with no corresponding record of what was done.

---

## Proposed Structure

Three bodies, with separate and non-overlapping authority.

**Clinical Quality Committee.** Adopts clinical standards, sets the education agenda, and runs the quality audit program. At least two-thirds of its members are practicing clinicians; analytics, education, and enterprise priorities sit at the table as functions, without a vote. **It has no authority over any individual at any point** — it cannot restrict practice, cannot require anything of anyone, and cannot decide whether care met the standard of care. Where an audit finding raises a concern about a specific clinician, the finding is referred and the committee's involvement ends.

**Peer Review Committee.** Examines concerns about individual clinicians. Practicing clinicians only; no member holds management authority over the clinician under review. It screens referrals, opens reviews, and assigns each case to two of its own members from the same discipline. It recommends. It cannot impose a restriction.

Concerns reach it from two directions. The audit measures care against the standards Eventus has adopted and published. Everything else — a report from a colleague, a facility complaint, an adverse event, a manager, the clinician themselves — is measured against the generally accepted standard of care, which exists independently of anything Eventus writes down. **Most of clinical practice cannot be reduced to written standards, and a departure from accepted practice is reviewable whether or not a published standard addresses it.** The absence of a written standard is not a defense.

**Clinical Governance Council.** The deciding body. Nine voting members: the chairs of the Primary Care and Psychiatry Committees, five practicing clinicians elected by their colleagues, and the Chief Medical Officer and Chief Operating Officer. The two officers take part in governance business and are excluded by seat from any decision about an individual clinician; only the seven clinicians vote on those. It accepts, rejects, modifies, or returns recommendations from the Peer Review Committee. Chaired by an elected clinician who chairs neither committee and holds no management authority. Its decisions are final; there is no appeal to the Board.

---

## What Happens to Most Audit Findings

The audit will produce far more flags than concerns, and the charter is built around that rather than around the exception.

**Most flags are wrong or belong to a process rather than a person.** Before anything counts, every clinician is shown what was found and given ten business days to correct it — charts get attributed to the wrong person, diagnoses get miscoded, and notes sit where an automated search does not reach. What survives is then dispositioned as a systems problem, an individual finding, or no action. **A standing-order protocol that sends urine cultures on any change in mental status is worth finding, and finding it is worth more than any individual flag it produced.**

**A confirmed documentation gap, on its own, goes to no one.** It produces a letter stating what was found, an offer of help that carries no obligation and is not recorded either way, and a further look at a small sample of charts in about four months. If that look is clear, the matter ends and the clinician returns to the general population.

**Where a criterion would catch a large share of the clinicians it applies to, that is a systems condition by definition** and routes to fixing the standard, the audit question, or the teaching — not to a wave of referrals.

**And where the reviewers, who are clinicians, believe what they are looking at is a care problem rather than a documentation problem, that goes to the Peer Review Committee** on the ground of patient safety, with no threshold and no waiting period. The question is asked out loud at every disposition and the answer is recorded.

## Governance Safeguards

The Chair of Clinical Excellence chairs two of the three bodies and owns the audit program. The charters address this directly:

- The Chair is barred from chairing the Council.
- The Chair sits on the Council without a vote, and withdraws before deliberation on any matter concerning an individual clinician.
- Screening a referral requires two additional members.
- His committee cannot impose any restriction; it can only recommend.
- Hearing panels exclude all members of both committees.
- Any employee may refer a matter directly, bypassing him entirely.
- The Council receives an annual report of referral sources.

---

## How a Case Moves

1. A concern arrives — from the audit, a colleague, a facility, a manager, or the clinician. It is screened by three members within 10 business days. Most close here, with the disposition recorded in writing.
2. Where a review is opened, the clinician receives written notice and 20 days to respond.
3. A panel of clinicians from the same discipline reviews the care within 60 days.
4. Where the matter looks like a bounded gap in one clinical area, with nothing else in question, the committee may propose an **agreed resolution** — a defined educational plan, accepted voluntarily, closing the matter **with no finding, no professional review action, and nothing reportable.** The clinician may decline and take the ordinary review instead.
5. Otherwise, most outcomes are education, coaching, or a defined period of retrospective review. None of these restricts practice or is reportable.
6. Where a restriction is warranted, the clinician receives a hearing before clinicians who took no part in the review. The Council then decides.

Where a clinician presents immediate danger, three members may impose a restriction the same day. It expires automatically after 5 business days unless continued by vote of the full Council.

---

## What Does Not Change for Management

- **All employment authority is retained.** Hiring, termination, discipline, compensation, contracting, scheduling, assignment, and medical director nomination are unaffected. The charters state this expressly and add no delay to any of it.
- **Management is notified of every outcome** and of its operational effect. Committee deliberations are not provided, because their circulation would remove the legal protection from all participants, including the company.
- **The committees cannot access compensation, productivity, or revenue data.** This is the primary defense against any later claim that a decision was financially motivated.

---

## Benefits

- **An intermediate response becomes available.** Coaching, education, a defined period of review, or a required consultation. None of these currently exists as a formal option.
- **Federal legal protection attaches to the company and to participants.** It is available only to organizations that follow a formal peer review process; building the process is the qualifying condition.
- **Clinicians who report concerns are protected by federal law**, in good faith and regardless of outcome. This protection does not exist today and is relevant to recruiting and retention.
- **Diligence position improves.** A buyer's clinical team will ask what the peer review process is, what credentialing defines, and what performance actions have been taken. Current answers are none, nothing, and undocumented. This is a finding that is typically priced or held back at closing.

---

## Costs and Risks

**New reporting obligation.** Once scope of practice is defined, a restriction lasting more than 30 days becomes reportable to the National Practitioner Data Bank. This obligation does not exist today. It is material and it is new.

The alternative carries more exposure. Termination is not protected by federal peer review immunity and is the action most likely to result in litigation against those who recommended it. Reports are also infrequent by design; the majority of committee activity is education and oversight, which is not reportable.

**Financial.** One coordinator position, of which approximately one third is new scope; the remainder formalizes work already performed. Ongoing legal review, with counsel already engaged. Occasional external reviewers where a discipline cannot be matched internally. A costed figure will be provided separately.

**Time.** Two committees meeting monthly. The Council meeting quarterly for governance, convening on cases as needed. For most Council members, a few hours per quarter.

**Two instruments remain to be completed before the program operates.** The referral criteria — the schedule that decides which audit findings reach the Peer Review Committee — are deliberately kept outside the charters so they can be calibrated against real findings rather than guessed at, and are adopted by the committee and ratified by the Council. And the destination for matters raising a clinician's health or fitness to practice is designated by Council resolution rather than named in the charter; that is a question for the people and compliance functions with counsel, and the charters are drafted so that adoption need not wait on it.

**Speed.** Restricting how a clinician practices now requires a process. Employment decisions and emergency situations are unaffected.

This does not eliminate risk. It changes the risk from undocumented and unprotected to documented and protected.

---

## Recommendation

1. **Adopt the Clinical Governance Council charter by Board resolution**, delegating authority to decide matters concerning an individual clinician's competence or professional conduct, and retaining no role in individual cases.
2. **Approve the definition of clinical scope of practice** — unrestricted, requiring consultation in defined circumstances, or requiring prospective approval — so that an intermediate response becomes possible. Retrospective review sits outside that ladder deliberately: it does not restrict authority, and it is available to the Peer Review Committee without any action of the Council.
3. **Fund the Executive Coordinator position.** Legal protection depends on the process having been followed. That requires minutes kept to standard, records segregated, deadlines tracked, and recusals documented. This is not administrative support; it is the operational basis of the protection.
