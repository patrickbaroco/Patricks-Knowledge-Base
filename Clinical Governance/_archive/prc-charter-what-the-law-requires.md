# What the Peer Review Committee Charter Requires

**Memo to Jeff Wells, from Patrick Baroco · 2026-11-17**
*Companion to* The Case for Privilege *(2026-11-13). This is a physician's sorting of the Charter, written for you to test.*

---

## The short version

Most of the Peer Review Committee Charter is Eventus's own design. The law requires a small part of it, and another part is there only because Eventus has chosen to rely on a particular protection. I have sorted the Charter into those groups below. I need you to confirm or correct the sorting, so that we know which rules we can change freely and which we cannot.

## How the rules are sorted

- **Legal duty.** Binding whenever its conditions are met, whatever the Charter says.
- **Condition of a protection.** Required only because Eventus relies on a specific protection, or has chosen a specific safe harbor. Without that protection, the rule would be a choice.
- **Eventus choice.** Our design. Many of these support a protection without being required for it.
- **Statement of law.** A sentence describing the law. It needs your check, and it is not itself a rule.

## 1. Legal duties

These apply when their conditions are met:

- Data Bank reporting for physicians: an adverse action on Clinical Authorization lasting more than thirty days, a restriction in force more than thirty days while a review runs, and a resignation or surrender during an investigation. With these come the copy to the state licensing board, registration, and the attestation (§§ 9.4, 15.5 to 15.9).
- Any state duty to report an impaired practitioner. The Charter leaves it with whoever owns it (§ 4.3(d)).
- Indiana's right of a clinician under investigation to see the records about their own practice, and to appear and make a rebuttal (§ 13.4.1).
- Preserving records once litigation is reasonably foreseeable (§ 13.8).

## 2. Conditions of a protection

**Federal immunity under HCQIA.**

- An immediate restriction for imminent danger, followed by notice and a hearing or other adequate procedure (§§ 9.1, 9.3).
- The fourteen-day investigatory restriction (§ 9.8).
- The notice-and-hearing safe harbor: no restriction without Article X; what the notice must say; thirty days to request a hearing and thirty days' notice of it; a panel not in direct economic competition with the clinician; the rights at the hearing; a written recommendation and a written decision (§§ 3.2(a), 5.5, 8.4, 10.1 to 10.7, 10.10). This is the route we chose to meet the statute's fairness standard. It is one route, and missing a single condition does not by itself lose the immunity.

**State privilege.**

- A committee lawfully formed by an eligible organizer (§ 1.1). Indiana and Kentucky are open, as my earlier memo sets out.
- Virginia: adoption of the Medical Society of Virginia guidelines (§ 1.4).
- North Carolina: members licensed under Chapter 90 (§ 2.2).
- Indiana: at least half the members licensed providers (§ 2.2).

## 3. Statements of law to check

- What the Committee is established to serve as in each state (§ 1.3).
- Clinical Authorization as clinical privileges under 42 U.S.C. § 11151(3) (§ 1.6(b)).
- The description of the federal protections and their limits (§§ 15.1 to 15.4).
- That underlying facts and medical records are not protected (§ 13.7). This describes a limit of the privilege. It does not create a duty to disclose.

## 4. Eventus choices you should know about

Everything not listed above is our design. These are the choices most likely to matter to you:

- **The Committee carries the burden of proof at a hearing, by a preponderance** (§ 10.5(e)). The safe harbor does not require this; it gives the clinician more than the statute does.
- **The Chief Executive Officer establishes the Committee** (§ 1.1). Lawful formation is the condition, and the CEO is our chosen way of meeting it. In Indiana this is the open organizer question.
- **Deliberations are passed to no one** (§ 3.3). This supports privilege without being required for it, and it gives way to any access the law grants (§ 13.4.1).
- **The Panel investigates and the Committee decides** (§ 6.7). The law needs one identifiable, authorized review body. How the work is divided inside it is ours.
- **Minutes, separated records and a case register** (§§ 2.11, 13.1, 13.2). These are built to give an Ohio court document-level proof. No statute prescribes this system.
- **The Committee works outside any patient safety evaluation system** (§ 1.5). This is the open PSO question.
- **The agreed resolution and its two later chart examinations** (§ 5.7). Our design. Whether a resignation during the examinations is reportable is a legal question, and § 15.9 leaves it to you.
- **Reporting APCs.** Federal reporting for APCs is permissive. The Charter applies one consistent rule, to be set with you (§ 15.9).
