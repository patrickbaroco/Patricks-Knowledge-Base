# Commendation and recognition — proposed Charter text

Incorporated into `cqc-charter-draft.md` on 2026-09-28, with the two-committee revision. The Charter text is now the source; this note keeps the Bridgewater worked example.

## § 5.5(d) Commendation

**(d)(1) What it is.** Where the audit examines a chart and finds care that meets an improvement target designated under § 4.4(b), or documentation the Committee would adopt as an example of conforming documentation under § 4.3, the Committee may record a commendation attributed to the Practitioner. **A commendation records what the chart shows. It is not a disposition under (b), and it determines nothing about the Practitioner's competence**, favorably or otherwise.

**(d)(2) Selection.** Charts may be selected for this purpose under § 5.6(a), including by selecting facilities or Practitioners whose rate on a measure falls well below that of their comparison group. The thresholds are recorded in the audit register. Selection for this purpose carries no consequence, as selection under § 5.6(a) carries none.

**(d)(3) Notice.** The Practitioner is told of the commendation in writing, stating the chart, the date of service and what the chart showed. Nothing is required in response.

**(d)(4) It offsets nothing.** A commendation is not a factor in any disposition under (b), in any criterion under § 6.2, or in follow-up under § 5.11, and is not transmitted under § 6.3. **A finding on one chart is not answered by excellence on another.** The Practitioner may supply a commendation to the PRC.

**(d)(5) Record.** A commendation is part of the protected record under § 8.1(b). The Committee enters it in no employment or performance record. A commendation is a determination concerning an identified Practitioner, and § 2.5 applies to it.

**(d)(6) Use as an example.** Where a commended chart is to become an example of conforming documentation, a clean version prepared for that purpose enters the open record under § 8.1(a), de-identified as to the resident and, unless the Practitioner consents in writing to be named, as to the Practitioner. The commendation and the chart remain protected.

**(d)(7) The Committee does not publish a commendation.** Public recognition is governed by § 7.6 and draws on nothing in the protected record.

## § 7.6 Recognition and shared practice

**(a) Recognition is of facilities and teams.** The Committee may recognize a facility, or the clinical team serving it, for performance on an organizational measure. **Recognition rests only on information outside the Committee's records** — operational reports the Entity produces in the ordinary course — and never on an observation, a finding, a commendation, or the constitution of a comparison group. A rate is not attributed to an individual Practitioner under § 4.4, and recognition is subject to that rule as any other use of a rate is.

**(b) Shared practice is learned openly.** Where the Committee wishes to learn how a facility achieves its performance, the inquiry is conducted by the education function under § 2.8 by interview and observation of process, not by chart audit, and its product is written for the open record under § 8.1(a). A practice description states what the facility does, names the facility only with the consent of its leadership, identifies no resident, and **makes no comparison naming any other facility or Practitioner.**

**(c) Nothing public refers to the audit.** No recognition or practice description states or implies that the audit examined, selected or found anything.

## § 8.1.1 addition

- **Open, under (a):** recognitions under § 7.6(a) and practice descriptions under § 7.6(b).
- **Protected, under (b):** commendations under § 5.5(d) and the selection thresholds that produced them.

## Questions for counsel (Jeff Wells)

- Whether a Practitioner's own disclosure of a commendation letter affects protection of the record it came from.
- Whether a practice description prepared by the education function after the audit selected the facility is safely outside the protected record, given § 8.1's document-by-document approach in Ohio.

## Worked example: Bridgewater (drafted 2026-09-28)

Data (August 2026 PIMS): Bridgewater Home SNF, census 118, 47 residents with dementia, 1 aged 65+ with dementia on AP (2%); 5 on AP in the whole building. Comparison band (30–60% dementia share): median 20%, 10th percentile 6%. Emily Johnson NP is the primary clinician.

**Commendation track (protected; requires an adopted AP standard in effect):**
1. Selection under § 5.6(a): analytics flags Bridgewater below the band's 10th percentile; first confirms the medication data are complete (5 of 118 on AP is low enough to check).
2. Attribution: panel data confirm which residents are Emily Johnson's.
3. Chart review by Committee clinician members without Employment Authority: residents with dementia, including any tapered off AP and the one still on it, read against the standard.
4. Disposition in executive session: commendation under § 5.5(d) for charts meeting the improvement target or fit to serve as model documentation.
5. Letter from the Chair to Emily Johnson only: charts, dates of service, what they showed; nothing required; asks consent to use a de-identified note as a model example and whether to be named.
6. With consent, a clean de-identified version enters the open record.

**Study track (open; can start now):**
1. Trigger is the operational PIMS rate, not the audit.
2. Request through Steve Colvin to Bridgewater's administrator, DON and medical director; leadership consents to the visit and to being named.
3. Conducted by the education function with a clinician fluent in LTC behavioral care (e.g., consultant pharmacist or psychiatric NP), neither of whom took part in the commendation.
4. Interviews: Emily Johnson NP, DON, unit managers, consultant pharmacist, social work/activities. Observe a behavior huddle and a pharmacy review. Questions: PRN antipsychotic orders; behaviors handled before a call; residents admitted on AP from hospital; dose-reduction scheduling; outside psychiatry; family expectations; whether admissions of behaviorally complex residents are declined.
5. Product: practice description in the open record; team recognition under § 7.6(a); pairing with a high-rate Virginia building through Colvin; analytics tracks whether the paired building moves.
