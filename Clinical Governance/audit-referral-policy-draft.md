## Article I — Status, Scope, and What a Referral Is

**1.1 Status.** This Policy is the written schedule of referral criteria required by § 6.2 of the Charter of the Clinical Quality Committee ("the Committee"). It is adopted by the Committee, takes effect on the concurrence of the Peer Review Committee under § 11.5 of that Committee's Charter, and is reviewed annually under Article IX. Capitalized terms not defined here have the meanings given in § 1.6 of the Peer Review Committee's Charter and in § 5.1 of the Committee's Charter.

**Cycle.** A **cycle** is the sweep of one measure across the population to which it applies, as provided in the audit plan and recorded in the audit register with its opening and closing dates. The term fixes the population over which the brake is computed under Article V, the point at which adequacy is determined under § 4.3, and the span within which findings on one measure are a single occasion under § 7.1, and it is defined here because the Charter uses it without fixing its boundaries.

**1.2 What this Policy governs.** One route: the Committee's referral of an individual finding to the Peer Review Committee ("the PRC") under §§ 6.1–6.5 of the Committee's Charter. It states the criteria on which such a referral is made, the conditions every criterion depends on, and the manner in which the determination is taken.

**1.3 What this Policy does not govern.** A matter may come to the PRC from any source under § 4.1 of its Charter — from any Practitioner or employee, from Management, from a contracted facility, from a patient or family member, from an adverse event or an external event, or from the Practitioner concerned. **Such a matter is evaluated against the standard of care under § 8.1 of that Charter and requires no adopted standard, no criterion under this Policy, and no notice period.** A member of the Committee holding a concern about the standard of care refers under § 6.7(c) of the Committee's Charter as a person, and § 5.5(b)(1) of that Charter puts that question at every disposition so the route is an ordinary item of business.

**Nothing in this Policy limits what may be referred to, or reviewed by, the PRC.**

**1.4 What the criteria therefore bound.** These criteria govern the route by which noncompliance with an adopted standard, unaccompanied by an independent concern about the standard of care or about patient safety, may reach professional review. That is the proposition § 4.7 of the PRC Charter states and enforces at screening.

**The consequence for how this Policy is calibrated.** Every criterion here operates on a Practitioner about whose care no one has raised a concern. **The question at each annual review is whether the criteria refer the right matters, not whether they refer enough of them**, and a criterion drawn so that it fires readily converts ordinary nonconformity into professional review — the outcome that §§ 4.4(b), 5.7, 5.9, 5.5(c) and 5.11 of the Charter are each directed at avoiding.

**1.5 What meeting a criterion establishes.** A referral under this Policy establishes two things and no others: that an individual finding was made under § 5.1 of the Charter, and that a criterion stated here was met. **The Entity's adoption of these criteria does not by itself establish that care meeting one of them fell below the standard of care.** The PRC screens every referral, makes its own findings on its own record, and is not bound by the Committee's view.

**1.6 This Policy is open.** It is part of the open record under § 8.1(a) of the Charter, classified there by § 8.1.1, **because a Practitioner is entitled to know what may cause a referral.** A copy is held in the PRC's governance record under § 13.1(a) of its Charter, together with the record of its concurrence.

**1.7 No standard or measure is named here.** Standards are adopted, revised and retired by the Committee under Article IV of its Charter. This Policy states what a criterion requires of a measure; **which measures satisfy those requirements is recorded per standard in the standards register**, under Article VIII. This division exists so that a standards revision does not require this Policy to be revised, or the PRC's concurrence to be sought again.

## Article II — Preconditions

**2.1 The gate.** No criterion in Article III is reached unless all of the following are true. They are stated once here so that no criterion restates them and so that the determination can be checked in one place.

- (a) The matter is an **individual finding** under § 5.1 of the Charter — a validated observation, and not an observation, a rate, or a selection.
- (b) The standard was designated a **minimum expectation** under § 4.4(a), by express vote, recorded in the standards register.
- (c) The standard was **disseminated**, the dissemination documented, and the standard **in effect at the time of the care reviewed**. (§ 4.5)
- (d) The period under § 5.7 had run **against the care**, and not against the audit.
- (e) The correction step under § 5.5(a) is complete: the notice under § 5.5(a)(5) was issued, the response period has expired or a response has been received and dispositioned, and the grounds sustained are recorded. **Where the criterion is Criterion B and its first occasion is a review report under § 5.4.1, the step has also reached the observations that report recorded**, under § 5.4.2(a).
- (f) The observation was dispositioned **Individual** under § 5.5(b).
- (g) Where the criterion rests on a rate, the rate has been **recomputed** under § 5.5(a)(7).
- (h) The observation was **generated on or after the effective date of the Charter**, § 8.7 excluding observations and findings generated before that date from the Committee's record and from every referral criterion.
- (i) The standards register carries an entry or a designation under Article VIII under which **the criterion in question** may reach a finding on this measure.

**2.2 What never counts toward any criterion.** A closed list. Each item is excluded by a provision of the Charter, and stating them together prevents the question being argued item by item at the point of referral.

- An observation dispositioned **Systems** or **No action**, and any observation corrected or withdrawn on a ground under § 5.5(a)(1).
- A **thin entry** under § 5.10(c).
- An observation arising from the sample of charts that satisfied a standard under § 5.10(b).
- A finding against a standard designated an **improvement target** under § 4.4(b), or against a standard carrying no recorded designation.
- A **rate target** under § 4.4, which is not a standard and is never attributed to an individual.
- **Whether an offer of support under § 5.5(c)(5) was taken up.** Excluded by § 5.5(c)(5)(iii); restated here because that subsection names this Policy.
- **Selection** — that a Practitioner or a chart was selected for examination, that they were selected again, and the thresholds by which they were selected. (§ 5.6(a))
- **A rate standing alone.** A rate may be the trigger for referring findings that exist; it is never itself the matter referred.
- **Volume.** (§ 5.8(b))
- **An observation in a review report under § 5.4.1 that the correction step has not validated.** It may open a follow-up look under § 5.11.2 and nothing more; it is part of a first occasion under Criterion B only once validated under § 5.4.2(a).
- **A clinical comment under § 5.5(e)**, including a comment made again at a follow-up look.
- **A review report issued in a cycle to which § 5.7 of this Policy applied.**

## Article III — The Criteria

**3.1 Three criteria, and a designation.** A finding satisfying Article II is referred where it meets Criterion A, Criterion B, or Criterion C. **Criterion A operates only on a standard carrying a single-occasion designation**, made at adoption under Article VIII.

**3.2 Criterion A — single-occasion referral.** **One individual finding refers, where the standard carries a single-occasion designation.** No repetition and no rate is required.

The designation takes one of two forms, recorded at adoption:

- **(a) Unqualified** — any individual finding on the standard refers on a single occasion.
- **(b) Decision-departure** — an individual finding refers on a single occasion where § 5.5(a)(9) records the apparent departure as being **in the decision** rather than only in its documentation. A departure recorded as being in the documentation alone is referable on that standard under Criterion B or Criterion C, and not under this Criterion.

**Why the designation attaches to the standard and not to the case.** A criterion asking how serious a particular instance was would ask this Committee for a judgment about an identified Practitioner's care, which § 1.3 of the Charter reserves to the PRC and which § 5.5(b)(1) routes to § 6.7(c) as the act of the member who holds it. **Designating a standard asks a different question — whether a single departure from this requirement warrants professional review — and it is answered before anyone knows whose chart it will be**, on the record, and in a register open to every Practitioner it applies to.

**What this criterion does not reach.** A serious case arising on a standard carrying no such designation. **The audit-referral process is not the route for it**, and §§ 4.1 and 8.1 of the PRC Charter and § 6.7 of the Committee's Charter are, on terms carrying no threshold, no standard requirement and no notice period.

**3.3 Criterion B — repetition after notice.** **A second individual finding on the same standard, for care furnished in the follow-up window opened by the first.**

Its elements:

- (a) A first occasion on the standard, issued to this Practitioner: **a review report under § 5.4.1 recording one or more observations standing**, or **a first individual finding closed out under § 5.5(c).** Section 5.5(c)(3) distinguishes that notice from dissemination of the standard to everyone, and this criterion rests on the former.
- (b) A second individual finding **on the same standard**.
- (c) **For care furnished in the follow-up window opened under § 5.11.2**, and not merely audited in it.
- (d) **The follow-up look under § 5.11.2 has been conducted.**
- (e) Both findings satisfying Article II.
- (f) The count still running under Article VII.

**Where the first occasion is a review report, its observations are validated at the second.** Section 5.4.2(a) of the Charter runs the correction step on the observations standing in that report together with the new ones, in one notice and one response period. An observation the step corrects or withdraws is no part of the first occasion. **Where none survives as an individual finding, there is no first occasion and this criterion is not met.** The first review is kept to a report so that the correction step, which asks time of the Practitioner and of the Committee, is spent where a referral could follow, and this is the point at which one could.

**Why (c) is bounded by the window at both ends.** Section 5.11.2 provides that the period before the window opens is time given to the Practitioner to act, and that **no care furnished in it is examined** — it is set so that a Practitioner who takes up the offer under § 5.5(c)(5) can request it, have it arranged, attend it, and begin applying it first. **A criterion referring on care furnished in that period would refer on care the Charter declines to look at**, and would make the escalation turn on conduct occurring before the Practitioner had the time the Charter allots them.

**The window closes the criterion at the other end for a different reason.** Once the follow-up has been conducted, the matter either produced a finding, which is the second occasion, or it did not and the count ends under § 7.3. **Care furnished after the window has no second occasion to attach to**, and a departure found in it opens a matter of its own under § 5.4.1 or § 5.5(c), and § 5.11.2, in the ordinary way.

**A finding on care furnished in the opening period is not a second occasion.** The observation is reported to the Practitioner under § 5.4.1, or dispositioned and reported as § 5.5(c)(1) requires where § 5.4.2 applies to it, and it neither opens a separate count nor a separate follow-up; it is recorded with the matter already open on that standard.

**Why (d) is an element and not an administrative step.** Section 6.3 of the Charter provides that a referral resting on repetition after notice carries the review report or close-out notice that opened the follow-up, the disclosure of the follow-up made under § 5.11.4, and **the fact and date of the follow-up conducted** — and that such a referral is not capable of being screened without them. **The chain is what the PRC screens under § 4.4 of its Charter**, and a referral made before the follow-up has been conducted would arrive without a third of it.

A finding produced by the follow-up look itself satisfies (b), (c) and (d) by construction, since that look examines only care furnished in the window. **That is § 5.11.3(b) operating as drafted**, and the follow-up is the ordinary way this criterion is met. **Where a second observation arises otherwise** — an ordinary sweep, or an examination directed by a selection rule, reaching care furnished in the window before the follow-up has been conducted — **it is checked with the follow-up look under § 5.4.2(a) of the Charter, and the criterion is not met until the follow-up has been conducted**, which the Coordinator diaries under § 2.7 and brings to the Committee when it falls due. The Committee is not put to a choice between referring without the chain and losing the finding.

**3.4 Criterion C — pattern against a comparison group.** **Individual findings on a measure at a rate materially above the Practitioner's comparison group, where an adequate group exists.**

Its elements:

- (a) A comparison group **adequate under Article IV** exists for this Practitioner on this measure. Where it does not, **no pattern criterion applies to that measure for that Practitioner** — § 5.6(d), which admits of no exercise of judgment.
- (b) The Practitioner's rate, **recomputed under § 5.5(a)(7)**, exceeds the group rate by [a margin adopted under § 9.3].
- (c) The Practitioner has not fewer than **[n]** cases in the denominator for that measure in that cycle, and not fewer than **[m]** individual findings. **This floor can only prevent a referral and never cause one**, and is not a criterion of volume under § 5.8(b).
- (d) The instrument is validated and currently revalidated under § 5.4 and § 5.5(a)(8)(i).
- (e) The volume brake under Article V has been applied and has not tripped.

**How findings arise for this criterion.** An observation in a review report under § 5.4.1 is not a finding. **The group rate under (b) is computed on the observations of the group as reported, with corrections received under § 5.4.1(b) applied**; the group's observations are not put through the correction step. Correction lowers the referred Practitioner's rate and not the group's, so the comparison errs in the Practitioner's favor. Where a Practitioner's reported observations on a measure, on the figures as reported, would meet elements (a) through (c), § 5.4.2(c) of the Charter runs the correction step on them, and this criterion is evaluated on what survives the step and the recomputation under § 5.5(a)(7).

**What is referred is the findings.** The referral under § 6.3 of the Charter identifies the individual findings and carries them; the rate is the reason the Committee referred them. **A rate describes a population and never describes a Practitioner's care in a given episode**, and this criterion does not alter that.

**This criterion operates on a measure once the values under § 9.3 have been adopted for it, and not before.** Its elements cannot be evaluated until the margin, the floors and the denominator exist, and § 9.3 provides that they are set against the distributions the first cycles produce. **This is a consequence of the criterion having no content until it is calibrated**, and not a period of dormancy: no separate activation is required, no further concurrence of the PRC is needed, and adequacy is thereafter determined for each measure in each cycle rather than assumed. **On a measure whose denominator per Practitioner is small, and in a discipline too small to compose a group, this criterion will not operate at all.** It is stated here so that a Practitioner reading this Policy can determine for themselves whether it can reach them, and the adequacy test in Article IV is what they would apply.

## Article IV — Comparison Groups

**4.1 Construction.** Comparison groups are constructed by the analytics function on the terms of this Article, as § 5.6(c) provides. **A group definition is adopted with the standard it attaches to and recorded in the standards register**, not in this Policy — a definition is measure-specific, and § 1.7 keeps what is measure-specific out of an instrument that takes effect only on the PRC's concurrence. A group is defined by the characteristics that plausibly affect the measure, ordinarily including discipline, setting and panel size, and where relevant acuity, length of stay or facility. **The construction of each group is recorded, and a group is not constructed after the Practitioner's rate is known.**

**4.2 A group is two things, and they are held separately.** **Its definition** is the characteristics by which it is constructed, adopted under this Article before any rate is known. **Its constitution in a cycle** is which Practitioners fell within it and what their rates were.

**The definition is open under § 8.1.1 of the Charter, is recorded in the standards register, and is disseminated with the standard it attaches to**, because a Practitioner is entitled to know the terms on which a comparison is drawn against them. **The constitution is protected under § 8.1(b), is recorded in the audit register, and is disseminated to no one** — it is derived from observations and is a record of other Practitioners' care. The two are maintained as separate documents, § 8.1 providing that no document is ever in both records.

**4.3 Adequacy.** Adequacy is determined for each measure in each cycle, on the data then held. A group is adequate where:

- (a) it comprises not fewer than **[ten]** Practitioners;
- (b) each has not fewer than **[d]** cases in the denominator for that measure in that cycle, **[d]** being adopted separately from the floor under § 3.4(c); and
- (c) the group rate is neither zero nor complete, a group at either extreme affording nothing above which a rate can be.

**4.4 Where adequacy fails.** **Element (a) of Criterion C is not satisfied and the criterion is not met** for that measure for that Practitioner, and the failure is recorded as a fact about the measure and reported with the cycle. Section 5.6(d) of the Charter limits pattern criteria only: Criterion A, Criterion B, and every route outside this Policy are unaffected.

**4.5 Groups are constructed whether or not a criterion can use them.** The analytics function reports organization-wide performance and whether interventions moved the measures they targeted, under § 2.6 and § 7.4 of the Charter. **A group too small to support a referral remains what shows the Committee where to teach**, which is the larger part of what the audit program is for.

**4.6 An external benchmark is not a comparison group.** A rate published by a payer, registry or rating program is an organizational performance target maintained by the analytics function under § 2.6 and § 5.8(c), and **is never attributed to an individual or used to satisfy Article IV.** Where no adequate internal group exists, Criterion C does not apply.

## Article V — The Volume Brake

**5.1 The computation.** Before a referral is made on a measure in a cycle, the Committee determines how many Practitioners **Criterion B and Criterion C** would refer on that measure in that cycle. Criterion A is not counted, the brake not applying to it. **A follow-up look is counted with the cycle in which the review report or close-out that opened it issued, and a look opened at a follow-up look with the cycle of that look.** The determination under Criterion B for a cycle is made when the looks counted with it have been conducted. **Referrals under Criterion C are made when the correction steps under § 5.4.2(c) of the Charter for the cycle have closed**, and count toward the number under § 5.2 for that cycle; where the number then exceeds the brake, no further referral under Criterion B is made on that measure in that cycle. **The determination is made after the corrections under § 5.5(a) and the recomputation under § 5.5(a)(7)**, on the findings as they stand.

**5.2 When the brake trips.** Where the number exceeds **[five] percent** of the Practitioners to whom the measure applies **and** exceeds **[three]** Practitioners, **no referral is made on that measure in that cycle under Criterion B or Criterion C**, and the matter routes under § 5.9 of the Charter to standards revision, to the audit question, or to the education agenda.

**Both conditions are required.** A proportion alone misdescribes a small group, in which two referrals may be a quarter of its members. The floor is what distinguishes a wave from ordinary volume where the population is small.

**5.3 What becomes of the findings.** **A finding in a cycle in which the brake tripped is extinguished for referral under Criterion B and Criterion C on that measure.** It is not held over to a later cycle. Referral would otherwise turn on when a Practitioner's charts happened to be examined.

**Criterion A is unaffected**, consistently with § 5.5 of this Policy.

**5.4 And of the count.** **Such a finding does not count as a first occasion under Criterion B.** Section 5.9 of the Charter treats a criterion that would refer a large fraction of Practitioners as a systems condition, and a count preserved out of such a cycle would carry that condition forward against individuals.

**The follow-up look is unaffected and is conducted.** The finding was closed out under § 5.5(c) without referral, which is the condition on which § 5.11.2 requires the Committee to examine a sample of that Practitioner's charts, except for a finding § 5.11.2(b) excludes. **Where that look produces an individual finding, that finding is a first occasion under Criterion B**, and the close-out issued on it opens the count in the ordinary way.

**5.5 What is unaffected.** The close-out notice issues under § 5.5(c) as it otherwise would. **The brake does not apply to Criterion A**, and § 5.9 provides expressly that it does not preclude referral under § 6.7.

**5.6 Activations are visible.** Each activation is recorded with the cycle and reported under § 7.4 of the Charter, which requires the report to state each occasion on which referral on a measure was withheld under § 5.9 and what was done instead. **A brake whose operation is not reported is indistinguishable from a criterion nobody meets**, and neither the Committee nor the PRC can calibrate this Policy under Article IX without knowing which it was.


**5.7 At the review report.** Where the review reports in a cycle record observations on a measure for more than **[twenty-five] percent** of the Practitioners whose charts were examined **and** for more than **[three]** Practitioners, **§ 5.9 of the Charter applies at the review report.** No follow-up look under § 5.11.2 opens on those reports, they open no count under Criterion B, the correction step under § 5.4.2(c) of the Charter does not run on that measure in that cycle, and the measure routes to standards revision, to the audit question, or to the education agenda. The determination is made on the cycle summary under § 5.4.1(e), before corrections, and is recorded and reported as § 5.6 provides. **Criterion A is unaffected.**

**The fraction is higher than the one in § 5.2** because it counts observations as reported, before correction, and a first sweep of a measure produces many more of them than it would referrals. Its purpose is the same: a measure most Practitioners do not yet meet is a condition of the standard, the audit question or the teaching, and individual follow-up looks across a large share of the population would spend the program's reviewing time on a systems condition.

## Article VI — Making the Referral

**6.1 Referral is mandatory.** Where a finding satisfies Article II and meets a criterion under Article III, the Committee refers. Section 5.5(b) of the Charter classifies the observation and leaves the consequence to these criteria; this Article is where that consequence is stated. **The determination is not an occasion to reconsider the disposition.**

**The judgment this program asks of the Committee is made at the disposition under § 5.5(b)** — Systems, Individual, or No action — on the record, with the safety question put aloud under § 5.5(b)(1), and it is made once. A condition of staffing, process, tooling, workflow or the standard itself is dispositioned Systems there, and a finding so dispositioned reaches no criterion in this Policy. **A discretion exercised after that point operates on the same facts with no further information, and leaves no record capable of review.**

**6.2 What a referral costs.** A referral establishes what § 1.5 states and nothing more. The PRC screens every referral, and § 4.6 of its Charter forbids any finding adverse to a Practitioner at screening. **Declining to refer a validated finding on a criterion in effect is the more consequential act**, which is why the obligation runs the way it does.

**6.3 The schedule is a floor and a ceiling.** The Committee refers under §§ 6.1–6.5 of its Charter where a criterion here is met, and **does not refer under §§ 6.1–6.5 where none is.** A member holding a concern the criteria do not reach refers under § 6.7(c) as a person.

**6.4 The standing exception, and it is the only one.** The volume brake under Article V, which is a determination recorded with the cycle. **Section 6.6 of this Policy provides a further and expiring exception**, and adds to this section only by amendment under § 9.4.

**Two things that are not exceptions to the obligation**, stated because each is otherwise mistaken for one. Where no adequate comparison group exists, element (a) of Criterion C fails and the criterion is not met. Where a finding was withdrawn or corrected under § 5.5(a), or rests on a measure that failed revalidation under § 5.5(a)(8)(i), the precondition in Article II fails and there is no finding to refer. **In neither case does an obligation arise that something then displaces.**

**6.5 The obligation does not depend on what is before the PRC.** **The Committee neither inquires nor is required to establish whether the same care is already before the PRC by another route**, and refers in the ordinary course. Section 6.5 of the Charter ends the Committee's involvement at referral and gives it no report of what follows; **a rule requiring it to establish the state of the PRC's docket would open a channel in the other direction.** What is done with a referral once made is determined at screening under Article IV of the PRC Charter.

**6.6 Discretion during the first period.** **Until the conclusion of the first annual review under § 9.1, and in no event beyond [eighteen] months from the effective date, the Committee may decline to refer where a criterion is met.** A determination under this section is recorded with its reasons and reported under § 7.4 of the Charter. **A finding on which referral is declined is closed out under § 5.5(c), and counts as a first occasion under Criterion B where § 5.11.2(b) of the Charter opens a follow-up look on it.**

**At the first annual review, each such determination is either carried into § 6.4 as an express exception, by amendment under § 9.4, or discarded — and this section then ceases to have effect.** Section 6.2 of the Charter keeps these criteria out of the Charter so that they may be calibrated against actual findings; this section makes the first period's exercise of judgment an input to that calibration. **It is limited on its face and does not renew.**

**6.7 Who determines.** The determination is made by the members eligible under § 2.5 of the Charter and not recused under § 9.6, with not fewer than the number § 9.3 requires present, in executive session under § 9.5, and recorded in the minutes under § 9.4.

**6.8 What accompanies the referral.** What § 6.3 of the Charter requires, together with **the criterion met**. Not the Committee's deliberations, and not any concern recorded under § 5.5(b)(1), which travels under § 6.7 as the act of the person holding it or does not travel.

**6.9 Sequence.** The close-out notice under § 5.5(c) issues, stating the fact of referral as § 5.5(c)(4) requires and nothing about what the PRC may do. The Coordinator records the referral and diaries any follow-up owed under § 5.11.

## Article VII — Counting

**7.1 An occasion.** An observation is chart-level under § 5.1 of the Charter and an individual finding is a validated observation, so a single sweep of a Practitioner's charts may produce several findings on one measure. **An occasion under Criterion B is a review report under § 5.4.1 recording one or more observations standing on a standard, or a close-out notice under § 5.5(c) recording one or more individual findings on it**, and not each observation or finding it records.

**The unit is the notice because the notice is what Criterion B rests on.** Section 5.5(c)(3) makes the escalation depend on this Practitioner having been told what this audit found in these charts, and a Practitioner told once has been told once however many charts the notice covered. **A cycle producing three findings on one measure is one occasion**, and the second occasion is a finding on care furnished in the follow-up window the first opened.

**7.2 Findings do not aggregate across measures.** Criterion B counts occasions on one standard, and no criterion here counts findings across measures.

**Why the count is confined to one standard.** A count spanning measures adds findings from instruments carrying different validations under § 5.4, different rates of grounds sustained under § 5.5(a)(8), and different rates of thin entries under § 5.10(c). It also rises with the number of denominators a Practitioner appears in, so that a large and complex panel accumulates findings across measures at rates at or below the group on each of them — and § 5.8(b) forecloses a criterion operating in that way. **No comparison group can be constructed for such a count**, which is the condition § 5.6(d) treats as disqualifying.

**Findings across several measures remain a sound reason to examine more charts.** That is selection under § 5.6(a), which requires no comparison group, no notice period and no adequate denominator and carries no consequence, and the thresholds for it are recorded in the audit register. **A rule of that kind is applied to the population in each cycle and selects whoever meets it**; an examination directed at a named Practitioner by decision, recurring, is the monitoring status § 5.11.3(c) denies this Committee the authority to impose.

**7.3 The count ends when the follow-up look closes the matter.** Where the follow-up under § 5.11 produces no individual finding, or where there was no chart within the population to examine, the matter closes under § 5.11.3(a) or § 5.11.5 and **the first occasion no longer counts under Criterion B.**

**Unless another examination has already found one.** Where an individual finding on that standard has been made for care furnished in the follow-up window, by any examination, the count does not end on the ground that the follow-up sample did not reach it. **A sample that misses what another examination found is evidence about the sample**, and § 5.11.3(a) rests on there being nothing to find.

**The count is extinguished rather than expiring.** Section 5.11.3(a) returns the Practitioner to the general population of the audit program. A count surviving that closure would allow a finding years old to combine with a new one and reopen a matter the Charter has closed, and an examination directed at a named Practitioner on the strength of it would be the recurring examination § 5.11.3(c) denies this Committee the authority to direct. **A referral under Criterion B therefore rests on a look the Committee designed and conducted, and not on accumulated history.**

**A later observation or finding on the same measure is a new matter**, reported under § 5.4.1 or closed out under § 5.5(c), and followed up under § 5.11.2 in the ordinary course.

**7.4 Backstop.** A finding that has not been extinguished under § 7.3 ceases to count **[twelve]** months after the date of the review report or close-out notice that recorded it as an observation or a finding, or [sixty] days after the follow-up look is conducted, whichever is later. **A count does not cease while the determination under § 5.1 is pending.** This section governs the case in which a follow-up has not fallen due or has not been conducted, **so that a count is not held open for administrative reasons.**

**7.5 Findings later corrected.** A finding withdrawn or corrected under § 5.5(a) is removed from every count, and a referral resting on it is withdrawn to the PRC with the reason recorded.

**7.6 Revision of a standard.** **The record of adoption of a revision states whether the revision resets the count on that standard. Absent such a statement, the revision resets it.**

**The determination is made at adoption.** A test applied at the point of referral is argued at the point of referral, by a Practitioner with reason to say the revision changed the requirement and a Committee with reason to say it did not — which is the reason § 4.4 gives for making its own designation at adoption. **The default resets** because a revision is an adoption under § 4.3, carrying dissemination under § 4.5 and a fresh period under § 5.7; preserving a count is accordingly a deliberate act, recorded.

**A reset requires a fresh review report or close-out before Criterion B operates again.** A report or close-out issued on the standard as it stood does not supply the notice that criterion requires for the standard as revised.

**7.7 Redesignation and retirement.** Redesignation to § 4.4(b), and retirement, end the count. Redesignation from § 4.4(b) to § 4.4(a) begins a count from the date of redesignation, and nothing from the period of the improvement target carries into it.

**7.8 A revision that narrows the requirement.** **Where a revision removes or narrows the requirement on which a finding rested, the finding counts toward no criterion**, whether or not the revision resets the count and whether or not it falls within the period in § 7.4.

---

## Article VIII — What the Standards Register Records

**8.1 Two records at adoption, made independently.** On adoption of a standard the Committee records the following in the standards register, each by express vote.

**(a) Which of the pattern and repetition criteria may reach a finding on the measure** — **None**, **Criterion B**, or **Criterion B and Criterion C**. Where a criterion is not named it does not reach a finding on that measure. **Criterion C is not recorded alone**, a measure whose rate will support a comparison supporting a finding on a chart.

**(b) Whether the standard carries a single-occasion designation** under § 3.2, and if so in which of the two forms there stated.

**The two are independent of each other.** A standard may carry a single-occasion designation with any entry under (a), including None — the questions they answer are different, (a) asking what the measurement of the standard will bear and (b) what a single departure from the requirement is worth. **A designation under (b) is the entry under Article VIII by which Criterion A reaches a finding**, for the purpose of § 2.1(i).

**8.2 What the entry answers.** Whether a measure can bear a consequence is a question about the measurement and not about the requirement. **A standard may be a minimum expectation under § 4.4(a) while the measure of it is not yet capable of supporting a referral** — because the substance it asks about is habitually recorded by another discipline, because the denominator per Practitioner will not support a rate, or because the care it measures is routinely shared and attribution is sustained often under § 5.5(a)(1)(i). Section 4.4(a) is permissive in its terms, an individual finding **may** support a referral; this Policy is where that is granted or withheld.

**8.3 An entry of None is an ordinary entry.** A measure so recorded runs as a review: charts are examined, review reports issue under § 5.4.1 with the offer of support, corrections are applied and rates recomputed, and the education agenda and standards revision follow. **The follow-up look under § 5.11 does not run on it, and the correction step and disposition under § 5.5 run only as § 5.4.2(b) of the Charter provides** where the standard carries a designation under § 8.1(b). It produces no referral under Criterion B or Criterion C. The measure is re-measured under § 5.11.6. **Most measures the Committee adopts will carry this entry**, and § 5.9 states what follows where a measure appears otherwise.

**8.4 The entry is revisited at review.** It is reviewed with the standard under § 4.6, on the evidence Article IX requires the annual review to consider.

**8.5 Selection thresholds are not recorded here.** They are recorded in the audit register and are part of the protected record under § 8.1.1, and appear neither in a standard nor in this Policy.

## Article IX — Review, Calibration, and Amendment

**9.1 Annual review.** The Committee reviews this Policy annually and submits it to the PRC, which reviews its concurrence under § 11.5 of its Charter.

**9.2 What the review considers.** Referrals made, by criterion. The dispositions of the PRC in the de-identified aggregate under § 7.2 of the Charter. Grounds sustained by measure under § 5.5(a)(8). Rates of thin entries under § 5.10(c). Activations of the volume brake and what was done instead. Measures on which no adequate comparison group could be constructed. Determinations under § 6.6 and what each was converted into. Follow-up outcomes under § 5.11 as reported under § 7.4.

**And the distribution of individual findings per Practitioner across measures**, on which no criterion here operates. It is reported because the confinement of the count to a single standard under § 7.2 should be examined against what the program encounters, and the review cannot examine it without the figure.

**9.3 Values set at first adoption.** The margin under § 3.4(b), the floors under § 3.4(c), and the group denominator [d] under § 4.3(b) are adopted by the Committee against the distributions the first cycles produce, and reviewed annually. **They are not set before there is a baseline**, and each of them can only narrow a criterion.

**9.4 Amendment.** By the Committee, taking effect on the PRC's concurrence in the manner of adoption.

**9.5 Effective date.** [DATE], being the date of the PRC's concurrence or such later date as the Committee states. **Every period in this Policy that runs from "the effective date" runs from that date**, which is not the effective date of the Charter under § 10.5 of that instrument and is not earlier than it.
