Clinical governance framework · draft for discussion

Where the existing work goes

Both committees take over work the group already does, in a more defined form.

This traces the five clinical governance activities running today into what will hold them. None of the work disappears, and one forum keeps running under a name that describes it.

Not adopted. Charters drafted, under revision, prepared for counsel review.

Where each activity lands

Read left to right. Everything on the left runs today.

Move one

The steering committee becomes the Clinical Quality Committee

Today

A standing committee that sets an agenda for each meeting and works through clinical quality topics with broad attendance.

Becomes

The Clinical Quality Committee — established by the CEO's written Authorization, with defined membership, a clinician floor written into the charter, and three named jobs: adopt the standards, set the education agenda, run the audit.

What that adds

  • A priority becomes a defined measure, adopted by express vote and recorded in a standards register.
  • Each measure carries the date its measurement period begins and the date the committee looks again — both set at adoption.
  • A standing obligation to report whether a measure moved, quarterly, to the Chief Medical Officer and the Chief Operating Officer, with the same report to the CEO.
Move two

Signature standard audits are rebuilt as the audit program

Today

Audits run against the signature standards, on their own schedule, separate from any stated quality priority.

Becomes

An audit program designed by the Quality Committee and aligned to enterprise quality priorities — with materially more rigor in what is asked, how it is measured, and what follows.

What that adds

  • Every audit question maps to a standard that has been adopted and is in effect. A question that maps to nothing is retired, or its standard is adopted first.
  • The instrument is validated against clinician review before it can produce anything about an individual.
  • A result is an observation until the clinician has had the chance to answer it — then it is a finding, or it is withdrawn.
  • Every observation gets a recorded disposition and the clinician is told what became of it.
Move three

ACO education becomes part of all clinical education

Today

ACO education has its own monthly slot, held separately from the rest of the clinical education calendar.

Becomes

A component of every clinical education channel rather than a separate entity. ACO measures are one of the inputs the Quality Committee draws on when it sets the agenda, and they are taught inside the specialty didactics.

What that adds

  • Education follows the current priority. If heart failure is the six-month initiative, the sessions track heart failure.
  • One subject at a time, across every channel at once — seminars, the weekly meeting, the newsletter, the one-page version.
  • Education and quality stop being planned by different people on different calendars. Someone from the education function sits with Quality.
Move four

The roundtable splits, and gets a name that fits

Today

The experienced clinician roundtable. Most of what gets asked there is operational — the EMR, the Scribe application, day-to-day questions about how something works or who to take it to.

Clinical questions come up as well, but they are not the bulk of it. The name describes neither half, so nobody is quite sure what the forum is for.

Becomes

The forum continues, renamed for what it actually is — the place to bring a question when you would not otherwise know where to take it. That function is worth keeping and is kept intact.

The clinical questions move. They get a home built for them by the Quality Committee, as one of its early projects.

What that adds

  • The operational half keeps a front door, under a name that tells people it is the right door. Questions about the EMR, the Scribe application and day-to-day process have somewhere to go.
  • A resource for clinical questions, so an answer given once is available afterwards instead of living in one meeting with whoever attended.
  • Two different kinds of question stop competing for the same half hour.
Move five

Peer review becomes a chartered process

Today

Peer review is conducted informally. There is no charter, no defined process, and no clinical authorization framework for a review to act on — so there are also none of the legal protections that attach to a formal peer review body.

Becomes

A chartered Peer Review Committee whose members review the cases under a written procedure, and which takes final action after notice and the opportunity for a hearing. The hearing is held within peer review, before clinicians who took no part in the review.

What that adds

  • Nobody with authority over a clinician's employment sits on the committee or reviews a case.
  • Proportionate responses between doing nothing and ending someone's relationship with the organization — feedback, education, mentoring, focused review, an agreed resolution that closes with no finding.
  • Notice, two discipline-matched reviewers, and an independent hearing where clinical authority may be restricted.
  • Federal and state peer review protection for the organization and for everyone who serves — which is unavailable to an informal process however careful it is.

What does not change

Worth stating plainly, because the question comes up.

Billing and coding stay exactly where they are

Coding is compliance and operations — financial rather than clinical. The auditing and monitoring done there is untouched by any of this, and no committee in this framework has any role in it.

All employment authority stays with management

Hiring, termination, discipline, compensation, contracting, scheduling, assignment and medical director nomination are unaffected, and the charters say so expressly. Management receives notice of every final action and its operational effect.

Facility QAPI participation is unaffected

Clinicians take part in facility quality assurance and performance improvement individually, in the facilities they serve. That is separate from this framework and stays separate from it.

The net count. Five standing activities today, spread across four forums and an informal review process. Afterwards: every one of the five still running — three of them consolidated into the Quality Committee, one chartered as the Peer Review Committee, and one continuing as a renamed forum with its clinical half rehoused.
