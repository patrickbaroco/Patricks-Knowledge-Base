## Recitals

**WHEREAS**, the Corporation provides health care services through licensed physicians, nurse practitioners, physician assistants, psychiatric nurse practitioners, and other licensed clinicians (each, a "Practitioner") in long-term care and assisted living settings in the Commonwealth of Virginia and the States of North Carolina, Ohio, Indiana, and Kentucky; and

**WHEREAS**, review of the clinical performance of individual Practitioners already occurs within the Corporation, and professional review actions affecting the circumstances under which Practitioners furnish care have already been taken, **without a chartered body, defined procedure, due process protection, or the immunity and confidentiality that federal and state law make available to a formal peer review process**; and

**WHEREAS**, the Board has determined that the Corporation should conduct such review through a formal peer review process for the purpose of furthering quality health care, and that doing so protects both the patients the Corporation serves and the Practitioners and clinical leaders who participate in review; and

**WHEREAS**, Section 11151(4)(A)(ii) of Title 42 of the United States Code defines a health care entity to include an entity that provides health care services and follows a formal peer review process for the purpose of furthering quality health care, **so that the existence of the formal process is the condition of qualification and not merely evidence of it**; and

**WHEREAS**, the Board wishes to establish a body composed principally of licensed clinicians in active practice, competent to exercise clinical judgment, and to delegate to that body the authority to conduct professional review activity and to take professional review actions, in order that such determinations are made by clinical peers rather than by management or by this Board; and

**WHEREAS**, the Board has reviewed the proposed Charter of the Clinical Governance Council attached hereto as **Exhibit A** (the "Charter");

**NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED:**

---

## 1. Establishment

**RESOLVED**, that the Clinical Governance Council (the "Council") is hereby established as a committee of the Corporation, and that the Charter attached as Exhibit A is hereby adopted as the Charter of the Council.

**RESOLVED FURTHER**, that the Council is a peer review, medical review, quality assurance, or professional review committee within the meaning of Va. Code § 8.01-581.17; Ohio Rev. Code § 2305.25 et seq.; N.C. Gen. Stat. § 90-21.22A; Ind. Code § 34-30-15; and Ky. Rev. Stat. § 311.377, and is a professional review body within the meaning of 42 U.S.C. § 11151(11).

---

## 2. Delegation of Authority

**RESOLVED**, that the Board hereby delegates to the Council, to the fullest extent permitted by law, the authority to:

- (a) conduct professional review activity concerning any Practitioner;
- (b) take professional review actions affecting the clinical authorization of any Practitioner, including the imposition, modification, and termination of conditions upon the furnishing of care;
- (c) establish and amend the Corporation's clinical authorization framework;
- (d) charter, amend, and dissolve committees reporting to the Council, and approve their charters;
- (e) constitute hearing panels and adopt the procedures governing them;
- (f) determine what must be reported to the National Practitioner Data Bank and to state licensing boards, and cause such reports to be made; and
- (g) adopt such procedures as the Council determines necessary to its function, not inconsistent with the Charter.

**RESOLVED FURTHER**, that the Council shall exercise this authority independently of the management of the Corporation, and that no officer or employee of the Corporation holding authority over the employment, compensation, contracting, scheduling, or assignment of Practitioners shall direct, veto, or participate in the decision of any professional review action.

---

## 3. The Board Is Not in the Case Flow

**RESOLVED**, that the Board does not retain, and shall not exercise, any authority to review, approve, modify, reverse, or hear an appeal from any professional review action taken by the Council or from any determination of a hearing panel constituted under the Charter. **The final action of the Council is final, and there is no appeal to this Board.**

**RESOLVED FURTHER**, that this delegation is made to a body competent to exercise clinical judgment, and that **the Board does not thereby assume, and does not undertake, any duty with respect to the clinical care furnished to any individual patient**, the Board's role being limited to the corporate governance functions reserved in Section 4.

---

## 4. Reserved to the Board

**RESOLVED**, that the following are reserved to the Board:

- (a) adoption of the Charter and any material amendment to it, material amendment meaning any amendment to Articles I, III, IV, V, VI, or IX of the Charter;
- (b) ratification of the election of the Chair of the Council, which may be declined only for cause stated in writing;
- (c) receipt of the annual report described in Section 8; and
- (d) dissolution of the Council.

---

## 5. Composition and Chair

**RESOLVED**, that the composition of the Council shall be as provided in Article III of the Charter, including the requirement that not less than a majority of voting members hold no authority over the employment of Practitioners, and that the Chair be elected by and from members who are licensed clinicians in active clinical practice holding no such authority.

**RESOLVED FURTHER**, that the Corporation shall conduct the election of At-Large Practitioner Members provided for in the Charter and shall make its Practitioners available to serve.

---

## 6. National Practitioner Data Bank

**RESOLVED**, that the officers of the Corporation are authorized and directed to register the Corporation with the National Practitioner Data Bank as a reporting entity, and to take such further action as is necessary for the Corporation to satisfy its reporting and attestation obligations, such reports to be made as the Council determines under the Charter.

---

## 7. Indemnification of Participants

**RESOLVED**, that the Corporation shall indemnify and hold harmless each member of the Council, each member of any committee reporting to it, each member of any hearing panel, each external reviewer, the Executive Coordinator, Clinical Excellence, and each other person assisting any of them, against all claims, liabilities, judgments, settlements, and expenses, including reasonable attorneys' fees, arising out of the good-faith performance of their functions under the Charter, to the fullest extent permitted by law, and shall advance expenses as incurred.

**RESOLVED FURTHER**, that no adverse employment action shall be taken against any person by reason of good-faith participation in, provision of information to, or performance of any function under the Charter.

---

## 8. Reporting to the Board

**RESOLVED**, that the Council shall report to the Board annually in aggregate, stating the volume of matters considered, their categories and dispositions, reports filed, and program-level observations, **and containing no identification of any Practitioner and no case-level detail.**

---

## 9. Counsel

**RESOLVED**, that the Council shall have access to counsel to the Corporation, and that where the Chair of the Council certifies in writing that the interests of the Corporation and the function of the Council may diverge in a matter, the Corporation shall engage separate counsel to the Council in that matter, at the Corporation's expense, on notice to the Chief Executive Officer.

---

## 10. Effective Date and Further Acts

**RESOLVED**, that these resolutions and the Charter take effect on [DATE].

**RESOLVED FURTHER**, that the officers of the Corporation are authorized and directed to take all action necessary or appropriate to carry these resolutions into effect, including amendment of employment agreements, policies, and credentialing documents as required.

**RESOLVED FURTHER**, that all action heretofore taken by any officer or Practitioner of the Corporation in furtherance of the foregoing is hereby ratified and approved.

# Notes for the board packet

**Length is the design.** A private equity board will read three pages. Everything that can live in the Charter lives in the Charter; this document does four things — establishes the body, delegates authority, keeps the Board out of the case flow, and protects participants.

**Section 3 is what makes this easy to say yes to, and it should be said out loud.** The usual board objection to clinical governance is that accepting authority means accepting duty. This resolution delegates authority *and* removes the Board from the case flow, including from any appeal. The Board is better off after adoption than before, because today professional review actions are being taken with no chartered body, no process, and no immunity, and the Board owns that exposure without any of the corresponding protection.

**Section 7 is not boilerplate and should not be traded away.** Participants are understood to be defended under the Corporation's existing umbrella coverage, so this Section asks for indemnification only — a contractual promise by the Corporation to stand behind them, which is a different thing from a policy that pays a defense lawyer. HCQIA immunity is a rebuttable presumption against *damages*. It does not prevent anyone from being sued, does not reach injunctive or declaratory relief, and expressly does not reach civil rights claims. Clinicians do not serve on peer review bodies without knowing the company stands behind them. If the program cannot recruit members, nothing else in the architecture matters.

**Section 9 is the provision most likely to be struck**, and it is survivable if it is. It is included because the Corporation's counsel reports to management, and the Council's function is to be independent of management. If the Board will not fund separate counsel on the Chair's certification, a fallback is that the Chair may raise a conflict directly with the Board.

**Substantive additions beyond the project brief:**

1. **Section 7, indemnification.** Not previously discussed anywhere in the project. It is the single most practical determinant of whether people will serve. Insurance is not requested — participants are covered and defended under the Corporation's existing umbrella policy.
2. **Section 3, no appeal to the Board.** Serves both purposes at once — it keeps the Board out of the clinical chain, and it makes the Council's action genuinely final, which matters for reporting timing and for HCQIA.
3. **Section 9, counsel.** New.

**For counsel:**

- **Whether the disclaimer of clinical duty in Section 3 does anything.** A board may not be able to disclaim a duty it would otherwise owe simply by resolving that it does not. The delegation to a competent body is the substantive protection; the recitation may be surplusage. Confirm before the Board is told it is protected by that sentence.
- Whether the Board will adopt in these terms at all, and whether General Atlantic's governance practice permits a delegation of this kind.
- Whether Section 2(b) is sufficient to constitute the Council a professional review body under 42 U.S.C. § 11151(11) given the Corporation's structure.
- Whether Section 6 creates any obligation before the clinical authorization framework exists.
- Whether the indemnification in Section 7 is enforceable as to the Executive Coordinator and other non-clinician staff.
- Confirmation, not a request: that the Corporation's existing umbrella coverage in fact reaches service on a peer review body and the defense of claims arising from it. This is understood to be the case; it is the kind of understanding worth having in writing before anyone is recruited to serve.
- Whether "no appeal to this Board" is consistent with the Corporation's bylaws and with each state's requirements for a fair hearing process.
- Whether ratification in Section 10 of prior action reaches the professional review actions already taken without a chartered process, and whether ratifying them is desirable or whether the effective-date boundary in the Charter is the better treatment. **These two provisions may conflict — resolve before adoption.**
