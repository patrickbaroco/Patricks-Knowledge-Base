Clinical governance framework · draft for discussion

How a review unfolds

Two questions get asked about this program, usually in the same breath. What happens to me if my chart gets picked? And why would this move a number that hasn't moved in nine months?

They have the same answer. The steps that make this safe to be on the receiving end of — define the standard, teach it, give notice, report back with an offer of help, look again, and check with the clinician before anything counts — are the same steps that close a quality loop. Nothing today does either.

Not adopted. Charters drafted, under revision, prepared for counsel review.

Four things true of every matter

Before the detail, the shape of it.

The clinician hears about it first

An audit result is an observation, not a finding. Every clinician whose charts were read gets a report, and on most standards that is the end. An observation becomes a finding only where a referral could follow, and only after the clinician has had a formal chance to answer.

Most matters end early

Sixteen places a matter can end before it reaches peer review at all. None of them restricts what the clinician is authorized to do, and none is reportable anywhere.

Almost nothing is reportable

One outcome in the whole framework reaches the National Practitioner Data Bank: a restriction on what a clinician is authorized to do, lasting more than 30 days, taken as a final action by the Peer Review Committee after notice and the opportunity for a hearing.

The clinician's job is not in the room

Nobody with authority over the clinician's employment sits on the Peer Review Committee or reviews the case, and no committee may see the clinician's productivity, revenue, or cost.

The path, end to end

Every stage, and every place a matter can leave it. The line on the left is the reportability of what has happened so far — it stays neutral for the whole length of the process and changes once, at the very end.

Before anything is measured

Clinical Quality Committee

nothing here is reportable ↓

A standard is adopted CQC § 4.4

Each standard is designated at adoption as a minimum expectation or an improvement target. The vote is express and there is no default.

Where guidance from a professional body exists it is the preferred basis, and any adaptation made for this population or setting is identified in the standard itself.

Ends here

It's an improvement targetThen no departure from it can ever support a finding about an individual, or a referral. It measures the organization.

It is taught before it is measured CQC §§ 3.1(b), 4.3

The education that supports a standard is built with it — what the standard asks for, and what a conforming note actually looks like. Both live in the open record, not in committee files.

The clinician gets notice, and time CQC § 5.7

Only care furnished 60 days or more after the standard was disseminated can support a finding that counts toward a referral. The clock runs against the care, not against the audit.

Longer where the standard asks for a change in practice rather than a change in documentation. The period is chosen for each standard and recorded at adoption.

Ends here

The care predates the notice periodWithdrawn under § 5.5(a)(1)(v). A standard disseminated on 1 January does not reach care furnished on 10 January.

Help is there without an audit CQC § 7.7

A clinician can ask the Quality Committee for help in a clinical area at any time, without having been audited and without giving a reason. The education function decides how to answer it.

A request opens nothing. It selects no charts, starts no follow-up and counts toward nothing, and it is never recorded against the clinician who asked. It also shields nothing: the audit and every route to peer review run as they would have.

The audit, and the report

Clinical Quality Committee

The audit runs CQC §§ 5.3, 5.4, 5.6, 4.7(f)

Every audit question maps to a standard in effect. The instrument is validated against clinician review before it can produce anything about an individual.

Being selected is not a finding. A rate identifies charts to look at. It is never itself a conclusion about anyone, and the charter says so in terms.

Where the physician's periodic note is in the chart (the regulatory visit in skilled nursing, the periodic visit in assisted living, or a supervising psychiatrist's visit or review), it is read too, as the physician's own entry, and the physician gets a report of their own. Nothing about the APC's results passes to the physician this way.

What comes out of a chart that misses the standard is an observation.

The Committee sees the cycle whole CQC §§ 5.4.1(e), 5.5(b)(1)

Before any report goes out, the Committee receives a summary of the cycle. The Chair asks aloud whether any member thinks what they see departs from the standard of care: in the charts carrying observations, and in any chart the reviewer flagged because it met the standard while the care it records raised a concern. The minutes record that the question was put and answered. It is asked every time, so that raising a concern is an ordinary item of business rather than an act of individual initiative.

The Committee may record an observation as a systems condition. Otherwise it decides nothing about anyone.

Ends here

SystemsAttributable to process, staffing, tooling, workflow, or the standard itself. It goes to whoever can fix it and individual attribution is not carried forward.
Widespread across the groupWhere more than a quarter of the clinicians reviewed, and more than three, miss a standard, nobody gets an individual follow-up. The standard, the audit question or the teaching is wrong, and it routes to revision or education. § 5.9

Every clinician gets a report CQC §§ 5.4.1, 5.5(c)(5)–(e)

Everyone whose charts were read receives a review report, including those whose charts all met the standard. For each chart: the resident and date, whether it met the standard, and for each observation what the standard required, what was found, and whether the apparent problem is in the decision, in the documentation, or in both.

It carries any commendation, and may carry a clinical comment: a colleague's advice on management the standard does not reach, such as an indication that may not warrant the drug or a dose reduction given up early. A physician writes it where it concerns a physician's note. It counts toward nothing.

It carries an offer of help, sent to everyone who receives a report, so its presence says nothing about the clinician. Taking it up is by request only, and whether the clinician does is never recorded, counted, transmitted, or tracked in an LMS. An APC may share the report with their supervising physician, or ask for help without sharing it, using a separate page that names only the clinical area. The APC decides, and the committee never knows which was chosen.

The report invites the clinician to say where the review got something wrong, with no deadline. Nothing in it is a finding, and the Committee decides nothing about the clinician.

Ends here

Every chart met the standardThe report says so, and nothing follows.
Most standardsOn a standard that carries no referral criterion, the report is the whole of the individual process. The totals go to the teaching, the tools and the standard.
CorrectedAnything the clinician corrects comes off the report. Correct every item before a follow-up look, and the look does not run.

One look back, where a repeat could be referred CQC § 5.11

On a standard where a gap repeated after a review can be referred, the Committee samples the clinician's charts against the same standard, once, for care in a window opening 45 days after the report and running 90 days. The first 45 days are the clinician's time to use the offer and make the change.

The report tells the clinician it will happen, on which standard, and roughly when.

Ends here

Clear on the second lookThe matter closes. This is also the step that lets the program say whether anything it did actually worked.
Nothing to examineThe clinician had no resident the standard applies to in the window, and the follow-up closes.

The formal check, only where a referral could follow CQC §§ 5.4.2, 5.5(a)

If the same gap appears at the look back, the clinician gets a formal notice and ten business days to show where the review is wrong, covering the first report's items as well. The same check runs at once, with the report, on the rare standard where a single gap can be referred, and where the clinician's figures on a measure stand well above those of comparable clinicians.

The notice lists every ground that will withdraw an observation, in the order they arise in practice, and every ground that will not. The Committee is barred from inviting a response on grounds it will not accept, so the window isn't spent assembling material that cannot help.

Withdrawn on any of these

AttributionNot the clinician's patient, not the clinician's panel, the clinician was covering, or another clinician managed the condition under a documented arrangement.
PopulationThe resident isn't in the group the standard applies to — diagnosis absent, miscoded, or superseded. They come out of the numerator and the denominator.
It didn't happenThe medication was ordered, the assessment done, the communication made — and the MAR, order, or transmitted document shows it.
RetrievalThe clinician's entry exists and the search didn't reach it: a different encounter, a scanned document, a part of the note the instrument doesn't parse. Available whatever the standard requires, because what it produces is exactly what was asked for.
TimingThe care predates the date the standard could count, or the record was examined before the entry was due.

Disposition and close-out CQC § 5.5(b), (c)

Each checked observation gets one of three dispositions, systems, individual, or no action, after the Chair has put the standard-of-care question again. Where nothing from the first report survives, the new items are treated as a first report of their own.

The clinician is told what became of every observation and which grounds were sustained. A close-out imposes nothing. It states no obligation, requires no response, sets no date, names no monitor, and creates no record of remediation. It repeats the offer of help.

Ends here

Withdrawn or systemsEvery observation withdrawn, or put down to process: the clinician is told so, and nothing further follows.
Most findingsA validated finding that meets no referral criterion closes here.

If it goes further

Peer Review Committee

Referral CQC §§ 6.1–6.5

An individual finding is referred only where it meets a criterion in the Audit Referral Policy: the same gap repeated after a review, a single gap on one of the few standards designated for it, or a pattern well above comparable clinicians. The Policy is a written schedule adopted by the Quality Committee, in effect only with the Peer Review Committee's concurrence, and reviewed annually.

What travels: the finding, the underlying data, what the clinician submitted and which grounds were sustained, and the disposition history. Not the Committee's deliberations. The Quality Committee's involvement then ends completely — it gets no report of the outcome and imposes nothing at any point.

The audit is one route in and not the principal one. A patient-safety or standard-of-care concern can be referred directly by anyone, with no threshold and no written standard required.

Screening PRC § 4.4

The Chair and the department chair for the clinician's service line, primary care or psychiatry, screen every referral within ten business days. Screening decides the route, not the merits. Nothing about the clinician's care is determined here. The department chair screens for the Peer Review Committee without a vote, so every member comes to a review without having seen the referral; where the department chair took part in the Quality Committee's decision on an audit referral, a Peer Review Committee member of the clinician's discipline screens instead.

Ends or leaves here

No actionOutside scope, or not supported by information capable of review.
SystemsBack to the Quality Committee, attribution stripped.
AdministrativeA performance or conduct matter, handled on its own track.
Health, impairment or fitnessReferred to the practitioner health pathway the CEO designates, which handles it under its own policies. Peer review does not investigate it, adjudicate it, or receive any information about the clinician's health, evaluation, treatment or accommodation.

A review is opened PRC §§ 5.1, 6.3, 2.3

The notice states the whole range of outcomes, not just the serious end — including that a review may close with no adverse finding at all.

Two members of the Committee, matched to the clinician's discipline, are assigned to review it. They investigate and propose; the Committee decides. No one holding authority over the clinician's employment sits on the Committee or reviews the case — supervisors may refer a matter and be interviewed, but they do not review, deliberate, or vote.

What the review can conclude PRC §§ 5.7, 8.2, 8.5

Four of the five available outcomes end the matter without restricting anything the clinician is authorized to do, and none of the four is reportable.

Ends here — none reportable

Closed, no findingThe care met standard. The clinician is notified in writing that the review closed without adverse finding.
Finding, no interventionA finding is recorded and the matter closes. The clinician is notified in terms distinguishable from the notice above.
Agreed resolutionFor a bounded gap in a defined clinical area: a plan the clinician agrees to, no finding, nothing reportable. The clinician may decline without giving a reason, and the review then proceeds with every right unaffected. Completion is confirmed as a fact — what the clinician learned is never graded. The matter closes when the plan is completed. The care that follows is then looked at twice, once in each of the next two quarters, and a problem found there is a new matter. The clinician is told this before deciding.
Tier OneWritten feedback, education, mentoring, or retrospective focused review. None restricts the clinician's authority to practise and none is reportable. Each states its duration, its exit criteria, and who monitors it in writing on the day it is imposed — and may be extended once, never rolled forward indefinitely.

The far end

Peer Review Committee

A proposed action PRC §§ 8.4, 10.1, 2.8

Only where the Committee concludes a restriction on what the clinician is authorized to do is warranted does it propose one — mandatory consultation, proctoring, a scope restriction, non-renewal, or termination of clinical authorization.

A proposal needs a majority of all members not recused, and is not taken with fewer than four. Nothing proposed becomes final until the clinician has had notice and the opportunity for a hearing.

Notice, and a hearing PRC Art. X

The notice states the reasons, the action proposed and how long it would last, and comes with the material the Committee relied on. The clinician has 30 days to ask for a hearing. Every notice also offers an accelerated calendar, about 50 days instead of about 120, which applies only if the clinician accepts it in writing, and declining it carries no inference.

The hearing is held before a panel of at least three clinicians who are not members of the Peer Review Committee and took no part in the review, the audit, or the decision to propose, none holding employment authority, matched to the clinician's discipline. The Committee's representative carries the burden of proof. The panel recommends, and the Committee accepts its recommendation unless it is unsupported by the hearing record, the hearing had a material procedural defect, or it rests on an error about the Committee's authority or the standard.

Ends here

Not taken, or modifiedThe panel may recommend that the proposed action not be taken, or be modified, and the Committee is bound by that recommendation except on those three grounds, stated in writing.

Final action, and the one reportable outcome PRC §§ 10.7, 15.6

The Committee takes final action, and the Chair confirms in writing that the process was followed and signs the decision. The decision is final. No officer of the company, including the CEO, can review it.

A restriction on clinical authorization lasting more than 30 days, taken as a final action by the Peer Review Committee, is reportable to the National Practitioner Data Bank and to state licensing boards.

This is the whole of it. Everything above this line — every close-out, every agreed resolution, every Tier One intervention, every review that closed with or without a finding — is not reportable and does not appear anywhere outside the process.

The obligation

NPDB and state licensing boardsDefining clinical authorization is what creates this duty. The organization does not have it today — and does not have the legal protections that arise from the same status either.

Why this moves a number

The same machinery, read from the organization's side rather than the clinician's.

What the CGSC has, and what it doesn't. It has an agenda for each meeting. What it does not have is a measure defined precisely enough to be audited, education built to teach that measure, an audit that tests it, or a date on which someone looks again and reports whether it moved. Antipsychotic and anxiolytic reduction have been on its agenda all year, and the September number is the same as the January number — unchanged, not merely unimproved.

That is the case for this framework, and it is stronger than any argument from structure. Every measure this program adopts carries the date its measurement period begins and the date the Committee looks again, both recorded at adoption. A program that intervenes and never measures again has no basis for claiming it improves care — which is exactly what the Charter requires this one to report on, quarterly, to the Chief Medical Officer and the Chief Operating Officer, with the same report to the CEO.

What it cannot do to a clinician

Written into both charters, not left to practice.

It cannot touch the clinician's job

Neither committee has authority over employment, compensation, contracting, scheduling, facility assignment, or medical director designation — or may direct management as to any of them. Employment consequences run on a separate track, on a separate record.

It cannot see the clinician's economics

No body in this framework may receive or consider a clinician's productivity, revenue, or cost in connection with any review. This is the provision that answers a later claim that a clinical decision was financially motivated.

A supervisor cannot judge the clinician here

Nobody holding employment authority serves on the Peer Review Committee, reviews the case, or sits on a hearing panel. The Chair of both committees presides at the Peer Review Committee without a vote.

The Quality Committee cannot discipline a clinician

It is not a professional review body. It makes no finding about competence, does not decide whether care met the standard of care, and imposes no consequence of any kind on any individual, at any point. It refers and stops.

An audit finding is not a verdict

Failing an internal standard does not establish substandard care. A referral establishes only that a finding was made and a criterion met. Peer review starts fresh, on its own record, and is not bound by the Quality Committee's view.

The clinician cannot be retaliated against

No adverse action may be taken against anyone because they took part in, gave information in good faith to, or became the subject of a review.
