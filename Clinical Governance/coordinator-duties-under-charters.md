# Executive Coordinator, Clinical Excellence

## Duties Under the Clinical Governance Charters

*Drafted 2026-08-26. Companion to `executive-coordinator-resourcing-case.md` and `Executive_Coordinator_Clinical_Excellence_role_spec.docx`.*
*Every duty below is assigned by charter, with the citation given. This is the 30% new scope made concrete.*

---

## Why the position exists

The legal protection this program depends on is conditional. Federal immunity is available only where the process was in fact followed, and state privilege turns on records that were segregated correctly at the moment each document was created. Ohio requires document-by-document proof of what was covered; generalities lose the privilege. Kentucky distinguishes retrospective committee work product from documentation created to satisfy an independent obligation, and that line is drawn correctly or incorrectly when the file is made, not later.

The program does not fail on legal theory. It fails on minutes, deadlines, and misfiled documents.

The charters therefore assign one position custody of every record, every clock, and every register across three bodies. That position is non-voting, exercises no clinical judgment, and makes no decision on any matter. It is also the only role in the structure whose failure disables the protection for everyone else.

## Clinical Quality Committee

Monthly meetings. The work is standards administration and audit administration.

**Standards register.** Maintain the register of every adopted standard: adoption date, effective date, the designation as a minimum expectation or an improvement target, the disciplines it applies to, the review date, and retirement where applicable. A standard without a recorded designation cannot support a referral (CQC § 4.4), so the register is not a list — it is the control that determines what can and cannot be acted on.

**Dissemination and notice.** Distribute every adopted standard to the clinicians it applies to and document the distribution (CQC § 4.5). Track the notice period that must run before findings against a new standard count toward any referral criterion (CQC § 5.7). Without a documented distribution record, no *audit* referral against that standard is available — the record has no bearing on a concern raised directly to the Peer Review Committee.

**Audit register.** Maintain the map of every live audit question to the adopted standard it measures against (CQC § 5.3). A question with no mapped standard is not live. Maintain the record of instrument validation against clinician review (CQC § 5.4), and the selection thresholds by which Practitioners and charts are chosen for examination (CQC § 5.6(e)). Selection thresholds are not referral criteria and are recorded separately from the referral schedule.

**Disposition log.** Record a disposition for every audit observation — systems, individual, or no action (CQC § 5.5(b)). This is the highest-volume duty in the role and the one with the most direct consequence: an undispositioned observation is an unanswered question in a plaintiff's exhibit.

**The correction window.** Before any observation is dispositioned as Individual, issue it to the Practitioner and track the [ten] business days allowed for identifying factual error, attribution error, or information already in the record (CQC § 5.5(a)). Record the date on which the record was examined — that state of the record governs and the date has to be captured at the time, not reconstructed later. Record what the Committee did with the response: corrected, withdrawn, or unchanged. **No observation becomes an individual finding without this step having been completed and recorded**, which makes it a gate rather than a courtesy.

Also record, where the Practitioner reports one, that the medical record was amended after notification and when. The amendment does not change the observation, and the disclosure protects the Practitioner — an amendment surfaced later without explanation reads very differently from one disclosed at the time.

**Referral criteria and the count.** Maintain the referral schedule adopted by the committee and ratified by the Council (CQC § 6.2), and hold the running count against thresholds. Threshold tracking is administrative and is expressly not the chair's judgment. The schedule governs referrals generated by the audit only; it has no bearing on concerns raised directly to the Peer Review Committee, which carry no threshold (CQC § 6.7).

**Referral packets.** Assemble what accompanies a referral to the Peer Review Committee: the individual finding, underlying data, anything the practitioner submitted during the correction window together with what the committee did with it, and the disposition history — and nothing from the committee's deliberations (CQC § 6.3).

**Records.** Custody of two separate records: the open record of standards, education, and any draft circulated outside the Committee, which is not confidential; and the protected record of observations, individual findings, dispositions, deliberations, and the internal drafting record (CQC § 8.1). Documents do not move between them, and a draft becomes open at the moment it is circulated — which makes circulation a records decision, not an administrative one.

## Peer Review Committee

Monthly meetings, with screening running continuously between them. The work is case administration under deadline.

**Referrals arrive from every direction, and only some come from the audit.** A colleague, a facility, a manager, an adverse event, an external event, or the clinician themselves — each is logged, screened, and dispositioned on the same terms as an audit referral (PRC § 4.1). Referrals arising outside the audit are not measured against any written standard and are not subject to the audit's mapping, notice-lag, or threshold rules. The register does not distinguish them except by source.

**Case register.** Maintain a register of every matter: identifier, source, the date of each step, disposition, and the location of the file (PRC § 13.2). The register is itself a committee record.

**Intake and screening.** Log every referral on arrival and schedule screening within 10 business days (PRC § 4.4). Screening does not wait for a meeting. Record a disposition for every referral, and notify the referrer that the referral was received and screened — without disclosing the outcome (PRC § 4.5).

**Notice.** Issue written notice to the clinician within 5 business days of a review being opened, containing every element the charter requires (PRC § 5.1). Track the 20-day response window and any extension granted (PRC § 5.2).

**Panel logistics.** Support constitution of review panels from the standing membership, drawing on the Reviewer Pool only where the membership cannot supply a discipline match, confirming that discipline-matching requirements are met and that no panelist is subject to a recusal trigger (PRC §§ 6.1–6.3). Where the pool cannot supply a matched panel, arrange an external reviewer under a written confidentiality undertaking (PRC § 6.6).

**Recusal documentation.** Record every recusal, the departure from the room, and the return (PRC § 12.2). This is a small task with a disproportionate consequence — an undocumented recusal is an argument that it did not happen.

**Recommendation packets.** Assemble what goes to the Council: findings, evidence considered, notice given, the clinician's response, and the recommendation. Not the deliberations (PRC § 10.1).

**Remand tracking.** Track matters returned by the Council and the step each remand requires, retaining both records (PRC § 10.2).

**Aggregate reporting.** Prepare the quarterly de-identified aggregate report to the Clinical Quality Committee (PRC § 11.2).

## Clinical Governance Council

Quarterly for governance, convened on cases as required.

**Minutes to standard.** Minutes stating each matter and its identifier, the documents reviewed identified individually, the members present, the members recused with times of departure and return, the action taken, and the vote (CGC § 7.6). Deliberative content summarized, never transcribed. This specificity is set by Ohio case law, not by preference.

**Convening obligations.** Schedule the Council within 15 calendar days of an adverse recommendation, and within 5 business days on 24 hours' notice for a precautionary ratification (CGC §§ 7.1.1–7.1.2).

**Composition check.** Report the Council's composition against the clinician floor, the majority-without-employment-authority floor, and the size cap, at the first regular meeting of each year (CGC § 3.10).

**At-large elections.** Maintain the eligible roll, give notice, run the ballot, and record the result (CGC § 3.4).

**Reporting to the Data Bank.** Maintain the reporting calendar, track the 30-day clock on each action, and prepare the biennial attestation that all reportable actions were submitted (CGC § 11.4). The determination of whether a matter is reportable is the Council's with counsel; the tracking and filing are not.

**Annual report to the Board.** Assemble the aggregate report — volume, categories, dispositions, reports filed — containing no clinician identity and no case-level detail (CGC § 5.4).

**Referral-source report.** Prepare the annual report of where referrals originated.

## The clocks

Every deadline in the program runs through this position. This table is the clearest statement of why the work cannot be distributed across whoever has time.

| Clock | Runs from | Duration | Charter |
|---|---|---|---|
| Screening | Referral received | 10 business days | PRC § 4.4 |
| Notice to clinician | Review opened | 5 business days | PRC § 5.1 |
| Clinician response | Notice issued | 20 calendar days | PRC § 5.2 |
| Panel findings | Review opened | 60 calendar days | PRC § 14.4 |
| Committee recommendation | Panel findings | 15 calendar days | PRC § 14.4 |
| Matter reported to Council as open | Review opened | 90 calendar days | PRC § 14.5 |
| Council convenes on a recommendation | Recommendation received | 15 calendar days | CGC § 7.1.1 |
| Precautionary — notice issued | Imposition | 24 hours | PRC § 9.3 |
| Precautionary — reportability warning | Imposition | Day 20 | PRC § 9.4 |
| Precautionary — automatic lapse | Imposition | 5 business days | PRC § 9.2 |
| Precautionary — hard stop | Imposition | 45 days | PRC § 9.5 |
| Data Bank initial action report | Action taken | 30 days | CGC § 11.2 |
| Tier One intervention review | Imposition | 2 extensions or 6 months | PRC § 8.3 |
| Standards review | Adoption | 3 years | CQC § 4.6 |
| Data Bank attestation | — | Every 2 years | CGC § 11.3 |
| Composition check, charter reviews, referral-source report | — | Annual | Multiple |

The precautionary sequence is the one that cannot be missed. A restriction imposed on a Monday requires notice by Tuesday, a Council meeting inside five business days or it lapses on its own, a reportability warning at day 20, and a hard stop at day 45. Four clocks, one matter, all running from the same date.

## Records custody

Five separate records, maintained separately, with documents never moved between them:

1. The professional review record — Peer Review Committee, Council, and hearing panels
2. The Clinical Quality Committee's open record — standards and education, which circulates and is not confidential
3. The Clinical Quality Committee's protected record — observations, individual findings, dispositions, deliberations, and the internal drafting record
4. Employment and personnel records — held elsewhere, and never commingled
5. Any record later designated patient safety work product

Also within custody: retention to schedule, legal hold on any matter where litigation is filed or reasonably foreseeable, and the rule that documentation the company or a facility is independently required to create is not a committee record and does not become one by being filed with committee material.

## Protections and constraints

The charters treat this position differently from ordinary employment, in both directions.

**Duty runs to the committees, not to management.** No matter of the Council, either committee, or any hearing panel may be disclosed to anyone in the reporting line, including the position's own supervisor. Ordinary employment reporting obligations do not reach this material. An instruction from management that conflicts is referred to the chair and recorded (CGC § 3.7.1).

**Case content is barred from evaluation.** The content, subject, or outcome of any matter is not an input to any performance evaluation, compensation decision, or employment decision concerning this position. Performance of the charter functions is assessed by the chair (CGC § 3.7.2).

**Protection from adverse action.** No adverse employment action may be taken by reason of performing these functions or refusing to disclose protected material. Any such action is reviewable by the Council and reportable to the Board (CGC § 3.7.3).

**Immunity and confidentiality.** The position is staff of a professional review body and a person assisting it for purposes of 42 U.S.C. § 11111(a)(1). A written confidentiality undertaking is executed before receiving any protected record, and it survives termination of employment (CGC § 3.7.4).

**Screening for the operational role.** The position holds operational duties elsewhere that create regular working contact with clinicians who may become subjects of review. This is addressed rather than left undefined: no discussion of any matter with its subject or with anyone in the operational reporting line; operational duties performed without reference to any matter; and where the chair determines the working relationship is close enough that custody could reasonably be questioned, an alternate custodian handles that file. The determination is recorded, applies to that matter only, and is not a finding about the coordinator (PRC § 2.9).

**Alternate.** The Council designates an alternate to perform these functions during unavailability or a screening determination. The alternate is subject to the same provisions in full (CGC § 3.7.5).

## What the role is not

- Not a scribe. Minutes are drafted to a legal standard, not transcribed.
- Not a decision-maker. No vote, no disposition of any matter on the merits, no clinical judgment.
- Not a screener. Screening requires three committee members; the position schedules and records it.
- Not the determiner of reportability. That is the Council's with counsel; the position tracks the clock and files the report.

## Open items

**Volume is unknown.** The program is new and audit flag volume has not been measured. The case-administration load scales with referrals and cannot be estimated until a baseline exists. The registers, clocks, minutes, and elections do not scale with volume and exist from day one.

**The 70/30 split will not hold during the build.** Charter drafting, the standards register, the audit map, the first at-large election, and Data Bank registration are front-loaded and land in the same twelve to eighteen months. Either the split is stated as temporary, or the build gets its own allocation.

**Training required before the role begins.** Peer review confidentiality and privilege — not a credential, a program requirement, and the easiest line item in this document to justify. Records segregation and minute-taking to the Ohio standard. Data Bank registration and reporting mechanics.

**Unresolved for counsel.** Whether state peer review privilege in each of the five states reaches a non-clinician employee serving as records custodian. Federal law covers staff and persons assisting; the state statutes were written around clinicians and this has not been analyzed. The records architecture described above rests on the answer.
