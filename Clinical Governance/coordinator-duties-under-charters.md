## Why the position exists

The legal protection this program depends on is conditional. Federal immunity is available only where the process was in fact followed, and state privilege turns on records that were segregated correctly at the moment each document was created. Ohio requires document-by-document proof of what was covered; generalities lose the privilege. Kentucky distinguishes retrospective committee work product from documentation created to satisfy an independent obligation, and that line is drawn correctly or incorrectly when the file is made, not later.

The program does not fail on legal theory. It fails on minutes, deadlines, and misfiled documents.

The charters therefore assign one position custody of every record, every clock, and every register across both committees and every hearing panel (PRC §§ 2.7, 13.1; CQC § 2.7). That position is non-voting, exercises no clinical judgment, and makes no decision on any matter. It is also the only role in the structure whose failure disables the protection for everyone else.

## Clinical Quality Committee

Monthly meetings. The work is standards administration and audit administration.

**Standards register.** Maintain the register of every adopted standard: adoption date, effective date, the designation as a minimum expectation or an improvement target, the disciplines it applies to, the review date, and retirement where applicable. A standard without a recorded designation cannot support a referral (CQC § 4.4), so the register is not a list — it is the control that determines what can and cannot be acted on. The register also records the definition of each comparison group with the standard it attaches to. It is open record (CQC §§ 2.7, 5.6(c), 8.1.1).

**Dissemination and notice.** Distribute every adopted standard to the clinicians it applies to and document the distribution (CQC § 4.5). Track the notice period that must run before findings against a new standard count toward any referral criterion (CQC § 5.7). Without a documented distribution record, no *audit* referral against that standard is available — the record has no bearing on a concern raised directly to the Peer Review Committee.

**Audit register.** Maintain the map of every live audit question to the adopted standard it measures against (CQC § 5.3). A question with no mapped standard is not live. Maintain the record of instrument validation against clinician review (CQC § 5.4), and the selection thresholds by which Practitioners and charts are chosen for examination (CQC § 5.6(e)). Selection thresholds are not referral criteria and are recorded separately from the referral schedule. The register also holds the follow-up sample sizes (CQC § 5.11.2), the selection thresholds used to find charts for commendation (CQC § 5.5(d)(2)), and the constitution of each comparison group in each cycle: which Practitioners composed it and what their rates were (CQC § 5.6(c)). The audit register is protected record (CQC § 8.1.1).

**Disposition log.** Record a disposition for every audit observation — systems, individual, or no action (CQC § 5.5(b)). Record in the minutes that the Chair put the safety question before observations against a clinical standard were dispositioned, and that it was answered (CQC § 5.5(b)(1)). This is the highest-volume duty in the role and the one with the most direct consequence: an undispositioned observation is an unanswered question in a plaintiff's exhibit.

**The correction window.** Before any observation is dispositioned as Individual, issue it to the Practitioner with the notice the charter prescribes and track the [ten] business days allowed for a response on the grounds stated in the notice (CQC § 5.5(a), (a)(1), (a)(5)). Record the date on which the record was examined — that state of the record governs and the date has to be captured at the time, not reconstructed later. Record what the Committee did with the response: corrected, withdrawn, or unchanged. **No observation becomes an individual finding without this step having been completed and recorded**, which makes it a gate rather than a courtesy.

Also record, where the Practitioner reports one, that the medical record was amended after notification and when. The amendment does not change the observation, and the disclosure protects the Practitioner — an amendment surfaced later without explanation reads very differently from one disclosed at the time (CQC § 5.5(a)(6)).

Record each ground sustained against the measure, for report to the analytics function (CQC § 5.5(a)(8)). Where an observation is withdrawn on attribution or population grounds, the rate is recomputed before it is used for selection or for a pattern criterion (CQC § 5.5(a)(7)).

**Close-out notices.** Every Practitioner notified of an observation is told what became of each one: its disposition, which grounds were sustained, and where the standard and its examples of conforming documentation are found in the open record (CQC § 5.5(c)(1)). The close-out issues whether or not a referral criterion is met (CQC § 5.5(c)(4)). It carries the offer of support in identical terms to every recipient, and it discloses the follow-up look: that one will occur, on which standard, and approximately when (CQC §§ 5.5(c)(5), 5.11.4). **Whether support is taken up is not recorded against the Practitioner, tracked to completion, or entered in any system that reports individual completion to Management** (CQC § 5.5(c)(5)(iii), (v)). Any record of delivery belongs to the open education record (CQC § 5.5(c)(5)(vi)).

**Follow-up looks.** Diary the follow-up on every individual finding closed out without referral and bring it to the Committee when it falls due (CQC § 5.11.2). The window opens [forty-five] days after the close-out and runs [ninety] days, unless a longer period was set with the standard. The look runs once and is not repeated or extended (CQC § 5.11.3). The trigger is the diary, because it cannot depend on anyone remembering a finding across a change of audit topic.

**Commendations.** Record each commendation in the protected record and see that the Practitioner is told of it in writing, stating the chart, the date of service, and what the chart showed (CQC § 5.5(d)(3), (d)(5)). A commendation enters no employment or performance record and is not transmitted with a referral (CQC § 5.5(d)(4), (d)(5)). Where a commended chart becomes an example of conforming documentation, the clean version entered in the open record is de-identified as to the resident, and as to the Practitioner unless the Practitioner's written consent to be named is on file (CQC § 5.5(d)(6)).

**Minutes.** Minutes state the matters considered, the documents reviewed identified individually, the members present, any withdrawal of a member holding Employment Authority and its time, and the action taken. Deliberative content is summarized in substance and not transcribed (CQC § 9.4). Determinations about a named clinician are not taken with fewer than [three] eligible members present, and the minutes show that the count was met (CQC § 9.3).

**Systems transmittals.** Record each transmittal of a systems condition to the person or function able to act on it, with its date, and whether it was acted on (CQC § 7.5). A transmittal identifies no Practitioner and is open record.

**Referral criteria and the count.** Maintain the referral schedule, the Audit Referral Policy, adopted by the committee and in effect on the Peer Review Committee's concurrence (CQC § 6.2; PRC § 11.5), and hold the running count against thresholds. A copy of the Policy as in effect is held in the PRC's governance record (PRC § 13.1(a)). Threshold tracking is administrative and is expressly not the chair's judgment. The schedule governs referrals generated by the audit only; it has no bearing on concerns raised directly to the Peer Review Committee, which carry no threshold (CQC § 6.7).

**Referral packets.** Assemble what accompanies a referral to the Peer Review Committee: the individual finding, underlying data, anything the practitioner submitted during the correction window together with what the committee did with it, and the disposition history — and nothing from the committee's deliberations (CQC § 6.3). Where the referral follows a second finding on the same standard, the packet also carries the close-out notice, the disclosure of the follow-up look, and the fact and date of the follow-up conducted. Nothing concerning support offered after a close-out is included, including whether it was offered, taken up, or declined (CQC § 6.3; PRC § 11.3).

**Records.** Custody of two separate records: the open record of standards, education, and any draft circulated outside the Committee, which is not confidential; and the protected record of observations, individual findings, dispositions, deliberations, and the internal drafting record (CQC § 8.1). The charter classifies the instruments expressly (CQC § 8.1.1). Open: the standards register, the Audit Referral Policy, comparison-group definitions, and recognitions of facilities and teams with any practice descriptions (CQC § 7.6). Protected: the audit register, the constitution of each comparison group in a cycle, and commendations. Documents do not move between them, and a draft becomes open at the moment it is circulated — which makes circulation a records decision, not an administrative one.

**Quarterly report and audit plan.** Assemble the material for the Chair's quarterly report of the Committee's work at the meeting with the Chief Medical Officer and the Chief Operating Officer: standards adopted, education delivered, audit cycles run, dispositions by category, follow-up looks and their outcomes in aggregate, systems conditions transmitted and whether they were acted on, and each occasion on which referral on a measure was withheld under § 5.9 (CQC § 7.4). No Practitioner is identifiable in it. When adopted, the annual audit plan is presented at the same meeting (CQC § 3.1(f)).

## Peer Review Committee

Monthly meetings, with screening running continuously between them. The work is case administration under deadline.

**Referrals arrive from every direction, and only some come from the audit.** A colleague, a facility, a manager, an adverse event, an external event, or the clinician themselves — each is logged, screened, and dispositioned on the same terms as an audit referral (PRC § 4.1). Referrals arising outside the audit are not measured against any written standard and are not subject to the audit's mapping, notice-lag, or threshold rules. The register does not distinguish them except by source.

**Case register.** Maintain a register of every matter: identifier, source, the date of each step, disposition, and the location of the file (PRC § 13.2). The register is itself a committee record. Record in it any condition on a Practitioner's authority that was in effect on the charter's effective date and continues under its own terms (PRC § 16.4).

**Intake and screening.** Log every referral on arrival and schedule screening within 10 business days (PRC § 4.4). Screening does not wait for a meeting. Record a disposition for every referral, and notify the referrer that the referral was received and screened — without disclosing the outcome (PRC § 4.5).

**Practitioner Health dispositions.** Register every referral dispositioned to the Practitioner Health pathway with its disposition, and notify counsel of each one at the time (PRC § 4.3(d)). Diary each at [thirty] days, when the Committee establishes two facts and no others: whether the matter has been acted on, and whether the Practitioner continues to furnish care. The diary repeats at [thirty]-day intervals until either fact changes (PRC § 4.3(d)(2)). The Committee receives no information about the Practitioner's health, and the entry holds none.

**Notice.** Issue written notice to the clinician within 5 business days of a review being opened, containing every element the charter requires (PRC § 5.1). Track the 20-day response window and any extension granted (PRC § 5.2). Where an agreed resolution is proposed, its notice issues on the same five-day timing, names the Coordinator as the person to whom the Practitioner may put questions, and allows not less than [ten] business days to decide (PRC § 5.7.3).

**Panel logistics.** Support constitution of review panels from the standing membership, drawing on the Reviewer Pool only where the membership cannot supply a discipline match, confirming that discipline-matching requirements are met and that no panelist is subject to a recusal trigger (PRC §§ 6.1–6.3). Where the pool cannot supply a matched panel, arrange an external reviewer under a written confidentiality undertaking (PRC § 6.6).

**Recusal documentation.** Record every recusal, the departure from the room, and the return (PRC § 12.2). This is a small task with a disproportionate consequence — an undocumented recusal is an argument that it did not happen.

**Minutes to standard.** Minutes stating each matter and its identifier, the documents reviewed identified individually, the members present, the members recused with times of departure and return, the action taken, and the vote (PRC § 2.11). Deliberative content summarized, never transcribed. This specificity is set by Ohio case law, not by preference. The Committee's meetings are not recorded; the hearing record is the one exception.

**Convening obligations.** Case business does not wait for the monthly cycle. Schedule the Committee within [five] business days of a precautionary restriction for ratification, and within [fifteen] calendar days of receiving a hearing panel's recommendation for final action (PRC § 2.10). Special meetings are called on [two] business days' notice, or shorter where a precautionary restriction is contemplated or in effect.

**Voting floor and appointed members.** A vote to propose or take a Professional Review Action is not taken with fewer than [four] members not recused. Where recusal leaves fewer, record the Chair's appointment of a member for that matter and its reason, obtain the appointee's confidentiality undertaking, and tell the Practitioner the appointee's name so that an objection can be made (PRC §§ 2.8, 10.3.2, 13.5).

**Signed decisions.** File with each decision that closes a review, imposes a Quality Intervention, proposes or takes a Professional Review Action, or ratifies, modifies, or ends a restriction the Chair's written confirmation that the process was followed, and the signature (PRC § 2.1.1). Where the Chair returns a matter with a stated defect, record the return and bring the matter back to the Committee to be cured and decided again.

**Interventions and restrictions of defined duration.** Record the terms, exit criteria, and named monitor at the time of the disposition, track completion and duration from the record, and bring the matter to the Committee on its exit date (PRC §§ 8.6(b), 10.8(c)). Bring back any Quality Intervention that has been extended once and reached the end of the extension, or has run beyond [six] months (PRC § 8.3). A Professional Review Action of defined duration ends on its stated date, and the register shows it (PRC § 10.8(e)). This is administration from the record and requires no contact with the Practitioner.

**Precautionary restrictions.** On the day a restriction is imposed by the Chair and [two] members, notify every member (PRC § 9.2) and issue notice to the Practitioner within [twenty-four] hours (PRC § 9.3). Record the expedited schedule the Chair sets at imposition (PRC § 9.3). Track the automatic lapse at [five] business days unless ratified by members who did not join in imposing it, with at least [three] present (PRC §§ 9.2, 9.2.1). Notify the Chair and the members at day [twenty] of the thirty-day reportability clock, which runs from imposition (PRC § 9.4). Track the hard stop at [forty-five] days (PRC § 9.5). Where the restriction continues because a hearing was requested, diary its review every [thirty] days and see that the offer of an expedited schedule is made again at each review. Where it continues while the hearing-request period runs, track that period and the [five] business days for final action if no request is made (PRC § 9.5).

**Investigatory restrictions.** Record the terms stated at imposition and issue written notice to the Practitioner stating the restriction, its end date, that it is imposed while a review is conducted, and that it rests on no finding (PRC § 9.8(a), (d)). Track the fourteen-day end, which requires no action by anyone. The register flags the facts on which it rested, because it may not be extended, renewed, or imposed again in the same matter or on the same facts (PRC § 9.8(b)). Where a precautionary restriction follows in the same matter, the thirty-day clock runs from the first imposition (PRC § 9.8(f)).

**Hearing administration.** Administer notice and the periods in Article X (PRC § 2.7):

- Deliver the notice of Proposed Action by a method producing proof of receipt, and the material relied on with it or within [five] business days. The material is the findings, the evidence considered, and the record of any response. Not the deliberations (PRC §§ 10.2, 3.3).
- Record the offer of an expedited schedule that accompanies every notice, and the Practitioner's response to it (PRC § 10.2.1(c)).
- Receive the written request for a hearing and track the [thirty]-day request period from receipt, or [ten] days on the expedited schedule (PRC §§ 10.2, 10.3, 10.10).
- Support constitution of the hearing panel within [fifteen] days of the request by confirming each panelist's eligibility: not a PRC member; no part in the review, the audit behind it, or the decision to propose; no Employment Authority; no recusal trigger; no direct economic competition; discipline-matched. A CQC member may sit where that member took no part in the audit behind the matter (PRC § 10.3).
- Track the [five] business days for an objection to a panelist and record the decision on it (PRC § 10.3.2).
- Issue notice of hearing not less than [thirty] days before it ([ten] expedited), and track the pre-hearing exchange [ten] business days before (PRC § 10.4). Record any continuance and its reason.
- Attend the hearing and see that a record is made by transcript or recording, held in the hearing panel's record (PRC §§ 10.5(c), (i), 13.1(c)).
- Track the Practitioner's written closing statement at [five] business days (PRC § 10.5(h)), and the panel's written recommendation at [thirty] days ([ten] expedited), delivered to the Committee and the Practitioner at the same time (PRC § 10.6).
- Track the Committee's final action within [fifteen] days of receiving the recommendation ([ten] expedited), or on the existing record where a hearing is waived, forfeited, or withdrawn, and issue the signed written decision (PRC §§ 10.7, 10.2.2, 10.9).
- Send Management written notice of each final Professional Review Action: what it is, when it takes effect, and how long it lasts or the criteria on which it ends. Not the findings, the evidence, or the deliberations (PRC § 3.4).

The full table of ordinary and expedited periods is at PRC § 10.10.

**Aggregate reporting.** Prepare the quarterly de-identified aggregate report to the Clinical Quality Committee: volume by source, by discipline, by category, and by disposition (PRC § 11.2). The count by source is the record of where referrals originate.

**Reporting to the Data Bank.** Maintain the reporting calendar, track the 30-day clock on each action, and prepare the biennial attestation that all reportable actions were submitted (PRC § 15.8). The determination of whether a matter is reportable is the Committee's with counsel (PRC §§ 15.6, 15.7, 15.9); the tracking and filing are not.

**Quarterly report.** Assemble the aggregate report the Chair gives at the quarterly meeting with the Chief Medical Officer and the Chief Operating Officer: volume and categories of matters and their dispositions, dispositions to the Practitioner Health pathway, matters open beyond [ninety] days with the reasons, reports made to the Data Bank, and the systems conditions reviews have identified (PRC §§ 15.10, 14.5). The Chief Executive Officer receives the same report. It identifies no Practitioner and describes no matter. The annual charter review is reported in the next quarterly report (PRC § 16.1).

**Exception notices.** Track and bring to the Chair, for prompt notice to the Chief Executive Officer outside the quarterly cycle, the fact of each of four events: litigation filed or threatened arising from a review; a report submitted to the Data Bank; a precautionary restriction in effect longer than thirty days; and any allegation that the process was not followed (PRC § 15.11). The notice is of the fact only.

## Records custody

Seven separate records, maintained separately, with documents never moved between them (PRC § 13.1; CQC §§ 8.1, 8.2):

1. The PRC governance record — the charter, the Authorization, any designation of the Practitioner Health pathway, procedures, the concurrence in the Audit Referral Policy with a copy of it, and the quarterly reports and exception notices
2. The PRC case record — each matter screened, reviewed, or decided, with its minutes
3. Each hearing panel's record, held apart from the case record
4. The Clinical Quality Committee's open record — standards and education, which circulates and is not confidential
5. The Clinical Quality Committee's protected record — observations, individual findings, dispositions, deliberations, and the internal drafting record
6. Employment and personnel records — held elsewhere, and never commingled
7. Any record later designated patient safety work product

Also within custody: retention to schedule (PRC § 13.8; CQC § 8.6), legal hold on any matter where litigation is filed or reasonably foreseeable, and the rule that documentation the company or a facility is independently required to create is not a committee record and does not become one by being filed with committee material.

## Protections and constraints

The charters treat this position differently from ordinary employment, in both directions.

The protections are stated in PRC §§ 2.7.1–2.7.5 and apply in full to the Clinical Quality Committee (CQC § 2.7).

**Duty runs to the committees, not to management.** No matter of either committee or any hearing panel may be disclosed to anyone in the reporting line, including the position's own supervisor. Ordinary employment reporting obligations do not reach this material. An instruction from management that conflicts is referred to the chair and recorded (PRC § 2.7.1).

**Case content is barred from evaluation.** The content, subject, or outcome of any matter is not an input to any performance evaluation, compensation decision, or employment decision concerning this position. Performance of the charter functions is assessed by the chair (PRC § 2.7.2).

**Protection from adverse action.** No adverse employment action may be taken by reason of performing these functions or refusing to disclose protected material. The Chair reports any such action to the Chief Executive Officer promptly (PRC § 2.7.3).

**Immunity and confidentiality.** The position is staff of a professional review body and a person assisting it for purposes of 42 U.S.C. § 11111(a)(1). A written confidentiality undertaking is executed before receiving any protected record, and it survives termination of employment (PRC §§ 2.7.4, 13.5).

**Screening for the operational role.** The position holds operational duties elsewhere that create regular working contact with clinicians who may become subjects of review. This is addressed rather than left undefined: no discussion of any matter with its subject or with anyone in the operational reporting line; operational duties performed without reference to any matter; and where the chair determines the working relationship is close enough that custody could reasonably be questioned, an alternate custodian handles that file. The determination is recorded, applies to that matter only, and is not a finding about the coordinator (PRC § 2.9).

**Alternate.** The Chair designates an alternate to perform these functions during unavailability or a screening determination. The alternate is subject to the same provisions in full (PRC § 2.7.5).

## What the role is not

- Not a scribe. Minutes are drafted to a legal standard, not transcribed.
- Not a decision-maker. No vote, no disposition of any matter on the merits, no clinical judgment.
- Not a screener. Screening is by the Chair and the Department Chair for the clinician's service line, or a substitute member under PRC § 4.4.1 (PRC § 4.4); the position schedules and records it, and tracks which Department Chair took part in a CQC determination so that substitution is applied.
- Not a monitor. The position administers interventions and restrictions from the record; clinical oversight belongs to the clinical monitor, and whether exit criteria were met is the Committee's determination (PRC §§ 8.6, 10.8(d)).
- Not the determiner of reportability. That is the Peer Review Committee's with counsel; the position tracks the clock and files the report.

## Open items

**Volume is unknown.** The program is new and audit flag volume has not been measured. The case-administration load scales with referrals and cannot be estimated until a baseline exists. The registers, clocks, and minutes do not scale with volume and exist from day one.

**The 70/30 split will not hold during the build.** Charter drafting, the standards register, the audit map, and Data Bank registration are front-loaded and land in the same twelve to eighteen months. Either the split is stated as temporary, or the build gets its own allocation.

**Training required before the role begins.** Peer review confidentiality and privilege — not a credential, a program requirement, and the easiest line item in this document to justify. Records segregation and minute-taking to the Ohio standard. Data Bank registration and reporting mechanics.

**Unresolved for counsel.** Whether state peer review privilege in each of the five states reaches a non-clinician employee serving as records custodian. Federal law covers staff and persons assisting; the state statutes were written around clinicians and this has not been analyzed. The records architecture described above rests on the answer.
