Evergreen concept note. Atrioventricular block is impaired conduction between atria and ventricles, graded by severity and worked up with a base-rate-out schema that starts with common, reversible causes. RLR / Clinical Problem Solvers teaching points are cited per episode; general clinical detail is scaffolding.

## Degrees of Block
- **First-degree** — every P conducts but with a **prolonged PR (>200 ms)**; usually benign. (PR prolongation is a minor criterion in rheumatic fever.)
- **Second-degree, Mobitz I (Wenckebach)** — progressive PR lengthening until a dropped beat; the block is in the **AV node**, often vagal/drug-related, and usually benign.
- **Second-degree, Mobitz II** — fixed PR with sudden non-conducted P waves; the block is **infra-nodal (His-Purkinje)**, unstable, and prone to progression — pacemaker territory.
- **High-grade** — two or more consecutive non-conducted P waves.
- **Third-degree (complete)** — **AV dissociation** with an independent escape rhythm; the P waves and QRS march to their own rates ([[RLR 060 - Transient Loss of Consciousness and Cardiac Sarcoidosis|RLR 060]]).

## Diagnostic Schema — Base Rate Out
Work AV block from the common and reversible before reaching for rare diagnoses; **rare manifestations of common diseases beat rare diseases**, so don't let one odd feature (e.g., a positive troponin) stampede you past the base rate ([[RLR 060 - Transient Loss of Consciousness and Cardiac Sarcoidosis|RLR 060]]). See [[Base Rate]] and [[Schema-Based Reasoning]].

1. **Ischemia** — inferior MI / **RCA** territory (the RCA supplies the AV node in most people); look for ischemic EKG changes.
2. **Medications** — beta-blockers, non-dihydropyridine calcium channel blockers, digoxin, amiodarone, and other nodal agents.
3. **Vital signs** — **never miss hypothermia** as a reversible cause.
4. **Electrolytes** — **hyperkalemia** foremost (also severe acid-base disturbance).
5. Then **age-related (degenerative) conduction disease** (Lev/Lenègre) and **tachy-brady / sick sinus syndrome** — check old EKGs and a fib-flutter history.
6. Then **[[Arrhythmogenic Cardiomyopathy]]** — infiltrative/genetic disease destroying conduction disproportionately: sarcoid, amyloid, genetic/HCM (troponin with *preserved* pump function is the tell).
7. Then **infectious/inflammatory** — **[[Lyme Carditis]]** (the U.S. "champion"), rheumatic fever, Chagas, and endocarditis.

## The Fever + Troponin Trifecta
- **AV block + fever + troponin is its own differential**, and **[[Endocarditis]] with a perivalvular/aortic-root abscess is the not-to-miss** ([[RLR 060 - Transient Loss of Consciousness and Cardiac Sarcoidosis|RLR 060]]).
- Alongside endocarditis: **[[Lyme Carditis]]**, rheumatic fever, and Chagas — the infectious conduction diseases.
- Fever with a heart rate that "should be" faster is **[[Relative Bradycardia]]** — either conduction disease unmasked by the febrile stress test or a [[Faget Sign]] infection ([[RLR 060 - Transient Loss of Consciousness and Cardiac Sarcoidosis|RLR 060]]).

## Management
- **Unstable bradycardia:** atropine, then transcutaneous pacing and chronotropic infusions (dopamine/epinephrine) as a bridge; transvenous pacing for refractory high-grade block.
- **Reverse what's reversible first** — hold offending drugs, correct potassium, rewarm, treat ischemia/infection; inflammation-driven block (e.g., cardiac sarcoid) may recover with steroids before a permanent device is placed.
- **Permanent pacemaker** for symptomatic or high-grade/complete block that is not reversible (Mobitz II and third-degree generally qualify).
