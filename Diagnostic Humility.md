## Definition

Diagnostic humility is the disciplined acknowledgment of the limits of one's knowledge and of the available data — treating "I don't know" as an earned, legitimate endpoint rather than a failure, and marking the edges of one's expertise out loud. It is the recurring closing note of the later RLR cases, where a rigorous workup often ends unresolved and the hosts style themselves "the champions of *I don't know*."

Crucially it is **operational**, not passive: naming a knowledge gap is paired with an action — a literature search, a call to a pharmacist or obstetrician, serial imaging over time — so the admission changes what happens next. Across later episodes the same posture extends beyond individual data points to the diagnostic *process* itself: judging a workup by the reasoning available at the time rather than by how it turned out, and extending the same non-punitive stance to one's own misses that the hosts extend to trainees and colleagues.

## How it operates

- **"I don't know" can be the correct answer.** When the fingerprint of a diagnosis never appears, an honest non-diagnosis beats a forced label; management is often set regardless (see [[Premature Closure]] — separating closure from the decision to act).
- **Mark the edges of knowledge, then act on them.** "I don't know every side effect of every drug" becomes a search; an unfamiliar script becomes a consult; not knowing whether an exam maneuver's mechanism differs by cause becomes an explicit "I need to refresh this" rather than a guess.
- **Calibrate to your own operator skill.** Refusing to claim a bedside test (e.g. HINTS) you can't reliably perform prevents manufactured reassurance.
- **Live in limbo deliberately.** Using time and serial data rather than forcing a lesion into a name is humility, not indecision.
- **Audit the process, not the outcome.** Judge a diagnostic decision by whether it was reasonable given what was known at the time, not by whether it happened to turn out benign or catastrophic — outcome bias should not be allowed to retroactively relabel sound reasoning as an overreaction, or an honest miss as negligence.
- **Treat a consultant's recommendation as data, not a verdict.** Sub-specialty input can be wrong or incomplete; humility includes noticing when deference to authority is substituting for one's own reasoning — without swinging into reflexive distrust of consultants either.
- **Extend the same grace to yourself that you'd extend a colleague.** Repeated, explicit self-compassion for diagnostic misses — treating worry and self-criticism as a byproduct of caring rather than evidence of incompetence — is what keeps the practice sustainable over a career instead of curdling into burnout or overcorrection.

## Examples from RLR episodes

- **Wrong answer as fuel.** Rabih reframes getting it wrong as practice, so the reflex is right when a real patient is in front of you. ([[RLR 017 - Back Pain and Disseminated Nocardiosis|RLR 017]])
- **Settling on unsettled.** Both hosts choose limbo — serial imaging over time — rather than force closure on a lesion that resolved alongside INR correction. ([[RLR 018 - Gross Hematuria in End-Stage Renal Disease|RLR 018]])
- **Humility as a named move.** Reza marks his ignorance of a drug's side-effect profile and of appendicitis epidemiology, and converts each into a search or a plan to read. ([[RLR 024 - Confusion and Ertapenem-Induced Encephalopathy|RLR 024]])
- **Humility operationalized.** An explicit "n of 2," running every medication past a pharmacist, and a low threshold to call obstetrics. ([[RLR 025 - Liver Chemistry Abnormalities and Intrahepatic Cholestasis of Pregnancy|RLR 025]])
- **Where errors cluster.** Rabih names not-yet-declared presentations as his own error terrain and commits to priming for the subtle clue next time. ([[RLR 026 - Syncope and Occult Gastrointestinal Bleeding|RLR 026]])
- **"I don't know" as an earned endpoint.** The vertigo case closes unresolved, alongside the discipline of guiding — not treating — family. ([[RLR 027 - Dizziness and Peripheral Vertigo|RLR 027]])
- **Humility and self-disclosure.** Both hosts volunteer their own vitamin-D overdoses; the shared-mistake candor keeps a "solved" case open to question. ([[RLR 028 - Hypercalcemia and Vitamin D Toxicity|RLR 028]])
- **Humility and the relational layer.** The VZV case ends genuinely undetermined (disseminated zoster vs primary varicella), and a non-judgmental stance preserved the therapeutic alliance. ([[RLR 029 - Dyspnea and Disseminated Varicella-Zoster|RLR 029]])
- **Naming the gap out loud.** Uncertain whether the Pemberton sign's mechanism differs for internal (device-related) versus external (e.g. goiter) causes of SVC compression, the discussant says outright he needs to refresh his knowledge rather than guess — the edges-of-expertise habit applied to a bedside exam maneuver, not just to a diagnosis. ([[RLR 074 - Bilateral Facial Swelling and Pacemaker Lead-Induced SVC Syndrome|RLR 074]])
- **Holding multiple hypotheses without forcing one.** "I don't have an answer yet," "I can't blame the urine yet" — myositis, viral syndrome, thyrotoxicosis, sympathomimetic toxicity, and pyelonephritis are all kept alive simultaneously rather than one being prematurely promoted. ([[RLR 075 - Myalgias, High Fever, and Bilateral Pyelonephritis in Anorexia Nervosa|RLR 075]])
- **Naming your own limits as a discussant.** An explicit acknowledgment of being "limited as discussants of unknowns" without access to the primary imaging, paired with repeated uncertainty over whether a single imaging finding really explained the hypoxemia rather than forcing it to carry the whole case. ([[RLR 077 - Progressive Dyspnea, Pulmonary-Renal Syndrome, and Silicosis-Triggered ANCA Vasculitis|RLR 077]])
- **Taking an element out to get unstuck, and naming your own anchor.** When a rare case won't resolve, deliberately setting aside the most salient symptom and re-examining the rest of the presentation as its own differential can unstick the reasoning; the discussant also names, in real time, that he is anchoring on a specific diagnosis while doing it — not just recognizing the anchor in hindsight. ([[RLR 078 - Hearing Loss, an Isolated White Matter Lesion, and Primary CNS Vasculitis|RLR 078]])
- **Auditing the process, not the result.** The standard for judging a workup isn't "was the final diagnosis benign" but "would I have wanted this same workup for the person I love most, given what was known at the time" — a named "cherished person" standard that keeps outcome bias from relabeling sound reasoning as an overreaction after the fact. ([[RLR 144 - A Doubleheader Plus a Rapid-Fire Case- Coxsackievirus B Meningitis, Myxomatous Aortic Insufficiency, and Progressive Iron Deficiency Anemia|RLR 144]])
- **Restraint as its own form of humility.** If the body isn't "complaining," either nothing serious is wrong or something is wrong but mild enough to likely self-correct — over-medicalizing an asymptomatic finding can do more harm than the finding itself. The right posture for ambiguous, non-emergent findings: overthink the possibilities, undertest in the moment, document a contingency plan, and stay ready to pivot the moment new data arrives. ([[RLR 145 - Hypokalemia, a High Bicarb, and a Dilute Urine- Nephrogenic Diabetes Insipidus Unmasked by an AKI on the Psych Ward|RLR 145]])
- **Naming a bias specific to your own role.** As a consultant, most calls for a given specialty turn out not to be that specialty's disease — a useful skepticism heuristic that simultaneously risks dismissing the rare true positive; naming this "signal-to-noise bias" out loud is itself a humility move, distinct from simply having good pattern recognition. ([[RLR 148 - Nocturnal Leg Cramps That Weren't Painful- A Meningioma Presenting as Focal Seizures with Todd's Paralysis|RLR 148]])
- **Self-compassion as a sustained practice.** A closing discussion frames worry and self-criticism after a diagnostic delay as byproducts of caring rather than evidence of incompetence, and credits the intergenerational transmission of specific clinical pearls as a core part of how practice actually improves over time — humility toward one's own trajectory, not just toward the case in front of you. ([[RLR 150 - The Falsely Reassuring Creatinine- Cefepime Neurotoxicity Unmasked by an Above-Knee Amputation|RLR 150]])
- **Grace for your own misses, made explicit.** Diagnostic misses and mismanagement stop being discouraging once they're understood as an inescapable part of practicing medicine rather than evidence of not caring — a stance that makes it easier to keep learning from mistakes instead of becoming debilitated by them, offered from one host to the other after watching him arrive at the same conclusion faster. ([[RLR 189 - Static Left, Racing Right- A Six-Month Chase from Suspected Lung Cancer to Acute Fibrinous and Organizing Pneumonia|RLR 189]])
