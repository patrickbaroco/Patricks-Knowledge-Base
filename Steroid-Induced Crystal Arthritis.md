Evergreen concept note. A rare post-injection flare in which the injected corticosteroid itself precipitates into crystals and drives an acute synovitis. RLR / Clinical Problem Solvers teaching points are cited per episode; general clinical detail is scaffolding.

## Definition
Steroid-induced (post-injection) crystal arthritis is an acute crystal-induced synovitis occurring hours to a few days **after an intra-articular corticosteroid injection**, caused by the **viscous corticosteroid suspension precipitating into crystals** within the joint ([[RLR 061 - Knee Swelling and Calcific Tendinitis|RLR 061]]).

## Clinical Features
- Onset typically within ~24–72 hours of the injection; a **vigorously inflammatory** flare with pain, warmth, and swelling ([[RLR 061 - Knee Swelling and Calcific Tendinitis|RLR 061]]).
- It is self-limited, usually resolving over a few days.
- It is a **rare entity, catalogued in the arthrocentesis literature** (e.g., UpToDate's arthrocentesis discussion of post-injection crystal-induced synovitis) ([[RLR 061 - Knee Swelling and Calcific Tendinitis|RLR 061]]).

## Diagnostic Reasoning
- Consider it when a joint worsens shortly after a steroid injection — but the key move is that a **post-injection worsening first demands excluding [[Septic Arthritis|infection]]** ([[RLR 061 - Knee Swelling and Calcific Tendinitis|RLR 061]]). Re-tap before attributing worsening to any benign cause.
- Because it is *vigorously inflammatory*, it should show an **inflammatory** [[Synovial Fluid Analysis|synovial]] cell count. A **bland, non-inflammatory** tap argues *against* it — as in RLR 061, where the entity was considered and rejected precisely because the fluid was too quiet, pointing instead to a periarticular [[Calcific Tendinitis|calcific tendinitis]] ([[RLR 061 - Knee Swelling and Calcific Tendinitis|RLR 061]]).
- Apply the [[Law of Proportionality]]: the vigor of the expected inflammation must match the fluid.

## Management
- Supportive — NSAIDs and rest; the flare is self-limited. Confirm cultures remain negative.
