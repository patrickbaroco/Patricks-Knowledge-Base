Evergreen concept note. A schema for approaching illness in a young patient: **bad luck, bad genes, and risky behavior** — a quick frame for why an otherwise healthy young person becomes ill, to be layered on top of (not instead of) the organ-based differential.

## The three buckets

Attributed on RLR to a Rebecca Berger / CP Solvers case, the schema organizes the *why* of illness in the young:

- **Bad luck** — acute, unpredictable events: trauma, accidents, infections, an unlucky embolus or torsion.
- **Bad genes** — heritable conditions that declare themselves in the second-to-third decade (connective tissue disease, channelopathies, inborn metabolic/autoinflammatory disease, early cancers with germline predisposition).
- **Risky behavior** — exposure- and behavior-related risk (substances, sexual exposures, occupational/recreational hazards). Best framed non-judgmentally as *risk exposure* rather than "bad behavior."

## How to use it

The schema is a **backdrop**, not a localizer. Keep the [[Center of Gravity|center of gravity]] and the organ-based differential in the foreground, and use the three buckets to make sure you have not omitted a category — especially **genetic/autoinflammatory** disease, which is easy to forget in adults-trained reasoning. In [[RLR 063 - Diarrhea and Behçet's Disease|RLR 063]], a young woman's recurrent GI illness ran the schema in the background while the workup pursued colitis; the "bad genes" bucket ultimately mattered when [[A20 Haploinsufficiency|A20 haploinsufficiency]] (a heritable [[Behçet's Disease|Behçet]]-like disease) was raised given the unusually young onset. ([[RLR 063 - Diarrhea and Behçet's Disease|RLR 063]])

## Cautions

- Youth lowers but does not eliminate common adult disease — anchor on age as a modifier of base rates, not an exclusion.
- "Risky behavior" invites bias; frame it as exposure and ask everyone the same questions.
- Pair with [[Illness Tempo|tempo]]: a *recurrent* or early-onset course strengthens the genetic/autoinflammatory bucket.
