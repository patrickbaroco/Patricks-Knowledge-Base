Spinal dural arteriovenous fistula — a treatable, frequently missed cause of progressive myelopathy in older men that mimics transverse myelitis.

## Definition and Pathophysiology
A spinal dural arteriovenous fistula (dAVF) is an abnormal shunt, usually within a dural nerve-root sleeve, connecting a radicular artery to a radicular vein, which arterializes the **coronal venous plexus** on the cord surface ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).

- **Venous congestive myelopathy:** the elevated venous pressure impairs cord venous drainage, causing chronic edema and ischemia — the injury is venous congestion, not hemorrhage or direct compression ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).

## Who and How It Presents
- **Demographics:** classically **older men**, most often at the thoracolumbar levels ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).
- **Course:** a **progressive, stepwise or subacute myelopathy** — ascending sensorimotor deficits, gait difficulty, and later bladder/bowel and sexual dysfunction; symptoms may worsen with exertion or upright posture.
- **The great mimic:** it looks like **transverse myelitis** but is not inflammatory; misdiagnosis leads to immunosuppression that does not help ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).

## Red Flags That Should Prompt the Diagnosis
- **Paradoxical worsening with steroids** and after **lumbar puncture** — a valuable clue that separates dAVF from true inflammatory myelitis ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).
- A "gourmet" not-to-miss diagnosis in an older man with an unexplained subacute cord syndrome — worth actively excluding ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).

## Diagnosis
- **MRI:** central cord T2 hyperintensity spanning multiple segments with cord swelling, often with **serpentine dorsal flow voids** representing dilated perimedullary veins; contrast enhancement may occur ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).
- **Spinal (catheter) angiography** is the **gold standard** — it localizes the fistulous point and feeding radicular artery for treatment planning ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).

## Management
- **Definitive treatment** is obliteration of the shunt by **microsurgical ligation** or **endovascular embolization**; earlier intervention gives better neurologic recovery, so prompt recognition matters.
- Distinguish from higher-flow intradural AV malformations/fistulas, which have different treatment considerations.
