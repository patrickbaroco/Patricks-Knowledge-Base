Evergreen concept note. Acute immune-mediated polyradiculoneuropathy causing rapidly ascending weakness and areflexia — a neurologic emergency because of respiratory and autonomic failure. RLR / Clinical Problem Solvers teaching points are cited per episode; general clinical detail is scaffolding.

## Definition and Pathophysiology
Guillain-Barré syndrome (GBS) is an acute, immune-mediated **polyradiculoneuropathy**; the prototypic form, **acute inflammatory demyelinating polyradiculoneuropathy (AIDP)**, is driven by autoimmune attack on peripheral myelin, often **post-infectious** (e.g., following gastrointestinal or respiratory illness) via molecular mimicry ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).

- **Demyelinating physiology** makes it **non-length-dependent** — proximal and distal, upper and lower limbs can be affected early, unlike common axonal neuropathies (see [[Length-Dependent vs Non-Length-Dependent Neuropathy]]) ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).

## Clinical Features
- **Ascending, symmetric weakness** evolving over days to ~4 weeks, with **early areflexia** and variable distal paresthesias.
- **Cranial nerve involvement** is common — **bilateral facial weakness**, and the **Miller-Fisher variant** (ophthalmoplegia, ataxia, areflexia; anti-GQ1b antibodies) ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).
- **Autonomic instability** (arrhythmia, labile blood pressure) and **neuromuscular respiratory failure** are the life-threatening complications.
- **Bowel and bladder are usually spared** — prominent early sphincter involvement should redirect you toward a cord/cauda-equina process instead (see [[Spine vs Peripheral Nerve]]) ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).

## Diagnosis
- **CSF: albuminocytologic dissociation** — elevated protein with a normal (or near-normal) cell count, often after the first week ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]). A significant pleocytosis should prompt a search for alternatives (HIV, leptomeningeal disease).
- **Nerve conduction studies** show demyelinating features (conduction block, prolonged distal latencies, slowed velocities); variants (AMAN) are axonal.
- **Monitor forced vital capacity / negative inspiratory force** — the bedside numbers that trigger ICU transfer and airway protection.

## Management
- **IVIG or plasma exchange (PLEX)** — equally effective; do not combine. Steroids alone are ineffective.
- **Supportive care** is central: respiratory monitoring, cardiac/autonomic surveillance, VTE prophylaxis, and rehabilitation. Most patients recover, though deficits and time course vary.
