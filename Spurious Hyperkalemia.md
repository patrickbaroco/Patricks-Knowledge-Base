A surprising hyperkalemia result is not always true hyperkalemia.

## How it operates

- **Two questions for any surprising hyperkalemia:** is it real, and if real, could it reflect adrenal insufficiency?
- **Confirm with plasma or point-of-care potassium.** A standard serum draw is vulnerable to the tourniquet phenomenon and, in a patient with a very high white count, to in vitro cell lysis within the collection tube itself — both can produce a falsely elevated result.
- **Very high white counts are a specific risk factor.** Extreme leukocytosis means a large number of fragile cells are present in the sample; ongoing lysis after the draw can release intracellular potassium into the specimen without reflecting the patient's true serum level.

## Examples from RLR episodes

- **A hyperkalemia that wasn't real.** A patient with an extremely elevated white count develops a surprising potassium of 6.2 despite an undetectable uric acid and an improving creatinine; a repeat plasma potassium is normal, confirming spurious (in vitro) hyperkalemia from ongoing ex vivo cell lysis rather than true hyperkalemia or worsening renal failure.
