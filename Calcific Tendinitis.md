Calcific tendinitis is calcium crystal deposition within a tendon that can inflame the tendon itself and spill inflammation into the neighboring joint — mimicking arthritis while the joint fluid stays bland.

## Definition and Pathophysiology
Deposition of calcium crystals — **hydroxyapatite** or **calcium pyrophosphate ([[CPPD]])** — within a tendon, producing an acute or chronic tendinopathy. The supraspinatus is the classic site, but it occurs in the quadriceps and other tendons ([[RLR 061 - Knee Swelling and Calcific Tendinitis|RLR 061]]).

- **A calcific tendon can rage while the joint is quiet** — the disease lives in the tendon, not the joint space ([[RLR 061 - Knee Swelling and Calcific Tendinitis|RLR 061]]).
- **CPPD is not only an arthritis** — it can cause calcific tendinitis ([[RLR 061 - Knee Swelling and Calcific Tendinitis|RLR 061]]). See [[CPPD]].

## Clinical Features
- Severe, well-localized peritendinous pain, often pointing to a spot *outside* the joint line (e.g., above the patella for the quadriceps tendon) ([[RLR 061 - Knee Swelling and Calcific Tendinitis|RLR 061]]).
- **Inflammatory tendonitis hurts with both active and passive motion**, so it mimics an intra-articular process ([[RLR 061 - Knee Swelling and Calcific Tendinitis|RLR 061]], [[RLR 062 - Oligoarthritis and Polyarticular Gout Flare|RLR 062]]) — see [[Active vs Passive Range of Motion]].
- Regional (non-circumferential) swelling favors a periarticular source over a true [[Joint Effusion|effusion]].

## Diagnosis
- **Plain films** may show tendon calcification or chondrocalcinosis; **MRI** best demonstrates the tendon inflammation and periarticular extent ([[RLR 061 - Knee Swelling and Calcific Tendinitis|RLR 061]]).
- **[[Spillover Phenomenon|Spillover]] into the synovium gives a bland joint tap** — inflammation *adjacent* to the sampled fluid yields deceptively quiet fluid, the brain-abscess-LP principle ([[RLR 061 - Knee Swelling and Calcific Tendinitis|RLR 061]]). A non-inflammatory cell count in a severely painful joint should trigger reframing via the [[Law of Proportionality]].
- A **repeatedly dry tap** is itself a clue that the fluid is not where a joint effusion should be ([[RLR 061 - Knee Swelling and Calcific Tendinitis|RLR 061]]).

## Management
- **Anti-inflammatory therapy** — NSAIDs, and corticosteroids (oral taper or targeted injection) for severe cases; the RLR 061 patient responded to an oral prednisone taper ([[RLR 061 - Knee Swelling and Calcific Tendinitis|RLR 061]]).
- Rest, activity modification, and physical therapy; ultrasound-guided barbotage/needling or surgical removal for refractory hydroxyapatite deposits.
