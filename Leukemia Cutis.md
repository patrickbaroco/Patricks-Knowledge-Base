Leukemic infiltration of the skin.

## What it is

Leukemia cutis is dermal infiltration by leukemic cells — a "liquid tumor" appearing on the [[Skin Nodule|deep-nodule]] differential. In the worked case it presented as a nodular erythematous rash whose biopsy showed dermal infiltration by large agranular **blasts positive for myeloperoxidase, lysozyme, and CD56** (a myeloid/myelomonocytic acute leukemia), and the marrow confirmed [[Chronic Myelomonocytic Leukemia|CMML-2]]. ([[RLR 032 - Fever and Chronic Myelomonocytic Leukemia|RLR 032]])

## Reasoning points

- **The skin can be the fastest biopsy for a marrow malignancy.** When blood [[Monocytosis|monocytosis]] and a cutaneous infiltrate coexist, an accessible skin nodule biopsied by dermatology can clinch a diagnosis before the marrow does — "monocytes love tissue." ([[RLR 032 - Fever and Chronic Myelomonocytic Leukemia|RLR 032]])
- **It marks aggressive/higher-grade disease** — its appearance in a myelomonocytic process signals blast infiltration and a worse trajectory. ([[RLR 032 - Fever and Chronic Myelomonocytic Leukemia|RLR 032]])
