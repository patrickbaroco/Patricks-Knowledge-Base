Cranial nerve V — its three sensory divisions and motor branch — and the anatomic map used to localize facial numbness.

## Anatomy and Divisions
The trigeminal nerve carries sensation from the face and motor supply to the muscles of mastication. It has three sensory divisions ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]):

- **V1 (ophthalmic):** forehead, upper eyelid, corneal reflex afferent; exits via the superior orbital fissure.
- **V2 (maxillary):** cheek, upper lip, lower eyelid, upper teeth; exits via foramen rotundum and the infraorbital foramen.
- **V3 (mandibular):** jaw, lower lip, lower teeth, and — via the **mental nerve** — the chin; the only division carrying **motor** fibers (muscles of mastication). It exits via foramen ovale.
- **Course:** peripheral branches → Gasserian (trigeminal) ganglion → root entry at the **pons**; central connections descend in the spinal trigeminal tract to the upper cervical cord.

## The Localization Map for Facial Numbness
Facial sensory loss localizes to one of four compartments ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]):

- **Brainstem (pons/medulla):** rarely produces an *isolated* single-division numbness — the nucleus is small and neighbored by other tracts, so a lone division points away from brainstem and toward the periphery ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).
- **Meninges:** leptomeningeal disease coating the nerve as it courses through the subarachnoid space — in a cancer patient, this raises [[Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|leptomeningeal carcinomatosis]] ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).
- **Skull base:** perineural spread, bony metastases, or skull-base tumors compressing a division at its foramen.
- **Facial compartment (orbit/cheek/jaw):** peripheral branch lesions, including **mental-nerve infiltration** producing [[Numb Chin Syndrome|numb chin syndrome]] ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).

## A Key Localization Rule
- **The face is *not* the cervical cord.** Facial sensation is trigeminal, tied to the pons and above — so facial numbness cannot be explained by a cervical or thoracic cord lesion and forces a second, higher process (see [[Hickam's Dictum]]) ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).
- **A solitary division in isolation points peripherally**, prompting a hunt along the meninges, skull base, or facial compartment ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).

## Mimics of "Facial Numbness"
- **Perioral paresthesias** can be non-structural: **hyperventilation → respiratory alkalosis → increased albumin binding of calcium → functional [[Hypocalcemia|hypocalcemia]]** producing circumoral and acral tingling ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).
- Always reconcile reported numbness with the exam before over-localizing.
