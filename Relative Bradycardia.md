Relative bradycardia (pulse-temperature dissociation) is a heart rate lower than expected for the degree of fever, flagging either an intracellular infection or unmasked conduction disease.

## Concept
Fever normally drives a compensatory tachycardia. When the pulse fails to rise to the expected degree — off any rate-limiting drug — the mismatch is a physical-exam sign worth interpreting rather than tallying ([[RLR 060 - Transient Loss of Consciousness and Cardiac Sarcoidosis|RLR 060]]).

- **Expected rise:** as a rough rule, heart rate climbs roughly **10 beats/min per 1 °F (≈8/min per °C)** of temperature elevation. A febrile patient at 101–103 °F "should be" tachycardic; a normal or near-normal pulse is the dissociation.
- **Prerequisite:** the patient must be **off nodal blockers** (beta-blockers, non-dihydropyridine CCBs) and not have another confounder (e.g., a normally low resting rate) before the sign counts ([[RLR 060 - Transient Loss of Consciousness and Cardiac Sarcoidosis|RLR 060]]).

## Two Buckets of Causes
1. **Intracellular / specific infections (Faget sign).** Classically **typhoid (Salmonella)**, **Legionella**, **Brucella**, and others — see [[Faget Sign]] for the full list and eponym ([[RLR 060 - Transient Loss of Consciousness and Cardiac Sarcoidosis|RLR 060]]).
2. **Conduction disease unmasked by the febrile "stress test."** Latent sinus-node or **[[AV Block|AV-node]]** disease that could not augment the rate under the metabolic demand of fever; in the episode this was the herald of **complete heart block** ([[RLR 060 - Transient Loss of Consciousness and Cardiac Sarcoidosis|RLR 060]]).
- **Nodal drugs** blunting the response are the trivial third bucket to exclude first.

## Clinical Use
- Treat relative bradycardia as a **branch point**: pursue an EKG (looking for [[AV Block]]) *and* consider the Faget-sign infectious differential in parallel.
- In the AV-block workup, the fever context expands the differential to the fever+troponin trifecta — **[[Endocarditis]]** (perivalvular abscess), **[[Lyme Carditis]]**, rheumatic fever, and Chagas ([[RLR 060 - Transient Loss of Consciousness and Cardiac Sarcoidosis|RLR 060]]).
- It is a specificity-poor but attention-directing sign: its value is prompting the right next test, not clinching a diagnosis.
