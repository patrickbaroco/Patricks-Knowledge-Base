Paravalvular regurgitation after transcatheter aortic valve replacement — a source of mechanical hemolysis and, when persistent, a marker of poor outcome that is often inaudible on exam.

## Concept
A transcatheter aortic valve (TAVR/TAVI) is deployed within the native calcified annulus without surgical suturing, so an incomplete seal can leave a **paravalvular (perivalvular) leak** — a regurgitant jet passing *around* the prosthesis rather than through it ([[RLR 064 - Hematuria and Post-TAVR Perivalvular Leak|RLR 064]]). Even mild-to-moderate leaks are more common after TAVR than after surgical valves and carry prognostic weight.

## Consequences
- **Mechanical hemolysis.** The high-velocity paravalvular jet shears red cells, producing intravascular **[[Valve Hemolysis|valve hemolysis]]** — schistocytes, high LDH, low haptoglobin, hemoglobinuria — and can consume platelets ([[RLR 064 - Hematuria and Post-TAVR Perivalvular Leak|RLR 064]]).
- **Volume overload / heart failure** from chronic aortic regurgitation.
- **Acquired von Willebrand syndrome** from valvular shear, adding a bleeding diathesis (see [[Acquired von Willebrand Syndrome]]).

## Post-TAVR Thrombocytopenia as a Signal
A **transient** dip in platelets after TAVR is expected. A **persistent** post-TAVR thrombocytopenia is unusual and is a recognized **marker of poor outcome**, reflecting an underlying structural valve problem such as a paravalvular leak ([[RLR 064 - Hematuria and Post-TAVR Perivalvular Leak|RLR 064]]). Reframing such a count as "[[Acute but Persistent]]" (dropped in one step at the time of the procedure, then flat) relinks it to the valve rather than to indolent causes.

## Why It Is Easy to Miss
- **Often inaudible.** The murmur may be soft or absent, so the leak is easily overlooked on physical exam ([[RLR 064 - Hematuria and Post-TAVR Perivalvular Leak|RLR 064]]).
- **Presents at a distance.** It can surface in an unexpected organ — e.g., hemolysis mistaken for hematuria — so the valve is not the team's initial focus ([[RLR 064 - Hematuria and Post-TAVR Perivalvular Leak|RLR 064]]).

## Diagnosis and Management
- **Echocardiography** is the key test — transthoracic first, with **transesophageal echo** for better characterization of jet location and severity; CT and invasive assessment supplement.
- **Hemolysis labs** (LDH, haptoglobin, smear, bilirubin, urine free hemoglobin) quantify the impact.
- **Management** ranges from observation and supportive care (iron/folate, transfusion) for mild disease to **percutaneous paravalvular-leak closure** or **valve-in-valve / re-intervention** for hemodynamically or hematologically significant leaks.
