Evergreen concept note. The hyponatremia workup and its worked instances across the RLR / Clinical Problem Solvers series. General physiology is scaffolding; teaching points are cited per episode.

> Schema: [CP Solvers — Dx Schema: Hyponatremia](https://clinicalproblemsolving.com/dx-schema-hyponatremia/)

## Work it in order: tonicity → ADH → aldosterone

- **Step 1 — confirm true hypo-osmolar hyponatremia.** A serum osmolality establishes the sodium is *proportionally* low (excluding hyperosmolar/iso-osmolar causes) before any further reasoning. 80–90% of the time you are in the hypo-osmolar arm. ([[RLR 029 - Dyspnea and Disseminated Varicella-Zoster|RLR 029]], [[RLR 002 - Nausea, Vomiting, and Hyponatremia|RLR 002]])
- **Step 2 — read the urine osm for ADH on/off.** A high urine osm means ADH is on (excluding psychogenic polydipsia and [[Beer Potomania|beer potomania]], where ADH is appropriately suppressed). ([[RLR 029 - Dyspnea and Disseminated Varicella-Zoster|RLR 029]])
- **Step 3 — use the urine sodium as an aldosterone proxy, then ask appropriate vs inappropriate.** A *low* urine sodium means aldosterone is on — a low-effective-volume state (hypovolemia, heart failure, cirrhosis) driving appropriate ADH. A *high* urine sodium means aldosterone is off — inappropriate ADH. Respect the false positives: a urine sodium is uninterpretable on diuretics or in ATN. ([[RLR 003 - Hypertension and Severe Hyponatremia|RLR 003]], [[RLR 029 - Dyspnea and Disseminated Varicella-Zoster|RLR 029]])

## The high-urine-sodium (inappropriate-ADH) five

- **When the urine sodium is high and it isn't ATN or diuretics, five causes remain:** [[SIADH]], thiazides, hypothyroidism, [[Adrenal Insufficiency|adrenal insufficiency]], and [[Cerebral Salt Wasting|cerebral salt wasting]]. A [[Serum Uric Acid|serum uric acid]] under 4 (with a high [[Fractional Excretion of Uric Acid|FEUA]] >12%) supports SIADH. ([[RLR 003 - Hypertension and Severe Hyponatremia|RLR 003]], [[RLR 029 - Dyspnea and Disseminated Varicella-Zoster|RLR 029]])

## Arrow of causality and the correction hazard

- **When hyponatremia and a symptom complex coexist, default to the more common causal arrow.** Nausea/vomiting usually *drives* the hyponatremia via non-osmotic vasopressin release, not the reverse — though a self-reinforcing cycle can develop, and a sodium below ~126 starts to plausibly cause symptoms itself. ([[RLR 002 - Nausea, Vomiting, and Hyponatremia|RLR 002]])
- **Correction is a procedural hazard, not just a number to fix.** Volume repletion in a hypovolemic, non-osmotic-vasopressin-driven patient can switch off vasopressin once volume is restored, producing an abrupt water diuresis and overly rapid correction — risking [[Osmotic Demyelination Syndrome|osmotic demyelination]] even above the classically cited danger zone. ([[RLR 002 - Nausea, Vomiting, and Hyponatremia|RLR 002]], [[RLR 003 - Hypertension and Severe Hyponatremia|RLR 003]])
- **Don't let the sodium hijack the whole case.** A striking low sodium can consume an encounter it does not deserve; weigh whether it is severe enough to explain the presentation ([[Law of Proportionality|proportionality]]) before spending the whole workup on it. ([[RLR 003 - Hypertension and Severe Hyponatremia|RLR 003]])
- **Once SIADH is established, it is almost always secondary** — hunt pain, meds, infection (including thoracic zoster, a bigger trigger than Legionella), and occult malignancy. ([[RLR 029 - Dyspnea and Disseminated Varicella-Zoster|RLR 029]])
