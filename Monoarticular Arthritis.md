Evergreen concept note. A single acutely inflamed joint is crystal disease or infection until proven otherwise, and localizing the process within versus around the joint is the first task. RLR / Clinical Problem Solvers teaching points are cited per episode; general clinical detail is scaffolding.

## Definition and Framing
Monoarticular arthritis is inflammation of one joint, presenting as pain, swelling, warmth, erythema, and limited motion. The acute atraumatic monoarthritis is a can't-miss presentation because the two leading causes — **crystal-induced arthritis** and **septic arthritis** — are common and, in the case of infection, joint-destroying within days.

- **Crystals or infection by base rate — even with trauma** ([[RLR 061 - Knee Swelling and Calcific Tendinitis|RLR 061]]). Atraumatic single-joint swelling should be worked as crystal disease or infection until proven otherwise. Trauma does not exclude either, because injury can *set up* a joint for crystal precipitation or seeding.
- **Get an x-ray for fracture regardless of history** ([[RLR 061 - Knee Swelling and Calcific Tendinitis|RLR 061]]), which also documents chondrocalcinosis, effusion, or osteoarthritis.

## Differential Diagnosis
- **Crystal arthropathy** — [[Gout]] (monosodium urate) and [[CPPD]] (pseudogout). Most common cause overall.
- **Infection** — bacterial [[Septic Arthritis]] (staph, strep, gonococcal), and less commonly mycobacterial/fungal.
- **Trauma / internal derangement** — fracture, meniscal or ligamentous injury, hemarthrosis (consider anticoagulation).
- **Osteoarthritis flare** and avascular necrosis.
- **Periarticular mimics** — tendinitis, bursitis, cellulitis; a monoarticular *tendonitis* can masquerade as arthritis ([[RLR 061 - Knee Swelling and Calcific Tendinitis|RLR 061]]).

## Septic-Joint Exam Signs
The bedside exam raises or lowers the probability of infection before any tap.

- **Refusal to bear weight** and inability to use the joint ([[RLR 061 - Knee Swelling and Calcific Tendinitis|RLR 061]]).
- **Pain with both active AND passive range of motion** ([[RLR 061 - Knee Swelling and Calcific Tendinitis|RLR 061]], [[RLR 062 - Oligoarthritis and Polyarticular Gout Flare|RLR 062]]) — an intra-articular process hurts either way it is moved. See [[Active vs Passive Range of Motion]].
- Fever is unreliable (often absent in septic arthritis); its absence does not exclude infection.

## Swelling vs Effusion — Localizing the Problem
- **Swelling is not the same as intra-articular inflammation** ([[RLR 061 - Knee Swelling and Calcific Tendinitis|RLR 061]]). A true joint [[Joint Effusion|effusion]] is *circumferential*; swelling concentrated in one region (e.g., suprapatellar) should raise a **periarticular** process — tendon, ligament, meniscus, or bursa — via the [[Anatomical Approach]].
- **A hard-to-aspirate "effusion" is itself a clue** ([[RLR 061 - Knee Swelling and Calcific Tendinitis|RLR 061]]). A repeatedly dry tap suggests the fluid is not where a joint effusion should be — reframe toward the neighboring structure rather than blaming technique.

## Workup and Management
- **Arthrocentesis is the pivotal test.** Send [[Synovial Fluid Analysis|synovial fluid]] for gross appearance, WBC and differential, crystals, and Gram stain/culture. No single cell count clinches a diagnosis, and **multiple processes coexist** — a positive crystal does not exclude infection ([[RLR 061 - Knee Swelling and Calcific Tendinitis|RLR 061]]).
- **Imaging** — plain films first; MRI when a periarticular or bony process (calcific tendinitis, osteomyelitis, AVN) is suspected ([[RLR 061 - Knee Swelling and Calcific Tendinitis|RLR 061]]).
- **Management follows the fluid** — drainage plus empiric antibiotics for suspected sepsis; NSAIDs, colchicine, or steroids for crystal disease once infection is addressed.
- **When the data and diagnosis disagree, reframe** using the [[Law of Proportionality]] — a non-inflammatory cell count cannot explain a severely painful joint.
