Evergreen concept note. The disciplined first step of neurologic reasoning — asking *where* the lesion is along the neuraxis before asking *what* it is. RLR / Clinical Problem Solvers teaching points are cited per episode; general clinical detail is scaffolding.

## The Localization Ladder
Every neurologic complaint can be assigned to one level of the nervous system, and the level constrains the differential.

- **The levels, rostral to caudal:** cortex → subcortical white matter → brainstem → spinal cord → nerve root → plexus → peripheral nerve → neuromuscular junction (NMJ) → muscle.
- **Work the level first.** Naming the level narrows an unwieldy differential to a manageable one and dictates which test to order next ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).

## "Localize to the Spine, Not the Spinal Cord"
A subtle but load-bearing habit: for a sensorimotor syndrome below the neck, say **"spine"** rather than "spinal cord" so the **nerve roots and cauda equina** never fall off your map ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).

- **Why it matters:** the spinal *column* houses both the cord and, below the conus, the roots of the cauda equina. Saying "cord" prematurely discards the root explanation — which in leptomeningeal or cauda-equina disease is the correct one ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).

## Sensory-Plus-Motor Excludes Muscle and NMJ
A single exam observation collapses the bottom of the ladder.

- **Muscle and NMJ are purely motor.** If a patient has *both* sensory and motor findings, the muscle and neuromuscular junction are out, leaving the cord, roots, plexus, and peripheral nerve ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).
- **Corollary:** pure painless weakness with normal sensation reopens muscle and NMJ (e.g., myopathy, myasthenia).

## Signs That Fix the Level
- **UMN vs LMN:** hyperreflexia, spasticity, and an upgoing toe point to cord/brain (upper motor neuron); hyporeflexia, atrophy, and fasciculations point to root/nerve (lower motor neuron). See [[Spine vs Peripheral Nerve]].
- **A sensory level** on the trunk localizes to the cord at that dermatome; **saddle sensory loss with sphincter involvement** points to the conus/cauda equina.
- **Cranial-nerve findings** move the lesion up to the brainstem, meninges, or skull base — facial sensation, for instance, is trigeminal and cannot arise from the cervical cord (see [[Trigeminal Nerve]]) ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).

## Localizing Under Imperfect Data
Real exams disagree. The disciplined move is to localize *anyway*, name the uncertainty, and return to the bedside to re-examine (sit the patient up for reflexes, hunt for a sensory level) rather than force a false unifier ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).
