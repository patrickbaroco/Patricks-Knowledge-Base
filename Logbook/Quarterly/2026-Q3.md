Q3 2026 · July through September

> [!note] Written 2026-10-01
> Built from the July and August monthlies, the W28–W39 weeklies, the September dailies, and Outlook Sent for 9/29–9/30. The September monthly, the W38 and W39 rollups and the 9/29–9/30 dailies were written later the same day.

## The quarter

I began the quarter as regional CMO for Virginia and ended it as chair of a structure that does not yet exist. Between those two points sit a signed role agreement, a transition executed in public on 8/12 and in fact on 8/31, three charters drafted in a week, a COO who said *"I love it,"* a CEO who raised no structural objection, and a design that kept getting smaller the closer it came to being real. In August the governance plan was three bodies with a seventeen-seat Council over them and a board resolution underneath. By 9/29 it was two committees authorized by the CEO, with the word "board" removed from every document. Each cut came from someone who would have to live inside the structure, and each one made it more likely to happen.

The old work kept arriving. Avardis terminated Pheasant Ridge the morning after a cordial sit-down with Ashley Winkle. A social services director asked a fair question about hospice referrals, and my answer became the fullest account I have written of how I think about them. A warfarin event at KD put an INR of 8.15 on the QAPI table. The medical directorships stayed mine, and the quarter showed me exactly where that line runs.

## July: the last month of the old job

July belonged to the regional CMO role. BRC and ANR were still working through a regulatory catch-up, Kissito was transitioning, and I was covering Kendal, Heritage Hall and Summit Square for Gavin's paternity leave on top of my own buildings. On 7/8 a competing group pitched itself as "small and local" against our "corporate, private equity" image, in what amounted to an interview, and the white paper I sketched in response that day stayed open until 9/5, the longest-lived item of the quarter.

The clinical cases that stayed with me were small failures of attention caught by someone paying attention. At BRC a TSH of 8 on a patient newly started on amiodarone was missed until Rachel Binda, PA, caught it; the repeat was 15, and the case became a handbook change on thyroid monitoring. At ANR a poorly worded email about DDNR consent unravelled a goals-of-care conversation with Julia Jarvis's daughter that had seemed settled, and exposed a social services department working past its remit. The two NP consult calls on 7/24, a tacrolimus toxicity and a probable vasculitis, were the conversations I enjoyed most all month.

The transition letter arrived the same day. The agreement was signed 7/28, which formally launched the [[Clinical Quality Framework]], and on 7/31 in Baltimore the compensation landed at or above current pay, with an annual equity share and a bonus. The Orioles won at Camden Yards that night. We flew to Belize the next morning.

## August: two jobs at once, then one

Belize was four days at El Pescador: Vincent in the copilot seat of the Tropic Air puddle jumper, grand slams for both of us on the first full day, his tarpon in the 50–60 lb range. Then the transition ran the whole month. The announcement on 8/12 drew no controversy, and what I wrote that night was relief. On 8/14 I built the new role in one sitting as five notes; on 8/16 I pulled all five back out because they assumed too much, and decided to rebuild one project at a time. Peer review picked itself as the first.

Jeff Wells met me on 8/20 eager to be a partner, and from 8/24 to 8/29 the drafting ran almost without stopping: the coordinator role and its resourcing case, the counsel memo, the board resolution, objections and rebuttals, three charters in a four-minute window on 8/28, the fair hearing procedure, and an executive summary at 12:48am on 8/29. The fact that unblocked all of it was simple: no privileges exist today, and the executive team supports creating them. On 8/31 the charters went to Cary at 10:44pm, a few hours after Jennie Byrne chaired her last CGSC and handed it to me, and after ANR's surveyor meeting went far better than the last one. That morning, before 8:10, I answered David Joyce's emergency on-call question at ANR, which I had misread the day before as Dr. Grumet's. The on-call process belongs to the medical director, and the directorships did not transfer with the CMO role.

## September: endorsed, then edited

Cary read the charters on 9/3 and endorsed them in an hour. What persuaded her was a graph: I own the antipsychotic and anxiolytic measures now, and September's number is the same as January's. She cut me off with *"You don't have to convince me,"* and asked for only one thing, that it not sound like another complicated thing Eventus won't follow through on. The governance visuals I built on Sunday 9/6, a seat map, a clinician path and a page on where existing work goes, were the answer to that request.

The gate then moved to Ben, slowly. Cary previewed the structure with him on 9/9, I waited five days for a readout, and on 9/15 Ben wrote asking to be taught. The read-in on 9/21 was forty minutes. He was fully supportive, took counsel and the corporate formalities himself, and gave me one assignment: put the structure on paper as it establishes privilege and delegated authority. Five weeks of waiting on other people's calendars became one document I could write.

Jen Rucci did most of the real editing, and every edit was a subtraction. On 9/11 she and I separated two audits that had been tangled together, a public, countable EQuIP measure and a reasoning-level CQC review, and corrective insulin became the measure because it is easy to count and impossible to pass without having looked at the resident. On 9/15 she found the conflict of interest I had built in: a governing council of operational leaders overseeing a peer review body designed to exclude them. On 9/18 the Council went from seventeen voting seats to nine, and Gavin Slitt's description of how his hospital's committee reviews its own cases let the PRC drop the separate reviewer pool it would have had to recruit. On 9/23 an HCQIA question made the Council unnecessary, and the 9/24 proposal dropped it, leaving Indiana's privilege statute as the one place a board-organized committee may still be required. By 9/28 both charters were rewritten for two committees under CEO authorization, and on 9/29 at 5:51pm I sent her the packet with the note that this could be very simple. The same session on 9/23 produced the quarter's two concrete approvals: recruit for the CQC, and use insulin as the EQuIP bonus measure. She briefs Ben today.

The CGSC became mine in practice on 9/14 and useful on 9/28, when I ran it on a single measure with numbers behind it: antipsychotic use in dementia, concentrated in about fifty buildings, ranked on count minus 0.167 × census so that large buildings with low rates fall off the list. The objections were about the denominator, which is the right kind of objection. Rucci closed it with *"we've made no progress, and it's almost October,"* and in the hour afterward told me all three 2026 company goals, antipsychotics, anxiolytics and ALF transitions of care, have been missed. She also dismantled the biggest outlier on my list in five minutes. Durham Ridge is a psychiatric building whose dementia diagnoses exist to satisfy a Medicaid rule, which is the strongest argument I have for never attributing a rate to a clinician.

## What the quarter showed

**The structure shrank as it became real.** The August design was rigorous and too large to seat. Seventeen seats became nine, then the Council disappeared; three-member panels became two; the board became the CEO. The people who cut it were the ones who would have to staff it, and their cuts all pointed at the same scarce resource: practising clinicians willing to sit on committees.

**The role was used before it was chartered.** Sandra Jurani turned a hospice signature I refused on 9/1 into a partnership within twelve minutes (*"consider me jockeying"*). Monica Leriger walked out of Office Hours and told me the new position was the right place to fix how MUO conversations reach clinicians. Cary asked for the handbook as the clinical resource for every NP and doctor in the group. FP&A turned out to have already built the [[Acuity-Based SNF Scheduling]] model I had shelved, and better than I would have. The BU microtransitions study went from a conversation on 9/2 to Saber's chain-level clearance on 9/18. The Pheasant Ridge loss on 9/2 became, by 9/25, a [[Weekend Coverage Pilot]] at ANR and King's Daughters with three named metrics. Much of the quarter's most useful work arrived from someone else.

**The boundary between the two jobs took three corrections to learn.** The Tompkins and Saval findings, the Cooke compliance report and Dr. Mitchell's PTO request all came to me shaped like the CMO role and all belonged to Dr. Grumet; each sat looking overdue until someone asked whose it was. The on-call question at ANR came shaped like hers and belonged to me. The quarter closed on the same line: Dr. Tompkins, whose open notes ran past a hundred in early September, resigned on 9/28 with them completed. I hate to see him leave, though I'm not surprised, and I'm very glad it is no longer my problem. It's Surah's.

**The log and the work came apart on the heaviest weeks.** Every drafting stretch emptied the dailies: eight backfilled in late August, four in W36, all seven in W37. On 9/8 I accepted it and made reconstruction the method. It works, and it costs the thing nothing else catches: three weeks running, the Sunday Week Ahead was built before its weekly existed. Todoist ran the same way, with almost nothing checked off during the most productive stretches and the backlog cleared in single passes afterward. The weekly split on 9/21 and the top-three convention on 9/16 are the two system changes that seem to be holding. The quarter's last two dailies were caught up on 10/1.

## Carrying into Q4

The quarter built the structure and had most of it approved. The next one has to show it working, which mostly means doing a small number of things in buildings.

1. **Seat the committees.** Rucci's 30-60-90 has everything running by year end. The open questions are Indiana's privilege statute and who sits on the CQC; recruiting is approved. A CQC that has met once by December is the test.
2. **Make antipsychotics the worked example.** The 2026 goal is missed, so the remaining months are for local intervention in the outlier buildings, after Lindbert answers where the diagnoses are pulled from and Freeburne and Hendress return their lists.
3. **Settle the insulin measure and start teaching.** The six-of-six question waits on Barb and the PIMS denominator. The 9/11 plan called for two months of diabetes education before launch, so a Q1 launch means teaching in November.
4. **Launch the weekend coverage pilot** at ANR and KD, measured on returns to hospital overall, returns Saturday through Tuesday, and Part A medication spend.
5. **Send the microtransitions letters.** The email to Drs. Miller, Jeffers, Fernandes and Mustaklem was drafted 9/23 and has not gone; the Saber leadership call is 10/6.
6. **Decide about the handbook export.** It was the most concrete thing Cary asked of me all quarter, and it ended the quarter untracked and owed.

At home: gully seeding 10/4 and the drainfield's second application, dormant seeding in November, the Snowshoe property tax by 10/31, both Fidelity packets mailed by mid-October with Allison's deferral election due 12/31, the start of ski season, and on December 7, fifty.
