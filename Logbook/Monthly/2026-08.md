The new role got its structure twice. On the evening of 8/14, off the back of the announcement, I stood up a **Chair of Clinical Excellence** umbrella note plus a Cadence Map, a Portfolio of candidate initiatives, a Clinical Quality Council note, and a Peer Review Council note — five drafts in one sitting. On Sunday 8/16 I pulled all five back out of the vault and parked them. Not an abandonment: they made too many assumptions to build on, and the plan instead was to rebuild them as real projects, one at a time. That decision is the hinge of the whole second half of the month, because it created the question of what gets rebuilt first, and peer review answered it by picking itself.

Peer review became rebuild #1 and then became most of August. I scheduled the meeting with Jeff Wells (compliance officer / in-house counsel) on 8/18 and wrote the primer to prepare for it the same day — foundations, legal architecture (HCQIA, NPDB, state privilege statutes), and program design, written for a physician chairing a program with no prior legal training — with final edits on 8/19. The meeting on 8/20 went about as well as it could have: he is eager to expand the process and eager to be a partner in it, which is close to the best possible starting position, since the legal architecture is exactly what the program is gated on. On 8/21 [[Peer Review]] became a real project note with a single gating task — Jeff's written answer on privilege by state, due 8/24 — alongside a PSO primer and a five-state privilege comparison, and a current-state assessment followed on 8/22. From 8/24 the drafting ran continuously: the Executive Coordinator role spec, the coordinator role proposal and the resourcing case that reframe Lindsey from a personnel ask into a program requirement (records discipline *is* the privilege architecture, not administrative support around it), and the counsel engagement memo at 11:09pm. Two structural decisions carry 8/25 — the primary care and psych committees are not peer review bodies, and the CQC sits outside the PSES. 8/26 was the design day: single-entity corporate structure confirmed across all five states, CGC composition resolved, the board framing switched from patient safety to exit diligence, the standards architecture settled (minimum expectation versus improvement target, the economic-credentialing rule rewritten, *"selection is not a finding"* added after stress-testing an antipsychotic-in-dementia scenario, *"the standard of care is not displaced"*), cadence decided, the records custodian made a chartered position — and the answer that unblocked everything, that no privileges exist today and the executive team supports creating them. The board resolution v0.1 landed 8/27, and 8/28 produced six documents: the objections and rebuttals brief v0.2, the coordinator duties tied line-by-line to charter citations, the three charters in a four-minute window at 5:12–5:16pm, and the fair hearing procedure plus a full project-brief rewrite at 10:03pm. The executive summary closed the package at 12:48am on 8/29. Then the review cycle started: on 8/30 an external governance review of the PRC charter raised eleven numbered points and two cleanup items — nine accepted as stated, two accepted with a different remedy, one only half right — plus three further defects found during verification, of which the § 4.7 audit-bypass exposure now addressed at § 8.1.2(d) carries more live risk than anything on the original list; the biggest structural fix makes the Panel investigative and proposing, with the Committee adopting or rejecting by vote. On 8/31 I opened the impairment question with Kristen Medlin, because the charter needs a destination for it that isn't peer review — her answer routes it to the Drug and Alcohol Policy, and the harder cognitive-impairment case to Accommodations and possibly Leave of Absence, with HR working through the practitioner's own provider on fitness for duty. That's the named external destination PRC § 4.3(d) had only been pointing at generically. The Council Charter review the same afternoon worked the seams between documents rather than defects inside them, and at 10:44pm the three charters went to Cary, just under the wire, with the caveat about how much of the drafting leaned on Claude and ChatGPT plus podcasts and legal articles. Much of the 8/24–8/29 detail above is reconstructed rather than contemporaneous — those dailies were backfilled on 8/29 from file timestamps and the documents' own internal dates, so the sequence is solid and the texture of those days isn't.
