## Monthly Rollup

October was the month the governance structure began acquiring members and a budget, and the month the microtransitions study lost two of the three partners it started with. It ended on a retention case that tested the new roles before either committee had met.

**The first days put the plan in front of the regional leaders.** The Regional CMO Sync heard the governance plan on Thursday 10/1 and received it well, and Dirk followed with a name for the distinguished physician seat, Dr. Sarah Cunningham. Will agreed to an education plan, the piece the insulin measure needs before it launches. On Friday I asked Barb Pereira for an overtreatment component, since facilities with nobody on corrective insulin pass automatically: A1c below 7.0% on any insulin or sulfonylurea, met by acknowledgment plus a change or a stated reason to continue. The Fidelity identity call unblocked Allison's Self-Employed 401(k). Sunday 10/4 was the handbook's biggest day in months: Diabetes in LTC rewritten at twice its length and all nine regulatory and orientation chapters taken from stub to draft. Mom and Dad brought a table they had made for Vincent's aquarium.

**The second week the documents reached Ben and the study lost Saber.** Rucci's final pass on Monday 10/5 produced a lateralized seat map and a one-page review flow by 5:50pm. Overtreatment was accepted as EQuIP's second diabetes component, and she wants all four quarters named at the December announcement. On Tuesday Dr. Istenes declined for every Saber community and asked that the study not come to any of their facilities, which removed most of the proposed Virginia sites. The ACO chapter went to Barb and Jurani on Thursday for its first outside read. Byrne took on the antipsychotic and anxiolytic standards through the BH committee and proposed its membership with herself as chair. Friday the respiratory memo lost molnupiravir and narrowed nirmatrelvir-ritonavir to the highest-risk patients, and Ben asked Patel and me to sponsor primary care for Athena's February 16 go-live. Vincent and I fished the Elk at Slatyfork without much to show for it.

**The third week was documentation policy from two sides.** On 10/12 the finance system was rebuilt around a $3,500 MES carry, three funds in place of the $75,000 target and a $400 home-maintenance set-aside from November, and the gully was seeded inside its deadline. On Thursday 10/15 Colvin circulated a tiered escalation procedure for incomplete notes. I worked it against the charters: the PRC takes up documentation delinquency only on a patient-welfare nexus or Management's referral. The same evening Cary asked whether the clinical-model assessment should assume two APC visits a month per long-stay patient instead of 1.5. I said two; Emily Johnson at BRC averages 2.97 per consented patient. Friday cleared a backlog in half an hour: the Athena sponsorship answered, the Leader Forum RSVP, the governance Teams page, the Fidelity Solo 401(k) opened and the $3,600 sweep. Kelly Anderson confirmed EQuIP falls under her. Saturday was Dad's birthday. The Sunday review did not run, and the W42 Handoff was written three days late.

**The fourth week turned three stuck threads.** The late-notes problem reached Cary on 10/19 as a tiered quarterly on-time bonus beside the 50% cut. The influenza outbreak ran as a small natural experiment: ANR prophylaxed the whole building and the spread stopped; Shenandoah did not, and it spread widely. The study settled on Kendal, BRC and KD at the 10/21 debrief and lost KD on 10/23 to its open plans of correction. Kelly Anderson backed the diabetes measure and Josh Gilpatrick confirmed the patients behind it. The signature-standards audit turned out to have added ACP without telling anyone, and Jamie Smith supports the transition away from it. The Athena essay went to Cary for the all-company newsletter. On 10/25 the Senate Finance RFI on long-term care reform opened as a project, due 12/18. Marcus ran the state championship in Mechanicsville and finished mid-pack.

**The last week belonged to Dr. Mitchell.** He had resigned, and on Wednesday I went through his concerns one by one. Eventus had already recognized every one and was making progress on all of them, which left a $20,000 retention bonus. Ben approved it by Friday morning, paid over 24 months, four structures were on his desk by 10:00, and he agreed to the shape of it. Rucci asked that Dr. Grumet take it over; I disagreed in writing and brought her in anyway, and by evening it was a joint effort and Mitchell was agreeable to a new contract. Colvin proposed an EQuIP multiplier in place of the late-note penalty. All three QAPIs ran. Four linked charter revisions settled who votes and who screens, and *Committee Time and Cost* answered Rucci at about 46–48 budgeted hours a month, roughly $44,000–47,000 a year; she chose the costlier model. Mary Linton Kops and Kat McDougal took CQC seats. Two clinical questions arriving through the pharmacist inbox became [[Clinical Questions Pathway]].

**What did not move.** The Kendal and BRC email for the study carried from 10/21 through month's end; it went to Kendal and Heritage Hall on 11/9. The Byrne and Drawe replies and HCC Module 10 crossed into November overdue. The record slipped twice: the W42 Handoff was late and its Rollup is still pending, and the Week Ahead fired provisionally both times.
