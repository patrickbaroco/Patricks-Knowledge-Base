## Monthly Rollup

September was the month the governance structure went in front of everyone who had to approve it, and came back smaller each time. It started as three bodies under a seventeen-seat Council and a board resolution, went to Cary on 8/31, to Rucci on 9/11, to Ben on 9/21, and on 9/29 went back to Rucci as two committees authorized by the CEO, with the board written out of every document. Every cut came from someone who would have to staff it. The other thing the month showed is that the new role was being used before it was chartered: Sandra, Monica Leriger, Colvin, Ashley Winkle and Cary each brought it a problem nobody had assigned to it.

**The first week turned three conversations into three projects.** On Tuesday 9/1 I signed two hospice documents for a patient I had seen and refused the rest, since I am neither medical director nor attending at Lexington, then re-sent the refusal at 3:37pm with Sandra Jurani copied. Twelve minutes later: *"So I'm fainting with joy at your perspective here… consider me jockeying."* Thursday's meeting with her produced [[Hospice Utilization]]: the same two high certifiers every month, patients on hospice for years, and the mechanism behind it, which is that the hospice lists its own medical director as attending and we never see another piece of paperwork. Length of stay became the metric, she took data and I took structure and education. On Wednesday 9/2 at 10:11am Avardis terminated Pheasant Ridge, one day after a cordial sit-down with Ashley Winkle, and within four hours Cary was asking whether we need seven-day coverage. That evening the BU microtransitions study became a commitment: a randomized trial of the MASS tool in twelve of our SNFs, introduced through QAPI, with naming the facilities on me.

**Cary endorsed the structure on Thursday 9/3, and a graph did it.** I told her I now own the antipsychotic and anxiolytic measures and that September's number is January's. *"You don't have to convince me. I'm in the exact same place as you."* She had stopped going to the CGSC herself. Scope settled in the same hour: note closures in, ACO measure disengagement in, chasing ACO visits for unassigned patients out, Jeff's billing and coding left exactly where it is, signature standards audits eliminated. Her one request was that it not sound like another complicated thing Eventus won't follow through on. Then she asked for the [[Clinical Handbook]] as the resource for every NP and doctor, the ground truth for Wednesday didactics, and the basis of new hire orientation, and I gave her the Virginia APC poll: nineteen respondents, three knew where the clinical pathways were, one had read more than one. On Friday FP&A showed me the [[Acuity-Based SNF Scheduling]] model I had shelved, already built and better than mine: 103% on the old dashboard, 75% on the new, with short stay as the place we are failing. Both my corrections went to Dan. On Sunday 9/6 at the in-laws' I built the governance visuals in under an hour, the seat map, the clinician path and where existing work goes, and emailed them to Cary from an airport on Labor Day at 5:43pm, two days ahead of her preview with Ben. The Universal Health white paper, open since 7/8, went out on 9/5, and so did the Executive Coordinator resourcing case to Lindsey.

**The second week was spent waiting on Ben and building the machinery anyway.** The 9/8 review accepted that my dailies vanish on drafting weeks and made reconstruction the method, killed the CCRC memo, closed [[Role Transition]], and split the handbook. On 9/9 Peer Review became [[Clinical Governance]], since the container had outgrown the name of one of its parts, and the [[Microtransitions Study]] opened with the nomination email to the four RDCOEs out at 4:06pm. Cary previewed the structure with Ben that day and I heard nothing until Friday evening's *"I will have an update on Monday."* Thursday evening produced the longest drafting run since the charters: an audit referral policy in nine articles, a standards register, both member guides, and nine defects in the CQC Charter found by reading it against the other two, including citations to a § 4.2(b) that does not exist. On Friday 9/11, in an hour with Rucci, the quality program split into two audits: a public, countable EQUiP measure that a bonus can rest on, and a CQC audit of clinical reasoning. Corrective insulin became the measure because it can be scored across every provider and cannot be passed without looking at what the resident received and stating a plan. The reviewer does not judge the plan. The eight-document packet went to her that afternoon. Colvin's VMRC question got a medical director's answer: for that size of building and that short-stay mix, VMRC is understaffed independent of our model, and it works only because it is well run internally.

**The third week the gate moved, and the hospice question got its fullest answer.** On Monday 9/14 I chaired my first CGSC, where Heather Drawe's Psychotherapy CCM turned into a discussion of acuity-based scheduling for psychotherapy, and sent the new biweekly series from my own calendar. Cary's readout came at 6:14pm as one sentence: Ben would reach out directly. Ashley Winkle, back in the country, spent most of our call on weekend coverage. She is hard over on it, has no plans to term our buildings (*"keep Alyssa happy and I'm happy"*), and will compare weekend outcomes at covered and uncovered facilities; I volunteered to pilot. Jennie's dementia-over-65 filter and Rucci's denominator objection turned the [[AP-AA Cut]] into a methods discussion before 10am, and I set out the score: a 100-bed building with ten residents on an antipsychotic scores −7 and drops off; with thirty it scores 13. On Tuesday Theresa Simmons asked for social services to be told before any hospice referral, after Mr. Campos's daughter came to her office. I answered the whole question. A referral is multidisciplinary, and the facility needs room to say the timing is wrong; on the clinician side it is a decision only an MD, NP or PA can make; most patients at the end of life do not need hospice, and the benefit has to justify its cost through symptom complexity overnight and on weekends, family complexity, and nursing need, in that order. The patient stays mine. I trust two agencies and tell families the choice is theirs. Within the hour the facts came back in the clinicians' favour, Michelle Bays wrote *"you were spot on with everything you said,"* and on Wednesday Theresa apologised for a tone I had not taken from her. Ben asked on 9/15 to be taught. Rucci found the conflict of interest I had built, operational leaders overseeing a peer review body designed to exclude them, and the answer was operations represented by Cary, plus the CMO, in place of every RCMO and RDCOE. On Friday 9/18 she endorsed the plan and booked Part 2 before I left the call. Gavin Slitt's account of his hospital's committee, where members review the cases themselves two to a case, let the PRC drop the separate reviewer pool it would have had to recruit, and that afternoon all three charters came down around the same finding: the Council from seventeen voting seats to nine, PRC panels from three to two, a physician floor of three. Dr. Istenes cleared the microtransitions study at the chain level the same day, with three conditions I can answer. I bowed out of the LTC ACO Summit on 9/16.

**The fourth week the structure went to the CEO and lost a committee.** Ben's read-in on Monday 9/21 took forty minutes. He was fully supportive of all three bodies, took the corporate documents, the D&O policy and the malpractice scheme himself, took counsel out of my queue, cautioned only against meeting proliferation, and gave me one assignment: put the structure on paper as it establishes privilege and delegated authority. He also suggested extending an initiative's definition of done to each provider teaching it in their own building. On Wednesday 9/23 Clinical Quality Part 2 produced the month's two approvals, recruiting for the CQC and corrective insulin as the EQuIP bonus measure. That evening's exchange with Rucci did the rest. HCQIA needs restrictions on practice to be the healthcare entity's own action, and nothing evident requires that authority to pass through a council, so the Council can go; Indiana, where privilege attaches to committees organized by the governing body, is the one exception pending counsel. I ended the thread by admitting I know hospital governance well and private equity governance hardly at all. The B1/B2 two-committee proposal went to her on Thursday with a rollout timeline that has everything running by year end. Thursday's KD QAPI raised a warfarin event: INR about 6 on 9/17, the planned hold never entered, warfarin given 9/20 through 9/22, INR 8.15 and an upper GI bleed. Friday's call with Rahul Kapoor and Steve Colvin confirmed the [[Weekend Coverage Pilot]] at ANR and King's Daughters, one provider working a defined list (late-Friday admissions, discharge medication reconciliation, residents at high risk of return), with 7-on/7-off rejected and three metrics: return to hospital overall, return Saturday through Tuesday, and Part A medication spend. The same day Lindbert's rerun on residents 65 and older with dementia let me send Y/N target lists to all four RDCOEs and the *AP usage* workbook to Rucci, Byrne, Patel and Lindbert, with my reading that the problem needs universal education and targeted intervention.

**The last three days put numbers in front of a committee.** On Monday 9/28 I ran the CGSC on antipsychotic use in dementia. The framing went unchallenged and the objections went to the denominator, which is where they belong. Rucci closed with *"we've made no progress, and it's almost October,"* told me afterward that all three 2026 company goals, antipsychotics, anxiolytics and ALF transitions of care, have been missed, and in five minutes took apart the largest outlier on my list: Durham Ridge is a psychiatric building whose dementia diagnoses exist to keep it clear of IMD status. That is the clearest argument I have for never attributing a rate to a clinician. The same morning I admitted two residents from assisted living who had run out of money, one on quetiapine without a clear indication, which comes off. Both charters were rewritten for two committees under CEO authorization, the three-body versions archived, and the CQC Agenda Queue opened with colorectal screening as its first entry. On Tuesday 9/29 Dr. Grumet asked the medical directors to hand QAPI scheduling and paperwork collection to the physician services coordinator. I told Rucci that at my well-run facilities this is a solution in search of a problem and at my less well-run ones a problem-generator, and asked that my two Avardis buildings be left out of it; on Wednesday morning I asked Surah to talk before it rolls out. At Wednesday's ANR QAPI, with Stephen Carl from Avardis in the room, I announced the weekend coverage plan, and it was well received. At 5:51pm the packet went to Rucci: *"The CEO authorization here is just an example, obviously. I just wanted to make the point that this could be very simple."* At 10pm I answered Jennie's psychiatrist team on the monthly note reviews with the clearest version yet of rates and rules: a facility rate is a signal to look, a rule is a standard a chart either meets or does not, and a supervising physician who finds the standard met can still say *"Good job, you met the standard. But let me share a few thoughts about quetiapine."* Rucci's comments were due Wednesday, and she briefs Ben on 10/1.

**Medical director work ran alongside, mostly on schedule.** Rounding held to the Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday pattern except for Labor Day. Charta came back at 100% in W37 and the Signature Standards review at 100% on 9/15. Clint Merritt took Augusta Health's refusal of sleep studies for KD and Shenandoah to the right person within a day. Routing to Dr. Grumet happened three more times, for the Cooke compliance report, a Tompkins audit follow-up, and Dr. Mitchell's PTO request. On 9/28 Dr. Tompkins resigned, with his outstanding notes completed. I hate to see him leave, though I'm not surprised, and I'm very glad it is no longer my problem. It's Surah's.

**Home ran on meal prep, the meadow and the boys.** We flew to the in-laws' on 9/4 and spent two hours on the tarmac waiting out lightning, and two more on the way home on Labor Day. Marcus ran on 9/8; in Salem on 9/12 Vincent finished 23rd of about 250 with a medal and Marcus ran in the top half, followed by pizza and *Paddington*. Cindi and Ryan came for dinner with my parents on 9/19 after Common Wealth Crush and Lightwell Survey, with a mezze table prepped the night before, and it went really well. On 9/26 we were at Centennial Park for Vincent's invitational, and on 9/27 he and I caught a few tiny fallfish.
