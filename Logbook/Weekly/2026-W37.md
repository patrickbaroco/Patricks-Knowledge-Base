**Friday's hour with Rucci produced the decision the whole program turns on: there are two kinds of audit, and they should not be the same audit.** One is public and general, the EQUiP bonus, which has to be easy to count and comparable across every provider because a bonus depends on comparing people. The other is the CQC audit, which is not looking at a number at all; it is looking at clinical reasoning. The point of keeping them separate is to align them, so the bonus measure pulls in the same direction as the committee rather than teaching people to chase a value while the committee teaches them to think. **That is why the measure is corrective insulin.** Countable enough to run at scale, and still impossible to pass without having looked at what the resident actually received and said what you intend to do about it. Vetted with Barb Pereira (charts are easy to pull; PIMS has many components, so no over-weighting), Colvin, and two of my NPs. Drafted at 4:20, sent to Rucci at 4:46: five charts per clinician per cycle from a list the reviewer doesn't choose, pass is amount received plus a plan, **the reviewer does not judge the plan**, APCs reviewed by their supervising physician, physicians by their regional CMO, no new role, no new staffing, about two hours a quarter for someone with six APCs. If it's chosen, announce early and spend two months on diabetes education first.

**The packet went to Rucci the same afternoon, the first time the structure has gone to anyone as something readable.** Eight documents: Start Here, Framework Overview, Seat Map, What Happens to a Clinician, Where the Existing Work Goes, How a Quality Audit Would Work, and two member guides. The cover email did the framing in four paragraphs and carried the line that matters most to an operator reading it cold: these are not audits of every clinician; they look for meaningful deficits and correct them through education, with intermediate steps before the HR route. The member guides and the audit machinery came out of Thursday evening, the longest governance drafting run since the charters themselves: an audit referral policy in nine articles, a standards register separated out so the Council isn't ratifying every standards revision, and, because checking the Policy against all three charters forced a read of them against each other, **nine defects in the CQC Charter**, two of which cited a § 4.2(b) that does not exist.
