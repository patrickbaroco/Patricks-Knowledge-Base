On Tuesday Ben wrote asking me to teach him what I'm preparing, and by Wednesday morning Shanda had him on my calendar for 9/21.

**Ashley Winkle said what the Avardis thread had been circling since Pheasant Ridge.** Back in the country and on the phone Monday, she is hard over on weekend coverage. She knows nothing happens overnight, has no plans to term our buildings (*"keep Alyssa happy and I'm happy"*), and will compare weekend outcomes at covered and uncovered facilities. She values weekday continuity, dislikes 7-on/7-off, and wants someone rounding and talking to families on weekends. I took it to Dr. Patel and volunteered to pilot at two of her buildings here, where the relationships would give us grace. Tuesday Rachel Wilson, Steve Colvin and I spent a long time on coverage at the Avardis facilities.

**Rucci read the packet and argued with it, which is what it was for.** Tuesday afternoon she called it fantastic, had it condensed into a deck for the exec team, and asked whether seventeen seats on the governing body was too many. Then she found the real problem: a council of operational leaders overseeing a peer review body built to exclude them brings the conflict of interest straight back. Operations represented by Cary or a delegate, plus the CMO, solved it. Her questions on the insulin measure the same day (six charts rather than five, so it can replace the signature standards at two a month; the pass/fail math; psychiatric APCs) waited for Friday. At Friday's Quality Program Review she endorsed the plan and booked Part 2 for Wednesday before I had left the call. That afternoon Gavin Slitt described how his hospital's peer review committee reviews its own cases, two members to a case, and all three charters came down around one finding, that practising clinicians are the scarce thing: the Council from seventeen voting seats to nine, PRC panels from three to two with the physician floor raised to three, and the CQC's psychotherapist seat traded for consultation.

**The hospice thread was the fullest statement I've made of how I think about referrals.** Tuesday afternoon Theresa Simmons, ANR's social services director, asked that social services be told before any hospice referral, after Mr. Campos's daughter came to her office. I answered the whole question. A referral should be multidisciplinary, because payor, therapy, paperwork and timing all bear on whether hospice is right at that moment. On the clinician side it is a decision only an MD, NP or PA can make, and one I certify. Most patients at the end of life don't need hospice; the benefit has to justify its cost through symptom complexity overnight and on weekends, family complexity, and nursing need, in that order. The patient stays mine and Crystal's. I trust two agencies, disclose my Legacy role every time, and tell families the choice is theirs. And I asked for a meeting in place of a thread. Within the hour Rebecca Tiger, Crystal and Michelle Bays had shown the referral was handled properly (*"Dr. Baroco, you were spot on with everything you said"*), and on Wednesday Theresa apologised for a tone I hadn't taken from her.

**Microtransitions cleared the chain.** Wednesday before eight the emails went to Dr. Istenes and to the RDCOEs: twelve facilities needed, five or six from Virginia. Freeburne answered Thursday with four, Woodcrest and Maple Grove under Dr. Lisa Miller and Creekside and Hopewell Grove under Dr. Jeffers. Friday at 2:01pm Istenes cleared the study for Saber with three conditions: the MASS form must not duplicate what staff already do on return from a transition, it must not add survey risk, and divisional nurses help choose facilities. I introduced Liz Nemecek to Ben Canter that afternoon.

**The AP/AA cut became a methods question.** Monday morning Jennie explained the dementia-over-65 filter: some ALFs mostly serve residents with serious mental illness, where aggressive dose reductions are dangerous. I set out the score, total AP minus 0.167 × census, under which a 100-bed building with ten residents on an antipsychotic scores −7 and drops off the list and one with thirty scores 13. Rucci added the denominator problem in buildings with an outside PCP. On Thursday Lindbert said he could rank on it.
