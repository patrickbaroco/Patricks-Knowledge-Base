**Chair Leadership cadence, 10:00.** Sandeep, Jennie, Matt and Rucci; Wispr Flow notes. Sandeep's question was where primary care's job ends and mine begins, since both role specs talk about setting clinical priorities (diabetes, major depressive disorder). My answer: the quality committee does two things. It measures and audits against whatever standards are set, and it is the one forum where primary care, psychiatry, OCH, operations and education settle priorities together and build one education plan around them. The standards themselves get written in the primary care and psychiatry committees, where the subject-matter experts sit. Rucci restated it as three branches: the chairs legislate, operations executes, quality is judicial. The flow runs both ways. If analytics cannot measure a proposed heart failure standard, it goes back to primary care to be made measurable. Top-down asks, such as an MUO worried about anxiolytic rates, come through quality, which decides how much psychiatry owns and how much primary care does. Jennie expects 6 to 12 months of redirecting people (pharmacy, education) who are used to going straight to a leader. Sandeep's worry is that BD and ops have always treated every MUO request as an emergency ahead of what clinicians need at the bedside; the committee votes, and they make their case to it. Jennie credited the outlier-first approach from the antipsychotic data: check whether a problem is broad or one building before changing everything.
