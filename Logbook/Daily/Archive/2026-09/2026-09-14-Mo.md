**The AP/AA thread turned into a methods discussion before 10am.** Jennie at 9:22am, direct to me: the data has been filtered to dementia over age 65 because a number of the ALFs serve primarily residents with serious mental illness, where high antipsychotic use is expected and aggressive GDRs are dangerous; an APC recently destabilized a building doing exactly that. Coded diagnoses are not accurate enough to filter those patients out, so dementia over 65 is the best proxy, and she would not run the outlier strategy on the overall set. She asked Jacob for the same view on that subset; he said it is already under section 5. I answered at 9:41: use whatever population makes the most sense, and the premise probably holds after the filter, because the volume sits in the NFs with more beds regardless of rate. The (total AP) − (0.167 × census) score is the way to account for a large building with a high count and a low percentage: a 100-bed facility with 10 residents on an antipsychotic scores −7 and drops off the list; with 30 it scores 13. Find the facilities with the biggest gap and intervene locally before the end of the year; anything that slipped through the filter gets skipped by hand. Rucci at 11:55am added the denominator problem: in MH-only buildings with an outside PCP, and in integrated buildings we share with another PCP, only referred patients are in the denominator while every AP user is in the numerator, so the rate is inflated. She recommends separating out the MH-only buildings, which would also remove most of the low-census ones.
