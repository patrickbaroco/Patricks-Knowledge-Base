**Work**

**The hour with Rucci at noon produced the decision the whole quality program turns on: there are two kinds of audit, and they should not be the same audit.**

One is **public and general** — the EQUiP quality bonus. It has to be easy to measure and trackable across every provider, because the bonus depends on comparing people. The other is the **CQC audit**, which is not looking at a number at all; it is looking at clinical reasoning. Keeping them separate is not a concession. **The goal is to align the public one with the CQC's goal structure so that the bonus measure pulls in the same direction**, rather than having the bonus teach people to chase a number while the committee teaches them to think.

**That is why the measure is corrective insulin.** It is countable enough to administer at scale and still samples reasoning, because what passes is not a value but whether the clinician looked at what the resident is actually receiving and said what they intend to do about it.

**Vetted before it went anywhere.** Barb Pereira — charts are not hard to pull, and she was enthusiastic; asked directly whether it over-weights insulin given SSI already sits inside PIMS, she said no, PIMS has many components. Steve Colvin, supportive. Two of my NPs, reasonable and achievable.

**Drafted at 4:20** as `equip-corrective-insulin-measure.md`, then **sent to Rucci at 4:46.** Five charts per clinician per cycle, taken in order from a list the reviewer doesn't choose. Pass is documenting the amount actually received plus a plan; **the reviewer does not judge the plan** — too many physicians reviewing, too much inter-operator variability to pretend otherwise. APCs reviewed by their supervising physician, physicians by their regional CMO, **no new role and no new staffing.** About 20 minutes a clinician, ~2 hours a quarter for someone with six APCs.

The case I made: it samples reasoning the way my heart-failure example did, it is a live priority for Saber and ALG, it is Beers / Choosing Wisely / endocrine-society backed, better diabetes management aligns with the ACO, and insulin misadventure is a common cause of ED transfer. Right now most notes read *"on Lantus 10 units, metformin 500 BID, and corrective insulin, adequately controlled, continue current regimen"* — with no indication whether the resident is getting 0 units a day or 40. **If it's chosen, announce early and spend two months on in-depth diabetes education first.** And the ask rode along: a route for a clinician to refer a peer to the quality committee for education and monitoring.

**The packet went to Rucci at 2:42 — the first time the structure has gone to anyone as something readable.**

The cover email did the framing in four paragraphs — the quality committee sets enterprise-wide standards, teaches them and audits toward them; standard-of-care determinations stay behind the privileged wall of peer review, *"something that I imagine would make Jeff happy"*; peer review dispositions almost everything as education or ongoing chart review, with restriction of practice as the rare case that carries the teeth; the Council owns final decisions and the board interface. And the line that matters most to an operator reading it cold: **these are not audits of every clinician, they look for meaningful quality deficits and correct them through education, with intermediate steps of enforcement before the HR route.**
