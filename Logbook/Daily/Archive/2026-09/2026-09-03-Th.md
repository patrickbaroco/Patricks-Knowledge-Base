**Met with Sandra Jurani (ACO lead).** Full capture in [[ACO Clinical Alignment — Sandra Jurani, 2026-09-03]]. This was the meeting where the ACO data function and the clinical quality mandate finally pointed at the same thing.

**Cary green-lit it before I ever got there.** Sandra has been beating this drum for years — *when do we actually use ACO data to decide clinical initiatives* — and kept getting deferred. She raised it with Cary this morning as *Dr. Baroco is into this now, can we move forward,* and Cary said **"I'm into it too. If you guys can come up with a plan, by all means do so."** So the constraint on the quality framework moved from permission to plan, four days before the 9/7 hold on [[Clinical Quality Framework]] expires. That's good timing rather than a broken hold.

**The framework argument.** I laid out the version I've been carrying: we do a lot of things, we kind of do a lot of things, we don't really do a lot of things. Initiatives anchored to ACO and enterprise-accepted measures; education folded into the didactics people already attend rather than standalone ACO sessions nobody wants to sit through; clinical pathways carrying the measures instead of disappearing into the wiki; systematic audits closing the loop back onto the initiatives, the education, and the measures themselves. And scrap the signature standard audits — they aren't meaningful. Athena widens what we can measure; until then aim at what's easy to measure now.

**The physician accountability gap is the real finding.** Everything currently lands on the APCs. Patel's framing — physician guides, APC executes — gets read by some docs as *all I have to write is goal A1c less than nine.* Physicians own the outcome, not just the plan, and audit feedback meetings need to include the physician and not only the APC. Sandra's read is fair and uncomfortable: they get crunchy when directed **because we never set an expectation of their involvement.** Setting it is the deliverable; peer review is where it goes when setting it doesn't work.

**Peer review landed better than expected.** I raised the committee unprompted — the argument that today we can only ask nicely, ask firmly, or terminate, and we need something with teeth, and they don't need to be big teeth. Sandra's team would be *ecstatic*. And **her team already keeps an informal list of physicians they have trouble with, and she offered it.** I'm not glad that list exists, but I'm glad I heard about it — it's the clearest outside evidence yet that the program is answering something real. Handle it carefully though: unprivileged, informal, predates the charters. That's a Jeff Wells question, not an enthusiasm question. She also raised the incentive structure as an intermediate lever, which the charters don't currently contemplate. Logged to [[Peer Review]].

**Hospice is the first concrete initiative — new project at [[Hospice Utilization]].** This is the first year we've hit every ACO quality measure, which is the signal to move past the gimmes; Sandra named hospice utilization and inpatient readmissions as what's next, and wants hospice physician-driven because she can see it in the data and can't diagnose it. Her monthly view: the same two high certifiers every month, disproportionate census across disproportionate facilities, patients on hospice for years.

I walked her through the mechanism, which is the part nobody at the ACO had. Medicare requires the hospice to ask the family who they want as attending; it isn't enforced; so the hospice lists its own medical director, that medical director signs the initial cert, the record says no primary care provider, and **we never see another piece of hospice paperwork.** Predatory outfits accelerate it by working the facility and calling families directly. The fix isn't less hospice, it's less bad hospice — good partners get more referrals, and a reputable hospice wants short lengths of stay anyway, so it's a genuine win-win. Length of stay, not volume, is the metric. And the ownership split: **the APC is the attending and works the census with the hospice nurse; the physician owns the relationship, not the patients** — Sandra had proposed physician ownership on the logic that they sign the certs, but in AL the doc is there once a quarter.

**Division of labor, agreed:** I take structure and education, Sandra takes data. She's putting me on the monthly ACO cadence and will pull custom data on request. Her closing ask — what data, what cadence, what support — is the one thing she's waiting on, and it's the next action on the hospice project.

**The Council explanation finally landed** — peer review produces findings and a recommendation, is deliberately staffed without supervisors so it's answering a purely clinical question, and therefore *somebody has to approve that recommendation*. **Her best question: isn't peer review worse than HR?** NPDB follows you; quitting Eventus doesn't.
