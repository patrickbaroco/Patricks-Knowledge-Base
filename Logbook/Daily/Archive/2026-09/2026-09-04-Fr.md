**Work**

**10am with Cary Trainor.** Full capture in [[Cary Trainor Connect — Operating to the Clinical Model & CSS, 2026-09-04]]. Two agenda items and two side items, all substantive.

**The acuity project I shelved in August has been built by FP&A.** Logged to [[Acuity-Based SNF Scheduling]], now back to active. The shelved on-deck task was *define the current scheduling process* — that is answered from the inside now. Scheduling runs on rules of thumb that ignore short-stay mix and happen to average out to the clinical model, which is why the gap was invisible. The new facility-level view applies the model's own visit rules to each building's actual mix: **103% on the old dashboard, 75% on the new one.**

I contributed the two corrections and both were accepted. **The model counts APC days only** — ANR reads 3.75 days against 5.5 modeled but is covered five, Crystal four and me the fifth; Cary said twice that this matters and it's going to Dan. And **a physician day is not a fungible APC day**: in a low-short-stay building the doc is doing what the NP does and they're interchangeable, but where short stay is high the physician is an add-on, not a backfill. Short-stay mix is being added as a displayed dimension for exactly that call.

The next step on my side is **separating short stay from long stay** — the model already derives from short-stay mix but reports one blended presence-days number per building, and that blend is what hides everything below.

**The finding worth carrying past the model: short stay is where we're failing.** Cary confirmed from the underlying memo that short-stay quality measures are poor and worse in the under-scheduled, low-fidelity buildings. That connects straight to [[Clinical Quality Framework]] — we train to the long stay, and the short-stay skill set is the hospital-medicine one nobody's comfortable with, which is the whole reason I say heart failure over and over. Logged there too.

On medical necessity, which was Cary's actual worry: not in SNFs. The exposure is in **assisted living**, where acuity runs from Brookdale buildings that are effectively SNFs to CCRCs — the Bridgewater AL patient I still can't catch for an ACO visit because he drives to his hunting lodge every day. The answer to both her questions is individual-level risk stratification, which is now a Steve conversation to restart.

**CSS — the answer is to split the role in two.** Not opening a project note for this; it isn't mine, I was opining. Athena removes most of what a CSS does today, so the question is what the job is now. Cary's reframe is right: start from what **every** patient is owed regardless of who treats them — consent, face sheet, complete and accurate record with current meds — which produces facility-level assignment rather than provider-level. I had no counter; Valley Family and Elder Care did exactly this and it's where Steve's Care Navigator idea came from. Today some buildings have four CSSs walking into them.

What I pushed is that **physician support is a different job and we've diluted both by calling them the same thing.** Pre-rounding and firefighting, absorbing interruptions, order transcription into a sign queue, filtering the 15 useful minutes out of a UR meeting — and being the person the facility would rather text than the doctor. The ANR regional VPO asked *where's Lindsey, we're in survey* with me standing in front of her. Mechanism we landed on: facility CSS is owed to the patient and not tied to productivity; physician support is tied to productivity, hired alongside the physician, part of the offer, with a ramp and then a threshold. It only has to cover cost.
