Referrals should be multidisciplinary; payor source, therapy needs, facility paperwork and timing all bear on whether hospice is a good idea at that moment, and the facility needs room to say so. On the clinician side a referral is a clinical decision that only an MD, NP or PA can make and that I have to certify, and I do not think most patients at the end of life need hospice; the benefit has to justify the cost. The three things that make it worth it are symptom complexity overnight and on weekends, family complexity, and nursing need, in that order. A referral is not sending someone my patient. The person stays my patient and Crystal's; I am asking for help, and it matters whom I ask.
