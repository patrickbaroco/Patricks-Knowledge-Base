**Evening — the governance drafting run, and it went further than the Policy.**

Started at 3:43pm with `audit-referral-policy-skeleton.md`: the criteria architecture, not prose — the six jobs the Policy has to do, each one something an existing provision already assumes is done somewhere. **Separated the standards workstream out at 3:45** into `audit-standards-queue.md`, and the reason is structural: standards move on the Committee's own cadence, but the Policy is ratified by the Council, so a Policy that named measures would put a Council ratification in front of every standards revision. **The Policy states what a criterion requires of any measure; the register holds the measures.**

Then the draft itself at 4:54 — `audit-referral-policy-draft.md`, v0.1, nine articles, with the cycle defined as the unit that fixes the population for the volume brake.

**The part I didn't expect: checking the Policy draft against all three Charters found nine defects in the CQC Charter.** Recorded in `cqc-charter-revisions.md`. Two of them matter beyond tidiness — § 1.3 still asserted the automatic referral that § 5.5(b) had been amended to leave to the criteria, and **§§ 2.8 and 5.9 both cited a § 4.2(b) that does not exist**, which would have sent anyone following the citation to the wrong Article. That one predates the Policy entirely. Council § 7.4 amended in the same pass so the CQC and Council descriptions of the Employment Authority rule stop claiming to be identical when they aren't.

**Then the two member guides, 5:33 and 5:39** — `cqc-member-guide.md` and `prc-member-guide.md`. Plain language, for someone joining the committee, with section numbers back to the charter. **These are the first governance documents written for the people who will sit on these bodies rather than for counsel or the board.** The PRC guide opens on three separations: the Panel investigates and the Committee decides; the Committee can teach and only the Council can restrict.
