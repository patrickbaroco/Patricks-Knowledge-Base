**The CQC Charter was then tested against it and three of the four gaps needed Charter text.** Record at the end of `Clinical Governance/cqc-charter-revisions.md`; pre-revision copy in `_archive/`. Files written in the evening, 7:12–7:13.

- **§ 4.6.1, objection to a standard (new).** Any Practitioner to whom a standard applies may object in writing and gets a recorded response within two meetings: a revision, the reason the standard stands, or a review date. The standard stays in effect meanwhile and the objection touches no observation, disposition or criterion. § 10.2 now lists objecting among the protected acts, so the protection does not depend on reading an objection as "information."
- **§ 5.6.1, routine examination of clinical leadership (new).** The Chair, the Department Chairs and the RCMOs, read in the same cycle as everyone else with no "leaders first" step; committee members are not covered. Patrick replaced the Command Center draft's five charts from every Leader on every measure — 2 to 3½ review hours a month — with rotation: at least one Leader in every measure swept, at least five charts of each Leader a year, the order fixed in the annual audit plan before the year begins. About 40 charts a year, 30 to 55 minutes a month, and fixing the order in advance keeps the Chair from choosing whose charts are read and when. Independence: appointment is excepted, the other members designate who reads the Chair's charts, and § 9.6(g) and § 9.6.1 carry the same test into determinations and presiding.
- **§ 7.5.1, a stated response (new).** The recipient of a systems transmittal answers in writing within thirty days — will act and how, will not and why, or when it will decide. The duty rests on the Authorization rather than on the Committee, and where the condition lies with a contracted facility, the Entity-side owner of that relationship responds.
