## Definition

Diagnostic momentum is the tendency for a label, once attached, to be carried forward from clinician to clinician and note to note as though it were established fact — each successive reader inheriting the frame and being progressively less likely to reopen it. A working impression hardens into a diagnosis simply by repetition and transfer, not by verification.

Its stationary cousin is **diagnostic inertia**: a single abnormal value copied forward across years of notes rather than ever worked up.

## How it operates

- **A transfer diagnosis is a hypothesis, not a fact.** The label that arrives with the patient should be re-derived from the data before you act on it.
- **The "don't fit" findings are the lever.** Momentum is broken by the data that never matched the inherited label — re-ask *why is this happening?*
- **It is the vehicle that carries [[Premature Closure]] between people.** One team closes; everyone downstream inherits the closed frame.
- **It compounds with [[Anchoring Bias|anchoring]].** A tidy, unfalsifiable prior label is the strongest anchor because no one is looking to disprove it.

## Examples from RLR episodes

- **Treating downstream effects vs the trigger.** A student's iterative "why constipation? why retention?" converts a two-problem picture into one upstream cause (oxybutynin) — the counter-move to momentum. ([[RLR 005 - Abdominal Distention, Constipation, and Urinary Retention|RLR 005]])
- **The unfalsifiable inherited label.** A 1995 Hodgkin-radiation history supplied a tidy "radiation-induced neuropathy" that every subsequent clinician inherited and none reopened. ([[RLR 009 - Progressive Sensorimotor Polyneuropathy|RLR 009]])
- **Re-derive at transfer.** The transferred cholecystitis label is reopened precisely by the features that don't fit — momentum-carried transaminases, indirect hyperbilirubinemia, a pleural effusion, leg edema. ([[RLR 013 - Right Upper Quadrant Pain and Heart Failure|RLR 013]])
- **Referral framing.** A hematology-supplied "sinister" frame built the entire workup off the chart before anyone asked the patient how he was functioning. ([[RLR 014 - Hypercalcemia and Immobilization|RLR 014]])
- **Momentum resisted, out loud.** Reza praises the colleague who "did not let diagnostic momentum take her," re-asked *why*, and converted a non-contrast into a contrast study. ([[RLR 018 - Gross Hematuria in End-Stage Renal Disease|RLR 018]])
- **Inertia made concrete.** A years-elevated alkaline phosphatase copied forward rather than evaluated carried a morbid diagnosis for years. ([[RLR 021 - Chronic Pruritus and Primary Biliary Cholangitis|RLR 021]])

## Counter-moves

- **Re-derive the transfer diagnosis from primary data before operating on the label** ([[RLR 013 - Right Upper Quadrant Pain and Heart Failure|RLR 013]]).
- **Hunt the "doesn't fit" findings and let them reopen the case** ([[RLR 013 - Right Upper Quadrant Pain and Heart Failure|RLR 013]]).
- **Keep asking "why is this happening?" — trace to the trigger** ([[RLR 005 - Abdominal Distention, Constipation, and Urinary Retention|RLR 005]]).
- **Evaluate the single copied-forward abnormal value, don't inherit it** ([[RLR 021 - Chronic Pruritus and Primary Biliary Cholangitis|RLR 021]]).
