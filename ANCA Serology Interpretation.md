## Definition

cANCA typically correlates with anti-PR3 antibodies and granulomatosis with polyangiitis (GPA); pANCA typically correlates with anti-MPO antibodies and microscopic polyangiitis (MPA) or eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis (EGPA) — though the correlation is not absolute. Simultaneous strong positivity of both cANCA/PR3 and pANCA/MPO is an atypical, "inelegant" pattern that should prompt a search for a trigger rather than a forced overlap-syndrome label.

## How it operates

- **Read the pattern, not just each antibody in isolation.** A clean single-pattern positivity (cANCA/PR3 alone, or pANCA/MPO alone) supports a single classic disease; dual strong positivity is the signal to look further, not to average the two into "overlap."
- **Know the classic drug trigger for dual positivity.** Levamisole-adulterated cocaine is a recognized cause of simultaneous cANCA/PR3 and pANCA/MPO positivity and should prompt a toxicology history even when the rest of the presentation looks like primary vasculitis.
- **Environmental and occupational triggers matter too.** Chronic silica exposure is an established, dose-related driver of ANCA-associated vasculitis — documented regional spikes in incidence have followed earthquakes that aerosolize ambient silica.
- **Weigh the renal biopsy pattern over serology alone when they conflict.** A pauci-immune, normal-complement crescentic glomerulonephritis argues against lupus nephritis even when ANA and dsDNA are both strongly positive, because lupus nephritis is expected to show an immune-complex, "full house" immunofluorescence pattern instead.

## Examples from RLR episodes

- **Inelegance as a clue, not a diagnosis.** Both cANCA/PR3 and pANCA/MPO return strongly positive at high titer in a construction worker with pulmonary-renal syndrome; rather than accepting a forced GPA/MPA overlap label, the atypical dual pattern prompts a search for a trigger, ultimately revealed as chronic occupational silica exposure (confirmed by eggshell mediastinal lymph node calcifications and transbronchial biopsy showing massive silicosis) — with a pauci-immune renal biopsy pattern overriding strongly positive ANA/dsDNA to keep lupus off the table.
