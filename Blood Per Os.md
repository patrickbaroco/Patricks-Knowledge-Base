Evergreen concept note. Definition plus worked examples, drawn from the RLR / Clinical Problem Solvers series and elsewhere. Source episodes are cited per example. Sibling heuristic to [[Blood Per Perineum]].

## Definition

**Blood per os** is Rabih Geha's de-anchoring reframe for any blood that appears from the mouth. Because the mouth is a shared exit for the airway, the lungs, the GI tract, and the oropharynx itself, blood seen there is not yet [[Hemoptysis|hemoptysis]], not yet [[Hematemesis|hematemesis]], and not yet oropharyngeal bleeding — it is only *blood per os* until the source is actually localized. Committing to one of those named diagnoses at the moment of presentation is a premature anatomical claim; the disciplined move is to hold the neutral label and gather the data that localize the bleed.

The point is not that the distinction doesn't matter — it matters enormously, because the differential and the workup diverge sharply by source — but that the label should *follow* localization rather than precede it. Naming it "hemoptysis" too early anchors the entire encounter on the lungs and lets contradicting data get absorbed into that frame instead of triggering a reframe.

## The principle

Blood exiting the mouth can originate from any of four territories, and the observer cannot distinguish them by the blood alone:

- **Nasopharynx / upper airway** — posterior epistaxis and nasopharyngeal sources drip back and are coughed or spat out, masquerading as lung or GI bleeding.
- **Lower airway / lungs** — true [[Hemoptysis|hemoptysis]] (bronchiectasis, malignancy, infection, vasculitis, PE). Frothy, bright-red, blood mixed with sputum, often preceded by cough.
- **GI tract** — [[Hematemesis|hematemesis]] (varices, peptic ulcer, malignancy, Mallory-Weiss). Darker, may contain food, often preceded by nausea/retching.
- **Oropharynx / mouth itself** — gingival, tongue, tonsillar, or mucosal bleeding local to the mouth.

The features that *do* localize — color and character, whether it was coughed versus vomited, preceding cough versus nausea, and the exam of nose, mouth, and oropharynx — are exactly the data the neutral label keeps you hunting for. This parallels [[Blood Per Perineum]], where blood at the perineum is neither hematuria nor hematochezia nor vaginal bleeding until source and substance are confirmed. In both, the reframe protects against the two classic errors: **misattributing the source** (the humbling diaper that reads as GI bleeding but is urinary) and **assuming the red substance is even blood**.

## Examples from RLR episodes

- **The reframe stated as principle.** Working a young man with Frank hematuria, Rabih generalizes his approach: just as he refuses to call perineal bleeding by an anatomical name prematurely, blood from the mouth is *blood per os* — you do not know if it came from the nose, the upper airway, the airway itself, or the GI tract until you localize it. He uses it to argue against reflexively differentiating [[Hemoptysis|hemoptysis]], [[Hematemesis|hematemesis]], and oral bleeding at the moment of presentation. ([[RLR 018 - Gross Hematuria in End-Stage Renal Disease|RLR 018]])

## Counter-moves (de-anchoring)

- **Hold the neutral label until localized.** Say "blood per os," not "hemoptysis," until color/character, the act (coughed vs vomited), the preceding symptom (cough vs nausea), and a nose/mouth/oropharynx exam point to a territory.
- **Confirm it is blood.** Red does not equal blood; verify the substance before building the differential.
- **Examine the shared exit.** Deliberately inspect the nasopharynx and oral cavity, since posterior epistaxis and oropharyngeal sources are the classic mimics of both lung and GI bleeding.
- **Let the label follow the data, then commit fully.** Once localized, the neutral placeholder is discarded and the source-specific workup proceeds — the reframe delays the anatomical claim, it does not avoid it.
