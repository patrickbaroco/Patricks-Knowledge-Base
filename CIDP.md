Chronic inflammatory demyelinating polyradiculoneuropathy — the chronic, treatable cousin of Guillain-Barré syndrome, defined by its tempo and non-length-dependent pattern.

## Definition and Relationship to GBS
CIDP is an acquired, immune-mediated **demyelinating polyradiculoneuropathy** that develops or relapses over **at least 8 weeks** — distinguishing it by tempo from the acute course of [[Guillain-Barré Syndrome|GBS]], its "chronic cousin" ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).

- **Course:** may be progressive, stepwise, or relapsing-remitting over months.

## Clinical Features
- **Both proximal and distal weakness** with large-fiber **sensory loss** and **areflexia** — the proximal involvement is a hallmark that separates it from a length-dependent axonal neuropathy.
- **Non-length-dependent pattern:** because the process is demyelinating and attacks roots and nerves broadly, hands and feet can be affected together — a clue that pushes the differential away from diabetic/B12 axonal neuropathy toward demyelinating disease or a central process (see [[Length-Dependent vs Non-Length-Dependent Neuropathy]]) ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).
- **Sphincters typically spared**, as in GBS — prominent bowel/bladder involvement should reopen a cord/cauda-equina localization (see [[Spine vs Peripheral Nerve]]) ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).

## Diagnosis
- **Nerve conduction studies** are central — demyelinating features (conduction block, temporal dispersion, marked slowing) in a multifocal distribution.
- **CSF** often shows **albuminocytologic dissociation** (high protein, normal cells).
- **MRI** may show nerve-root/plexus thickening and enhancement; **nerve biopsy** is reserved for atypical cases.
- **Look for mimics:** paraproteinemic neuropathies (check SPEP/immunofixation — MGUS, POEMS), and consider a subacute demyelinating pattern as a clue that a neuropathy is inflammatory and treatable rather than purely axonal ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).

## Management
- **First-line immunotherapy:** corticosteroids, IVIG, or plasma exchange — unlike GBS, **steroids are effective** in CIDP.
- **Maintenance** with periodic IVIG or steroid-sparing immunosuppression; response supports the diagnosis, and CIDP is important precisely because it is treatable.
