## The master schema: auto / allo / drug

Immune-mediated hemolysis is destruction of red cells by antibody, and its **master schema has three branches** — do not reflexively collapse it to "autoimmune":

- **Autoimmune ([[Autoimmune Hemolytic Anemia|AIHA]])** — antibody against the patient's own red cells; the most common branch and the one clinicians erroneously jump to. Warm (IgG, extravascular/splenic) vs cold (IgM, complement) subtypes; secondary to lymphoproliferative disease (CLL), autoimmune disease, or drugs.
- **Alloimmune** — antibody against *foreign* red cells from a prior transfusion or pregnancy. The [[Delayed Hemolytic Transfusion Reaction|delayed hemolytic transfusion reaction]] is the key entity: an antigen on cells transfused weeks earlier drives alloantibody formation, and the antibodies hemolyze on re-exposure — often *after* the index hospitalization.
- **Drug-induced** — the drug itself drives the hemolysis, so *withdrawal stops it*; this dechallenge response distinguishes it from autoimmune disease.

## Reasoning points

- **Recent transfusion should raise alloimmune, not autoimmune.** Rabih's explicit self-critique is that he anchored on autoimmune when the correct top node is *immune-mediated* with three branches; the recent transfusions were the clue to a delayed hemolytic transfusion reaction. ([[RLR 030 - Dark Urine and Delayed Hemolytic Transfusion Reaction|RLR 030]])
- **A positive [[Direct Coombs Test|direct Coombs]] confirms an immune mechanism but does not by itself separate auto from allo** — alloantibody screening and matching to the antigens of previously transfused units make the call. ([[RLR 030 - Dark Urine and Delayed Hemolytic Transfusion Reaction|RLR 030]])
- **[[Spherocytes|Spherocytes]] narrow the field.** A large spherocyte burden points to AIHA or hereditary spherocytosis (with PNH and Babesia behind); the Coombs splits immune from non-immune. ([[RLR 030 - Dark Urine and Delayed Hemolytic Transfusion Reaction|RLR 030]])
- **Low-titer alloantibodies can be missed on type-and-screen and "wake up" on re-exposure** — so a patient with a delayed hemolytic transfusion reaction needs the specific alloantibodies flagged for all future crossmatches. ([[RLR 030 - Dark Urine and Delayed Hemolytic Transfusion Reaction|RLR 030]])
- **A primary hematologic malignancy can drive immune cytopenias** (AIHA + ITP = an Evans-like picture), so a monoclonal marrow process discovered on a hemolysis workup is not incidental. ([[RLR 032 - Fever and Chronic Myelomonocytic Leukemia|RLR 032]])
