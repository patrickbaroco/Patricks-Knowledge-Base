Evergreen concept note. Definition plus worked examples, drawn from the RLR / Clinical Problem Solvers series and elsewhere. Source episodes are cited per example.

## Definition

A unifying diagnosis is the single explanation a reasoner seeks for *all* of a patient's findings, rather than assigning each abnormality its own cause. It is the clinical form of **Occam's razor** (the law of parsimony — "entities should not be multiplied beyond necessity") and its bedside cousin, "when you hear hoofbeats, think horses, not zebras." The move is not merely aesthetic: a hypothesis that accounts for the whole picture is usually both more probable and more testable than a bag of coincidences.

Its most powerful special case is **anatomic or physiologic convergence** — when several otherwise-separate problems all pass through one point, a single lesion there becomes the parsimonious answer, often deducible *before* confirmatory imaging. Its necessary counterweight is [[Hickam's Dictum|Hickam's dictum]] ("a patient can have as many diseases as he pleases"), which governs the elderly, the immunosuppressed, and the multi-risk-factor patient in whom plurality is the rule.

## How it operates

- **Look for the crossroads.** When findings cluster at one convergence point — bowel *and* biliary *and* pancreatic obstruction all meet at the [[Ampulla of Vater|ampulla of Vater]] — a single lesion there is the parsimonious unifier, and the diagnosis can be reasoned out ahead of the scan.
- **The unifier must explain the *whole* panel.** Test the candidate against every abnormality; a finding it genuinely cannot cover is either noise or evidence for a second, independent process — the signal to invoke [[Hickam's Dictum|Hickam]].
- **Parsimony is a prior, not a proof.** Occam ranks hypotheses; [[Base Rate|base rate]], [[Illness Tempo|tempo]], and host factors decide when to abandon it for plurality. It weakens as comorbidity and immunosuppression rise.
- **"Explains" is not "co-occurs."** When two findings appear together you cannot assume one cause — they may have arrived by separate routes. Occam proposes; verification (and [[Worst-Case Reasoning|worst-case reasoning]] on each finding) disposes.
- **Beware forcing the fit.** Over-unifying — squeezing an outlier into an elegant single story it doesn't belong in — is the mirror-image failure of over-splitting. The [[Cognitive Autopsy|cognitive autopsy]] often finds a case where one clean diagnosis was reached by rationalizing away the one clue that pointed elsewhere.

## Examples from RLR and CPS episodes

- **The ampulla as the one crossroads (Occam's cleanest win).** A 51-year-old with constipation, nausea/vomiting, a lipase of 2424, and a cholestatic pattern shows features of bowel obstruction (esophagus-to-anus), biliary obstruction (sphincter-to-liver), and pancreatic disease — which converge only at the [[Ampulla of Vater|ampulla of Vater]], so a periampullary mass "was the only real possibility," reachable before the CT confirmed it. ([[RLR 039 - Nausea, Vomiting, Constipation, and a Metastatic Ampullary Mass|RLR 039]])
- **The unifying cascade.** Asking "can *one* driver explain everything?" makes hypercalcemia the single cause of a whole metabolic panel — new anemia, bumped creatinine, low bicarbonate — via a pathophysiologic chain rather than a coincidence list. ([[RLR 014 - Hypercalcemia and Immobilization|RLR 014]]; see [[Center of Gravity]])
- **A syndrome, not a symptom.** Refusing to anchor on the chief concern, transient loss of consciousness plus parkinsonism plus autonomic failure unify under a single synucleinopathy (multiple system atrophy) rather than three unrelated problems. ([[CPS 211 - Episodic Transient Loss of Consciousness and Multiple System Atrophy|CPS 211]])
- **The vital sign is the unifier.** A blood pressure of 211/114 ties the headache, the encephalopathy, and the "myoclonus" (seizures on EEG) into one process — PRES — rather than a scattered neurologic list. ([[CPS 276 - Lethargy, Myoclonus, and Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome|CPS 276]])
- **Occam holding in a complex host.** Recurrent strokes *through* anticoagulation in an elderly man could read as many vascular events, but unify under one intravascular large B-cell lymphoma — parsimony winning even where [[Hickam's Dictum|Hickam]] would be tempting. ([[CPS 386 - Rapidly Progressive Dementia and Intravascular Large B-Cell Lymphoma|CPS 386]])
- **The counterweight — when *not* to unify.** Weeks of visual hallucinations resolve to three simultaneous contributors (hyperglycemia-provoked occipital seizures, a possible occipital stroke, and diabetic-retinopathy Charles Bonnet); the honest formulation is multifactorial, and the hosts invoke [[Hickam's Dictum|Hickam's dictum]] and "embrace the ambiguity." ([[CPS 323 - Visual Hallucinations and Occipital Seizures|CPS 323]])
