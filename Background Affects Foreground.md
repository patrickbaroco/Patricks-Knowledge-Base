"The background always affects the foreground" — a patient's context (medications, comorbidities, devices, physiology) reframes the meaning of a presenting finding, often converting a mundane sign into an urgent one.

## The principle

The same foreground finding means different things against different backgrounds. A bulge with a bruise is trivial in most people but is **spontaneous bleeding** in someone anticoagulated; a fever with a heart rate of 68 is unremarkable until you know the patient is *not* on a beta-blocker. Good reasoners read the background *first* and let it set the prior for the foreground. A key corollary: when the background raises vulnerability, the **trigger can be trivial or unremembered**, because what changed is the host's susceptibility, not the force applied.

## Examples

- **Anticoagulation reframes a bulge.** A cough-provoked flank/groin bulge with subtle ecchymosis in a man on warfarin is a [[Rectus Sheath Hematoma|rectus sheath hematoma]] until proven otherwise; the patient may not recall the trivial strain that set it off. ([[RLR 058 - Inguinal Bulge and Rectus Sheath Hematoma|RLR 058]])
- **A recent device reframes a cytopenia.** A platelet drop is read very differently once you know a valve was placed weeks earlier — the background (a TAVR) turns an "idiopathic" thrombocytopenia into a valve-related one. ([[RLR 064 - Hematuria and Post-TAVR Perivalvular Leak|RLR 064]])
- **Missing physiology reframes a vital sign.** Relative bradycardia is only visible once you note the *absence* of a beta-blocker to explain it. ([[RLR 060 - Transient Loss of Consciousness and Cardiac Sarcoidosis|RLR 060]])

## How to use it

Before interpreting the chief finding, inventory the background: **medications** (especially anticoagulants, antiplatelets, nodal blockers, immunosuppressants), **devices** (valves, catheters, lines), **comorbidities**, and baseline **physiology**. Ask, "does any of this change what this finding means?" When the background raises bleeding, infection, or arrhythmia risk, downgrade your demand for a dramatic trigger. This is the flip side of [[Anchoring Bias|anchoring]] on the presenting complaint: the context, not the complaint, often carries the diagnosis.
