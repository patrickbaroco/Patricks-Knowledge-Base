Numbness of the chin and lower lip from mental-nerve (V3) dysfunction — a small symptom with an outsized, often malignant, significance.

## Definition and Anatomy
Numb chin syndrome (mental neuropathy) is sensory loss over the chin and lower lip in the distribution of the **mental nerve**, the terminal branch of the inferior alveolar nerve from the **mandibular division (V3)** of the trigeminal nerve (see [[Trigeminal Nerve]]) ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).

- **The route to the chin:** V3 → inferior alveolar nerve through the mandibular canal → mental foramen → mental nerve. Disease anywhere along this path — or more proximally at the skull base or meninges — can present here.

## Why It Is Ominous
Despite sounding trivial, numb chin syndrome is a **red-flag sign strongly associated with malignancy** ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).

- **Metastasis to the mandible** or infiltration of the nerve — breast and lung carcinoma are classic solid-tumor sources.
- **Hematologic malignancy**, particularly **lymphoma and multiple myeloma/plasma-cell disease**, and leukemia.
- **Leptomeningeal or perineural spread** coating the nerve proximally, linking it to [[Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|leptomeningeal carcinomatosis]] ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).
- In a patient with known or suspected cancer, a numb chin is malignant infiltration until proven otherwise; it can be the presenting sign of relapse or of an occult primary ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).

## Benign Causes (Diagnoses of Exclusion)
- **Dental/iatrogenic:** tooth infection, mandibular fracture, or dental/surgical trauma to the inferior alveolar nerve.
- **Inflammatory/other:** sarcoidosis, vasculitis, MS, sickle cell disease, and occasionally benign trigeminal neuropathy — pursued only after malignancy is excluded.

## Workup
- **Dedicated imaging** of the mandible, skull base, and neuraxis — contrast MRI of the brain and skull base (look for perineural and **leptomeningeal enhancement**; see [[Pachymeningeal vs Leptomeningeal Enhancement]]), plus mandibular imaging.
- **CSF analysis** including large-volume cytology when leptomeningeal disease is suspected (cytology is insensitive; see [[Test Interpretation]]).
- **Systemic malignancy evaluation** — cross-sectional imaging, and marrow/plasma-cell studies (SPEP/free light chains) when myeloma is a consideration.
