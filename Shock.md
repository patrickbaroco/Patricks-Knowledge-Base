Evergreen concept note. Shock is inadequate tissue perfusion; a fast bedside first fork — systemic versus thoracic — sorts the four physiologic buckets before any test returns. RLR / Clinical Problem Solvers teaching points are cited per episode; general clinical detail is scaffolding.

## Definition
Shock is circulatory failure producing inadequate oxygen delivery to tissues, classically with hypotension (though pressure can be maintained early). The end-organ signal is anaerobic metabolism and rising lactate.

## The Systemic-vs-Thoracic First Fork
Before enumerating the four buckets, ask whether the shock is **systemic or thoracic** ([[RLR 062 - Oligoarthritis and Polyarticular Gout Flare|RLR 062]]).

- **Cardiogenic and obstructive shock carry a thoracic signature** — chest pain, dyspnea, tachy/bradycardia, or a chest finding — because the resistance to flow is *in the chest* ([[RLR 062 - Oligoarthritis and Polyarticular Gout Flare|RLR 062]]).
- **Distributive and hypovolemic shock are systemic** — no thoracic weight ([[RLR 062 - Oligoarthritis and Polyarticular Gout Flare|RLR 062]]).
- Practical payoff: a hypotensive patient — **even one with a bad heart** — who has **no thoracic symptoms** most likely has a systemic (distributive or hypovolemic) cause, not cardiogenic shock ([[RLR 062 - Oligoarthritis and Polyarticular Gout Flare|RLR 062]]). In RLR 062 this pointed to meds/hypovolemia despite a known cardiomyopathy.

## The Four Buckets
- **Hypovolemic** — hemorrhage, GI/renal losses, diuresis, third-spacing; cold, low filling.
- **Distributive** — sepsis, anaphylaxis, neurogenic, and vasodilatory states; warm, vasodilated, low SVR. Rarely, a massive tophaceous gout burden — see [[Gout and Distributive Shock]] ([[RLR 062 - Oligoarthritis and Polyarticular Gout Flare|RLR 062]]).
- **Cardiogenic** — pump failure (MI, decompensated cardiomyopathy, arrhythmia, valvular).
- **Obstructive** — tamponade, tension pneumothorax, massive PE.

## Assessment
- **Lactate** as a marker of hypoperfusion — a normal lactate in a warm, well-perfused patient argues against decompensated/cardiogenic shock ([[RLR 062 - Oligoarthritis and Polyarticular Gout Flare|RLR 062]]).
- **Fluid responsiveness** — correction of pressure with a fluid bolus (e.g., 500 cc LR) supports a hypovolemic/distributive mechanism and lets you "tuck away" the hypotension ([[RLR 062 - Oligoarthritis and Polyarticular Gout Flare|RLR 062]]).
- Exam of perfusion (warm vs clamped-down), JVP, lungs, and bedside echo refine the bucket.

## Management Principles
- Treat the mechanism: volume and hemorrhage control (hypovolemic), source control plus vasopressors (distributive), inotropes/revascularization (cardiogenic), and immediate decompression/thrombolysis (obstructive). Reassess with lactate clearance and dynamic measures.
