Systemic lupus erythematosus's atypical presentations, distinct from the textbook malar-rash-plus-arthritis picture.

## How it operates

- **Lupus can present through its complications rather than its classic features** — acquired angioedema, serositis-type swelling, or a nonspecific rash can be the presenting complaint, with the more recognizable features (malar rash, oral ulcers, alopecia) absent or subtle at onset.
- **Complement and cytopenia patterns matter as much as serology.** Low C3/C4 alongside leukopenia (see [[Complement Levels]], [[Leukopenia]]) can be as informative as ANA/dsDNA titers, and sometimes surface before the antibody picture is even sent.
- **Renal biopsy pattern can override serology when they conflict.** A pauci-immune (not immune-complex) crescentic glomerulonephritis argues against lupus nephritis even with strongly positive ANA/dsDNA — see [[Rapidly Progressive Glomerulonephritis]] and [[ANCA Serology Interpretation]].

## Examples from RLR episodes

- **Presenting as swelling, not a rash.** Persistent lip and facial swelling following a dental abscess, initially treated as angioedema, is ultimately traced to systemic lupus erythematosus — floridly positive ANA and double-stranded DNA, on re-examination revealing a malar-appearing rash with nasolabial sparing that had been overlooked while the team focused on the swelling. ([[RLR 076 - Lip Swelling, Rash, and Systemic Lupus Erythematosus|RLR 076]])
