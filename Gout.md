Gout is monosodium urate crystal deposition disease — an acutely painful, often provoked crystal arthritis that can be mono-, oligo-, or polyarticular and, over time, tophaceous.

## Definition and Pathophysiology
Chronic hyperuricemia leads to precipitation of **monosodium urate (MSU)** crystals in and around joints. Phagocytosis of crystals by neutrophils triggers an intense inflammatory (NLRP3 inflammasome, IL-1β) response — the acute flare.

- **Crystals** — needle-shaped and **negatively birefringent** under polarized light ([[RLR 062 - Oligoarthritis and Polyarticular Gout Flare|RLR 062]]).
- **Tophi** — chronic aggregates of urate with surrounding granulomatous inflammation, in soft tissue, cartilage, and bone.

## Triggers / Precipitants
- **Diuretics and volume shifts** ([[RLR 062 - Oligoarthritis and Polyarticular Gout Flare|RLR 062]]) — a recently increased loop diuretic (e.g., Bumex) can cause volume contraction that both drops the blood pressure and provokes a flare. See [[Diuretic]].
- **Infection, surgery, trauma, and rapid urate shifts** (including starting urate-lowering therapy). Infection can even trigger a flare in a joint it also infects ([[RLR 062 - Oligoarthritis and Polyarticular Gout Flare|RLR 062]]).
- Dietary purine loads, alcohol, dehydration.

## Clinical Spectrum
- **Acute flare** — abrupt, exquisitely painful, warm, red joint; classically first MTP (podagra) but any joint, and can be **polyarticular** ([[RLR 062 - Oligoarthritis and Polyarticular Gout Flare|RLR 062]]).
- **Intercritical** — asymptomatic between flares, though crystals persist in the joint.
- **Chronic tophaceous gout** — recurrent flares, tophi, and structural joint damage; a massive tophaceous burden can rarely drive [[Gout and Distributive Shock|distributive shock]] ([[RLR 062 - Oligoarthritis and Polyarticular Gout Flare|RLR 062]]).

## Diagnosis
- **[[Synovial Fluid Analysis|Synovial fluid]]** with negatively birefringent needle crystals is the gold standard.
- **Crystals persist forever** — a positive crystal proves the joint *has had* gout, not that it is flaring now; an **active** flare needs **intracellular** crystals and exclusion of infection ([[RLR 062 - Oligoarthritis and Polyarticular Gout Flare|RLR 062]]). See [[Crystals in Macrophages]].
- Serum urate may be normal during an acute flare, so it does not rule gout in or out at the bedside.
- Always **rule out [[Septic Arthritis|septic arthritis]]** — the two coexist, and morbidity lives in missed infection ([[RLR 062 - Oligoarthritis and Polyarticular Gout Flare|RLR 062]]).

## Management
- **Acute flare** — colchicine, NSAIDs, or corticosteroids (oral, intra-articular, or systemic). Steroids are reasonable even when infection is not fully excluded **as long as antibiotics cover a possible co-infection** ([[RLR 062 - Oligoarthritis and Polyarticular Gout Flare|RLR 062]]).
- **Urate-lowering therapy** — allopurinol or febuxostat (xanthine oxidase inhibitors), titrated to a target serum urate; do not stop or start during an untreated flare without flare prophylaxis.
- **Address triggers** — reassess diuretics and volume status, hydration, and diet.
