Evergreen concept note. Cardiac sarcoidosis is granulomatous infiltration of the myocardium that characteristically causes conduction disease and ventricular arrhythmia out of proportion to pump dysfunction. RLR / Clinical Problem Solvers teaching points are cited per episode; general clinical detail is scaffolding.

## Concept and Pathophysiology
Non-caseating granulomas infiltrate the myocardium — with a predilection for the **basal septum and conduction system** — producing scar that is electrically active before it is mechanically significant.

- **Conduction disease out of proportion to pump function.** The hallmark: advanced **[[AV Block]]** or ventricular tachycardia in a patient whose ejection fraction may still be preserved. This pattern of disproportionate conduction destruction defines the [[Arrhythmogenic Cardiomyopathy]] family ([[RLR 060 - Transient Loss of Consciousness and Cardiac Sarcoidosis|RLR 060]]).
- Granulomas are patchy and multifocal, explaining why sampling error limits biopsy and why imaging patterns are heterogeneous.
- A member of [[Granulomatous Disease]]; systemic sarcoid (pulmonary, mediastinal nodes, skin, eye) frequently coexists and supports the diagnosis.

## Clinical Presentation
- **Unexplained high-grade AV block in a middle-aged or older adult** should prompt consideration of cardiac sarcoid ([[RLR 060 - Transient Loss of Consciousness and Cardiac Sarcoidosis|RLR 060]]).
- **Ventricular arrhythmias / [[Transient Loss of Consciousness]]** (syncope), palpitations, and sudden cardiac death.
- Later, heart failure from restrictive or dilated physiology.
- Systemic clues: prolonged fever without an infectious source (autoimmune disease, with sarcoid and SLE the few true fever-producers), mediastinal/hilar lymphadenopathy ([[RLR 060 - Transient Loss of Consciousness and Cardiac Sarcoidosis|RLR 060]]).

## Diagnosis
- **Cardiac MRI** — **patchy delayed gadolinium enhancement** (often mid-wall/epicardial, non-coronary distribution) is a specific granulomatous signature and, with lymphadenopathy, strongly suggests cardiac sarcoid ([[RLR 060 - Transient Loss of Consciousness and Cardiac Sarcoidosis|RLR 060]]).
- **FDG-PET** — focal/patchy myocardial uptake reflects active inflammation; useful for diagnosis and for tracking treatment response (requires dietary prep to suppress physiologic glucose uptake).
- **Tissue** — extracardiac biopsy (e.g., mediastinal node) showing non-caseating granulomas with negative stains/cultures is higher-yield and safer than endomyocardial biopsy, which suffers sampling error from patchy involvement ([[RLR 060 - Transient Loss of Consciousness and Cardiac Sarcoidosis|RLR 060]]).
- Exclude mimics: TB (cardiac TB is usually *pericardial*, not AV-nodal), other granulomatous disease, and — always — lymphoma. **Never lock in sarcoid at time zero** given [[Sarcoid-Lymphoma Syndrome]] ([[RLR 060 - Transient Loss of Consciousness and Cardiac Sarcoidosis|RLR 060]]). Guidance follows the **HRS expert consensus on cardiac sarcoidosis**.

## Management
- **Immunosuppression:** corticosteroids are first-line to quiet active inflammation and can recover conduction; steroid-sparing agents (e.g., methotrexate, azathioprine) for maintenance or steroid intolerance.
- **Steroids before a permanent pacemaker.** Because inflammation-driven block may be reversible, a trial of steroids to recover conduction is considered before committing to permanent pacing ([[RLR 060 - Transient Loss of Consciousness and Cardiac Sarcoidosis|RLR 060]]).
- **Device therapy:** pacing for high-grade AV block; an **ICD** for ventricular arrhythmia or high sudden-death risk (often favored given the arrhythmogenic substrate even at implant for block).
- **Heart failure therapy** and rhythm management as needed; monitor treatment response with PET.
