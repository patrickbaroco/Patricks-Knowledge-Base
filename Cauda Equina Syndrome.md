A polyradiculopathy of the lumbosacral nerve roots below the conus medullaris, producing lower-motor-neuron leg weakness, saddle sensory loss, and sphincter failure — a compressive form of which is a surgical emergency.

## Anatomy and Definition
Below roughly L1–L2 the cord ends (conus medullaris) and the canal carries the **cauda equina** — the lumbosacral roots. Injury here is a **lower-motor-neuron** process, distinguishing it from a cord myelopathy ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).

- **Roots, not cord:** because these are roots, expect **absent reflexes** and flaccid rather than spastic weakness ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).

## Clinical Features
- **Saddle anesthesia** (perianal/perineal sensory loss) and **sphincter dysfunction** — urinary retention (often with overflow), decreased anal tone, and fecal incontinence.
- **Bilateral, often asymmetric** leg weakness and sensory loss in a multi-root distribution, with severe low back and radicular pain.
- **Urinary retention** is a sensitive and worrying feature; its absence lowers, but does not exclude, the diagnosis ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).

## The First Branch Point: Structural or Not
The initial fork is **compressive (structural) vs non-compressive** — MRI answers it ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).

- **Compressive:** large central disc herniation, severe spinal stenosis, epidural abscess or hematoma, and **metastatic/epidural tumor**. Severe L4–L5 stenosis with mass effect on the cauda equina is a classic compressive cause ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).
- **Non-compressive (infiltrative/inflammatory):** **leptomeningeal carcinomatosis** coating the roots, **neurosarcoidosis**, **arachnoiditis**, and **neurolymphomatosis**; also inflammatory polyradiculopathies (e.g., CMV in the immunocompromised, GBS variants) ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).
- **Both can coexist:** a compressive cauda equina and a diffuse leptomeningeal process are not mutually exclusive (see [[Hickam's Dictum]]) ([[RLR 059 - Weakness and Numbness and Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|RLR 059]]).

## Workup
- **Urgent MRI of the lumbosacral spine with and without contrast** is the test of choice — non-contrast for compression, contrast for **root enhancement** signaling leptomeningeal/infiltrative disease (see [[Pachymeningeal vs Leptomeningeal Enhancement]]).
- **Lumbar puncture** when a non-compressive cause is suspected — CSF cytology, cell count, protein, and infectious studies; a spinal block below a compressive lesion produces a [[Froin Syndrome|Froin]] high-protein pattern.
- **Post-void residual / bladder scan** and rectal tone to document sphincter involvement.

## Management
- **Compressive cauda equina is a surgical emergency** — emergent decompression, ideally early, to preserve bladder and ambulatory function; add glucocorticoids and radiation/oncology input for malignant compression.
- **Non-compressive causes** are treated by etiology (chemotherapy/radiation for carcinomatous disease, immunosuppression for sarcoid, antimicrobials for infection).
