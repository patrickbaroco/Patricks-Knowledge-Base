## Definition

The anatomical approach builds a differential by walking the relevant structures in space — every organ, layer, or compartment that could generate the finding — rather than by free-listing diseases. It turns a vague, coarse complaint ("UTI," "back pain," "hematuria") into a localized one, and it exploits adjacency: neighboring structures share causes, so one upstream lesion can produce several "organ" findings at once.

## How it operates

- **Refine the coarse label down the tract.** "UTI" is as crude as "respiratory tract infection"; urethra, prostate, bladder, ureter, and kidney each localize differently.
- **Use the exam to localize by exclusion.** A normal neurologic exam makes cord and root disease unlikely and pushes reasoning toward the musculoskeletal column itself.
- **Reason from a downstream finding back to a shared source.** Adjacent structures let one cause (hepatic congestion) explain several findings (gallbladder-wall edema *and* a right pleural effusion).
- **Enumerate the compartments of a region.** For the febrile back: psoas, vertebrae, epidural space/dura — then let the systemic signature promote a deep or endovascular infection.

## Examples from RLR episodes

- **Localize the "UTI" before treating.** Walking the urinary tract distinguishes pyelonephritis (fever, rigors, CVA tenderness) from simple cystitis and from prostatitis. ([[RLR 012 - Dysuria and Esophageal Perforation|RLR 012]])
- **Adjacency explains three findings at once.** The liver sits between the gallbladder and the right pleural space, so congestion yields gallbladder-wall edema and a right effusion from one upstream cause. ([[RLR 013 - Right Upper Quadrant Pain and Heart Failure|RLR 013]])
- **A normal exam localizes by exclusion.** Intact strength, sensation, gait, and no Horner's push away from cord and root and toward the musculoskeletal column. ([[RLR 016 - Neck Pain and Crowned Dens Syndrome|RLR 016]])
- **Compartments of the febrile back.** Psoas, vertebrae, epidural space/dura enumerated; the rigors promote a deep or endovascular infection over non-specific back pain. ([[RLR 017 - Back Pain and Disseminated Nocardiosis|RLR 017]])
- **Hematuria up the tract.** Bladder, prostate, ureter, and kidney each carry their own lesions, with volume and flank pain helping localize. ([[RLR 018 - Gross Hematuria in End-Stage Renal Disease|RLR 018]])
- **What one organ can't do.** Isolated cholecystitis cannot drive a hepatocellular transaminitis, so the inflammation must be sited more centrally. ([[RLR 020 - Chest Pain and Ascending Cholangitis|RLR 020]])
