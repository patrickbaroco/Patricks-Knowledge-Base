# Leader Components: Tippet Rings & Micro Swivels

> **Summary:** Tippet rings and micro swivels are simple additions that extend leader life and simplify maintenance. Tippet rings are best suited for nymph leaders, while micro swivels excel on streamer and bass leaders by preventing line twist. Both allow you to replace only the tippet instead of repeatedly shortening the entire leader.

## Tippet Rings

### Purpose

**0:29** A tippet ring is a tiny metal ring inserted between the leader's midsection and the terminal tippet. Instead of trimming back the tapered leader each time the tippet becomes too short, you replace only the tippet attached below the ring. This preserves the taper and dramatically extends the life of the leader.

### Best Uses

**2:19** Tippet rings are particularly useful for:

- Indicator nymphing
- Tight-line (Euro) nymphing
- General subsurface trout fishing
- Hand-tied nymph leaders

Brian generally avoids them on dry-fly leaders because the ring often lands before the leader, creating additional disturbance on the water. While many anglers successfully use them for dry flies, they provide their greatest benefit on subsurface presentations.

### Attaching a Tippet Ring

**3:40** Most tippet rings come packaged on a snap swivel that serves only as a storage carrier.

To attach one:

1. Clamp the last ring with a pair of hemostats.
2. Slide it off the snap swivel
3. Leave it clamped while tying the leader material to the ring.
4. Tie a standard clinch knot.
5. Wet and tighten the knot while holding the ring with the hemostats.

**4:39–7:23** Brian prefers removing the ring before tying because the hemostats provide a secure grip and make it easier to tighten the knot properly. Some anglers tie the ring while it remains on the carrier. Both methods work; choose whichever you find easier.

He also notes that properly used hemostats will not damage a quality tippet ring.

### Modifying a Commercial Leader

**7:40** A commercial tapered leader can easily be converted to use a tippet ring.

1. Find where the tippet transitions into the thicker midsection.
2. Cut the leader at that point.
3. Tie the tippet ring onto the midsection.
4. Tie the original or replacement tippet onto the opposite side of the ring.

From that point forward, only the terminal tippet is replaced, preserving the tapered leader for months—or even multiple seasons.

## Micro Swivels

### Purpose

**8:23** A micro swivel functions like two tippet rings connected by a tiny barrel swivel. In addition to preserving the leader, it eliminates twist caused by large or wind-resistant flies.

Micro swivels are particularly useful when fishing:

- Streamers
- Articulated flies
- Deer-hair bass bugs
- Poppers
- Divers
- Large weighted flies

Large flies naturally rotate during casting and retrieval. Without a swivel, this twist transfers into the leader and eventually causes tangles and poor turnover.

### Attaching a Micro Swivel

**9:13** Installation is identical to a tippet ring.

1. Clamp one end of the swivel with hemostats.
2. Tie the butt section to one side.
3. Tie the tippet to the opposite side.

Once installed, the swivel becomes a permanent junction between the leader and the replaceable tippet.

### Modifying a Commercial Streamer Leader

**10:15** Commercial streamer leaders are easy to upgrade.

1. Locate the transition between the taper and the terminal tippet.
2. Cut the leader.
3. Tie in a micro swivel.    
4. Reattach the tippet.

As the tippet shortens, replace only that section. The tapered portion of the leader remains unchanged.

### Choosing a Size

**12:10** Two common sizes are available:

- **25 lb** — lighter trout streamers
- **40 lb** — larger streamers, bass bugs, articulated flies

Brian generally prefers the 40-pound version because it offers additional strength with little downside.

Although the swivel adds a small amount of weight, this is rarely a concern when fishing streamers or bass flies. In some cases, it can even improve the action of poppers and diving flies.

## Practical Recommendations

**21:47**

Use a **tippet ring** when:

- Building nymph leaders
- Modifying commercial trout leaders
- Frequently replacing tippet
- Fishing indicators or Euro rigs

Use a **micro swivel** when:

- Fishing streamers
- Casting articulated flies
- Throwing bass bugs
- Fishing poppers or divers
- Using flies that tend to twist the leader

Both systems preserve the butt and midsection of the leader while making tippet replacement faster and more economical.

## Key Takeaways

- Tippet rings preserve tapered leaders by allowing repeated tippet replacement.
- They are most useful on nymph leaders and other subsurface presentations.
- Micro swivels provide the same benefit while eliminating leader twist.
- Hemostats make both components much easier to install.
- Build durable leader butt sections once, then replace only the terminal tippet as needed.

# Nymph & Streamer Leader Formulas

> **Summary:** These are Brian Flechsig's simple leader formulas built around Maxima monofilament, tippet rings, and micro swivels. The philosophy is to build durable butt sections once, then replace only the terminal tippet as needed.

## General Philosophy

**13:16** Rather than replacing complete leaders, build reusable butt sections ahead of time. Each consists of a butt section, one or more transition sections, and either a tippet ring or micro swivel. During normal fishing, only the tippet below the ring or swivel is replaced.

---

## Standard Nymph Leader

**14:06–18:30**

### Formula

| Section | Material |
|---------|----------|
| Butt | 3 ft – 20 lb Maxima Chameleon |
| Midsection | 2 ft – .012" Maxima Ultragreen |
| Midsection | 2 ft – .010" Maxima Ultragreen |
| Junction | Tippet Ring |
| Tippet | 3X–5X |

### Notes

**14:39** Maxima Chameleon provides a stiff, abrasion-resistant butt section that transfers energy efficiently from the fly line.

**15:10** Maxima Ultragreen creates a smooth transition into the tippet while maintaining excellent turnover.

**15:59** The tippet ring allows repeated tippet replacement without shortening the tapered leader.

**Best for:**

- Indicator nymphing
- Tight-line nymphing
- Euro nymphing

## Sink-Tip Streamer Leader

**18:30–21:16**

Brian generally fishes streamers on sink-tip fly lines using relatively short leaders.

### Formula

| Section | Material |
|---------|----------|
| Butt | 2 ft – .017" Maxima Chameleon |
| Transition | 1 ft – .015" Maxima Chameleon |
| Junction | Micro Swivel |
| Tippet | 1 ft – .012" Maxima Ultragreen |

### Notes

**19:26** The stiff Chameleon butt section efficiently transfers casting energy from the fly line into the leader.

**20:14** Brian prefers a nail knot or other low-profile connection to the fly line instead of a loop-to-loop connection on sink-tip lines.

**20:30** Because the micro swivel marks the beginning of the replaceable tippet, the butt section often remains attached for an entire season.

**20:58** Brian rarely fishes streamer tippet smaller than .012" when using large articulated flies. Smaller streamers may justify .010–.011" tippet.
