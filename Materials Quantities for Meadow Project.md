## Glyphosate

### Product
- Standard 41% glyphosate concentrate (e.g., Compare-N-Save, Ranger Pro, Glyphosate 4
  Plus, Hi-Yield Killzall, or similar — functionally interchangeable)
- **Bought: Hi-Yield Killzall, 41% glyphosate, double-surfactant formulation (already
  fully loaded — no separate surfactant needed).** Note for future reference: Killzall runs
  expensive per gallon (~$76/gal at DoMyOwn); Compare-N-Save or Ranger Pro 2.5-gal jugs are
  the same active ingredient + surfactant for roughly a third to a quarter of the per-gallon
  cost, if buying again.
- Avoid formulations marketed for brush/woody plants (e.g., triclopyr, "extended control")
  — standard grass/turf formulation is what's needed here

### Dosing principle
Glyphosate is dosed by **amount of product per acre**, not by spray-solution volume. The
water is only a carrier — mix enough solution to cover the area spraying to
wet-but-not-dripping (runoff wastes product and reduces kill). Get the per-acre **product**
rate right and the water volume can flex to the sprayer.

- Standard rate for killing existing lawn/pasture grass: **2–3 qt of 41% product per acre**
- Tough, mature perennial grass: up to **5 qt/acre** (check the label's annual maximum)
- Mix strength lands around a **1.5–2% solution** in a backpack; adjust to hit the per-acre
  rate given actual coverage

### Quantity for ~0.897 acres (~39,080 sq ft)

#### Per application
- Product needed: **~1.8–2.7 qt** (2–3 qt/acre)
- Diluted solution needed: **~22–37 gallons**, depending on mix strength (1.5–2%)
- Backpack loads (4-gallon): **~6–9 loads**

#### For both applications (2x)
- Total product: **~3.6–5.4 qt (0.9–1.35 gal)** across both rounds
- **One 2.5-gallon jug of 41% concentrate still covers both applications** with buffer for
  overlap, irregular borders, and spot retreatment

> [!warning] Don't over-apply
> An earlier draft of this note back-calculated from "300–400 sq ft per gallon of
> solution," which implies ~8–11 qt/acre — roughly 2–3× the normal rate and at/above most
> label maximums. More glyphosate does not mean a better kill; follow the per-acre product
> rate and the label.

> [!warning] Application conditions
> Spray while grass is **green and actively growing** (this is what the late-Aug / mid-Sept
> timing is about — see [[Meadow Timeline]]). No rain forecast for 24–48 hours, wind under
> 10 mph, early morning ideal. Avoid drought-stressed or frost-touched growth — the
> herbicide won't translocate and won't kill. Shaded front zone may green up/dormant on a
> different schedule than the sunny zones — check it separately before each application.
> Gully (part-sun) may also lag the fully sunny drainfield slightly.

> [!warning] Fruit trees and small trees — spray caution
> The drainfield's top strip has fruit trees, and the gully has many small trees. Glyphosate
> is generally safe around mature, established trees with hardened bark, but avoid drift or
> direct contact onto green bark, low limbs, suckers, foliage, or exposed roots/root flare —
> young or thin-barked trees are more vulnerable to uptake. Use a low-pressure setting or
> shield/spot-treat near trunks rather than broadcast spraying right up to the base, and
> avoid spraying on windy days near these areas in particular.

> [!note] Equipment
> Sprayer purchased: **4-gallon battery backpack sprayer**. Calibrate before first use: at
> a fixed nozzle/pressure setting, measure how much solution is actually applied over a
> known area, then set mix strength to deliver the per-acre product rate. Battery sprayers
> hold steadier pressure than manual pump units, so coverage is more consistent once dialed in.
