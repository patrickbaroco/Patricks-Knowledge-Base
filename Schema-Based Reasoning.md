## Definition

Schema-based reasoning is solving a problem by consulting a pre-built, branching framework rather than free-associating from memory. A schema takes a presenting problem (hypercalcemia, syncope, a pleuritic pain) and partitions its differential into a small number of mechanistic buckets that can be walked at the bedside. Its great virtue is on *rare* and *high-entropy* complaints, where gestalt and recall fail but a checklist consulted at the moment of framing does not.

The related idea of **schema multiplicity** ([[RLR 014 - Hypercalcemia and Immobilization|RLR 014]]) is that a strong reasoner holds *many* schemas for one problem — PTH-based, PTHrP-based, systemic-syndrome, spontaneously-resolving — like reading an EKG with extra leads, rather than one favored framework.

## How it operates

- **It replaces recall with structure.** A "40-item differential" collapses into a few moves once you impose a partition (solid/liquid/gas; intrinsic/extrinsic; the three pleural surfaces).
- **It works best where memory is weakest.** For a diagnosis seen once or never, distrust gestalt and "unleash the schema" — the framework does not care how *available* the answer is.
- **A hard constraint chooses the schema.** When two findings compete, let the one that *can't* be explained loosely (air in a hydropneumothorax) pick the schema, not the broad one (effusion).
- **Predict from the schema, then check.** A good schema lets you forecast the expected findings (hypercalcemia → nephrogenic DI → acid-base shift) and localize the answer by where observation departs from prediction.

## Examples from RLR episodes

- **Constraint-first schema.** A hydropneumothorax must be explained as a *pneumothorax first* (trauma vs spontaneous → parenchyma vs esophagus); the effusion differential generates no air. ([[RLR 012 - Dysuria and Esophageal Perforation|RLR 012]])
- **Suppressed-PTH opens a schema, not a door closed.** A low PTH doesn't end the hypercalcemia case; it opens the full non-PTH schema (PTHrP, vitamin-D/granulomatous, lytic/myeloma, milk-alkali, thyrotoxicosis, vitamin A, thiazides, immobilization). ([[RLR 014 - Hypercalcemia and Immobilization|RLR 014]])
- **Live schema construction.** The hemolysis hour builds intrinsic-vs-extrinsic scaffolding, then nests a Heinz-body/oxidative sub-schema. ([[RLR 015 - Hemolysis and Heinz Body Hemolytic Anemia|RLR 015]])
- **Schema over recall for two rare problems.** A spatial schema for pleuritic pain (three surfaces) and a distribution schema for cystic lung disease convert memory-dependent diagnoses into checklists. ([[RLR 023 - Pleuritic Chest Pain and Pulmonary Langerhans Cell Histiocytosis|RLR 023]])
- **Taming a high-entropy complaint.** Rather than free-associate on "confusion," Reza runs Dhaliwal's [[MIST Mnemonic|MIST]] plus a chem-7 fishbone and a MIST-negative residual bucket. ([[RLR 024 - Confusion and Ertapenem-Induced Encephalopathy|RLR 024]])
- **Compact schemas for overwhelming findings.** Sinus tachycardia (shock/sympathetic-toxicity/fever) and distention (solid/liquid/gas) each shrink a 40-item list to a few moves. ([[RLR 025 - Liver Chemistry Abnormalities and Intrahepatic Cholestasis of Pregnancy|RLR 025]])
- **Three interlocking schemas for syncope.** The orthostatic triad, the S's of transient loss of consciousness, and the hemorrhagic-vs-non-hemorrhagic split of hypovolemia catch each datum. ([[RLR 026 - Syncope and Occult Gastrointestinal Bleeding|RLR 026]])
- **Stacked schemas for a formless symptom.** Three-organ localization maps dizziness while triggered-vs-continuous is the real-time discriminator. ([[RLR 027 - Dizziness and Peripheral Vertigo|RLR 027]])
- **Category-first over lab-first.** Reasoning in disease categories (endocrine/malignancy/ingestion + the two I's) before the PTH fork, then predicting the electrolytes from mechanism. ([[RLR 028 - Hypercalcemia and Vitamin D Toxicity|RLR 028]])
- **A schema beats inertia at the moment of framing.** A years-copied alkaline phosphatase is fixed not by more memory but by a schema in front of you when you frame the case. ([[RLR 021 - Chronic Pruritus and Primary Biliary Cholangitis|RLR 021]])
