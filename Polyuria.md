Evergreen concept note. Polyuria as a schema — the fork that turns a big urine output into two or three moves. General physiology is scaffolding; teaching points are cited per episode.

## The definition and the first fork

- **Polyuria is >3 L/day of urine, and off diuretics it is essentially DM or DI.** The clean first split is **solute (osmotic) diuresis** — glucose, mannitol, sodium, or urea dragging water out — versus a **water (solvent) diuresis**, i.e. [[Diabetes Insipidus|diabetes insipidus]]. ([[RLR 034 - Polyuria and Post-Obstructive Nephrogenic Diabetes Insipidus|RLR 034]])

## The discriminators

- **Specific gravity and urine osmolality split solute from water.** A **dilute** urine (specific gravity ~1.001, urine osmolality 179) is a water diuresis; a solute diuresis concentrates the urine more. When the output is massive *and* dilute, think DI, not osmotic. ([[RLR 034 - Polyuria and Post-Obstructive Nephrogenic Diabetes Insipidus|RLR 034]])
- **The glucose sizes the osmotic contribution.** Hyperglycemia far too mild to explain a massive dilute output (e.g., glucose 241 in a diabetic with A1c 13) rules DM *out* as the driver and points to DI. ([[RLR 034 - Polyuria and Post-Obstructive Nephrogenic Diabetes Insipidus|RLR 034]])
- **The serum sodium places primary polydipsia.** [[Test Interpretation|Primary (psychogenic) polydipsia]] tends to run the serum sodium *low*; a normal-to-high serum sodium in a polyuric patient argues against it and toward DI, where free-water loss pushes sodium up. ([[RLR 034 - Polyuria and Post-Obstructive Nephrogenic Diabetes Insipidus|RLR 034]])

## The iatrogenic trap

- **Matching urine output with IV fluids can feed the diuresis.** In a solute or post-obstructive diuresis, running fluids to "keep up" with the output sustains it iatrogenically; stopping the fluids and using the [[Return to the Bedside|test of time]] lets the urine concentrate and both treats and clarifies the picture. ([[RLR 034 - Polyuria and Post-Obstructive Nephrogenic Diabetes Insipidus|RLR 034]])
