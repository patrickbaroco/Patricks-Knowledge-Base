Evergreen concept note. Copeptin as a measurable surrogate for ADH in the diabetes insipidus workup. Teaching points cited per episode.

## What it is and why it's used

- **Copeptin is the C-terminal fragment of the ADH precursor, co-secreted 1:1 with vasopressin — and far easier to measure.** ADH itself is unstable and hard to assay; copeptin is stable and serves as its surrogate, letting you read endogenous ADH activity from a blood test. ([[RLR 034 - Polyuria and Post-Obstructive Nephrogenic Diabetes Insipidus|RLR 034]])

## How it splits the DI fork

- **Low in central deficiency, high in nephrogenic resistance.** In [[Diabetes Insipidus|diabetes insipidus]], a **low** copeptin points to **central DI** (the hormone isn't being made/secreted), while a **high** copeptin points to **nephrogenic DI** (plenty of hormone, but the kidney won't respond). It (or a water-deprivation-plus-vasopressin challenge) is the formal way to split central from nephrogenic when the distinction changes management. ([[RLR 034 - Polyuria and Post-Obstructive Nephrogenic Diabetes Insipidus|RLR 034]])
- **Reach for it when the sequence and the scan disagree.** When [[Base Rate|base rate]] and an incidental [[Pituitary Macroadenoma|pituitary finding]] pull toward central but a nephrogenic trigger sits in the timeline, copeptin gives an objective read rather than [[Test Interpretation|reasoning from an ambiguous image]]. ([[RLR 034 - Polyuria and Post-Obstructive Nephrogenic Diabetes Insipidus|RLR 034]])
