Evergreen concept note. Definition plus worked examples, drawn from the RLR / Clinical Problem Solvers series and elsewhere. Source episodes are cited per example.

## Definition

Search satisficing is calling off the diagnostic hunt as soon as one *satisfactory* explanation is found — accepting the first sufficient answer rather than the best or the complete one. It is the near-synonym of [[Premature Closure|premature closure]], with a specific flavor: the danger is not just stopping early but stopping when a problem is *multifactorial* or has a *second, co-occurring* finding that the first explanation quietly absorbs. In radiology it is the "satisfaction of search" that misses the second fracture once the first is seen.

## How it operates

- **Stop at the first *sufficient* cause.** A plausible explanation ends the search before a better or additional one is reached — especially costly when the truth is "and, not or."
- **It can be a knowledge boundary, not a slip.** Sometimes the search satisfices because the correct branch is *absent from the schema* — an [[Illness Script|schema gap]] rather than a lapse of effort (see [[Cognitive Autopsy]]).
- **A salient early finding invites it.** A named result ("mild esophagitis," a documented UTI) that plausibly "explains it" is exactly what stops the hunt too soon.
- **It rides on [[Anchoring Bias|anchoring]] and hands off via [[Diagnostic Momentum|momentum]].** The anchor narrows the search; satisficing declares it finished; momentum carries the closed frame to the next clinician.

## Examples from RLR episodes

- **"And, not or" against single-cause bias.** Reza's insistence that hyponatremia is usually multifactorial keeps thiazide, hypovolemia, and SIADH-like physiology all in play, resisting the pull to stop at SIADH alone. ([[RLR 003 - Hypertension and Severe Hyponatremia|RLR 003]])
- **Satisficing as a schema gap.** Reza's self-audit is that the miss was not faulty reasoning but a missing branch — hereditary ATTR was absent from his polyneuropathy list, so the search satisficed at "paraneoplastic/hereditary-unspecified"; a knowledge boundary, not a cognitive slip. ([[RLR 009 - Progressive Sensorimotor Polyneuropathy|RLR 009]])
- **The satisfying early read that was the answer.** A day-one CT's "mild esophagitis" was named and moved past because most mild esophagitis is inconsequential — the search stopped at the first satisfying (urinary) explanation, when the esophagitis was the tear's earliest signature. ([[RLR 012 - Dysuria and Esophageal Perforation|RLR 012]])

## Counter-moves

- **Assume multifactorial until each contributor is excluded** — reason in "and, not or" ([[RLR 003 - Hypertension and Severe Hyponatremia|RLR 003]]).
- **Audit for schema gaps** — ask whether the search stopped because the right branch isn't on your list, then add it ([[RLR 009 - Progressive Sensorimotor Polyneuropathy|RLR 009]]).
- **Distrust the convenient early finding** — a plausible incidental can be the signature of the real disease ([[RLR 012 - Dysuria and Esophageal Perforation|RLR 012]]).
