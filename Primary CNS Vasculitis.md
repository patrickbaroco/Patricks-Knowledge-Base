## Definition

A rare small-vessel vasculitis confined to the central nervous system, diagnosed only by brain biopsy. Its clinical criteria — headache, an inflammatory CSF, and an abnormal, evolving MRI — are each necessary but individually nonspecific, and the disease is mimicked by enough more common conditions that it should function as a diagnosis of near-last resort rather than an early guess.

## How it operates

- **Vascular imaging is neither sensitive nor specific.** CTA, MRA, and even digital subtraction angiography miss the small vessels this disease affects; irregularities on CTA are far more often atherosclerosis than vasculitis. A radiologist's "cannot exclude vasculitis" should not rule it in, and a normal study should not rule it out.
- **Biopsy is the only way to confirm it — and even biopsy is imperfect.** Published pathology-outcome series ("Pathologist's Revenge") show most patients worked up for suspected primary CNS vasculitis do not have it on biopsy, and some patients treated empirically as "biopsy-negative vasculitis" turn out, at autopsy, still not to have had it.
- **Reserve the diagnosis for when every more common alternative is excluded** and headache, inflammatory CSF, and an abnormal evolving MRI are all present together.
- **Biopsy is safer than its reputation suggests** in skilled hands, and is the appropriate next step once the diagnosis is genuinely "maybe all that's left."

## Examples from RLR episodes

- **A diagnosis of last resort, reached honestly.** A neurologist who had spent years teaching residents not to over-diagnose primary CNS vasculitis names his own anchor on it in real time, only committing to the workup once headache and progressive weakness appear on top of an evolving, nonspecific lesion and a mildly inflammatory CSF — with every other explanation already excluded. Brain biopsy shows perivascular and transmural lymphocytic inflammation without necrosis, granulomas, or organisms, confirming the diagnosis; treatment is steroids and cyclophosphamide induction, then rituximab maintenance, with full recovery at one year.
