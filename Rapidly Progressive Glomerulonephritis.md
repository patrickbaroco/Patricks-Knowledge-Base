Rapidly progressive glomerulonephritis organized by renal biopsy immunofluorescence pattern into three buckets.

## The three buckets

- **Immune-complex-mediated** — lupus, endocarditis, cryoglobulinemic vasculitis, IgA vasculitis; typically hypocomplementemic (except IgA vasculitis, which is normocomplementemic).
- **Pauci-immune** — ANCA-associated vasculitis (GPA, MPA, EGPA); see [[ANCA Serology Interpretation]].
- **Anti-GBM** — linear glomerular basement membrane staining, as in Goodpasture syndrome (first described by Ernest Goodpasture during an influenza epidemic); classically a pulmonary-renal syndrome via diffuse alveolar hemorrhage plus RPGN.

## How it operates

- **Start the workup from the biopsy pattern, not the serology.** Immunofluorescence pattern should drive which serologic tests are prioritized and how conflicting serologies are interpreted.
- **Weigh biopsy over serology when they conflict.** A pauci-immune, normal-complement crescentic pattern argues against lupus nephritis even when ANA and dsDNA are both strongly positive, since lupus nephritis is expected to show an immune-complex "full house" pattern instead.
- **Diffuse ground-glass opacities plus RPGN** raise concern for diffuse alveolar hemorrhage, supporting either anti-GBM or ANCA-associated vasculitis as a pulmonary-renal syndrome.

## Examples from RLR episodes

- **Biopsy pattern overrides tempting serology.** Strongly positive ANA and dsDNA initially raise concern for lupus nephritis, but a pauci-immune crescentic pattern on kidney biopsy — with normal complement levels — argues against it, correctly redirecting the diagnosis toward ANCA-associated vasculitis (ultimately silicosis-triggered).
