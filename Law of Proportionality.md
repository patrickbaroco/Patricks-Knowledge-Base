## Definition

The law of proportionality is weighing the *magnitude* of a finding against the clinical whole, and distrusting a finding that is out of proportion to everything around it. A number that doesn't fit the rest of the picture is often the one telling the truth; a "positive" that is disproportionate to its expected corroborators is often an artifact or a different problem. Reza names it explicitly as an anti-[[Anchoring Bias|anchoring]] discipline: read the disproportion, not the single headline value.

## How it operates

- **A value out of proportion to the syndrome should be trusted over the convenient one.** When one number implies a severity the rest of the exam doesn't support, the disconnect is the signal — don't let the fitting number silence the mismatched one.
- **A "positive" with too few corroborators is a different problem.** A headline dipstick or lab that isn't matched by its expected companions points elsewhere.
- **It reframes rather than excludes.** The disproportion doesn't close a question; it relocates the center of gravity to whatever the mismatch implies.

## Examples from RLR episodes

- **Sodium of 125 vs clinically silent heart failure.** A sodium that low belongs to *florid, advanced* heart failure, so in a euvolemic-appearing patient with flat neck veins and clear lungs, the BNP earns an echo, not diuresis — trust the number that *doesn't* fit (the sodium), not the one that does (the BNP). ([[RLR 029 - Dyspnea and Disseminated Varicella-Zoster|RLR 029]])
- **"Large blood" with only a few RBCs.** A dipstick reading large occult blood with just 4 RBCs on microscopy is a disconnect: it is heme *pigment*, not [[Hematuria|hematuria]], reclassifying the case from a bleeding problem to [[Intravascular Hemolysis|intravascular hemolysis]] — and keeping ACS therapy on the table rather than withholding it for a misread. ([[RLR 030 - Dark Urine and Delayed Hemolytic Transfusion Reaction|RLR 030]])
- **Don't let the sodium hijack the case.** A striking low sodium can consume an encounter it doesn't deserve; ask whether it is severe enough to explain the presentation before spending the whole workup on it. ([[RLR 003 - Hypertension and Severe Hyponatremia|RLR 003]])
