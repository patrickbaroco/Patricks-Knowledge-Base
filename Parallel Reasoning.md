## Definition

Parallel reasoning is the practice of actively pursuing more than one diagnostic hypothesis at the same time — carrying each forward with its own workup and cognitive investment — rather than committing to a single leading diagnosis and chasing it to confirmation before the others get a fair hearing. Each live hypothesis becomes its own workstream: its own tests to order, its own disconfirming data to watch for, its own trajectory to track. The reasoner expects most of these threads to turn out unproductive and fall away, leaving the one that reaches the diagnosis.

The contrast is with **serial (sequential) reasoning**, where you pick the most probable explanation, order tests to confirm or refute it, and only move to the next hypothesis if the first collapses. Serial reasoning is faster and usually adequate, but it is the mode most exposed to [[Anchoring Bias|anchoring]] and [[Premature Closure|premature closure]]: once you are down one road, contradicting data tend to get absorbed into the leading frame ("keep testing to confirm") rather than reopening the field.

## How it operates

- **Multiple threads, each genuinely resourced.** Parallel reasoning is more than holding a broad differential. A differential is a *list*; parallel reasoning *invests* real test-ordering and interpretive effort in two or three items concurrently. The defining feature is that each hypothesis has a live workstream, not merely a place on a roster.
- **Selective, not exhaustive.** You cannot run every hypothesis in parallel or you would order infinite tests. The skill is choosing *which* few threads to carry at once — typically the most probable diagnosis plus the most dangerous-if-missed — and letting the rest sit as an unresourced differential until something promotes them.
- **Each thread watches its own trajectory.** Because the threads run together, a datum that is neutral for one hypothesis can be decisive for another. Trends (a doubling enzyme, a rising direct bilirubin, a normalizing lactate) do the work of pruning: the unproductive jogs quietly drop off while the productive one keeps going.
- **It buys insurance against anchoring.** By keeping a second frame alive and resourced, the reasoner is structurally less able to explain away disconfirming data — there is already a competing hypothesis waiting to receive it.

## Distinguished from adjacent ideas

- **Not a broad differential.** A wide differential is necessary but passive; parallel reasoning is the *active* concurrent investigation of more than one of its entries.
- **Not dual-process theory.** System 1 (intuition) vs. System 2 (analysis) is about *how* you think; parallel reasoning is about *how many hypotheses* you run at once. You can reason in parallel using either system.
- **Related to but distinct from [[Dual Diagnosis|dual-diagnosis]] thinking.** Dual diagnosis is the recognition that *two processes coexist in this patient*; parallel reasoning is the *method* of keeping competing (often mutually exclusive) hypotheses in play until one wins. The two overlap when the parallel workups both turn out positive.

## Examples from RLR episodes

- **Two 12-mile jogs at once.** A well-appearing man with a single transient episode of chest pain and a quiet AST/ALT bump. Reza never lets the chest-pain complaint own the case: he keeps the cardiac thread alive (repeat troponin, CK, EKG, the [[4+2+2 Chest Pain Schema|4+2+2 schema]]) *while simultaneously* working the transaminitis as its own independent problem (hepatocellular pattern → extrahepatic sources of AST/ALT → the biliary tree). Both threads run in parallel; the cardiac one falls off and the liver-enzyme one reaches [[Ascending Cholangitis|ascending cholangitis]] from a common-bile-duct stone. Rabih names this explicitly in his reflection — the diagnostic path is not one road but several traversed at once, hoping the unproductive ones fall away — and calls sustaining two disciplined workouts at once elite-athlete territory. ([[RLR 020 - Chest Pain and Ascending Cholangitis|RLR 020]])
- **Treating for two diagnoses at once.** Rather than choose between decompensated heart failure and a possible superimposed cholecystitis, the team diureses hard *for the heart* while *keeping antibiotics* for the gallbladder, letting each thread be settled on its own evidence (the brisk response to diuresis becoming its own diagnostic test). Parallel reasoning carried all the way into management. ([[RLR 013 - Right Upper Quadrant Pain and Heart Failure|RLR 013]])
- **Keeping the differential open after the first positive.** During an mpox outbreak, the team deliberately kept HSV, VZV, and secondary syphilis as live threads alongside mpox, so a positive syphilis test did not close the search — which is how the co-infection was still caught. Running the alternatives in parallel, not serially, prevented an early stop. ([[RLR 004 - Rectal Pain and Proctitis|RLR 004]])

## Examples elsewhere

- **The trauma "kill you first" list.** Advanced trauma care runs several catastrophic hypotheses in parallel by protocol — airway, tension pneumothorax, hemorrhage, tamponade are all evaluated and acted on concurrently rather than one-at-a-time, precisely because serial workup would be too slow to catch the lethal one.
- **Empiric coverage while awaiting cultures.** Broad antibiotics that cover several plausible organisms at once, pending speciation, is parallel reasoning expressed as therapy: you resource multiple hypotheses simultaneously and narrow as data return.
- **The "worst-case in parallel with most-likely" ED reflex.** Chest pain worked simultaneously for ACS, PE, and dissection — each with its own test — is the everyday instantiation: the most dangerous hypotheses get their own live workup regardless of which is most probable.

## When to deploy it (and its costs)

- **Deploy when a dangerous hypothesis and a probable one diverge.** If the most likely diagnosis and the most lethal one call for different workups, run both threads rather than betting the encounter on the probable one.
- **Deploy when an unexplained abnormality won't fit the leading story.** A datum that the leading hypothesis cannot absorb (here, deranged liver enzymes in a chest-pain patient) is the cue to open a second resourced thread rather than explain it away.
- **The cost is real and bounds the technique.** Parallel reasoning is cognitively demanding and test-intensive; run too many threads and you order everything and anchor on nothing. Discipline means each thread stays a *rigorous* workup, not hand-waving — the failure mode is letting a parallel thread degrade into a vague "we're also keeping an eye on the liver" that never actually gets tested.
- **Prune deliberately.** The payoff comes from letting threads *fall away* on their trajectory. Name what would retire each hypothesis up front, and actually retire it when that evidence arrives, so you converge rather than accumulate.
