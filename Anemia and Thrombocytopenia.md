Evergreen concept note. The five-cause schema for a coexisting anemia and thrombocytopenia. Teaching points cited per episode.

## The five-cause schema

When anemia and thrombocytopenia appear *together*, the differential collapses to five patterns — the reflex diagnosis is the first, but the discipline is to run all five:

1. **[[Microangiopathic Hemolytic Anemia|MAHA]]** — the no-miss. Mechanical shearing (TTP/HUS, DIC, malignant hypertension, mechanical valve) destroys red cells and consumes platelets. A two-hit anemia-plus-thrombocytopenia should reflexively raise MAHA the way chest pain raises ACS/PE/dissection.
2. **Sepsis** — the most common; usually a *mild* anemia and thrombocytopenia, and often accompanied by a neutrophilic leukocytosis.
3. **Reduced production** — a marrow process suppressing both lines at once ([[Myelodysplastic Syndrome|MDS]], leukemic infiltration, aplastic states).
4. **Separate mechanisms** — the two cytopenias are *not* from one cause (e.g., anemia of chronic disease plus superimposed heparin-induced thrombocytopenia).
5. **One is causing the other** — low platelets → bleeding → anemia, or chronic hemolysis → splenic sequestration → thrombocytopenia.

## Reasoning points

- **Assume the most morbid, confirm the most likely.** Stop and assume MAHA (scrutinize the smear for schistocytes) while recognizing that in a septic, sick patient the most *probable* answer is ordinary sepsis-associated cytopenias with no localizing value. ([[RLR 033 - Persistent Altered Mental Status and Spinal Epidural Abscess|RLR 033]])
- **The smear checklist: schistocytes, heparin, blasts.** Rabih's "hold your breath" rule — anemia or thrombocytopenia with a smear means look for schistocytes (MAHA), a heparin exposure (HIT), and blasts (leukemia) before defaulting to DIC; the default is usually right, which is why the exceptions need a hard-wired checklist. Trend the smear, since blasts a first look missed can appear later. ([[RLR 032 - Fever and Chronic Myelomonocytic Leukemia|RLR 032]])
- **MAHA is the reflex, but read the pace.** A two-hit anemia + thrombocytopenia triggers MAHA reflexively; compensated/chronic hemolysis behaves differently and rarely presents this way. ([[RLR 015 - Hemolysis and Heinz Body Hemolytic Anemia|RLR 015]])
