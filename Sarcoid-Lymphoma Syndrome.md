Sarcoid-lymphoma syndrome is the well-described coexistence of lymphoma with sarcoidosis, the basis for the rule never to lock in a sarcoid diagnosis at time zero.

## The Association
- **Elevated lymphoma risk in sarcoid.** A meaningful minority of sarcoid patients — on the order of **~10–15%** in the teaching frame — harbor a lymphoma **at diagnosis or within about two years** of it ([[RLR 060 - Transient Loss of Consciousness and Cardiac Sarcoidosis|RLR 060]]).
- **Two directions of the relationship:**
  - Sarcoidosis (and its chronic immune activation / immunosuppressive therapy) may predispose to lymphoma.
  - A **low-grade lymphoma can induce a sarcoid-like paraneoplastic granulomatous reaction** — non-caseating granulomas in nodes or organs that mimic sarcoid ([[RLR 060 - Transient Loss of Consciousness and Cardiac Sarcoidosis|RLR 060]]).

## Why It Matters — Never Lock In Sarcoid
- **You can never diagnose sarcoid with certainty at time zero** ([[RLR 060 - Transient Loss of Consciousness and Cardiac Sarcoidosis|RLR 060]]). Non-caseating granulomas with negative stains and cultures are compatible with sarcoid *and* with a granulomatous reaction to lymphoma (or other triggers). The practical question is only whether you are confident enough to immunosuppress.
- This is a specific instance of a broader [[Granulomatous Disease]] humility: sarcoid is a **diagnosis of exclusion** requiring a compatible picture, non-caseating granulomas, and exclusion of infection (TB, fungi) and malignancy.

## Management Principle — Treat Both
- **Treat the granulomatous disease while continuing to hunt the trigger.** The paradigm mirrors other "second-process" scenarios: **lymphoma-driven hypercalcemia** and **malignancy-associated HLH** — you treat the downstream process *and* the underlying malignancy ([[RLR 060 - Transient Loss of Consciousness and Cardiac Sarcoidosis|RLR 060]]).
- Practically: maintain vigilance with interval imaging and repeat/adequate biopsy; a discordant course, atypical nodes, or B-symptoms should re-open the lymphoma question even after granulomas are found.
