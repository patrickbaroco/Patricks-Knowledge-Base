Evergreen concept note. Transient loss of consciousness (TLOC) is the symptom-level presentation that "syncope" prematurely names; it is worked through the four S's and split by the quality of recovery. RLR / Clinical Problem Solvers teaching points are cited per episode; general clinical detail is scaffolding.

## Reframe the Complaint
Patients do not experience "syncope" — a *conclusion* — they experience **transient loss of consciousness**. Starting at TLOC keeps the differential open ([[RLR 060 - Transient Loss of Consciousness and Cardiac Sarcoidosis|RLR 060]]).

- **The four S's:** **Syncope, Seizure, Stroke/TIA, Sugar (hypoglycemia)** — the schema for any TLOC ([[RLR 060 - Transient Loss of Consciousness and Cardiac Sarcoidosis|RLR 060]]). See [[Schema-Based Reasoning]].

## Split by Recovery — Clean vs Impaired
- **Clean recovery to a completely clear sensorium → syncope.** True syncope (transient global cerebral hypoperfusion) reverses fully and fast.
- **Impaired/lingering recovery → the other three.** Post-ictal confusion (seizure), persistent focal deficit (stroke/TIA), or shakiness/altered mentation slow to clear (hypoglycemia) point away from syncope ([[RLR 060 - Transient Loss of Consciousness and Cardiac Sarcoidosis|RLR 060]]).

## The History Does the Work
- **Pre-event:** aura or unilateral numbness (partial seizure), exertional onset (cardiac), post-prandial timing (hypoglycemia), prolonged standing / pain / micturition / defecation (reflex), positional change on standing (orthostatic).
- **Post-event:** the recovery quality above; tongue-biting/incontinence and prolonged confusion favor seizure.
- **Chronicity as a discriminator.** Recurrent TLOC over **years without accumulating deficits must be syncope** — repeated seizures, strokes, or hypoglycemia would leave a wake ([[RLR 060 - Transient Loss of Consciousness and Cardiac Sarcoidosis|RLR 060]]). See [[Illness Tempo]].

## Syncope Subtypes
Once TLOC is narrowed to syncope, subclassify:

- **Reflex (neurally mediated) / vasovagal** — triggered by pain, emotion, prolonged standing, or Valsalva-type events (micturition, defecation, cough); prodrome of warmth/nausea/diaphoresis; usually benign.
- **Orthostatic** — on standing, from volume depletion, autonomic failure, or medications.
- **Cardiac** — arrhythmic (brady, e.g., **[[AV Block]]**; or tachy) or structural/obstructive (aortic stenosis, HOCM, PE, tamponade). Exertional or supine syncope, absent prodrome, structural disease, or an abnormal EKG marks the highest-risk group — pursue EKG/telemetry/echo. Advanced conduction disease (e.g., from [[Cardiac Sarcoidosis]]) can present exactly this way ([[RLR 060 - Transient Loss of Consciousness and Cardiac Sarcoidosis|RLR 060]]).
