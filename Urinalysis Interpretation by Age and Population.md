The diagnostic weight of pyuria and bacteriuria on a urinalysis depends heavily on the population being tested, not just the number itself.

## How it operates

- **The same result means different things in different patients.** In older adults, asymptomatic bacteriuria and pyuria are common background "noise" that usually should not be treated. In a young, otherwise healthy patient, the same finding is much more likely to reflect actionable infection.
- **Test characteristics are population-specific, not just diagnosis-specific.** A heuristic learned in one population ("don't treat asymptomatic bacteriuria") can systematically under-weight a genuinely meaningful finding when reflexively applied to a different population.
- **A positive nitrite plus pyuria without squamous cells** suggests a well-collected sample and an Enterobacteriaceae organism (capable of converting nitrate to nitrite) — more specific than leukocyte esterase alone.
- **Consider spillover before assuming a primary urinary source.** Pyuria can also reflect irritation from an adjacent process (severe appendicitis, diverticulitis) rather than a primary urinary infection; weigh this before closing the case on urine findings alone.

- **Age recalibrates the pretest probability.** Pyuria with nitrites and no dysuria or flank pain, in an asymptomatic-appearing young woman, is initially treated with skepticism (the older-adult heuristic), but her youth is exactly what makes the finding likely to be meaningful — and it is.
