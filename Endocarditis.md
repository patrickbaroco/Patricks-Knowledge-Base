Infective endocarditis is microbial infection of the endocardium/valves; this note emphasizes conduction disease from perivalvular (aortic-root) abscess — the not-to-miss in the AV-block-plus-fever patient.

## Why Endocarditis Causes Heart Block
The aortic valve sits adjacent to the membranous septum and the AV node/His bundle. Infection extending beyond the valve into the annulus forms a **perivalvular / aortic-root abscess** that mechanically and inflammatorily destroys nearby conduction tissue.

- **New or progressive [[AV Block]] in a febrile patient is a red flag for a perivalvular abscess** and para-valvular extension — the **not-to-miss** in the AV-block + fever + troponin trifecta ([[RLR 060 - Transient Loss of Consciousness and Cardiac Sarcoidosis|RLR 060]]).
- Abscess most often complicates **aortic-valve** and **prosthetic-valve** endocarditis; a lengthening PR interval on serial EKGs is an early warning.

## The AV-Block + Fever Differential
Alongside endocarditis, the infectious conduction diseases include **[[Lyme Carditis]]** (the U.S. champion), **rheumatic fever** (PR prolongation is a minor Jones criterion), and **Chagas** — all layered on the base-rate [[AV Block]] schema ([[RLR 060 - Transient Loss of Consciousness and Cardiac Sarcoidosis|RLR 060]]).

## Diagnosis — Modified Duke Criteria (brief)
- **Major:** (1) **positive blood cultures** with typical organisms (viridans strep, S. aureus, enterococci, HACEK) or persistent bacteremia; (2) **echocardiographic evidence** of endocardial involvement — vegetation, abscess, new prosthetic dehiscence — or new valvular regurgitation.
- **Minor:** predisposing heart condition/IVDU, fever, vascular phenomena (emboli, Janeway lesions), immunologic phenomena (Osler nodes, Roth spots, GN), and suggestive microbiology not meeting major.
- **TEE** outperforms TTE for **abscess** and prosthetic valves and should be pursued when abscess is suspected despite a non-diagnostic TTE (negative cultures and no vegetation lower but do not exclude the diagnosis).

## Management
- **Prolonged targeted IV antibiotics** guided by cultures/susceptibilities.
- **Surgery** for perivalvular abscess/heart block, heart failure from valve destruction, large mobile vegetations/embolization, or difficult organisms/prosthetic infection.
- **Pacing** for high-grade block; block from abscess is often *not* reversible with antibiotics alone and may require surgery plus a permanent device.
