A distribution-based schema for pulmonary nodules on CT.

## The three patterns

- **Centrilobular** — points toward aspiration, hypersensitivity pneumonitis, or, less classically, disseminated infection.
- **Scattered (hematogenous)** — suggests blood-borne spread of infection (TB, fungi) or malignancy (miliary pattern).
- **Perilymphatic** — suggests lymphatic spread of cancer or sarcoidosis.

## How it operates

- **No schema is bulletproof.** Disseminated infection can occasionally present with a centrilobular pattern despite the classic teaching favoring hematogenous spread for infection — don't over-fit the pattern to a single cause.
- **Combine with epidemiology, not instead of it.** A centrilobular pattern in a patient with a high regional base rate for an endemic infection still warrants testing for that infection even when the distribution itself points elsewhere.

## Examples from RLR episodes

- **Centrilobular nodules in a high-endemic-risk patient.** Diffuse centrilobular nodules in a Central Valley construction worker initially favor aspiration or hypersensitivity pneumonitis by distribution, while a strong regional base rate keeps coccidioidomycosis serologies in play despite the atypical pattern; the true cause (silicosis-triggered ANCA vasculitis) becomes clear only once new renal and hemorrhagic findings emerge.
