Evergreen concept note. "Acute but persistent" — a tempo category for a finding that appeared *abruptly* in a single step and then *stayed flat*, distinct from a slowly-sneaking "chronic" process. Naming it correctly relinks the finding to its inciting event.

## The distinction

The usual tempo ladder — hyperacute, acute, subacute, chronic (and, from [[RLR 063 - Diarrhea and Behçet's Disease|RLR 063]], **recurrent**) — can mislabel a finding that has simply been present for a long time. A lab value that was normal, dropped in one step on a specific date, and then held steady is **not "chronic"** in the sense of an indolent, gradually-evolving disease. It is **acute but persistent**: the *onset* was abrupt (pointing at a discrete trigger), even though the *duration* is now long. Reading duration as "chronic" scatters the differential toward slow processes; reading the abrupt onset correctly points straight at *what happened on that date*.

## Worked example (RLR 064)

A "chronic" thrombocytopenia (platelets ~200k → 60k) was reframed once the timeline showed the drop began **in a single step in February**, coinciding with a TAVR, and then stayed flat. Calling it *acute but persistent* rather than chronic relinked it to the valve — pointing to a perivalvular leak with valve hemolysis and platelet consumption, rather than to HIV, HCV, or ITP. ([[RLR 064 - Hematuria and Post-TAVR Perivalvular Leak|RLR 064]])

## How to apply it

- **Get the shape of the curve, not just its length.** Pull the trend: was the change a *step* (acute-but-persistent) or a *slope* (truly chronic)? Old values are often available for free.
- **Anchor onset to events.** For a step change, ask what procedure, drug, or exposure lines up with the date — the trigger is usually there.
- **Don't let a long duration launder an acute onset.** "It's been abnormal for months" can wrongly retire the search for an inciting cause.

This complements [[Illness Tempo|tempo-based reasoning]] and the [[Recurrence|recurrent-disease]] category: tempo is not only *how fast* but *what shape*.
