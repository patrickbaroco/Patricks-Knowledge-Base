Triaging "flank pain" before assuming it is renal.

## How it operates

- **Deep versus superficial is the first branch.** Pain that is mechanical — worse with movement, leaning, or position — favors a superficial, musculoskeletal (paraspinal) source; pain independent of movement favors a deep (renal) source. Most reported "flank pain" is actually the superficial, mechanical kind.
- **Flank pain is not just kidney and back.** The lower lobe/pleura, the adrenal gland, the pelvic organs (including ovarian torsion), the aorta, and the spleen can all present as flank pain.
- **A subacute systemic tail should reopen the differential.** Weight loss or fatigue layered onto an otherwise classic colicky story should prompt consideration of the alternatives above rather than anchoring on a single, convenient explanation (e.g., a kidney stone).
- **A remote, asymptomatic stone does not predict future symptoms.** Kidney stone disease behaves like gallstone disease — most stones sit quietly, and only a subset ever obstruct and cause pain (see [[Base Rate]]).

## Examples from RLR episodes

- **A classic story with a systemic tail.** Paroxysmal left flank pain with a remote asymptomatic stone looks like straightforward nephrolithiasis, but several months of unintentional weight loss and fatigue keep the broader differential alive in the background — ultimately revealing an obstructing stone in a kidney compromised by an underlying myeloproliferative neoplasm, not an isolated stone event.
