Evergreen concept note. A qualitative platelet defect of advanced renal failure — normal platelet count and PT/PTT, but impaired platelet function causing mucosal and procedural bleeding. RLR / Clinical Problem Solvers teaching points are cited per episode; general clinical detail is scaffolding.

## Concept
In the [[Bleeding Framework|bleeding framework]], uremic platelet dysfunction is a **qualitative platelet** problem: the platelet count is normal (or only modestly low) and PT/PTT are normal, yet platelets aggregate and adhere poorly ([[RLR 064 - Hematuria and Post-TAVR Perivalvular Leak|RLR 064]]). It is one of the three classic qualitative-platelet causes alongside **medications** and **genetic** defects ([[RLR 064 - Hematuria and Post-TAVR Perivalvular Leak|RLR 064]]).

## Pathophysiology
Multifactorial dysfunction of primary hemostasis in uremia:
- **Retained uremic toxins** (e.g., guanidinosuccinic acid, phenolic compounds) impair platelet activation and granule release.
- **Nitric oxide excess** and abnormal platelet–vessel wall interaction.
- **Impaired platelet–vWF/GPIb interaction** and altered GPIIb/IIIa function.
- **Anemia of CKD** contributes: fewer red cells means platelets are less marginated toward the vessel wall, reducing platelet–endothelial contact (part of why correcting anemia helps).

Severity roughly tracks the degree of uremia; **inadequate or shortened dialysis** worsens it ([[RLR 064 - Hematuria and Post-TAVR Perivalvular Leak|RLR 064]]).

## Clinical Features
- **Mucocutaneous bleeding** — ecchymoses, epistaxis, GI bleeding, prolonged oozing after procedures or vascular access.
- **Normal platelet count and coagulation screen**, so it is easily missed unless sought.

## Management
Directed at improving platelet function ([[RLR 064 - Hematuria and Post-TAVR Perivalvular Leak|RLR 064]]):
- **Dialysis** — optimizing (and not truncating) sessions removes uremic toxins and is foundational.
- **DDAVP (desmopressin)** — rapid, short-lived release of vWF/factor VIII; useful before procedures or for active bleeding (tachyphylaxis limits repeat dosing).
- **Cryoprecipitate** — provides vWF and factor VIII for more significant bleeding.
- **Conjugated estrogens** — slower onset but longer-lasting improvement.
- **Correct anemia** — raising hematocrit (transfusion or ESAs) improves platelet marination and function.

## Reasoning Note
Uremic platelet dysfunction rarely causes catastrophic bleeding on its own; it typically **amplifies** bleeding from a coexisting structural lesion. When bleeding is out of proportion, consider it as one contributor alongside other qualitative defects and [[Acquired von Willebrand Syndrome|acquired vWD]] ([[RLR 064 - Hematuria and Post-TAVR Perivalvular Leak|RLR 064]]).
