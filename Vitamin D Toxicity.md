Vitamin D toxicity as a cause of hypercalcemia.

## Illness script

- **Severe, persistent, and slow to clear.** [[Vitamin D Metabolism|Cholecalciferol]] (D3) is fat-soluble and leaches out of adipose stores for up to ~18 months, so the hypercalcemia is refractory long after the vitamin is stopped. ([[RLR 028 - Hypercalcemia and Vitamin D Toxicity|RLR 028]])
- **Manifestations are protean, all downstream of the [[Hypercalcemia|hypercalcemia]]:** neuropsychiatric change, [[Nephrogenic Diabetes Insipidus|nephrogenic DI]] and AKI, gastrin-stimulated gastritis/ulcers, and even bone thinning from chronic low-grade hypercalcemia. ([[RLR 028 - Hypercalcemia and Vitamin D Toxicity|RLR 028]])

## The second-hit model

- **Many people take high-dose vitamin D without significant hypercalcemia — risk climbs sharply with a *second hit*.** Layering calcium carbonate onto a host with no calcium reserve is what sends the calcium to 16, the same [[Physiologic Reserve|reserve logic]] as delirium on dementia or AKI on CKD; the picture can be an [[Hickam's Dictum|AND]] (vitamin D toxicity + [[Milk-Alkali Syndrome|milk-alkali]]), not an OR. ([[RLR 028 - Hypercalcemia and Vitamin D Toxicity|RLR 028]])

## Diagnosis and management

- **Confirm with the [[Vitamin D Metabolism|vitamin D metabolites]] read as a pair** — a high 1,25 with a *high* 25-hydroxy indicates ingestion (vs the low 25 of granulomatous/PTHrP conversion). Ask specifically about the dose and the actual pills. ([[RLR 028 - Hypercalcemia and Vitamin D Toxicity|RLR 028]])
- **Treatment is withdrawal plus [[Prednisone|prednisone]]**, with IV fluids, calcitonin, and bisphosphonate/denosumab as adjuncts — and it is the one hypercalcemia where you will *not* need to co-prescribe vitamin D with the steroid. Watch for [[Nephrocalcinosis|nephrocalcinosis]] and CKD. ([[RLR 028 - Hypercalcemia and Vitamin D Toxicity|RLR 028]])
