Calcium pyrophosphate deposition disease is a spectrum — from radiographic chondrocalcinosis to acute pseudogout to calcific tendinitis — whose crystals are notoriously easy to miss.

## Definition and Pathophysiology
Deposition of **calcium pyrophosphate dihydrate (CPP)** crystals in cartilage and periarticular tissues. Deposition is often age-related and may be associated with metabolic disease (hyperparathyroidism, hemochromatosis, hypomagnesemia, hypophosphatasia).

## The CPPD Spectrum
- **Chondrocalcinosis** — radiographic calcification of cartilage; the crystals "folded into" the cartilage ([[RLR 061 - Knee Swelling and Calcific Tendinitis|RLR 061]]). Often asymptomatic and incidental — its presence predicts that CPPD is *present*, not that it is driving today's picture.
- **Pseudogout** — acute CPP crystal arthritis, clinically mimicking gout (commonly the knee or wrist).
- **Calcific tendinitis** — CPPD can deposit in and inflame a tendon, not only a joint ([[RLR 061 - Knee Swelling and Calcific Tendinitis|RLR 061]]). See [[Calcific Tendinitis]].

## Crystal Identification
- **Rhomboid (rod-shaped)** crystals that are **weakly positively birefringent** under polarized light — contrast with the needle-shaped, strongly negatively birefringent crystals of [[Gout]].
- **Crystals are hard to see and prone to false-negatives** ([[RLR 061 - Knee Swelling and Calcific Tendinitis|RLR 061]], [[RLR 062 - Oligoarthritis and Polyarticular Gout Flare|RLR 062]]) — their weak birefringence and small size make them easy to miss on [[Synovial Fluid Analysis|synovial fluid]] microscopy, so a negative crystal exam does not exclude CPPD.
- Like all crystals, they persist in the joint and are only *active* when intracellular — see [[Crystals in Macrophages]].

## Clinical and Diagnostic Notes
- **Chondrocalcinosis on x-ray plus a non-inflammatory tap is a coincidence, not the cause** ([[RLR 061 - Knee Swelling and Calcific Tendinitis|RLR 061]]) — apply the [[Law of Proportionality]] when the cell count cannot explain the severity.
- Still **rule out [[Septic Arthritis|infection]]** in any acute CPP-associated joint.

## Management
- Acute attacks — NSAIDs, colchicine, or corticosteroids (intra-articular or systemic).
- Address associated metabolic conditions; no therapy dissolves existing deposits.
