## Summary

> [!summary] Scan Summary
> A Neurology VMR on **"[[Bilateral Leg Weakness|bilateral leg weakness]]"** that is really a masterclass in **[[Hickam's Dictum|Hickam's dictum]]** and **[[Diagnostic Uncertainty|diagnostic humility]]** — the rare VMR that **never reaches a unifying diagnosis**. A **man in his 70s** develops **≤3 months of progressive bilateral leg weakness** (can't rise from a chair or climb stairs → walker → commode) plus a large medicine "shopping list": weeks of **frank diarrhea**, nocturnal cough, orthostatic lightheadedness, one episode of urinary incontinence, 10-lb weight loss, and arm stiffness — on a background of **bipolar disorder** (20 years of perphenazine, recently stopped for a presumed **[[Drug-Induced Parkinsonism|drug-induced parkinsonian]]** tremor), a resected **GIST** (Whipple), diabetes, hypertension, and CKD. Berkowitz frames the localization crisply: **bilateral leg weakness is "spine or below"** — [[Spinal Cord|cord]], [[Cauda Equina|cauda equina]], [[Radiculopathy|root]], [[Peripheral Neuropathy|nerve]], [[Neuromuscular Junction|NMJ]], or [[Myopathy|muscle]] — **rarely brain** (parasagittal), *if* the "weakness" is truly weakness and not stiffness or ataxia. The whole episode is an exercise in **[[Unifying Diagnosis|lumping vs splitting]]**: split off the parkinsonism as drug-induced, package the leg (± diaphragm) weakness, and ask whether the weight loss + cancer history means **[[Paraneoplastic Neurologic Syndrome|paraneoplastic]]** disease or **[[Complex Bread and Butter|"complex bread and butter"]]** (ordinary diagnoses — spondylosis + neuropathy + parkinsonism — overlapping into a confusing gestalt). Exam shows **diffuse muscle [[Muscle Atrophy|atrophy]]** with only **mild, diffuse weakness** and **minimal length-dependent sensory loss** — a pattern that points to **[[Lower Motor Neuron|lower motor neuron]]** disease and raises the often-forgotten **[[Motor Neuron Disease|motor neuron disease]]**, while the **subacute tempo of a rapidly progressive neuropathy** flags **[[CIDP|CIDP]]**, **[[Paraproteinemic Neuropathy|paraprotein-associated]]** neuropathies (**[[POEMS Syndrome|POEMS]]**), and **[[Nutritional Neuropathy|nutritional]]** causes (**[[Beriberi|beriberi]]** — *"IV thiamine before IVIg"*). Then every confirmatory test **muddies** rather than clarifies: MRI shows **[[Cauda Equina|cauda equina]] and sacral root enhancement** (nerve), but EMG shows **myopathic units** and thigh MRI shows **muscle edema/enhancement** (muscle); **anti-NXP2** returns low-positive (a [[Dermatomyositis|dermatomyositis]] antibody), yet muscle biopsy shows **atrophic fibers without inflammation**; a **[[Ganglioside Antibodies|ganglioside panel]]** lights up (GM1, MAG, GD1b) and **[[Neurofilament Light Chain|NfL]]** is markedly elevated but **non-specific**; SPEP/VEGF (against POEMS), paraneoplastic panel, PET, and heavy metals are all negative. The team, like Berkowitz, lands on a **working diagnosis of immune-mediated neuropathy** (possibly a **[[Nodopathy|nodo-/paranodopathy]]** on the CIDP spectrum), superimposed on **[[ICU-Acquired Weakness|critical-illness weakness]]** from three septic ICU stays (recurrent **[[Stercoral Colitis|stercoral colitis]]** driven by a **[[Constipation–Retention Cycle|constipation cycle]]**). He gets IVIG, stabilizes, and dies ~6 months later **with no final diagnosis in the chart**. Coda: *the same uncertainty the experts wrestled with is the lesson* — real-world reasoning sometimes ends without the drumroll, and the discipline is to keep rearranging the pieces (Occam ⇄ Hickam) rather than force a fit.

## Pearls

- **[[Bilateral Leg Weakness|Bilateral leg weakness]] is "spine or below" — rarely brain.** Take it at face value and the [[Anatomical Approach|localization]] runs [[Spinal Cord|spinal cord]] → [[Cauda Equina|cauda equina]] → [[Radiculopathy|nerve root]] → [[Peripheral Neuropathy|nerve]] → [[Neuromuscular Junction|NMJ]] → [[Myopathy|muscle]]; only a **midline parasagittal** lesion on the leg homunculus fools you from above. But first ask whether "weakness" is *truly* weakness, or **stiffness, spasticity, or ataxia** — patients use "weak" for all of them. **[[Bowel and Bladder Dysfunction|Bowel/bladder]]** involvement pushes toward **cord or cauda equina** (you should *not* get it from nerve/NMJ/muscle).
- **[[Muscle Atrophy|Atrophy]] localizes to the [[Lower Motor Neuron|lower motor neuron]] — peripheral nerve, advanced muscle disease, or the *forgotten* [[Motor Neuron Disease|motor neuron disease]].** NMJ disease does **not** cause atrophy; central lesions cause only **disuse** atrophy. So diffuse atrophy with **minimal sensory loss** and **relatively preserved strength** should make you think **motor neuron disease** — the category we skip because our localization scheme files "nerve" and "motor neuron" separately.
- **Read atrophy against weakness: [[Muscle Atrophy|atrophy out of proportion to weakness]] is nerve; weakness out of proportion to atrophy is muscle.** A useful bedside discriminator when the EMG isn't back — here the severe diffuse atrophy with only mild weakness leans **neurogenic**.
- **A [[Peripheral Neuropathy|neuropathy]] that progresses over *weeks-to-months* is a red flag — it is not behaving like a garden-variety [[Diabetic Neuropathy|diabetic]] axonal neuropathy.** [[Illness Tempo|Subacute]] tempo raises **[[CIDP|CIDP]]**, **[[Paraproteinemic Neuropathy|paraprotein-associated]]** neuropathies (**[[POEMS Syndrome|POEMS]]** — polyneuropathy, organomegaly, endocrinopathy, M-spike, skin changes; clues are papilledema and elevated **VEGF**), and **[[Nutritional Neuropathy|nutritional]]** causes. Send **[[SPEP|SPEP with immunofixation]]** + free light chains.
- **In a malnourished patient with a fast neuropathy, give [[Thiamine|thiamine]] first — *"IV thiamine before IVIg."*** **[[Beriberi|Beriberi]]** (B1 deficiency) can mimic **[[Guillain-Barré Syndrome|Guillain-Barré]]**; **[[ANAN|acute nutritional axonal neuropathy]]** is the rapid nutritional variant; **[[B12 Deficiency|B12]]** usually disables through **sensory ataxia** (dorsal columns), not frank weakness. Thiamine levels take days to return, so **treat empirically**.
- **A confirmatory test *confirms* — but when tests conflict, weigh the *whole picture*, not the last result.** Here **[[Cauda Equina|cauda equina]] root enhancement** (nerve) collides with **myopathic EMG units + thigh-muscle edema** (muscle) and a low-positive **[[Anti-NXP2 Antibody|anti-NXP2]]**; the team resolves it clinically — nerve wins — and treats the **muscle findings as [[ICU-Acquired Weakness|critical-illness]] artifact / red herrings**. **[[Neurofilament Light Chain|Neurofilament light]]** is the *"CRP of the nervous system"*: high means injury, but points nowhere.
- **Not every case ends with a diagnosis — and that is a teachable outcome.** This one never resolves; the patient dies without a name in the chart. The discipline Berkowitz models is to **put every piece on the table and rearrange it** — lump, split, remove the parkinsonism, repackage the edema — cycling deliberately between **[[Occam's Razor|Occam]]** and **[[Hickam's Dictum|Hickam]]** rather than forcing parsimony onto a patient who may simply have several things at once.

## Problem Representation

A man in his 70s with bipolar disorder (two decades of perphenazine, recently stopped for a presumed drug-induced parkinsonian tremor), a resected GIST (prior Whipple), well-controlled type 2 diabetes, hypertension, and CKD presents with **≤3 months of progressive, symmetric bilateral leg weakness** — losing the ability to rise from a chair, climb stairs, and eventually transfer — accompanied by weeks of **frank diarrhea**, nocturnal cough, orthostatic lightheadedness, a single episode of urinary incontinence, ~10-lb weight loss, and bilateral arm stiffness. Examination reveals **diffuse muscle atrophy** (triceps, forearms, intrinsic hand and foot muscles, quadriceps) with only **mild, diffuse weakness** (4+/5 arms, 4−/5 legs), **cogwheel rigidity and bradykinesia** (attributed to drug-induced parkinsonism), a chronic right lower-facial droop, and only **mild length-dependent pinprick loss** in the feet — a **lower-motor-neuron-predominant, motor-greater-than-sensory** pattern. Labs show anasarca with hypoalbuminemia, alkaline phosphatase ~1100 with low vitamin D, and a normal CK. Neuroimaging shows **cauda equina and sacral nerve-root enhancement** and subtle right facial-nerve enhancement; CSF shows **albuminocytologic dissociation** (protein 85, one cell). The subsequent course is dominated by **three ICU admissions for septic shock from recurrent stercoral colitis** and progressive deconditioning; EMG (limited by anasarca) shows **myopathic units**, thigh MRI shows **muscle edema and enhancement**, and a broad serologic workup returns a **low-positive anti-NXP2**, a **positive ganglioside panel** (GM1, MAG, GD1b), and a **markedly elevated neurofilament light chain**, while SPEP/immunofixation, VEGF, paraneoplastic panel, PET-CT, and heavy metals are **negative** and muscle biopsy shows **atrophic fibers without inflammation**. The working diagnosis is an **immune-mediated (CIDP-spectrum) neuropathy** superimposed on **critical-illness weakness**; no unifying diagnosis is ever established, and the patient dies approximately six months later.

## Case Narrative

Andrew Sanchez, a hospitalist at Beth Israel Deaconess, presents a man in his 70s with **[[Bilateral Leg Weakness|bilateral leg weakness]]** of uncertain onset but **no more than three months**, severe enough to progress from difficulty rising from a chair to needing a walker and then a bedside commode. The history is deliberately noisy: weeks of **frank diarrhea**, a **nocturnal cough**, **orthostatic lightheadedness**, one episode of **urinary incontinence**, **~10 lb of weight loss**, and **arm stiffness** — layered on **bipolar disorder** (20 years of perphenazine, stopped by his neurologist for a **[[Drug-Induced Parkinsonism|drug-induced parkinsonian]]** resting/action tremor), a resected **GIST** (Whipple four years prior), well-controlled diabetes, hypertension, and CKD.

The discussants localize before they hypothesize. Sarah anchors on the **[[Bowel and Bladder Dysfunction|urinary incontinence]]** to favor a **[[Spinal Cord|spinal]]** process over pure peripheral nerve, flags an autoimmune "weakness + bowel/bladder" possibility (she names **DPPX**), and warns against jumping to a diagnosis before localizing. Valeria widens the [[Anatomical Approach|localization]] — [[Corticospinal Tract|corticospinal tract]], cord, root, nerve, NMJ, or muscle — noting the *proximal* "can't-rise-from-a-chair" weakness first suggested **muscle**, but incontinence and stiffness fit muscle poorly; she keeps a **[[CIDP|CIDP]]**-type neuropathy, a myelopathy, and a systemic/inflammatory or **recurrent-cancer** process in play.

Berkowitz sets the frame that carries the episode: **take bilateral leg weakness at face value and it is "spine or below"** — cord, **[[Cauda Equina|cauda equina]]** (the frequently-forgotten one), root, nerve, NMJ, or muscle — and **rarely brain** (a midline **parasagittal** lesion on the leg homunculus). The arms and legs *could* both be involved in a nerve/NMJ/muscle process (legs just get noticed first, through gait), so he keeps asking the pivotal question: **is the "weakness" truly weakness, or is it the stiffness — or ataxia?** Exam will decide via **[[Upper vs Lower Motor Neuron|upper- vs lower-motor-neuron]] signs**. He is skeptical of the "diarrhea," wondering whether it is really **autonomic** bowel dysfunction or overflow, and notes that neurologic bowel/bladder disease usually makes the **bladder** loud and the bowel quiet.

Asked to **[[Unifying Diagnosis|lump or split]]**, the group leans toward lumping but can't ignore the medicine. Sarah proposes **splitting** the parkinsonism (drug-induced) from the weight-loss-plus-weakness syndrome, and raises **[[Metastasis|metastatic]]** GIST as a possible unifier (lung mets → cough, spine mets → weakness). Valeria puzzles over the **nocturnal cough** and whether the brainstem/high cord or **[[Diaphragmatic Weakness|diaphragm]]** could be involved. Berkowitz uses the moment to teach **[[Orthopnea|orthopnea]]**: diaphragmatic weakness produces orthopnea (as does heart failure), with a classic timing difference after lying down, and he separates **[[Multiple System Atrophy|MSA]]** (dysautonomia + parkinsonism, but the legs shouldn't be *weak*) from the picture. Crucially, he names the trap the whole case embodies — **"[[Complex Bread and Butter|complex bread and butter]]"**: ordinary diagnoses (cervical spondylosis *plus* peripheral neuropathy; Parkinson's *plus* lumbar stenosis) combining in a complicated patient to produce a gestalt that *looks* like a zebra but is really common things overlapping.

The **exam** reframes everything. Vitals are unremarkable; there is **2+ peripheral edema to the thighs**. The neuro exam shows **moderate-to-severe diffuse [[Muscle Atrophy|atrophy]]** (triceps, forearms, first dorsal interossei, quadriceps, intrinsic foot muscles), **cogwheel rigidity and slow finger-tapping** (worse on the left), only **mild diffuse weakness** (4+/5 in the arms, 4−/5 in the legs), a **chronic right lower-facial droop**, and **length-dependent pinprick loss** confined to feet and calves. Reflexes are only partially recorded (a left pectoral reflex, negative Hoffman). Labs: mild leukocytosis and anemia, **normal CK**, **albumin 1.9**, **alkaline phosphatase ~1100** with **vitamin D 12**; CT abdomen shows **anasarca, pleural effusions, and a large stool burden**; chest x-ray shows lower-lobe opacities.

Berkowitz teaches off the atrophy: **[[Muscle Atrophy|atrophy localizes to the lower motor neuron]]** — peripheral nerve or *advanced* muscle disease — and, importantly, to the **[[Motor Neuron Disease|motor neuron disease]]** we forget because we file "nerve" and "motor neuron" separately. Diffuse atrophy over three months with **minimal sensory loss** and **strength better than the atrophy would predict** is **neurogenic and motor-predominant**. The **mild length-dependent pinprick loss** is best **split off** as background **[[Diabetic Neuropathy|diabetic]]/CKD** neuropathy rather than evidence of a severe sensorimotor process (which would look far worse on the sensory exam). He weighs **[[ALS|ALS]]** (usually starts focally and spreads "around the clock"; flail-arm/flail-leg and bulbar variants exist) and reminds the group that **motor neuron disease does not explain the high alk phos, low albumin, or low vitamin D** — while noting low vitamin D can itself cause a myopathy, and that AST/ALT can rise from *muscle*, not just liver (here they're normal, and CK is normal, arguing against a florid myopathy).

He then pulls the **medicine** back onto the table and rearranges: **anasarca, pleural effusions, constipation (autonomic?), and a motor neuropathy** — a **rapidly progressive neuropathy** with lots of systemic findings neurologists struggle to explain. That combination raises **[[Paraproteinemic Neuropathy|paraprotein-associated neuropathy]]**, above all **[[POEMS Syndrome|POEMS]]** (polyneuropathy, organomegaly, endocrinopathy, M-spike, skin changes; clues include papilledema and elevated **VEGF**), alongside **[[CIDP|CIDP]]** and **[[Nutritional Neuropathy|nutritional]]** causes. On the nutritional thread he teaches **[[Beriberi|beriberi]]** (B1/[[Thiamine|thiamine]]; can mimic **[[Guillain-Barré Syndrome|GBS]]**, and can cause wet beriberi heart failure), **[[ANAN|ANAN]]** (acute nutritional axonal neuropathy), and **[[B12 Deficiency|B12]]** (usually **sensory ataxia**, not weakness). Andrew supplies the memorable Marcus Pinto pearl — ***"sometimes the patient needs IV thiamine before IVIg"*** — the nutritional GBS-mimic that must be treated empirically. The group's plan converges: **MRI spine**, **[[Nerve Conduction Studies|EMG/nerve conduction studies]]** ("is this neuropathy, motor neuron disease, or both — and if neuropathy, axonal or demyelinating?"), **empiric thiamine**, and a nutritional/paraprotein screen.

The **data arrive — and conflict.** MRI brain shows **subtle right facial-nerve enhancement**; MRI spine shows **[[Cauda Equina|cauda equina]] and sacral nerve-root enhancement** (seen in GBS/CIDP and other polyradiculopathies); CSF shows **[[Albuminocytologic Dissociation|albuminocytologic dissociation]]** (protein 85, one cell) — all pointing at **nerve/root**. But the course then derails into **three ICU admissions for septic shock** from **recurrent [[Stercoral Colitis|stercoral colitis]]** (a **[[Constipation–Retention Cycle|constipation]]**-driven catastrophe), progressive **dysphagia** requiring a feeding tube, and deconditioning. The EMG — **limited by anasarca** — shows **myopathic motor units**, and **MRI of the thighs** shows **muscle edema with interstitial enhancement** — pointing at **muscle**. Now nerve and muscle findings sit side by side.

The confirmatory workup only deepens the split. **SPEP/immunofixation and free light chains** are negative and **VEGF normal** (POEMS off the table); **paraneoplastic panel and PET-CT** are negative (arguing against the [[Paraneoplastic Neurologic Syndrome|paraneoplastic]] disease the fast tempo had suggested); heavy metals negative; vitamins normal. The **myositis panel** returns a **low-positive [[Anti-NXP2 Antibody|anti-NXP2]]** (a **[[Dermatomyositis|dermatomyositis]]** antibody with a cancer association), but the **muscle biopsy** shows only **scattered atrophic fibers without inflammatory infiltrate**, and the **[[Ganglioside Antibodies|ganglioside panel]]** lights up (**GM1**, **MAG**, **GD1b**; anti-histone too) while the **[[Nodopathy|nodal/paranodal]]** antibodies (neurofascin, contactin) are **negative**. **[[Neurofilament Light Chain|Neurofilament light]]** is markedly elevated (365 vs ULN 35) but, as Berkowitz frames it, is the **"CRP of the nervous system"** — high signals injury, points nowhere.

The team resolves the conflict **clinically, not serologically**: the **objective evidence favored neuropathy** (root enhancement, albuminocytologic dissociation) over the **weaker myopathy** signal, so the low-positive **anti-NXP2** and the myopathic EMG/thigh MRI are read as **red herrings** or **[[ICU-Acquired Weakness|critical-illness weakness]]** in a repeatedly septic, edematous patient whose EMG could never be done in controlled conditions. They call it a **CIDP-spectrum immune-mediated neuropathy** — Andrew's reading of the modern literature stratifies **CIDP into [[Nodopathy|nodopathies/paranodopathies]]** (often IgG4-mediated, frequently **IVIG-refractory**, treated with **[[Rituximab|rituximab]]**, with deceptively "clean" pathology). Berkowitz walks the ganglioside antibodies one by one — **GM1** (**[[Multifocal Motor Neuropathy|multifocal motor neuropathy]]**, but that's asymmetric mononeuropathy multiplex, not this), **anti-MAG** (**[[DADS|DADS]]** — distal acquired demyelinating *sensory*), **GD1b** — and treats them as probably **epiphenomena** of an injured, inflamed nerve rather than the cause.

There is no clean ending. The patient receives **IVIG**, his weakness **stabilizes**, and other problems take center stage; he **dies roughly six months later** with **no unifying diagnosis in the chart**. Berkowitz closes on exactly that: it is valuable to discuss a case that **does not resolve** with a "drum-roll" diagnosis, because it mirrors real practice — and the reasoning discipline is to keep **rearranging the pieces**, moving deliberately between **[[Occam's Razor|Occam's razor]]** and **[[Hickam's Dictum|Hickam's dictum]]**, rather than forcing a single name onto a patient who may simply have had **several things at once**.

### Illness Scripts and Bias

- **[[Anatomical Approach|Localization first]]: "bilateral leg weakness is spine or below."** The scheme (cord → cauda equina → root → nerve → NMJ → muscle, rarely parasagittal brain) plus the discipline of asking whether "weakness" is really **stiffness or ataxia** — decoded on exam by **[[Upper vs Lower Motor Neuron|UMN vs LMN]]** signs and **[[Bowel and Bladder Dysfunction|bowel/bladder]]** involvement.
- **[[Muscle Atrophy|Atrophy]] as a localizing sign.** Atrophy = **[[Lower Motor Neuron|lower motor neuron]]** (nerve, advanced muscle, or **[[Motor Neuron Disease|motor neuron disease]]**); NMJ spares it; central lesions cause only **disuse** atrophy. "Atrophy out of proportion to weakness = nerve; weakness out of proportion to atrophy = muscle."
- **[[Illness Tempo|Tempo]] as a red flag.** A neuropathy worsening over **weeks–months** is *not* ordinary diabetic axonal disease; subacute tempo raises **[[CIDP|CIDP]]**, **[[Paraproteinemic Neuropathy|paraprotein]]** (**[[POEMS Syndrome|POEMS]]**), **[[Paraneoplastic Neurologic Syndrome|paraneoplastic]]**, and **[[Nutritional Neuropathy|nutritional]]** causes.
- **[[Unifying Diagnosis|Lumping vs splitting]] made explicit — Occam ⇄ Hickam.** The signature move of *putting every piece on the table and rearranging it*: split the parkinsonism, package the leg ± diaphragm weakness, repackage the anasarca/effusions/constipation. When the pieces won't cohere, **[[Hickam's Dictum|Hickam's dictum]]** ("a patient can have as many diseases as they please") beats forced parsimony.
- **[[Complex Bread and Butter|Complex bread-and-butter]].** Common diagnoses (spondylosis + neuropathy; Parkinson's + stenosis) overlapping in a sick patient masquerade as a zebra — a named cognitive trap for the "gestalt is confusing, the parts are mundane" case.
- **[[Test Interpretation|Test interpretation]] under conflict.** Root enhancement + albuminocytologic dissociation (nerve) vs myopathic EMG + thigh-muscle edema (muscle) vs a low-positive **[[Anti-NXP2 Antibody|anti-NXP2]]** and a lit-up **[[Ganglioside Antibodies|ganglioside panel]]**; resolved by weighting the **whole clinical picture** and demoting weak/late tests to **red herrings**. **[[Neurofilament Light Chain|NfL]]** = the nervous system's **CRP**. **[[ICU-Acquired Weakness|Critical-illness weakness]]** as the great EMG confounder.
- **[[Base Rate|Base rate]] and don't-miss.** Paraneoplastic and POEMS are invoked on "zebra hunts" but are **rare** (Berkowitz: he's never seen a DPPX case); the fast tempo + cancer history justified screening, and the screen was negative — probability weighed against morbidity.
- **[[Diagnostic Uncertainty|Diagnostic uncertainty as the endpoint]].** The meta-lesson: real cases sometimes **don't resolve**; naming a *working* diagnosis (immune-mediated neuropathy) and continuing to weigh alternatives is itself sound reasoning.
- **[[Illness Script|Illness scripts]].** [[CIDP|CIDP]] and its **[[Nodopathy|nodopathy/paranodopathy]]** subtypes; **[[Multifocal Motor Neuropathy|MMN]]** (anti-GM1); **[[DADS|DADS]]** (anti-MAG); **[[POEMS Syndrome|POEMS]]** and **[[Paraproteinemic Neuropathy|paraproteinemic neuropathies]]**; **[[Motor Neuron Disease|ALS]]** and variants; **[[Beriberi|beriberi]]**/**[[ANAN|ANAN]]**/**[[B12 Deficiency|B12]]**; **[[Dermatomyositis|dermatomyositis]]**/**[[Immune-Mediated Necrotizing Myopathy|IMNM]]**; **[[Drug-Induced Parkinsonism|drug-induced parkinsonism]]** and **[[Multiple System Atrophy|MSA]]**; **[[ICU-Acquired Weakness|critical-illness weakness]]**.

## Teaching Discussion

- Localize bilateral leg weakness before hypothesizing: it is spinal cord, cauda equina, root, nerve, neuromuscular junction, or muscle, and only rarely a midline parasagittal brain lesion — and always confirm the complaint is weakness rather than stiffness, spasticity, or ataxia by looking for upper- versus lower-motor-neuron signs and for bowel/bladder involvement (which points toward cord or cauda equina, not nerve/NMJ/muscle).
- Treat diffuse muscle atrophy as a lower-motor-neuron sign — peripheral nerve, advanced muscle disease, or the easily-forgotten motor neuron disease — remembering that the neuromuscular junction does not cause atrophy and that central lesions cause only disuse atrophy; when atrophy is out of proportion to weakness, think nerve, and when weakness is out of proportion to atrophy, think muscle.
- Recognize a subacute (weeks-to-months) progressive neuropathy as a red flag that mandates a broader workup than routine diabetic neuropathy — CIDP, paraprotein-associated neuropathies (POEMS, with its papilledema and elevated VEGF), paraneoplastic disease, and nutritional causes — and always add SPEP with immunofixation and free light chains.
- In a malnourished patient with rapidly progressive weakness, give empiric thiamine before immunotherapy ("IV thiamine before IVIg"): beriberi can mimic Guillain-Barré, ANAN is its acute axonal form, and thiamine levels return too slowly to wait on.
- When confirmatory tests conflict, weight the whole clinical picture rather than the most recent or most exotic result: cauda equina root enhancement and albuminocytologic dissociation localize to nerve, and myopathic EMG units plus thigh-muscle edema in a repeatedly septic, anasarcous ICU patient are better explained by critical-illness weakness than by a second primary muscle disease — and neurofilament light chain, like CRP, confirms injury without naming a cause.
- Make lumping-versus-splitting explicit and cycle deliberately between Occam's razor and Hickam's dictum: put every finding on the table, try removing one (the drug-induced parkinsonism) or repackaging others (anasarca, effusions, constipation), and beware "complex bread and butter" — ordinary diagnoses overlapping in a complex patient to counterfeit a zebra.
- Accept diagnostic uncertainty as a legitimate endpoint: some real cases never yield a unifying diagnosis, and the right practice is to name a working diagnosis, treat what is treatable, and keep the differential open rather than forcing a premature label.
