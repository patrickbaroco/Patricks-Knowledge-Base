## Pearls

- **Dyspnea is more than cardiac-versus-pulmonary — decompose it into oxygenation, ventilation, and perfusion, then reproduce the physiology to localize it.** Walk the patient: desaturation implicates **oxygenation**, a rise in work of breathing with a stable saturation implicates **[[Small Airways Disease|ventilation]]**, and tachycardia/hypotension implicates **perfusion** — and resist letting a single clue (a syncope that is actually **[[Vasovagal Syncope|vagal]]**) falsely box the lesion.
- **Slow tempo localizes to the airways, because the airways carry the most reserve.** Dyspnea on exertion progressing over years in someone who can still cross-country ski is **[[Illness Tempo|airways disease until proven otherwise]]**; an infiltrative cardiomyopathy, coronary disease, or interstitial lung disease severe enough to cause years of exertional dyspnea would rarely still be a diagnostic mystery.
- **"MINOCA of the airways": distal mucus impaction with a normal DLCO points to small-airways/bronchiolar disease — obstruction you cannot see endoscopically.** Just as **[[MINOCA|MINOCA]]** is ischemia without a visible epicardial culprit, **[[Bronchiolitis|bronchiolitis]]** is a ventilatory defect beyond the trachea and mainstem bronchi; the **[[Base Rate|base rate]]** for a ventilatory defect is still middle-airway disease (COPD/asthma), so distal impaction on CT plus a preserved **[[DLCO|DLCO]]** is what earns the rarer small-airways label.
- **Chronic pancreatitis is a "substance," not a "structure."** Structural causes (gallstones, cancer) declare themselves *acutely* and get source-controlled, so they rarely produce *chronic* pancreatitis; **[[Chronic Pancreatitis|chronic]]** disease means a molecule "coming and going" — most often unrecognized **alcohol**, otherwise hypertriglyceridemia, drugs, or **genes** (**[[CFTR|CFTR]]**, PRSS1, SPINK1, CTRC). A highly active athlete makes occult heavy alcohol implausible, pushing toward a genetic cause.
- **Unify by physiology, and let colonization stage the airway disease.** Small-airways disease plus **[[Exocrine Pancreatic Insufficiency|exocrine pancreatic insufficiency]]** (cystic pancreatic replacement, steatorrhea, low **[[Fat-Soluble Vitamin Deficiency|fat-soluble vitamins A/D/E/K]]**, borderline **[[Stool Elastase|stool elastase]]**) share a defect in **[[Mucociliary Clearance|secretion clearance]]** — the **[[CFTR|CFTR]]**/cilia axis — and **[[Aspergillus|Aspergillus]] colonization** signals the disease has progressed from bronchiolitis to **[[Bronchiectasis|bronchiectasis]]**, because distal bronchiolitis does not get colonized by nasty organisms.
- **Cystic fibrosis is a spectrum, not all-or-none — a negative panel and low-intermediate sweat chloride do not exclude a mild CFTR-related disorder.** Common-mutation panels miss rare/functional variants, and **[[Sweat Chloride Test|sweat chloride]]** *fluctuates* in **[[CFTR-Related Disorder|CFTR-RD]]** (documented CF exists below 30); more deeply, **[[What Is a Diagnosis|a diagnosis is a best estimate of natural history and how to help]]**, so a well-characterized, treatable illness (airway clearance, pancreatic enzyme replacement) is not "nothing" merely because it lacks a label.

## Problem Representation

A previously highly athletic never-smoker in his early 70s (past history of osteoarthritis, rotator-cuff injuries, hyperlipidemia, and gout) develops a chronic "tickle-in-throat" cough with a CT-negative chest and, over more than two years, insidiously progressive dyspnea on exertion, cough productive of tenacious clear phlegm, wheeze, and chest tightness, without fever or hemoptysis. Cardiac evaluation (coronary CTA, nuclear stress) is non-ischemic, laboratory studies show no eosinophilia and a normal IgE, and serial imaging reveals distal small-airway mucus impaction (upper-lobe predominant) with a preserved DLCO, while spirometry marches from an FEV1/FVC of 0.71 to 0.44. An acute febrile admission with a lobar pneumonia, transient nodules with ground-glass halos, and Aspergillus terreus in sputum uncovers near-complete cystic replacement of the pancreas with exocrine pancreatic insufficiency (steatorrhea, 20-pound weight loss, low vitamins A, D, and zinc, borderline stool elastase), and follow-up imaging shows new subtle bronchiectasis. The unifying hypothesis is a defect in secretion clearance (CFTR/ciliary), but a common-CFTR-mutation panel is negative, sweat chlorides are 24–25 mmol/L, and a hereditary-pancreatitis panel (CFTR, PRSS1, SPINK1, CTRC) is negative — leaving a working diagnosis of an atypical, late-presenting CFTR-related disorder (mild end of the cystic fibrosis spectrum) affecting the airways and pancreas while sparing fertility, managed supportively.

## Case Narrative

*A guest episode of RLR with Andrew Sanchez and Michael Arcieri of the* Diagnostic Atypia *podcast, who present a seven-aliquot case built to showcase the four-dimensional (time) nature of diagnosis. The patient's own line — "Galileo is crazy too" — titles it.*

**Aliquots 1–2 (the cough begins).** A man met in the hospital at 73 — an athlete (biking 15 miles/day, hiking, lap-swimming), never-smoker, with only osteoarthritis, rotator-cuff injuries, hyperlipidemia, and gout — first presented two years earlier with a months-long **[[Chronic Cough|intermittent cough]]** ("tickle in throat," phlegm, reflux controlled with Tums). A CT chest showed only a *possible* small upper-esophageal mass; an esophagram found no mass and mild dysmotility. **[[GERD|Omeprazole]]** didn't help; albuterol helped mildly. Reza frames community-acquired chronic cough as usually benign — **[[GERD|reflux]]**, **cough-variant asthma**, ACE-inhibitors, postnasal drip, irritants — with an empiric PPI or bronchodilator as reasonable diagnostic tests, the one must-not-miss being **lung cancer** (a negative CT makes it improbable). New asthma at 71 is unusual; he would reassure, monitor, review meds, and "keep the door open" rather than chase rare diagnoses. The story then accretes: a **positive lactose breath test** (milk-triggered postprandial emesis), and a **[[Vasovagal Syncope|syncopal]]** admission (post-emetic, stood up too fast) that revealed new **[[Atrial Fibrillation|AFib with RVR]]** (started on metoprolol). PFTs show an **[[FEV1/FVC|FEV1/FVC of 0.71]]**, FEV1 70%, without bronchodilator testing; he can reach ~80% of his usual exercise capacity.

**Aliquot 3 (localizing to the airways).** Reza decomposes **[[Dyspnea on Exertion|dyspnea]]** into three mechanisms — **[[Oxygenation Ventilation Perfusion|oxygenation, ventilation, perfusion]]** — and would **walk the patient** to reproduce and localize the physiology (desaturation → oxygenation; increased work of breathing with stable saturation → ventilation; tachycardia/hypotension → perfusion), cautioning that the syncope looks **vagal**, not cardiac, so it should not force a perfusion story. Over two years the disease worsens (barely finishing a cross-country ski, winded on one flight of stairs), cardiac workup is non-ischemic (coronary CTA mild, stress negative), labs show no eosinophilia and a normal IgE, and a repeat CT shows **distal small-airway mucus impaction, upper-lobe predominant**, with a **normal [[DLCO|DLCO]]**; the **[[FEV1/FVC|FEV1/FVC falls 0.71 → 0.44]]**. Rabih localizes: the falling ratio confirms a **[[Small Airways Disease|ventilatory defect]]**, and the **[[Illness Tempo|slow tempo]]** — years of exertional dyspnea in someone still able to exert — means **airways disease until proven otherwise**, because the airways hold the most **reserve** (an infiltrative cardiomyopathy, CAD, or ILD causing years of DOE would rarely still be a mystery). He coins "**[[MINOCA|MINOCA]] of the airways**": as MINOCA is ischemia without a visible epicardial culprit, this is a **[[Bronchiolitis|bronchiolar]]** obstruction invisible on bronchoscopy — and while the **[[Base Rate|base rate]]** favors *middle*-airway disease (COPD/asthma), the distal impaction pushes toward **small-airways disease / bronchiolitis**. Reza adds that he normally sees mucus plugging only in aspirating, poor-cough patients (dementia), so in a pristine athlete it suggests a problem *moving secretions upward* — a **[[Mucociliary Clearance|ciliary/clearance]]** defect — with the normal DLCO reassuring that the alveolar-capillary interface is intact.

**Aliquots 4–5 (the second organ).** He is admitted acutely ill — fever, worsening cough, dyspnea at rest, nausea/vomiting, abdominal discomfort, AFib with RVR, hypotension, hypoxemia — with WBC 18.5k and CRP >300. A CT abdomen/pelvis shows a lobar pneumonia and, incidentally, a **pancreas nearly replaced by innumerable cysts**; an MRCP confirms **near-complete cystic replacement of the pancreatic parenchyma** ("pancreatic cystosis"). Reza reasons by **[[Analogical Reasoning|analogy]]** from renal cysts (PKD vs CKD-related), asks whether the cysts are a *specific* disease or a *marker of lost function*, and — cued by the fat-soluble picture later — recognizes **[[Exocrine Pancreatic Insufficiency|exocrine pancreatic insufficiency]]**. Review of systems fills in: **20-pound weight loss**, friends noting frailty, **steatorrhea** (greasy floating stools), decades of low-grade dyspepsia, no acute pancreatitis episodes, no nasal polyps, recurrent childhood-rearing sinus infections, ~⅓ cup clear sputum daily, and preserved fertility (he has children). Rabih reframes: is the cyst *degeneration NOS* (like CKD cysts) or specific? — noting pancreatic degeneration usually **calcifies** rather than cysts, a possible clue.

**Aliquots 6–7 (converging on CFTR, and its limits).** Labs cement **[[Fat-Soluble Vitamin Deficiency|fat-soluble vitamin deficiency]]** (vitamin A 7, D 20, low zinc; E/K normal; MMA normal; A1c 5.6; normal alpha-1-antitrypsin) with a **[[Stool Elastase|stool elastase]]** of 237 (above the 200 cutoff, but that cutoff is ~60% sensitive for mild insufficiency). CT shows the pneumonia plus transient **nodules with peripheral ground-glass halos**; sputum grows **[[Aspergillus|Aspergillus terreus]]** with normal beta-D-glucan/galactomannan/antibodies and a negative ANCA. Family history is smoke-and-cancer heavy (both parents likely COPD, died of lung cancer; a sister with pancreatic cancer). He improves on antibiotics, **[[Pancreatic Enzyme Replacement|pancreatic enzymes (Creon)]]**, and aggressive **airway clearance** (hypertonic saline, nebs); a follow-up CT shows resolution of the pneumonia and nodules, new upper-lobe mucus plugging, and **new subtle [[Bronchiectasis|bronchiectasis]]** (noted by pulmonology, not the radiologist). Rabih works **[[Chronic Pancreatitis|chronic pancreatitis]]** as **[[Substance vs Structure|substance versus structure]]**: structural causes (gallstones, cancer) declare acutely and get controlled, so *chronic* pancreatitis means a molecule "coming and going" — usually unrecognized **alcohol**, otherwise hypertriglyceridemia, drugs, or **genes** — and the patient's athleticism makes occult heavy drinking implausible, favoring a **[[CFTR|CFTR]]**/genetic substance. He stages the lungs with the **[[Aspergillus|Aspergillus]]**: colonization by nasty bugs is a feature of **[[Bronchiectasis|bronchiectasis]]** (middle airways), not distal bronchiolitis, so the disease has progressed proximally — and upper-lobe (especially right>left) predominance further favors **[[Cystic Fibrosis|CF]]**. He crosses the **testing threshold**: sweat chloride, CFTR genetics, and possibly functional testing (nasal potential difference, intestinal current measurement). The results, however, are **non-diagnostic**: the common-mutation panel is negative, **[[Sweat Chloride Test|sweat chlorides are 24–25]]** (negative <30, though documented CF exists below 30), and a hereditary-pancreatitis panel (CFTR, PRSS1, SPINK1, CTRC) is negative. Specialists conclude "**not typical CF**," and the workup stalls.

**The synthesis.** Andrew reframes CF as a **[[CFTR-Related Disorder|spectrum]]**: two severe variants → classic CF; a severe plus a mild, or two mild, → **CFTR-related disorder (CFTR-RD, >30% CFTR function)**; milder still → **CFTR-associated disease (>50% function)** needing environmental triggers (here possibly **cannabis**). The phenotype fits — pancreas exhausted first (gone by presentation), lungs declaring later, fertility spared (the CFTR-RD reproductive spectrum runs from **CBAVD/infertility** to mere oligospermia; ~80% of infertile men carry ≥1 CFTR variant) — and sweat chloride is known to **fluctuate** across normal-to-high in CFTR-RD. Reza's advocacy point: an athlete who loses that much function over two years is *not* normal, and a negative test doesn't exclude an underlying driver — echoing how "psychosis NOS" patients later proved to have **[[CPS 359 - Altered Mental Status and Anti-NMDA Receptor Encephalitis|anti-NMDA-receptor encephalitis]]**, and how genuinely new diseases (e.g., VEXAS) keep being described. Mike adds an **[[Asthma Mimics|asthma-mimics]]** master-class (COPD overlap; a low **[[DLCO|DLCO]]** should redirect away from pure asthma; **[[Cardiac Asthma|heart failure]]** producing spirometric "asthma" — don't test spirometry when "wet"; GERD, obesity, postnasal drip, **[[Vocal Cord Dysfunction|vocal-cord dysfunction]]** co-occurring ~30%; bronchiectasis from traction or genes). Rabih closes on the philosophical core — **[[What Is a Diagnosis|what a diagnosis actually is]]**: a best estimate of natural history and how to help. Even unlabeled, this man's course (slow erosion of airways and pancreas, other organs spared) and management (supportive **airway clearance + [[Pancreatic Enzyme Replacement|enzyme replacement]]**, possibly an empiric CFTR-modulator trial) are clear — so the absence of a name leaves little on the table, and the diagnosis is best held as "behaving on the CFTR spectrum."

### Illness Scripts and Bias

- **Decomposing dyspnea ([[Oxygenation Ventilation Perfusion|O₂/ventilation/perfusion]]).** Beyond cardiac-vs-pulmonary, the mechanism is localized by *walking the patient* and reading the physiology — with the discipline not to let a **[[Vasovagal Syncope|vagal syncope]]** falsely anchor a perfusion story.
- **[[Illness Tempo|Tempo]] and reserve ([[Anatomical Approach|anatomical approach]]).** Years of progressive DOE in a still-exercising patient localize to the **[[Small Airways Disease|airways]]**, which tolerate disease longest; a heart-muscle, coronary, or ILD process that advanced would have declared itself.
- **[[Analogical Reasoning|Analogical reasoning]], twice.** "**[[MINOCA|MINOCA]] of the airways**" (invisible distal obstruction) for the lungs, and **renal-cyst analogies** (PKD-specific vs CKD-nonspecific) for the pancreas — each a bridge from a familiar concept to an unfamiliar organ.
- **[[Base Rate|Base rate]] held and overridden.** Most ventilatory defects are middle-airway (COPD/asthma); the distal CT impaction and preserved **[[DLCO|DLCO]]** are what justify the rarer **[[Bronchiolitis|bronchiolitis]]** label.
- **[[Substance vs Structure|Substance vs structure]] for chronic pancreatitis.** Structural causes declare acutely and get source-controlled; chronic disease implies a recurring **substance** — usually occult alcohol, here a **[[CFTR|gene]]** — a clean reframing that redirects the workup.
- **[[Unifying Diagnosis|Unification]] by physiology.** Two secretory organs (airway + pancreas) failing to clear secretions point to the **[[Mucociliary Clearance|CFTR/ciliary]]** axis; **[[Aspergillus|Aspergillus]] colonization** is used to *stage* the airway disease (bronchiolitis → **[[Bronchiectasis|bronchiectasis]]**).
- **[[Test Interpretation|Test interpretation]] at the edges.** A "normal" **[[Stool Elastase|stool elastase]]** (~60% sensitive for mild disease), a **[[Sweat Chloride Test|sweat chloride]]** that fluctuates in **[[CFTR-Related Disorder|CFTR-RD]]** (CF documented <30), and negative *common*-mutation panels that miss rare/functional variants — none excludes the diagnosis.
- **[[Diagnostic Humility|Humility]] and [[What Is a Diagnosis|"what is a diagnosis?"]].** The advocacy not to dismiss an unlabeled illness (the anti-NMDA/VEXAS lesson), the "**zone of learning**" stance toward the unknown, and the reframing of diagnosis as an estimate of *natural history and how to help* — so a well-characterized, treatable CFTR-spectrum illness is a sufficient working diagnosis.

## Teaching Discussion

- Decompose dyspnea into oxygenation, ventilation, and perfusion rather than only cardiac versus pulmonary, and reproduce the physiology by walking the patient to localize the defect, while refusing to let a single clue (a vagal syncope, a borderline lab) prematurely box the lesion.
- Localize by tempo: dyspnea on exertion that progresses over years in a patient still capable of vigorous exercise is airways disease until proven otherwise, because the airways carry the greatest functional reserve and other localizations would have declared themselves.
- Read distal small-airway mucus impaction with a preserved DLCO as bronchiolitis — a ventilatory defect invisible on bronchoscopy — while remembering that the base rate for obstruction is middle-airway disease (COPD, asthma), so the rarer label must be earned by imaging and physiology.
- Approach chronic pancreatitis as a substance rather than a structure, since structural causes present acutely and are source-controlled; when occult alcohol is implausible, pursue genetic causes (CFTR, PRSS1, SPINK1, CTRC) and confirm exocrine failure with stool elastase and fat-soluble vitamin levels.
- Unify multi-organ disease by physiology: airway plus pancreatic secretion failure points to the CFTR/ciliary clearance axis, and use airway colonization by organisms like Aspergillus to recognize progression from bronchiolitis to bronchiectasis.
- Treat cystic fibrosis as a spectrum: negative common-mutation panels and low-intermediate, fluctuating sweat chloride values do not exclude a CFTR-related disorder, and preserved fertility does not exclude milder CFTR phenotypes, so functional testing and clinical correlation remain necessary.
- Keep asthma mimics in view — COPD overlap, heart failure (do not perform spirometry when volume-overloaded), GERD, obesity, postnasal drip, vocal-cord dysfunction, and bronchiectasis — and let a low DLCO in a presumed asthmatic redirect toward a destructive process.
- Hold diagnosis as an estimate of natural history and how to help: when the trajectory and management are clear, an unlabeled but well-characterized illness is a legitimate working diagnosis, and advocacy for the patient (as with once-unnamed entities like anti-NMDA-receptor encephalitis or VEXAS) matters more than a definitive name.
