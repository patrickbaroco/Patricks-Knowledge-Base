## Summary

> [!summary] Scan Summary
> A neurology Virtual Morning Report with neurologist **Aaron Berkowitz** that spends half its time on the wrong schema — and turns on the moment the team abandons it. A **72-year-old man** has a year of **episodic transient loss of consciousness**, several times a month, each preceded by dizziness, occurring **shortly after eating**, lasting seconds to minutes with prompt recovery and **no post-ictal confusion**. Berkowitz works the classic **[[Syncope vs Seizure|syncope-versus-seizure]]** distinction in depth (before/during/after features, all with "thousands of exceptions" — [[Convulsive Syncope|convulsive syncope]], the poker-table "EEG of the heart," the German fainting-student videos), and broadens to hypoglycemia/[[Insulinoma|insulinoma]], narcolepsy, and why [[Transient Ischemic Attack|TIA]] doesn't belong. But the **review of systems** — a year of **frequent falls** (independent → walker) and **progressive urinary incontinence** — is the real case. It is the discussant **Ravi's** utterance of *"Parkinsonism"* that breaks Berkowitz's [[Anchoring Bias|anchor]] on the loss of consciousness and re-sorts the cards toward a **[[Parkinson-Plus Syndromes|Parkinson-plus]]** neurodegenerative syndrome. The exam seals it: **[[Neurogenic Orthostatic Hypotension|neurogenic orthostatic hypotension]]** (BP falls without a compensatory heart-rate rise), a coarse resting tremor with **[[Rigidity vs Spasticity|cogwheel rigidity]]**, a shuffling wide-based gait, **cerebellar dysmetria and dysdiadochokinesia**, and a low MMSE — Parkinsonism *plus* cerebellar signs *plus* prominent [[Dysautonomia|dysautonomia]]. With normal labs and a (non-diagnostic) MRI and only a partial, transient [[Levodopa Response|levodopa]] response, the unifying diagnosis is **[[Multiple System Atrophy|multiple system atrophy]]** (the dysautonomia-predominant **[[Shy-Drager Syndrome|Shy-Drager]]** phenotype), with side-teaching on the [[Romberg Sign|Romberg sign]] as proprioceptive (not cerebellar), the [[Hot Cross Bun Sign|hot cross bun sign]], and [[Synucleinopathy|synuclein-vs-tau]] classification.

## Pearls

- **"Episodic transient loss of consciousness" is [[Syncope vs Seizure|syncope vs seizure]] first — using before/during/after, all imperfect.** *Before:* a vasovagal prodrome (lightheadedness, warmth, diaphoresis, tunneling vision) favors [[Syncope|syncope]]; a rising epigastric sensation, fear, or foul smell is a temporal-lobe aura. *During:* rhythmic tonic-clonic movements, eyes open and deviated, and **lateral** tongue-biting favor seizure; loss of tone favors syncope. *After:* prompt full recovery favors syncope, a **post-ictal** state favors seizure. Every rule has thousands of exceptions — [[Convulsive Syncope|convulsive syncope]] (when the patient can't get horizontal) mimics seizure, so consider "an [[Electrocardiogram|EEG of the heart]]" when the story sounds cardiac.
- **Don't anchor on the chief concern.** The case's real signal was in the **review of systems** — a year of frequent falls and progressive urinary incontinence — and Berkowitz names how hard it was to pull his mind out of the syncope/seizure schema until the discussant said *"Parkinsonism."* When findings won't unify, re-sort the cards around a different organizing syndrome. [[Anchoring Bias|Anchoring]] to the presenting complaint is the trap.
- **[[Parkinsonism|Parkinsonism]] is a syndrome, not a diagnosis — build the differential.** Resting tremor, [[Rigidity vs Spasticity|cogwheel rigidity]], bradykinesia, hypophonia, and a shuffling gait can come from idiopathic **[[Parkinson Disease|Parkinson disease]]** (most common), **drug-induced** parkinsonism (dopamine blockers, antipsychotics, metoclopramide), or the **[[Parkinson-Plus Syndromes|Parkinson-plus]]** syndromes: **[[Multiple System Atrophy|MSA]]** (dysautonomia; cerebellar or parkinsonian subtypes), **[[Progressive Supranuclear Palsy|PSP]]** (early *backward* falls, vertical gaze palsy), **[[Corticobasal Syndrome|corticobasal syndrome]]** (very asymmetric, alien limb, myoclonus), and **[[Dementia with Lewy Bodies|DLB]]** (dementia, hallucinations, fluctuations).
- **[[Multiple System Atrophy|Multiple system atrophy]] = Parkinsonism + cerebellar signs + prominent [[Dysautonomia|autonomic failure]].** Suspect it when a "Parkinson-like" patient has features that don't fit — early severe dysautonomia (orthostatic hypotension, syncope, urinary and bowel dysfunction, stridor), cerebellar ataxia, and a poor/transient **[[Levodopa Response|levodopa response]]**. Look for the pontine **[[Hot Cross Bun Sign|hot cross bun sign]]** on MRI. Both MSA and Parkinson disease are **[[Synucleinopathy|synucleinopathies]]** (α-synuclein); PSP and corticobasal are **tauopathies**.
- **[[Neurogenic Orthostatic Hypotension|Neurogenic orthostatic hypotension]]: the blood pressure drops but the heart rate doesn't rise.** A sustained fall in BP on standing without the expected compensatory tachycardia points to autonomic failure (the 30/20/10 rule; distinguish from the chronotropic incompetence of beta-blockade). Measure it properly — supine, then at ~1 and ~3 minutes standing. Postprandial hypotension and swallow/deglutition syncope are autonomic mechanisms that fit an "after eating" trigger.
- **The [[Romberg Sign|Romberg sign]] tests proprioception, not the cerebellum.** A patient with cerebellar ataxia often can't even stand feet-together with eyes *open*, so Romberg requires baseline balance with vision, then removes it — a positive Romberg means **sensory ataxia** (dorsal column / neuropathy, e.g., B12 deficiency). Here the inability to perform it reflected cerebellar/gait failure, not a true Romberg. And [[Rigidity vs Spasticity|rigidity]] (uniform through the range) differs from spasticity (velocity-dependent catch).

## Problem Representation

A 72-year-old man with only hyperlipidemia and constipation presents with a year of episodic transient loss of consciousness — several times monthly, preceded by dizziness, occurring shortly after eating, lasting seconds to minutes with prompt spontaneous recovery and no post-ictal confusion or cardiopulmonary symptoms — on a background of one year of progressive falls (from independent ambulation to a walker) and six months of progressive urinary incontinence. His exam shows neurogenic orthostatic hypotension (a sustained BP fall without compensatory tachycardia), a low MMSE with slowed speech, a coarse resting tremor with cogwheel rigidity, a slow shuffling wide-based gait, and cerebellar dysmetria and dysdiadochokinesia, with normal labs and a non-diagnostic brain MRI. The unifying syndrome — Parkinsonism plus cerebellar signs plus prominent autonomic failure, with only a partial and transient levodopa response — is multiple system atrophy (Shy-Drager, the dysautonomia-predominant phenotype).

## Case Narrative

A CP Solvers Neurology VMR with **Aaron Berkowitz** teaching, **Maria** presenting, **Gabriel** and **Ravi** discussing, and **Rafa** on teaching points. The chief concern: **episodic transient loss of consciousness**.

**Syncope versus seizure.** Gabriel and Ravi open on the reflex split — [[Syncope|syncope]] versus [[Seizure|seizure]] — and Berkowitz spends real time on the [[Syncope vs Seizure|distinction]], stressing that loss of consciousness is usually unwitnessed and that "all the classic teachings have thousands of exceptions." He organizes the clues by **before, during, and after**: a vasovagal prodrome (lightheadedness, warmth, diaphoresis, tunneling vision) versus a temporal-lobe **aura** (rising epigastric sensation, fear, foul smell); rhythmic tonic-clonic movements with eyes open and deviated and **lateral** tongue-biting versus a simple loss of tone; and — most useful — **prompt recovery** (syncope, because lying flat restores cerebral perfusion) versus a **post-ictal** state (seizure). He salts it with the caveats: **[[Convulsive Syncope|convulsive syncope]]** when a patient can't get horizontal (the poker player who "seized" upright from an arrhythmia — "let's get an [[Electrocardiogram|EEG of the heart]]"), the taxonomy of seizures (generalized tonic-clonic, absence with automatisms, focal with/without preserved awareness, [[Non-Convulsive Status Epilepticus|non-convulsive status]]), and Tracy Milligan's videos of medical students fainting that even neurologists can't call. He rounds out the differential — [[Insulinoma|hypoglycemia/insulinoma]] (the NEJM CPC of "episodic confusion"), narcolepsy — and argues **[[Transient Ischemic Attack|TIA]]** doesn't belong (a focal deficit, not loss of consciousness).

**The HPI that doesn't fit.** Maria reveals a **72-year-old man** with a year of transient LOC, several times monthly, **preceded by dizziness**, **almost always shortly after eating**, lasting seconds to minutes with spontaneous recovery and **no confusion**, and no chest pain, palpitations, or dyspnea. But the review of systems reframes everything: **frequent falls** (walking independently a year ago, now using a **walker**) and **six months of progressive urinary incontinence** (no dysuria, no retention sensation). Past history is thin — hyperlipidemia, constipation, on aspirin, simvastatin, and laxatives.

**The reframe.** Ravi reads the LOC as non-seizure (no post-ictal state, dizziness prodrome) and probably syncopal, then puts the falls and incontinence into a Venn diagram and offers **[[Normal Pressure Hydrocephalus|NPH]]** — and, crucially, **Parkinson disease**. Berkowitz works the "after eating" trigger (postprandial autonomic change; he can't fully localize it in the endocrine axis), toys with a colloid-cyst/third-ventricle **[[Colloid Cyst|ball-valve]]** causing episodic hydrocephalus (textbook, never seen), then seizes on Ravi's cue and names the shift explicitly: it is hard to pull the mind out of the syncope/seizure schema, but *"Parkinsonism"* re-sorts the cards toward a **neurodegenerative** syndrome. He first teaches the **[[Gait Disorder|gait]] differential** (motor weakness, sensory/proprioceptive loss, cerebellar ataxia, Parkinsonism, higher-level/frontal — the "multifactorial gait disorder" of the elderly, and the "magnetic" gait of NPH), then the **[[Parkinson-Plus Syndromes|Parkinson-plus]]** family as the way to unify falls + incontinence + syncope: **[[Progressive Supranuclear Palsy|PSP]]** (early backward falls, vertical gaze palsy, surprised facies, tau), **[[Multiple System Atrophy|MSA]]** (parkinsonian MSA-P and cerebellar MSA-C subtypes, prominent [[Dysautonomia|dysautonomia]] — orthostatic hypotension, syncope, urinary dysfunction, stridor), **[[Corticobasal Syndrome|corticobasal syndrome]]** (very asymmetric, alien limb, myoclonus), and **[[Dementia with Lewy Bodies|DLB]]**.

**The exam.** Orthostatics are the anchor: supine 114/90 (HR 85) → 1 min standing 102/61 (HR 85) → 3 min 105/63 (HR 87) — a sustained BP fall **without** a compensatory heart-rate rise (**[[Neurogenic Orthostatic Hypotension|neurogenic orthostatic hypotension]]**). MMSE is **21/30** with slowed speech and impaired drawing/word-generation. Motor shows a **coarse resting tremor** (R > L), increased tone with **[[Rigidity vs Spasticity|cogwheel rigidity]]**, a slow shuffling **wide-based** gait, and **cerebellar dysmetria and dysdiadochokinesia**; sensation is intact, cranial nerves normal, reflexes not recorded, and Romberg could not be performed because he couldn't stand feet-together. Berkowitz assembles the package — **Parkinsonism + cerebellar signs + severe autonomic failure + mild cognitive impairment** — and drops two teaching pearls: **[[Rigidity vs Spasticity|rigidity]]** (uniform through the range) is not velocity-dependent spasticity, and the **[[Romberg Sign|Romberg sign]] is proprioceptive, not cerebellar** (its impossibility here reflects cerebellar/gait failure, not a true positive). He interprets the fixed heart rate as autonomic (not beta-blocker chronotropic incompetence) and flags his own humility about autonomic neurology.

**Landing the diagnosis.** Labs (CBC, BMP, HbA1c, B12, folate, RPR, HIV, EKG) are normal, and the single MRI image shown is unremarkable — though the **brainstem** (where the pontine **[[Hot Cross Bun Sign|hot cross bun sign]]** would appear in MSA-C) was not captured. Berkowitz frames MSA as a **clinical diagnosis without a biomarker**, teaches neurodegenerative classification by protein (**[[Synucleinopathy|synucleinopathies]]** — Parkinson disease and MSA both α-synuclein; **tauopathies** — PSP and corticobasal), and reviews the confirmatory logic: **[[Levodopa Response|levodopa]]** is robust and sustained in Parkinson disease but poor/transient in Parkinson-plus, and a **[[DaT Scan|DaT scan]]** shows a nigrostriatal deficit that separates degenerative from drug-induced parkinsonism but **cannot** distinguish PD from Parkinson-plus. Maria confirms: postganglionic autonomic testing showed orthostatic hypotension, and **levodopa gave only a partial, brief response** — **[[Multiple System Atrophy|multiple system atrophy]]**, with the chat surfacing the eponym **[[Shy-Drager Syndrome|Shy-Drager]]** for its dysautonomia-predominant form. The closing lesson is metacognitive: the team's willingness to abandon the syncope/seizure anchor and seek a **unifying diagnosis** is what solved the case.

### Illness Scripts and Bias

- **[[Anchoring Bias|Anchoring]] on the chief concern, named and broken.** Berkowitz explicitly narrates how the syncope/seizure schema held the room for half an hour, and how the discussant's "Parkinsonism" was what let everyone "rearrange the cards" — a live model of detecting and escaping premature framing, echoing the team-driven reframe in [[CPS 220 - Drowsiness and Bickerstaff Brainstem Encephalitis|CPS 220]].
- **[[Unifying Diagnosis|Unifying diagnosis]] over a pile of complaints.** The move that solved it was insisting that transient LOC, falls, incontinence, and a postprandial trigger belong to *one* disease — MSA's dysautonomia — rather than three unrelated geriatric problems (an Occam's-razor pull against a multifactorial account).
- **[[Anatomical Approach|Localization]] via the gait and the exam.** Reading the gait differential (motor/sensory/cerebellar/parkinsonian/frontal) and then finding Parkinsonism *plus* cerebellar signs is what distinguished a Parkinson-plus syndrome from idiopathic Parkinson disease.
- **The [[Parkinson-Plus Syndromes|Parkinson-plus]] illness scripts.** Each atypical syndrome carries a discriminating signature — PSP's backward falls and vertical gaze palsy, corticobasal's asymmetry and alien limb, DLB's dementia/hallucinations, and MSA's dysautonomia + cerebellar signs — and pattern-matching the dysautonomia-forward picture selected MSA.
- **[[Test Interpretation|Test interpretation]] as teaching.** Neurogenic orthostatic hypotension (BP drop without HR rise), the Romberg sign as proprioceptive rather than cerebellar, rigidity vs spasticity, the levodopa response as a diagnostic probe, and the DaT scan's inability to separate PD from Parkinson-plus — each finding is weighted for what it can and cannot prove.
- **[[Diagnostic Humility|Diagnostic humility]].** Berkowitz concedes his weakness in autonomic neurology and treats MSA as a biomarker-poor clinical diagnosis, comfortable landing on a syndrome confirmed by pattern and a levodopa trial rather than a single confirmatory test.

## Teaching Discussion

- Approach episodic transient loss of consciousness as syncope versus seizure, using before/during/after features (vasovagal prodrome and prompt recovery vs aura, tonic-clonic movements, lateral tongue-biting, and a post-ictal state) while recognizing that every classic sign has exceptions — convulsive syncope can mimic seizure, so pursue a cardiac cause ("an EEG of the heart") when the story fits.
- Resist anchoring on the chief concern: when the presenting complaint won't unify with the review of systems, re-sort the findings around a different organizing syndrome — here, falls plus incontinence plus a neurodegenerative feel, not the loss of consciousness, was the real case.
- Treat parkinsonism as a syndrome (resting tremor, cogwheel rigidity, bradykinesia, hypophonia, shuffling gait) with a differential of idiopathic Parkinson disease, drug-induced parkinsonism (dopamine blockers, antipsychotics, metoclopramide), and the Parkinson-plus syndromes (MSA, PSP, corticobasal, DLB), each with discriminating features.
- Diagnose multiple system atrophy when parkinsonism is accompanied by prominent early dysautonomia and cerebellar signs with a poor or transient levodopa response; look for the pontine hot cross bun sign, and classify neurodegeneration by protein (synucleinopathies — Parkinson disease and MSA; tauopathies — PSP and corticobasal).
- Recognize neurogenic orthostatic hypotension by a sustained fall in blood pressure on standing without the expected compensatory tachycardia (distinct from beta-blocker chronotropic incompetence), measured supine and at ~1 and ~3 minutes; postprandial hypotension and swallow syncope are autonomic mechanisms consistent with an "after eating" trigger.
- Use the Romberg sign correctly as a test of proprioception (a positive result indicates sensory ataxia from dorsal column disease or neuropathy, e.g., B12 deficiency), not a cerebellar sign, and distinguish rigidity (uniform tone through the range) from velocity-dependent spasticity.
- Interpret ancillary tests for what they prove: levodopa response supports idiopathic Parkinson disease and its absence favors Parkinson-plus, while a DaT scan confirms a nigrostriatal degenerative process but cannot separate Parkinson disease from the Parkinson-plus syndromes (useful mainly to distinguish degenerative from drug-induced parkinsonism).
