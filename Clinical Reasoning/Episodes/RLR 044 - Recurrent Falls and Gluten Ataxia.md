## Pearls

- **Triage a fall by whether the patient is *confused*.** By [[Base Rate|base rate]] most [[Falls|falls]] are CNS-driven in patients who are cognitively impaired ([[Dementia|dementia]]) or intoxicated; a **cognitively normal** faller (here MMSE 26/30, intact ADLs) removes the two commonest causes and makes a focused neuraxis workup high-yield. The [[Center of Gravity|center of gravity]] is the *fall*, not the fractures — though a standing-height fall that shatters bone is a **[[Fragility Fracture|fragility fracture]]** that earns [[Osteoporosis|osteoporosis]] a place on the problem list.
- **Dissect ataxia into [[Sensory Ataxia|sensory]] vs [[Cerebellar Ataxia|cerebellar]], because the cerebellum has two jobs.** It *receives* environmental/proprioceptive input (a lesion in that input = **sensory ataxia**) and *coordinates and communicates* to cortex (a lesion in the cerebellum itself = **cerebellar ataxia**). Sensory features here — length-dependent vibratory loss, lower-limb hyporeflexia, a positive [[Romberg Sign|Romberg]] — localize to nerve/[[Dorsal Root Ganglion|DRG]]/[[Dorsal Column|dorsal column]]; cerebellar features — [[Dysarthria|dysarthria]], [[Intention Tremor|intention tremor]], [[Dysdiadochokinesia|dysdiadochokinesia]] — localize to the cerebellum (vermis → truncal, hemisphere → limb).
- **Read Romberg with a caveat.** The [[Romberg Sign|Romberg test]] removes *visual* compensation to unmask a **proprioceptive (sensory)** deficit — but it's only interpretable if the patient's gait/stance is near-normal with eyes open; a patient ataxic at baseline confounds it.
- **Run [[Localization × Time Course|localization × time course]], and let tempo prune the trifecta.** For any cerebellar/sensory syndrome the reflex trifecta is **[[Stroke|vascular]], [[Brain Tumor|mass]], inflammatory**; a subacute-to-chronic course makes stroke and mass unlikely and leaves **inflammatory/antibody-mediated** disease (plus toxic, genetic, nutritional, metabolic). **Image-positive vs image-negative** is the next great branch point — a normal MRI here reframes the problem as **image-negative chronic sensory-predominant ataxia**, prioritizing [[Paraneoplastic Cerebellar Degeneration|paraneoplastic]] and [[Sensory Ganglionopathy|ganglionopathy]].
- **An isolated [[Thrombocytosis|thrombocytosis]] with a *normal hemoglobin* argues against a reactive cause.** Most thrombocytosis is reactive (cancer, infection) and usually rides with a *low* hemoglobin (inflammation, iron deficiency); a normal Hgb and no leukocytosis/weight loss push earlier toward a primary process — and the discipline is to decide whether it is **true-true-and-related or true-true-and-unrelated** ([[Test Interpretation|test interpretation]]) rather than force it into the neurologic story.
- **[[Celiac Disease|Celiac disease]] has a wildly [[Illness Script Flexibility|flexible illness script]] — most [[Gluten Ataxia|gluten ataxia]] has *no* gut disease.** ~41% of sporadic/idiopathic ataxias carry gluten antibodies; **anti-gliadin is highly sensitive, anti-tTG more specific**, yet **>60–70% have no enteropathy or GI symptoms** (Purkinje cells cross-react with tTG/gliadin epitopes). The clincher was **[[Oligoclonal Bands|CSF oligoclonal bands]] absent in serum** (intrathecal synthesis) — excluding a genetic [[Spinocerebellar Ataxia|spinocerebellar ataxia]] and drug effect — and a **gluten-free diet** produced measurable improvement, the ultimate confirmation.

## Problem Representation

A 67-year-old woman with long-standing, stable epilepsy (generalized tonic-clonic since age 14, seizure-free >10 years on oxcarbazepine and lamotrigine) presents with 6–8 months of progressive recurrent falls and gait instability, walker-dependent, without any premonitory lightheadedness, palpitations, or loss of consciousness and without post-ictal confusion or incontinence — sustaining mandibular, zygomaticomaxillary, and T12 burst fractures from standing-height falls (a fragility-fracture pattern). She is cognitively intact (MMSE 26/30) with preserved ADLs, and her examination shows a mixed **cerebellar ataxia** (dysarthria, bilateral intention tremor, dysdiadochokinesia, abnormal heel-to-shin, wide-based gait) and **sensory ataxia** (length-dependent vibratory loss, lower-limb hyporeflexia, positive Romberg). The problem is a **chronic, image-negative, mixed cerebellar and sensory-predominant ataxia** with an isolated thrombocytosis (normal hemoglobin) and retroperitoneal/mesenteric lymphadenopathy, with an unremarkable MRI brain/cord and CSF and a negative extended paraneoplastic and Whipple workup. Markedly positive anti-tTG and anti-gliadin antibodies, duodenal intraepithelial lymphocytosis, and CSF-restricted oligoclonal bands establish **gluten (celiac) ataxia** — the lymphadenopathy likely reflecting enteropathy-associated T-cell lymphoma — with clinical improvement on a gluten-free diet.

## Case Narrative

The episode welcomes back guest **Dr. Kirtan Patolia**, who titles his case "A Secluded Mystery." A **67-year-old woman** with a history of epilepsy presents with **6–8 months of recurrent falls** and difficulty ambulating, now using a walker for chores; her most recent fall came when the walker caught in a door, and prior falls occurred tripping over shoelaces and riding a bike. Crucially, she **denies** any lightheadedness, dizziness, vertigo, loss of consciousness, confusion, visual blurring, tinnitus, palpitations, or sweating before the falls, and no incontinence or confusion afterward. In the ED she is found to have a **left mandibular fracture, right zygomaticomaxillary fracture, and a T12 burst fracture**.

Reza declines to be distracted: this is a **67-year-old with [[Falls|recurrent falls]]**, and the fractures are a *consequence*, not the [[Center of Gravity|center of gravity]]. Invoking a favorite falls figure, he notes the threshold to fall drops with age (baseline functional status matters — "there are 67-year-olds and there are 67-year-olds"), but the key is that there's **no loss of consciousness** (which would open a [[Syncope|syncope]]-vs-[[Seizure|seizure]] differential). So the search is for an **impairment** in the neuraxis — visual, cerebellar/ataxic, or motor — and he sets out to analyze sensory, coordination, and motor systems. Rabih layers the [[Base Rate|base rate]]: most falls he sees in the ER are in patients who are **confused** — [[Dementia|dementia]] or intoxication — and this patient's **normal mental status** removes the commonest causes and makes the exercise "productive." He also anchors the fracture: a **standing-height fall** that breaks bone is a **[[Fragility Fracture|fragility fracture]]**, and the commonest underlying issue in an older woman is **[[Osteoporosis|osteoporosis]]** — a problem-list item for later, while "the signal is in the neuraxis, not the falls."

The history deepens the puzzle. Her epilepsy was diagnosed at **14** (1970s–80s, "unknown cause"), her last seizure was **>10 years ago**, and she has been stable on **oxcarbazepine and lamotrigine** for 15 years. She denies alcohol, tobacco, and drugs; lives alone; and a visiting sister corroborates the falls but confirms **preserved cognition and ADLs** (church, gardening, reading, TV, groceries). Rabih flags the epilepsy as "not fully tucked in" — is this a continuation? — but weakened by decades of stability, while noting the long list of **[[Antiepileptic Toxicity|cerebellar toxicities of antiepileptics]]**; he is intrigued that her function is so preserved despite the severity of her injuries, and asks whether this disorder is subacute-progressive or episodic.

The **exam** localizes it. She is alert and oriented with **MMSE 26/30**, intact cranial nerves, and **5/5 strength**, but with **occasional [[Dysarthria|dysarthria]]**. Sensory testing shows **length-dependent reduced vibration at the toes**; reflexes are **2+ in the arms, 1+ in the legs**, plantars downgoing, no clonus. Coordination and gait reveal **bilateral [[Intention Tremor|intention tremor]], [[Dysdiadochokinesia|dysdiadochokinesia]] (left > right), abnormal heel-to-shin bilaterally**, a **severely wide-based stance** (worse with eyes closed), and an inability to take more than a few steps without the walker.

Reza dissects the **ataxia**. Because the cerebellum has two jobs — *receiving* environmental/proprioceptive input (a lesion there = **[[Sensory Ataxia|sensory ataxia]]**) and *coordinating and communicating to cortex* (a lesion in the cerebellum itself = **[[Cerebellar Ataxia|cerebellar ataxia]]**) — he finds **flavors of both**. **Sensory** features: reduced vibration, lower-limb **hyporeflexia**, and a positive **[[Romberg Sign|Romberg]]** (with the caveat that Romberg requires a near-normal baseline stance to interpret, since it works by removing *visual* compensation for a proprioceptive deficit). **Cerebellar** features: **dysarthria** and **intention tremor** (speaking well requires an intact cerebellum), which he further localizes — **vermis** produces truncal ataxia (absent here) while a **hemispheric** lesion produces the limb ataxia (dysdiadochokinesia, dysmetria) she shows. The formulation: **[[Localization × Time Course|localization × time course]]** — cerebellum (input and output) × a **subacute-to-chronic** tempo. Rabih agrees this is a **pancerebellar** process (both hemispheres by heel-shin, vermis by the eyes-open wobble) *plus* a sensory ataxia, and — channeling Aaron Berkowitz — insists on stating the **time course out loud**: for any cerebellar/sensory syndrome the reflex trifecta is **[[Stroke|vascular]], [[Brain Tumor|mass]], inflammatory**, and the chronic tempo makes stroke and mass unlikely, leaving **inflammatory/antibody-mediated** disease squarely on the hook (then toxic, genetic, nutritional, metabolic), with **image-positive vs image-negative** as the next great branch point. Time-zero plan: **MRI brain and cord** plus **B-vitamin/metabolic** labs — and Reza flags, to *track* not yet reach for, the possibility of a **genetic ataxia** given the old epilepsy.

Labs return broadly negative — a meticulous vitamin (B12, MMA, folate, E, B1, copper), metabolic, endocrine, viral, rheumatologic, paraprotein (SPEP/UPEP/immunofixation), and heavy-metal workup — **except an isolated [[Thrombocytosis|thrombocytosis]] of 531,000**. Rabih resists lumping it into the ataxia and treats it as a separate problem: most thrombocytosis is **reactive** (cancer, infection), but the absence of leukocytosis or weight loss is atypical, and — the key clue — the **hemoglobin is normal**, whereas reactive thrombocytosis usually rides with a *low* hemoglobin (inflammation, iron deficiency). That pushes him earlier toward a **primary** process, while explicitly holding that it may be **true-true-and-unrelated**. The **MRI brain and cord are normal** (only microvascular change; **no atrophy, no FLAIR signal, no meningeal enhancement**) and the **CSF is bland**.

This reframes everything. Reza calls it **image-negative chronic sensory-predominant [[Ataxia|ataxia]]** — the negative imaging effectively excludes a mass and makes a meningeal process unlikely — and turns to the sensory differential: a **[[Paraneoplastic Cerebellar Degeneration|paraneoplastic]] phenomenon** (a 67-year-old — is she current on mammography? the neurologic syndrome can precede the cancer) and a **[[Sensory Ganglionopathy|sensory ganglionopathy]]** (nerve/[[Dorsal Root Ganglion|DRG]]/[[Dorsal Column|dorsal column]]), pursuing an **LP for paraneoplastic antibodies** and **EMG/nerve conduction** ± nerve biopsy. Rabih agrees paraneoplastic disease is the most pressing image-negative cause and the one that could **merge with the thrombocytosis**, and — reasoning that a cancer that hasn't declared itself in 8–9 months (no weight loss, jaundice, dyspnea) would more likely be an indolent tumor like a **[[Thymoma|thymoma]]** than small cell or Hodgkin — the hosts pull up their paraneoplastic schema ([[Anti-Yo Antibody|Yo]]/[[Anti-Hu Antibody|Hu]]/Ma for cerebellar degeneration; **anti-Hu** for ganglionopathy).

**CT** then shows **mesenteric fat stranding and several enlarged (retroperitoneal) lymph nodes** — read as possible **[[Sclerosing Mesenteritis|sclerosing mesenteritis]] vs indolent [[Lymphoma|lymphoma]]** — with a normal chest. But the **extended [[Paraneoplastic Neurologic Syndromes|paraneoplastic panel]]** (serum and CSF: anti-Tr, anti-Yo, anti-Hu, anti-GAD65) is **negative**, as is a **[[Whipple Disease|Whipple]] (T. whipplei) PCR** (CNS Whipple can present with ataxia alone). Rabih notes the panel's imperfect sensitivity and reframes around the nodes: **retroperitoneal lymphadenopathy always needs an explanation** — malignant (paraneoplastic driver), granulomatous infection (Whipple, [[Brucellosis|Brucella]], [[Tuberculosis|TB]], [[Bartonella|Bartonella]] — exposure-dependent), or autoimmune (**[[IgG4-Related Disease|IgG4 disease]]**, usually image-positive pachymeningitis in the CNS; **[[Neurosarcoidosis|sarcoid]]**, the go-to for a cryptic neuro syndrome plus lymph nodes). Reza converges on an **infiltrative disorder** with **tropism for peripheral nerve and retroperitoneum** — [[Neurosarcoidosis|sarcoid]], [[Lymphoma|lymphoma]]/[[Castleman Disease|Castleman]], **[[Amyloidosis|amyloid]]** — noting the **thrombocytosis argues *against* lymphoma** (which usually lowers platelets) — and proposes biopsying the **safest site** (an excisional node or the peripheral nerve) for atypical infection, malignancy, and amyloid.

The reveal, Kirtan says, was "hidden in the conversation." One serology sent in the first hour returns **positive**: **anti-tTG IgA 45.2 and anti-gliadin IgA 112.4**, with a duodenal biopsy showing **non-specific intraepithelial lymphocytosis** (sample inadequate for more). A debate follows — positive serology but *no GI symptoms*: false positive, or **[[Gluten Ataxia|gluten/celiac ataxia]]**? The **last** test decides it: the CSF has **five distinct [[Oligoclonal Bands|oligoclonal bands]] absent from serum** — proof of **intrathecal synthesis** — which excludes a genetic **[[Spinocerebellar Ataxia|spinocerebellar ataxia]]** (OCB-negative) and an antiepileptic effect, while the other OCB causes (MS, neurosarcoid, lymphomatous/carcinomatous meningitis) would be image-positive. Kirtan lays out the facts: **~41% of sporadic/idiopathic ataxias** carry gluten antibodies; **anti-gliadin is ~100% sensitive**, **anti-tTG more specific**; and only **~28% of gluten ataxia have enteropathy** (so most have *no* gut disease), the mechanism being **Purkinje-cell cross-reactivity** with tTG/gliadin epitopes. The mesenteric nodes raise **[[Enteropathy-Associated T-Cell Lymphoma|enteropathy-associated T-cell lymphoma]]** (a celiac complication — indolent, abdomen-confined, later aggressive), unconfirmed because the deep retroperitoneal nodes were unbiopsiable (worsened by the T12 fracture). With toxic/metabolic/endocrine/medication causes excluded, strongly positive serology, CSF OCBs, and — decisively — **improvement on a gluten-free diet** (within ten days she could write her name and speak without interruption), the diagnosis is **gluten (celiac) ataxia**, with possible **EATL**.

The reflection is a meditation on **[[Illness Script Flexibility|illness-script flexibility]] and [[Diagnostic Humility|humility]]**. Rabih marvels that celiac's extraintestinal manifestations so often come *without* intestinal ones — the retroperitoneal nodes, the osteoporosis of malabsorption, even **CEC syndrome** (celiac-epilepsy-cerebral calcification; she lacks the calcification) — and that the mechanism is **antibody-mediated**, not merely nutritional. Reza reads the UpToDate description back and admits the clues weren't there for *him* — "my illness script for celiac isn't that robust." Kirtan explains he suspected it early precisely because **ataxia plus peripheral neuropathy** demanded a unifying cause, and celiac (via nutritional deficiency *and* direct autoimmunity) links them — reasoning from an **adult woman + autoimmune priors + a negative ANA** to the few remaining antibodies, anti-tTG among them. Both hosts land on the lesson: this is the **extreme overlap of a common problem (ataxia) with an uncommon manifestation of a common disease (celiac)** — humbling, and a reminder (Rabih: an NEJM case that reached celiac only via lymph-node biopsy; the "A Spatial Cure" anagram of *celiac sprue*) that medicine's presentations are "infinitely complex."

### Illness Scripts and Bias

- **[[Base Rate|Base rate]] + [[Center of Gravity|center of gravity]] for falls.** Falls are triaged **with vs without confusion**; a cognitively normal faller removes [[Dementia|dementia]] and intoxication and makes the neuraxis workup productive — and the *fall*, not the dramatic fractures, is the problem to solve (the [[Fragility Fracture|fragility fracture]] parked for outpatient management).
- **[[Localization × Time Course|Localization × time course]] as the neuro engine.** The exam is compartmentalized (mental status/cranial nerves/motor/sensory/cerebellar), ataxia split into [[Sensory Ataxia|sensory]] vs [[Cerebellar Ataxia|cerebellar]] by the cerebellum's two functions, and the cerebellar signs further localized (vermis→truncal, hemisphere→limb) — then multiplied by a subacute-chronic tempo that prunes the vascular/mass trifecta.
- **[[Anatomical Approach|Anatomical]] sensory pathway + the [[Romberg Sign|Romberg]] caveat.** Sensory ataxia is localized along nerve → [[Dorsal Root Ganglion|DRG]] → [[Dorsal Column|dorsal column]], with Romberg interpreted correctly as a *visual-compensation-removal* test that requires a near-normal baseline stance.
- **[[Schema-Based Reasoning|Image-positive vs image-negative]] branch point.** A normal MRI is not a dead end but a *reframe* — image-negative chronic sensory ataxia elevates [[Paraneoplastic Cerebellar Degeneration|paraneoplastic]] disease and [[Sensory Ganglionopathy|ganglionopathy]] and keeps antibody-mediated causes central.
- **[[Test Interpretation|Test interpretation]] throughout.** The **thrombocytosis** is parsed reactive-vs-primary by the *normal hemoglobin* and held as possibly **true-true-unrelated**; the **paraneoplastic panel's** imperfect sensitivity is named; **anti-gliadin (sensitive) vs anti-tTG (specific)** are weighed against a *no-GI-symptom* pretest; and **CSF-restricted oligoclonal bands** prove intrathecal synthesis while excluding genetic and drug causes — with the **gluten-free-diet response** as the final confirmatory test.
- **[[Illness Script Flexibility|Illness-script flexibility]] of [[Celiac Disease|celiac disease]].** The teaching core: extraintestinal celiac ([[Gluten Ataxia|gluten ataxia]], [[Dermatitis Herpetiformis|dermatitis herpetiformis]], neuropathy, osteoporosis, retroperitoneal nodes/[[Enteropathy-Associated T-Cell Lymphoma|EATL]]) commonly appears **without** gut disease — a script so elastic it evades pattern recognition.
- **[[Diagnostic Humility|Diagnostic humility]] and the value of a read guest.** Both hosts reach a coherent *infiltrative* representation but not the answer, and openly credit the guest's script — the "common problem × uncommon manifestation" overlap that even UpToDate/Google only unlock once you decide to "review *all* etiologies of ataxia."

## Teaching Discussion

- Solve the fall, not the fractures: keep the center of gravity on the recurrent falls, and use the confusion filter — a cognitively intact faller lacks the two commonest causes (dementia, intoxication), making a structured neuraxis workup high-yield; still, flag a standing-height fragility fracture and osteoporosis for the problem list.
- Localize ataxia to sensory versus cerebellar using the cerebellum's two functions (receiving proprioceptive input versus coordinating output), and read the classic signs accordingly: vibratory loss/hyporeflexia/positive Romberg for sensory (nerve, DRG, dorsal column); dysarthria, intention tremor, and dysdiadochokinesia for cerebellar (vermis → truncal, hemisphere → limb).
- Interpret Romberg carefully — it isolates proprioception by removing vision and is only meaningful when the eyes-open stance is near-normal.
- Multiply localization by time course and state the tempo aloud: a subacute-to-chronic cerebellar/sensory syndrome makes stroke and mass unlikely and centers inflammatory/antibody-mediated disease, with image-positive versus image-negative as the pivotal next branch.
- Parse an isolated thrombocytosis before folding it into the main problem: reactive causes usually accompany a low hemoglobin, so a normal hemoglobin with no leukocytosis or weight loss should raise a primary process — and always ask whether the finding is true-true-and-related or true-true-and-unrelated.
- Treat a negative paraneoplastic panel as imperfect, and give retroperitoneal lymphadenopathy its own differential — malignancy, granulomatous infection (Whipple, Brucella, TB, Bartonella, exposure-dependent), and autoimmune disease (IgG4, sarcoid) — while remembering that thrombocytosis argues against lymphoma.
- Keep celiac disease's flexible illness script in mind for otherwise-idiopathic ataxia: most gluten ataxia has no enteropathy or GI symptoms; anti-gliadin is sensitive and anti-tTG specific; CSF oligoclonal bands absent from serum prove intrathecal autoimmune synthesis (and exclude genetic spinocerebellar ataxia and antiepileptic effects); and a gluten-free-diet response is confirmatory.
- Practice the humility the case teaches: an unusual manifestation of a common disease sits in the extreme overlap of two common things, and reaching it depends on having the script — so when no cause of ataxia is obvious, deliberately review every etiology rather than trusting pattern recognition.

- A **falls** card — the with/without-confusion triage, base-rate causes, and the neuraxis workup.
- A **gluten/celiac ataxia** card — antibodies (sensitive vs specific), enteropathy-optional, OCBs, and gluten-free-diet response.
- A **retroperitoneal lymphadenopathy** card — malignant, granulomatous-infectious (exposure-based), and autoimmune (IgG4, sarcoid) buckets.
- A **thrombocytosis** card — reactive vs primary, the hemoglobin clue, and hyposplenism/celiac.
