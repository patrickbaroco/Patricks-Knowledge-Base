## Summary

> [!summary] Scan Summary
> Rabih presents a case that turned on a single overnight lab: a thin, cachectic man with heavy alcohol use who first came to the SF VA ER with a mundane **itchy [[Blistering Rash|rash]]** on the dorsum of both hands — a three-minute visit that ended with topical steroids and a dermatology referral. Reza builds the rash schema in real time: pruritus and the absence of tenderness/blisters are reassuring **[[Rash Red Flags|red-flag]]** checks, and the first branch point for *any* rash is **[[Internal vs External Rash|internal (systemic, tends diffuse) vs external (contact, tends local)]]** — with the caveat that you only see the hands, so you must ask what's on the chest, the back, and the organs. The story then accretes clues that were never connected in the moment: a prior ER visit for **chronic diarrhea**, and — buried in the chart — years in a neurology clinic for **[[Parkinsonism|parkinsonism]], cognitive impairment, and abnormal movements** that had eluded an exhaustive workup. At dermatology the rash had evolved to **bullae** with subtle facial redness and a rash around the neck (a **[[Casal Necklace|Casal necklace]]**); she ran a **[[Bullous Disease|bullous-disease]]** schema — biopsy plus **[[Bullous Pemphigoid|BP180/BP230]]** — and, teaching that blistering on the hands should raise **[[Porphyria Cutanea Tarda|porphyria cutanea tarda]]**, sent porphyrins, ferritin, and hepatitis C. Everything came back negative or nonspecific (ferritin normal, HCV negative, BP antigens negative, biopsy nonspecific). The unlock came when Rabih read neurology's note listing "nutritional deficiency" in the differential — but with only B12 and thiamine sent — and, carrying the subconscious triad of dermatitis and diarrhea, added the missing test: **niacin was undetectable (<20)**. The diagnosis is **[[Pellagra|pellagra]]** (**[[Niacin Deficiency|niacin/vitamin B3 deficiency]]**), the classic **[[The Three Ds|dermatitis–diarrhea–dementia]]** triad, camouflaged for years by alcohol. Reza's meta-lesson: the knowledge was there (the necklace, the dorsal-hand distribution), but the diagnosis required **slowing down** — and a full **[[Nutritional Workup|nutritional panel]]** (B1, B3, B9, B12), not the reflexive B12 alone, in a nutritionally vulnerable patient with a cortical/movement syndrome.

## Pearls

- **The first branch point for any rash is internal versus external — systemic disease tends diffuse and symmetric, contact/irritant tends local.** Because you only see the exposed skin (here, the dorsal hands), a **[[Internal vs External Rash|diffuse, symmetric]]** eruption should prompt you to *undress the patient* and look at the chest, neck, and back, and to ask what might be happening at the level of the organs — the visible rash is a window, not the whole picture.
- **Run rashes through a red-flag filter first: tenderness and blisters raise alarm, and pruritus is reassuring.** A painful or blistering eruption pushes toward **[[Stevens-Johnson Syndrome|SJS/TEN]]**, drug hypersensitivity, or an autoimmune bullous disease, whereas itch argues against those emergencies — but "reassuring" is not "benign," and a rash that recurs or evolves deserves a return look.
- **Blistering on the dorsum of the hands is a classic trigger for porphyria cutanea tarda — and for pellagra.** The **[[Bullous Disease|bullous-disease schema]]** (immune-mediated: autoimmune like **[[Bullous Pemphigoid|bullous pemphigoid]]**/**[[Pemphigus Vulgaris|pemphigus]]** vs paraneoplastic; drug eruption; irritant; SJS/TEN — sorted by **[[Immunofluorescence|immunofluorescence]]**) should sit alongside **[[Porphyria Cutanea Tarda|PCT]]** (check ferritin, hepatitis C, urine porphyrins) and photodistributed nutritional dermatitis for a hand-predominant blistering rash.
- **Pellagra is dermatitis, diarrhea, and dementia — but the three Ds are rarely synchronous, and the dermatitis is nearly universal while diarrhea (~half) and neuro (~third) are variable.** The **[[Casal Necklace|Casal necklace]]** and a **[[Photodistributed Dermatitis|photodistributed]]** (sun-exposed: dorsal hands, neck, face) eruption are the tells; the neurologic picture is a **diffuse cortical/gray-matter (neuronal) syndrome** — hence **[[Parkinsonism|parkinsonism]]** (basal-ganglia) or a cortical phenotype — not a white-matter disease.
- **One nutrient deficiency can unify a rash, chronic diarrhea, and a years-long undiagnosed neuro syndrome — but only if you look, because alcohol camouflages everything.** Nutritionally vulnerable patients (**[[Alcohol Use Disorder|alcohol use disorder]]**, bariatric surgery, restrictive diets) often don't *look* deficient; a **[[Unifying Diagnosis|unifying]]** deficiency like **[[Niacin Deficiency|niacin]]** requires severe, prolonged depletion, so its presentation is heterogeneous and its diagnosis is typically delayed years.
- **When you do a nutritional workup for altered mental status or dementia, send more than B12 and thiamine.** A full **[[Nutritional Workup|panel]]** — B1 (thiamine), B3 (niacin), B9 (folate), B12 — is reasonable in a thin (BMI ~18), confused, or movement-disordered patient; a reflexive B12-only screen will miss **[[Pellagra|pellagra]]**, which is treatable (neuro and diarrhea recover; the dermatitis is the most stubborn to reverse).

## Problem Representation

A 59-year-old man with severe alcohol use disorder and a thin, cachectic habitus (BMI ~18) presents to the emergency department with a several-day history of a diffuse, symmetric, itchy erythematous rash on the dorsum of both hands, apparently recurrent over months, without tenderness or systemic complaints and with normal vital signs. In the preceding weeks and years he had an ER visit for chronic mild diarrhea and a long-standing neurology-clinic course for parkinsonism, cognitive impairment, and abnormal movements that had defied an extensive workup and was being treated empirically as presumed seizures. On dermatologic evaluation the rash had progressed to bullae with subtle facial erythema and a periauricular/neck (Casal-necklace) distribution; a bullous-disease and porphyria workup (skin biopsy, BP180/BP230, urine porphyrins, ferritin, hepatitis C) was negative or nonspecific. Recognition that the dermatitis, chronic diarrhea, and neuropsychiatric syndrome could represent a single nutritional deficiency prompted a niacin level, which was undetectable (<20), establishing pellagra (niacin/vitamin B3 deficiency) as the unifying diagnosis — long masked by alcohol use.

## Case Narrative

Rabih frames the case as one he "stumbled into." A man came to the ER with the board reading simply **"rash"** — an ED signal for low acuity. The first encounter went poorly: mid-history, Rabih's watch rang with a cardiology call about a complex ACS, he stepped out to defer it, and the patient was *furious* at being interrupted. The complaint itself was modest: a **diffuse erythematous [[Blistering Rash|rash]] on the dorsum of both hands**, present three to four days, **itchy but not painful**, and — by the patient's account — recurrent over recent months, though he acknowledged **heavy alcohol use over the last six months** that clouded his recall.

Reza works the rash from first principles. With any symptom, the mind should first seek **[[Rash Red Flags|red flags]]**: tenderness and blisters are worrisome, and — importantly — **pruritus is reassuring**. The history already hints at a **transient, self-limited, episodic** process that is **diffuse and symmetric**. His central move is the first branch point for *all* rashes: is this **[[Internal vs External Rash|internal]]** — a signal of a systemic process, tending to be diffuse — or **external** — an environmental/contact signal, tending to be local? He stresses a humility point: you see only the hands, not the chest, back, or organs, so the visible rash is a window rather than the whole story. He also flags that **[[Alcohol Use Disorder|alcohol]]** links to rash through several mechanisms. His real-time plan: check vitals, examine the skin everywhere, and only then decide on labs — while entertaining mundane external causes (prolonged water exposure, bilateral poison ivy), tempered by the fact that itch and symmetry fit those imperfectly.

The actual visit was brief and low-suspicion. The patient looked **thin, frail, and cachectic** as expected with severe alcohol use, without jaundice or withdrawal (he had, in fact, had a drink 30 minutes earlier); vitals were normal. Rabih examined the hands, saw diffuse erythema and itch without scaling or fungal features, and — with the roving dermatology nurse practitioners not in that day — prescribed **topical triamcinolone** and referred him to dermatology the following week. Only hours later, writing the note, did he glance at the chart and notice a visit **three weeks prior for chronic (months-long) mild diarrhea** with normal labs, treated with loperamide. Reza uses this to make a reasoning observation: **frequency breeds dismissiveness** — an ED provider sees rash constantly and triages it in seconds, whereas a hospitalist who rarely sees rash runs a careful schema (nec fasc, DRESS). Diarrhea, too, is so common (and so easily attributed to alcohol-related mucosal inflammation) that in isolation it wouldn't pause him; **rash and diarrhea are usually true-true-and-unrelated** ([[Base Rate|base rate]]) absent a pattern that ties them together.

At the dermatology visit, the picture had changed. The rash had **coalesced into bullae/blisters** on the hands, with **subtle facial redness and a rash around the neck**. The dermatologist activated her **[[Bullous Disease|bullous-disease schema]]**, performed a **[[Immunofluorescence|biopsy]]**, and — as Reza reasons through the same space — the schema runs: is the blistering **immune-mediated** (autoimmune, e.g., **[[Bullous Pemphigoid|bullous pemphigoid]]** or **[[Pemphigus Vulgaris|pemphigus vulgaris]]**, distinguished by where the antibody sits on immunofluorescence; vs **[[Paraneoplastic Pemphigus|paraneoplastic]]**), a **drug eruption**, an **irritant**, or early **[[Stevens-Johnson Syndrome|SJS/TEN]]**? She also made the key teaching point that **blistering on the hands should raise [[Porphyria Cutanea Tarda|porphyria cutanea tarda]]**, and sent **BP180/BP230**, a skin biopsy, and a PCT workup (**urine porphyrins, ferritin, hepatitis C**). Notably, Reza — before the blistering even — floated the possibility hovering over the whole case: in someone who drinks, consider **vitamin deficiencies**, and **[[Niacin Deficiency|niacin deficiency]]** causes **dermatitis, diarrhea, and dementia**, recalling Gurpreet Dhaliwal's NEJM "D Is for Delay." He set it aside initially because he "didn't have blisters in that script" and it is rare.

The workup then failed to explain the rash. **Ferritin normal, hepatitis C negative, porphyrins pending, BP180/BP230 negative, biopsy nonspecific** with no evidence of bullous pemphigoid. Puzzled, Rabih finally *read the chart*, and there found the missing context: since **2017** the patient had been followed in **neurology** for **[[Parkinsonism|parkinsonism]], cognitive impairment, and abnormal movements**, worked up exhaustively and treated empirically for **presumed seizures**, with the neurologists themselves documenting that they did not know the cause. Reading neurology's note — which listed a "thorough workup for altered mental status **including nutritional deficiency**," but had sent only **B12 and thiamine** — was the moment it clicked. Carrying the subconscious threads of **dermatitis and diarrhea** and now a **neuro syndrome** in a **BMI-18** patient, Rabih added the test neurology hadn't: a **niacin level**. It returned **undetectable (nicotinamide <20)**. The diagnosis is **[[Pellagra|pellagra]]**.

Reza teaches the entity. The **[[The Three Ds|three Ds]] — dermatitis, diarrhea, dementia — are rarely simultaneous**: the **dermatitis is nearly universal**, diarrhea occurs in roughly half and dementia in about a third of reported cases, and the neurologic picture is **heterogeneous**. Crucially, it is a **diffuse cortical, gray-matter (neuronal) syndrome** — not a white-matter disease — which is why it can manifest as **[[Parkinsonism|parkinsonism]]** (basal-ganglia neurons) or a cortical phenotype. The **[[Casal Necklace|Casal necklace]]** is near-pathognomonic, and the eruption is **[[Photodistributed Dermatitis|photodistributed]]** (dorsal hands, neck, face). Becoming this deficient requires **prolonged, severe** depletion, so presentations are vague and heterogeneous, and — critically — **[[Alcohol Use Disorder|alcohol]] camouflages** the whole syndrome; nutritionally vulnerable patients (alcohol, bariatric surgery, restrictive/psychiatric diets) often don't *look* deficient. On **reversibility**, Reza notes the counterintuitive point that the **neuro and diarrhea recover** with repletion while the **dermatitis is the most stubborn** to reverse — and that here the real barrier is the patient's ongoing alcohol use.

Both hosts close on the cognitive lesson. The practical takeaway: when doing a nutritional workup for **[[Nutritional Workup|altered mental status or dementia]]**, don't stop at the reflexive **B12** (and sometimes thiamine) — consider a full panel (**B1, B3, B9, B12**), especially in a thin, confused, or movement-disordered patient. And the humility: Rabih *knew* the Casal necklace and the dorsal-hand distribution, but "didn't slow down" to deploy it at the bedside; the diagnosis surfaced through **[[Return to the Bedside|returning to the chart]]** and effortful reasoning rather than snap pattern recognition. He commits to a concrete next-time behavior — on seeing nutritional vulnerability plus a dorsal-hand rash, ask the patient to **pull down their shirt** and look for the necklace. Reza is candid that his own confidence rested on a single hypothesis: "if it wasn't niacin deficiency, I would have been wrong 999 out of 1,000 — I had nothing else in my differential."

### Illness Scripts and Bias

- **The universal rash branch point ([[Internal vs External Rash|internal vs external]]).** Reza's opening schema — systemic (diffuse) vs environmental (local), with the reminder that the visible skin is only a window — is the move that keeps a "benign hand rash" open to a systemic cause, and motivates undressing the patient to find the **[[Casal Necklace|Casal necklace]]**.
- **Red-flag filtering and its limits.** Tenderness/blisters up, pruritus down — but the case shows "reassuring" is not "benign," and the evolution to **[[Bullous Disease|bullae]]** reactivated the schema. Early **[[Base Rate|base-rate]]** reasoning (rash + diarrhea = true-true-unrelated) is correct on average and is exactly what a **[[Anchoring Bias|low-acuity framing]]** exploits.
- **Frequency breeds dismissiveness (framing).** Reza's observation that an ED provider triages rash in seconds while a hospitalist runs a full schema is a framing/availability insight: how often you see a complaint shapes how hard you think about it.
- **The bullous/blistering schema ([[Schema-Based Reasoning|schema-based reasoning]]).** Immune-mediated (autoimmune vs **[[Paraneoplastic Pemphigus|paraneoplastic]]**) vs drug vs irritant vs **[[Stevens-Johnson Syndrome|SJS/TEN]]**, resolved by **[[Immunofluorescence|immunofluorescence]]** — run in parallel with **[[Porphyria Cutanea Tarda|PCT]]** for a hand-predominant blistering rash.
- **[[Test Interpretation|Test interpretation]] and the incomplete panel.** Negative BP antigens and a nonspecific biopsy correctly *subtract* bullous pemphigoid; the decisive positive came only after recognizing that "nutritional workup = B12 + thiamine" was **incomplete**, and adding **[[Niacin Deficiency|niacin]]**.
- **[[Unifying Diagnosis|Unifying diagnosis]] under camouflage.** One deficiency ties the dermatitis, the chronic diarrhea, and the years-long neuro syndrome together — a parsimony that only becomes visible once **[[Alcohol Use Disorder|alcohol]]**'s camouflage is set aside.
- **[[Illness Script|Illness script]] with a severity/tempo gate.** Pellagra requires prolonged, severe depletion; its heterogeneous, vague presentation and multi-year **diagnostic delay** ("D is for delay") are part of the script.
- **[[Diagnostic Humility|Humility]] and slowing down.** Rabih had the knowledge (necklace, dorsal hands) but didn't deploy it at the bedside; the answer came from **[[Return to the Bedside|returning to the chart]]**. Reza's admission that pellagra was his *only* hypothesis is an honest **[[Cognitive Autopsy|cognitive autopsy]]** of a right-for-maybe-fragile-reasons win.

## Teaching Discussion

- Approach every rash with the internal-versus-external branch point: diffuse, symmetric eruptions favor a systemic driver and warrant a full skin exam (chest, neck, back) and consideration of organ-level disease, since the visible area is only a fraction of the picture.
- Filter rashes for red flags — tenderness and blisters escalate concern toward SJS/TEN, drug hypersensitivity, and autoimmune bullous disease, while pruritus is reassuring — but treat "reassuring" as provisional, and re-examine a rash that recurs or evolves.
- For a hand-predominant blistering rash, run the bullous-disease schema (autoimmune vs paraneoplastic vs drug vs irritant vs early SJS/TEN, sorted by immunofluorescence) alongside porphyria cutanea tarda (ferritin, hepatitis C, urine porphyrins) and photodistributed nutritional dermatitis.
- Know pellagra as dermatitis, diarrhea, and dementia, recognizing that the triad is rarely synchronous — dermatitis is nearly universal, diarrhea and neurologic involvement are variable — and that the Casal necklace and a photodistributed eruption are the cutaneous signatures.
- Read the pellagra neuro syndrome as a diffuse cortical, gray-matter (neuronal) process that can present as parkinsonism or a cortical/dementia phenotype rather than a white-matter disease, and expect it to be delayed for years, especially when alcohol camouflages the presentation.
- Suspect nutritional deficiency in patients who are nutritionally vulnerable even when they do not look deficient (alcohol use, bariatric surgery, restrictive or psychiatric diets), and let a single deficiency unify seemingly unrelated skin, gastrointestinal, and neurologic findings.
- Order a complete nutritional panel — B1, B3, B9, B12 — rather than a reflexive B12 alone when working up altered mental status or dementia in a thin or vulnerable patient, since niacin deficiency is treatable and otherwise easily missed.
- Expect asymmetric reversibility with repletion: neurologic symptoms and diarrhea tend to improve while the dermatitis is the most stubborn, and durable recovery depends on addressing the underlying driver (here, alcohol use).
