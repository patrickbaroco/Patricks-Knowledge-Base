## Pearls

- **A tense serosanguinous bulla on the sun-exposed dorsum of the hand is porphyria cutanea tarda until proven otherwise — and hepatitis C is the fact that clinches it.** **[[Porphyria Cutanea Tarda|PCT]]** is confirmed by a high **[[Urinary Porphyrins|urinary porphyrin]]** level (with an elevated **[[Ferritin|ferritin]]**), and management targets the triggers — treating **[[Hepatitis C|HCV]]**, plus iron/HFE, alcohol, and estrogen — with photoprotection; skin fragility and blistering on light-exposed skin is the signature.
- **The "two S's": a rash involving the hands and the genitals is scabies or syphilis until proven otherwise.** Palms/soles reflexively prompt an **[[Syphilis|RPR for syphilis]]**, while **interweb-space burrows** plus itchy shared-living contacts point to **[[Scabies|scabies]]** — confirmable by skin scraping and empirically treatable with permethrin, adding oral **[[Ivermectin|ivermectin]]** for extensive/crusted disease.
- **Split every rash into an inside job versus an outside job.** A **[[Internal vs External Rash|diffuse, symmetric]]** eruption is hard to attribute to external contact (which tends to be focal), pointing to a systemic, infectious, or drug cause; a two-month course argues against a simple allergic/atopic process, and the setting (housing, contacts, medications, exposures) often carries more weight than the morphology.
- **Inspection is diagnosis — look under the shirt and the bandage, especially when the patient can't give a history.** A circular wet stain over the flank and a **[[Nephrostomy Tube|dislodged nephrostomy tube]]** solved a febrile **[[Alzheimer Disease|demented]]** patient in one minute; a tube that is fully *out* needs no confirmatory imaging, and a head-to-toe exam prevents hours of misdirected fever workup.
- **Obstruction breeds stasis breeds infection.** A displaced nephrostomy tube re-obstructs the kidney, and **[[Obstructive Uropathy|obstruction with urinary stasis]]** is a prime setup for **[[Pyelonephritis|pyelonephritis]]** and **[[Acute Kidney Injury|AKI]]** — so decompress (replace the tube) and treat rather than simply "work up fevers and chills," and expect a creatinine bump even with a contralateral functioning kidney.
- **A friable vascular papule that bleeds with the slightest contact, growing over weeks after local or repetitive microtrauma, is a pyogenic granuloma — not an indolent infection.** The **[[Pyogenic Granuloma|lobular capillary hemangioma]]** classically arises at sites of **[[Repetitive Microtrauma|repeated microtrauma]]** (a weightlifter's gripping finger), shows a scaly collarette, and is cured by removal/**[[Cryotherapy|cryotherapy]]** — sparing the patient months of futile antibiotics.

## Case Narrative

*A visual episode: Rabih presents five photographs and Reza reasons from each image plus a single historical clue. The value is in the cross-cutting method, not a unifying diagnosis; case 5 is left undisclosed.*

**Case 1 — bullous hand lesion in hepatitis C → porphyria cutanea tarda.** Reza describes an older hand with actinic changes and, between thumb and index finger, a **[[Blistering Rash|large tense blister]]** whose contents match the skin hue; incised, it is **serosanguinous**. Given the focal, hand-only distribution and a history of **[[Hepatitis C|hepatitis C]]**, he initially reaches for the HCV immunology (cryoglobulinemia, B-cell/lymphoproliferation) before recognizing — with decision-support prompting — that a blistering eruption on the **sun-exposed dorsum of the hand** is **[[Porphyria Cutanea Tarda|porphyria cutanea tarda]]**. The San Francisco VA's **[[Roving Dermatology|roving dermatology]]** NPs confirm it: a high **[[Ferritin|ferritin]]** and elevated **[[Urinary Porphyrins|urinary porphyrins]]**, with treatment directed at the underlying **[[Hepatitis C|HCV]]** plus photoprotection.

**Case 2 — diffuse pruritic rash, interweb + genital involvement → scabies.** Four photos show a **whole-body, intensely itchy, papular/macular** eruption of **two months** — including the palms — without systemic symptoms. Reza runs the rash schema: palms trigger a reflex **[[Syphilis|syphilis]]** check, and he splits **[[Internal vs External Rash|inside-job vs outside-job]]**, noting a diffuse symmetric rash resists an external-contact explanation and a two-month course resists a simple allergy. Labs (HIV, RPR, routine) are negative; the pivotal history is that the man is **marginally housed** with **affected close contacts**, and the images show **interweb-space** and **genital** involvement. He lands on **[[Scabies|scabies]]** — invoking the **[[Two S's|"two S's"]]** (hand + genital rash = scabies or syphilis until proven otherwise) — recommends isolation/PPE and empiric permethrin, and the diagnosis is confirmed by **skin scraping** (mite visualized); treatment is permethrin plus oral **[[Ivermectin|ivermectin]]** given the extent, with full resolution.

**Case 3 — stained shirt + dislodged nephrostomy tube → obstructive pyelonephritis.** A man presents with **fevers and chills**; Reza notices a **circular wet stain** on the center-back of his green shirt over the flank, with gauze/tape near the kidneys. History: **prostate cancer** with **bilateral [[Nephrostomy Tube|nephrostomy tubes]]** (for malignant ureteral obstruction) and **[[Alzheimer Disease|Alzheimer dementia]]** — and the patient is unaware his tube has **dislodged**. Reza reasons the physiology: a displaced tube re-**[[Obstructive Uropathy|obstructs]]** the kidney, and **obstruction → stasis → infection**, so he predicts **[[Pyelonephritis|pyelonephritis]]** with **[[Hydronephrosis|hydronephrosis]]** and an **[[Acute Kidney Injury|AKI]]** (compounded by hypovolemia and sepsis, and not fully spared by the contralateral tube). Rabih underscores the lesson — **[[Physical Examination|inspection]]** under the shirt and bandage cracked a febrile *demented* patient in one minute, with **no CT needed** (a tube that's fully out is its own diagnosis) — and the patient did well after **urgent tube replacement** and antibiotics.

**Case 4 — friable finger papule in a weightlifter → pyogenic granuloma.** The last revealed image shows a **gelatinous, dilated lesion** protruding from a puncture site on the index finger. Reza's first instinct is a hand-space infection (flexor **tenosynovitis** — a hand-saving surgical consult), but the history reframes it: **four months** of a non-resolving lesion after a **gym abrasion**, failing broad-spectrum antibiotics, **painless and non-erythematous**, that **bleeds with minimal contact** — plus visible **gym calluses**. It is a **[[Pyogenic Granuloma|pyogenic granuloma]] (lobular capillary hemangioma)**, driven by **[[Repetitive Microtrauma|repetitive gripping microtrauma]]**, with the classic **scaly collarette**; the dermatologist froze it off (**[[Cryotherapy|liquid nitrogen]]**, ~10% recurrence), ending a four-month ordeal in two minutes.

**Case 5 — withheld.** A patient with **alcohol-related cirrhosis** who develops a rash over a few hours; the diagnosis is intentionally not revealed on the episode (offered only to book purchasers) and so is not analyzed here.

### Illness Scripts and Bias

- **[[Pattern Recognition|Pattern recognition]] with a schema backstop.** Dermatologic diagnosis leans on visual gestalt, but each case is disciplined by a schema — **[[Internal vs External Rash|inside vs outside job]]**, the **[[Two S's|two-S's]]** rule, obstruction physiology, the friable-vascular-papule script — so recognition is checked, not blind.
- **One historical fact swings the differential.** Hepatitis C converts a nonspecific bulla into **[[Porphyria Cutanea Tarda|PCT]]**; "marginally housed with affected contacts" converts a diffuse rash into **[[Scabies|scabies]]**; "weightlifter, four months, bleeds" converts a feared hand infection into a **[[Pyogenic Granuloma|pyogenic granuloma]]** — a repeated demonstration that history reshapes the image.
- **[[Base Rate|Base rate]] and the palms reflex.** Palmar involvement reflexively raises **[[Syphilis|syphilis]]**; the two-month tempo down-weights allergy; and the reminder that a common diagnosis (scabies) can look dramatic keeps the differential grounded.
- **[[Physical Examination|Inspection]] as a diagnostic act.** The stained shirt and exposed **[[Nephrostomy Tube|nephrostomy]]** site collapse a fever workup into a one-minute diagnosis — the highest-yield "test" in a patient who cannot give a history — with the corollary that a fully dislodged tube needs no confirmatory imaging.
- **[[Test Interpretation|Confirm efficiently]].** PCT (urinary porphyrins/ferritin), scabies (scraping), and pyogenic granuloma (clinical/dermoscopic) each have a light, targeted confirmatory step rather than a broad workup — matching test to a specific hypothesis.
- **[[Diagnostic Humility|Humility]] and tooling.** Both hosts openly miss diagnoses they "don't think about regularly" (PCT, pyogenic granuloma) and model using **[[AI-Augmented Reasoning|decision-support tools]]** (or texting a dermatologist) when specialist eyes aren't available — and note how a **[[Roving Dermatology|roving dermatology]]** service repeatedly shortens ordeals.

## Teaching Discussion

- Recognize a tense, serosanguinous blister on the sun-exposed dorsum of the hand as porphyria cutanea tarda, confirm with urinary porphyrins and ferritin, and pursue and treat the triggers — hepatitis C, iron/HFE, alcohol, and estrogen — alongside photoprotection.
- Apply the two-S's rule to a rash involving the hands and genitals — scabies or syphilis until proven otherwise — checking an RPR (palms/soles) and examining the interweb spaces for burrows, and treat scabies with permethrin plus oral ivermectin when the disease is extensive.
- Divide every rash into an inside job versus an outside job, using distribution (diffuse/symmetric versus focal), time course (a two-month rash is not a simple allergy), and the exposure/social history rather than morphology alone.
- Treat inspection as a diagnostic act: examine under the shirt and dressings, especially in patients who cannot give a history, recognize a fully dislodged nephrostomy tube as its own diagnosis needing no imaging, and reason from obstruction to stasis to infection when a decompressing tube fails.
- Distinguish a pyogenic granuloma — a friable, rapidly growing vascular papule that bleeds easily at a site of repetitive microtrauma, often with a scaly collarette — from an indolent skin/soft-tissue infection, and treat by removal or cryotherapy rather than prolonged antibiotics.
- Match a light, targeted confirmatory test to each visual hypothesis, and when specialist dermatology is unavailable, use decision-support tools or a curbside to shorten the path to a treatable diagnosis.
