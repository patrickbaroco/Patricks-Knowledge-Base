## Summary

> [!summary] Scan Summary
> A neurology Virtual Morning Report with neurologist **Aaron Berkowitz** on **behavior change, headaches, and blurry vision** — a chief concern with no ready schema. A previously high-functioning **51-year-old businesswoman** declines over a **month** (no longer shopping or working), with a new throbbing headache, nausea/vomiting, weight loss, poor sleep, and blurred/double vision, punctuated by **two episodes of hours-long confusion and "irrelevant talk"** (arousable, no convulsion) with **incomplete recovery between them**. Berkowitz models "**[[Round to the Nearest Schema|rounding to the nearest schema]]**" — behavior change → [[Altered Mental Status|altered mental status]] → differential = [[Anatomical Approach|localization]] × [[Illness Tempo|time course]] (brain/frontal-limbic; subacute) — and then **[[Rapidly Progressive Dementia|rapidly progressive dementia]]**, walking through [[Autoimmune Encephalitis|autoimmune encephalitis]] ([[Anti-NMDA Receptor Encephalitis|NMDA]], [[LGI1 Encephalitis|LGI1]]), slow infections, toxic-metabolic (a petrol-station exposure), and mass. The exam is non-focal but shows **bilateral papilledema** — raised [[Intracranial Pressure|intracranial pressure]] — steering the room via the [[Monro-Kellie Doctrine|Monro-Kellie]] compartments (brain/CSF/blood) toward the **CSF space** ([[Chronic Meningitis|chronic meningitis]], [[Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|leptomeningeal carcinomatosis]], a [[Colloid Cyst|ball-valve]] ventricular mass). The **CT is the answer**: multiple small cystic lesions each with an **eccentric dot (the scolex)** — **[[Neurocysticercosis|neurocysticercosis]]** in the active vesicular phase. The episodic "confusion" proves to be **[[Non-Convulsive Seizure|discognitive (non-convulsive) seizures]]**, and she recovers fully on **albendazole plus steroids**. The teaching miss the team names: they reached for *one* large lesion or the CSF space and never considered **[[Multifocal Encephalopathy|many small lesions]]** — the pattern that pointed at the answer.

## Pearls

- **When a chief concern has no schema, [[Round to the Nearest Schema|round to the nearest one]].** "Behavior/personality change" has no dedicated framework, so fold it into [[Altered Mental Status|altered mental status]] and run differential = [[Anatomical Approach|localization]] × [[Illness Tempo|time course]]: behavior localizes to **frontal/frontotemporal-limbic** brain, and a **one-month** (subacute) tempo brings [[Autoimmune Encephalitis|immune-mediated encephalitis]], slow infection (fungal/TB/parasitic), toxic-metabolic, and mass — while a decline severe enough to impair ADLs over a month is **[[Rapidly Progressive Dementia|rapidly progressive dementia]]** (whose differential is far broader than the reflexive [[Creutzfeldt-Jakob Disease|CJD]]).
- **[[Papilledema|Papilledema]] means raised ICP — localize it to one of three compartments.** [[Monro-Kellie Doctrine|Monro-Kellie]]: brain (~80%), CSF (~10%), blood (~10%). A non-focal patient with papilledema points away from a focal brain lesion and toward the **CSF space** (hydrocephalus, meningitis) or, less likely here on tempo, the venous system ([[Cerebral Venous Sinus Thrombosis|CVST]]). "[[Chronic Meningitis|Chronic meningitis]]" (> ~3 weeks) opens fungal, tubercular, [[Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|neoplastic]], and immune/drug-induced aseptic meningitis.
- **The "[[Neurocysticercosis|circle with a dot]]" is pathognomonic — a cyst with its eccentric scolex.** Multiple small cystic lesions each containing a dot is **neurocysticercosis** ([[Taenia Solium|Taenia solium]] larvae), the world's commonest cause of acquired epilepsy in endemic regions. Read the **radiologic stage**: vesicular (active) → colloidal → granular-nodular → **calcified (inactive)**; racemose = subarachnoid. Only *cystic* (non-calcified) stages are active disease.
- **Treat neurocysticercosis by cyst count and location — always with steroids.** ≤2 cysts → **albendazole + steroids**; >2 → **albendazole + praziquantel + steroids**; intraventricular → **surgery**; calcified/inactive → no antiparasitic, treat seizures symptomatically. Antiparasitics break down cysts and trigger intense edema, so **steroids run concurrently**. The exception is **[[Neurocysticercotic Encephalitis|neurocysticercotic encephalitis]]** (miliary cysts, usually children/first-exposure, girls > boys): give **steroids alone** — antiparasitics there can cause fatal cerebral edema and herniation.
- **Neurocysticercosis usually causes seizures, headache, or hydrocephalus — not cognitive decline.** Even a high cyst burden in adults typically presents with seizures and an otherwise normal patient; cognitive change is the rare [[Neurocysticercotic Encephalitis|encephalitic]] form. Here the "confusion" was **[[Non-Convulsive Seizure|discognitive (non-convulsive) seizures]]** — a reminder that *anything episodic in neurology could be seizure*, EEG is the test of truth, and non-convulsive seizures present as behavior change, staring, or somnolence rather than convulsions.
- **Encephalopathy without focality can come from *many small lesions*, not just one big one.** The team reached for a single frontal mass or a CSF-space process and missed the third pattern — **[[Multifocal Encephalopathy|multiple small lesions]]** (a shower of emboli, disseminated cysticerci) disrupting cortico-cortical networks diffusely. Keeping "many small lesions" on the differential for a non-focal encephalopathy would have pointed at the answer.

## Problem Representation

A previously high-functioning 51-year-old woman presents with one month of progressive behavioral decline sufficient to impair her daily activities, accompanied by a new throbbing headache with nausea and vomiting, weight loss, reduced sleep and appetite, and blurred/double vision, punctuated by two discrete episodes of hours-long confusion and irrelevant speech (arousable, non-convulsive) with incomplete recovery between them, on a background of prior (non-cerebral) malaria in an endemic, resource-limited setting. Her exam is non-focal except for bilateral papilledema, with a normal motor, sensory, cerebellar, and gait exam; labs show a chronic mild anemia and mild leukocytosis with otherwise normal metabolic and thyroid studies. The syndrome is a subacute encephalopathy/rapidly progressive dementia with raised intracranial pressure localized (by exam) toward a diffuse or CSF-space process; a contrast CT reveals multiple small cystic lesions each with an eccentric scolex, diagnostic of active (vesicular) neurocysticercosis, with the episodic confusion representing discognitive non-convulsive seizures — resolving fully on albendazole and steroids.

## Case Narrative

A CP Solvers Neurology VMR with **Aaron Berkowitz** teaching, **A** (Athant) presenting, and **Vali** (Valeria) discussing. The chief concern: **behavior change** in a 51-year-old woman over a month.

**Rounding to the nearest schema.** Vali admits she has no dedicated schema for "behavioral change," and Berkowitz turns that into a teaching move — **[[Round to the Nearest Schema|round to the nearest schema]]**: behavior/personality change folds into **[[Altered Mental Status|altered mental status]]**, worked by his master equation, differential = [[Anatomical Approach|localization]] × [[Illness Tempo|time course]] (not [[MIST Mnemonic|MIST]]). Localization is not mysterious — behavior localizes to **frontal/frontotemporal-limbic** cortex (Phineas Gage) — so the leverage is tempo. Vali's instinct ([[HSV Encephalitis|herpes encephalitis]]) is too fast for a month; over a **subacute** month Berkowitz loads the bucket: **[[Autoimmune Encephalitis|immune-mediated limbic encephalitis]]** ([[Anti-NMDA Receptor Encephalitis|NMDA]], [[LGI1 Encephalitis|LGI1]], and rarer entities like anti-IgLON5), **slow infection** (fungal, tubercular, parasitic — with meningeal/ICP effects), **toxic-metabolic** (including B12 and a petrol/diesel exposure), and **mass**. He escalates the framing: a decline severe enough to impair ADLs over a month is **[[Rapidly Progressive Dementia|rapidly progressive dementia]]**, and while everyone reflexively names [[Creutzfeldt-Jakob Disease|CJD]] (~1 in a million; the 2011 "CJD that wasn't" series), the treatable causes are largely **immune-mediated**.

**HPI.** A previously active businesswoman (she sells petrol and diesel) has, over a month, stopped shopping and working. She developed a **throbbing headache with nausea and vomiting**, then — 1–2 hours later — **confusion and irrelevant talk**, arousable but with eyes closed, **no** convulsion, stiffness, incontinence, or weakness, lasting ~5 hours before returning to a quiet baseline; a **second such episode two weeks later** lasted 8 hours. She walks slowly (no falls or weakness), and has **weight loss, reduced sleep and appetite, and blurred/double vision** without hallucinations. Critically, she is **declining between the episodes**, not returning to normal. History includes **malaria the prior year** (no cerebral malaria) in an endemic, resource-limited setting; no vascular, psychiatric, or family neurologic history.

**Building the differential.** Berkowitz reads the episodes carefully: a throbbing headache turning into altered consciousness sounds migrainous, but the rest could be **discognitive (non-convulsive) seizure** ("seizure can do anything"; the nausea as a rising epigastric aura). The **decline-with-superimposed-episodes** pattern raises [[Dementia with Lewy Bodies|Lewy body]] fluctuations (which A pre-excludes — no hallucinations, no parkinsonism) and the possibility of **elevated-ICP crises** — invoking the textbook **[[Colloid Cyst|third-ventricular colloid cyst]]** with a ball-valve effect. The petrol exposure raises a **toxic encephalopathy**, likely a red herring. He keeps **mass vs limbic encephalitis** live but concedes the exam is unlikely to localize.

**The exam and the papilledema.** Vitals are unremarkable (HR 104). She is **alert, oriented to place and person but not time**, not following commands well, with an otherwise non-focal exam — normal cranial nerves and eye movements, **5/5 power, normal tone, reflexes 1+, downgoing plantars, no ataxia, normal gait** — except **bilateral papilledema**. Berkowitz pivots on it: papilledema = **raised [[Intracranial Pressure|intracranial pressure]]**, localized by the **[[Monro-Kellie Doctrine|Monro-Kellie]]** compartments (brain ~80%, CSF ~10%, blood ~10%). Vali reasons through them — **[[Cerebral Venous Sinus Thrombosis|venous sinus thrombosis]]** is too subacute-episodic to fit, a purely frontal **mass** big enough to raise ICP with *no* focality is a stretch (Berkowitz's own resident case: a huge frontal meningioma mistaken for depression, with only a subtle pronator drift) — and lands on the **CSF space**, asking A about meningismus (negative) out of respect for meningitis.

**The CSF-space differential.** Berkowitz builds "**[[Chronic Meningitis|chronic meningitis]]**" (> ~3 weeks): **infectious** (fungal, tubercular), **[[Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|leptomeningeal carcinomatosis]]** (usually a late complication of known cancer, but plausibly an occult breast/gynecologic/lung/GI primary in a resource-limited setting — with his cases of gastric carcinoma seeding the CSF and causing seizure-like posturing and ICP pressure waves), and **immune/drug-induced aseptic** meningitis (sarcoid, RA pachymeningitis, IgG4, ANCA vasculitis; NSAID/IVIG/Bactrim). Vali adds **[[Cerebral Malaria|cerebral malaria]]** (given the prior malaria) — which Berkowitz reframes as an acute illness of children/first-exposure travelers, not a month-long course — and **intraventricular [[Neurocysticercosis|cysticercal]] or [[Cryptococcal Meningitis|cryptococcal]] lesions** causing hydrocephalus.

**The scan.** Labs show a **chronic mild anemia** (Hgb 10.5, present for months), mild leukocytosis, and ESR 30, with normal metabolic/thyroid studies. The **contrast CT** shows multiple small cystic lesions, several with an **eccentric dot** — the **scolex** — on a somewhat swollen brain. Vali recognizes it: **[[Neurocysticercosis|neurocysticercosis]]**, and (reaching for the miliary picture in Berkowitz's book) wonders about [[Neurocysticercotic Encephalitis|neurocysticercotic encephalitis]]. Berkowitz confirms neurocysticercosis in the **active (vesicular) phase** with a moderately high — but not miliary — cyst burden.

**Diagnosis, treatment, and the teaching.** The episodic "confusion" is judged to be **[[Non-Convulsive Seizure|discognitive non-convulsive seizures]]** (no hydrocephalus or diffuse edema to invoke ICP crises), and she was treated with **albendazole (14 days) plus tapering IV steroids** — recovering **completely**. Berkowitz teaches the disease in full: the **[[Taenia Solium|Taenia solium]]** larval cyst with its **scolex** ("circle with a dot," pathognomonic); the **radiologic stages** (vesicular → colloidal → granular-nodular → calcified/inactive; racemose in the subarachnoid space) and his paper *The Many Faces of Neurocysticercosis*; the **count/location-based treatment** (≤2 cysts: albendazole + steroids; >2: add praziquantel; intraventricular: surgery; calcified: symptomatic only) always with **concurrent steroids**; and the crucial exception — **[[Neurocysticercotic Encephalitis|neurocysticercotic encephalitis]]** (miliary, children, girls > boys), treated with **steroids alone** because antiparasitics can precipitate fatal edema and herniation. He closes on the two cognitive lessons: **anything episodic in neurology may be seizure** (Tracy Milligan's "scared everything is a seizure → nothing → everything again," EEG as the arbiter), and a non-focal **encephalopathy can arise from *many small lesions*** (a shower of emboli, disseminated cysticerci) — the pattern the team never put on the board, reaching instead for one big lesion or the CSF space.

### Illness Scripts and Bias

- **[[Round to the Nearest Schema|Rounding to the nearest schema]].** With no framework for "behavior change," folding it into [[Altered Mental Status|AMS]] and then [[Rapidly Progressive Dementia|rapidly progressive dementia]] kept the reasoning productive — the same localization × time-course engine as [[CPS 269 - Headache and Leptomeningeal Lymphomatosis|CPS 269]], [[CPS 359 - Altered Mental Status and Anti-NMDA Receptor Encephalitis|CPS 359]], and [[CPS 386 - Rapidly Progressive Dementia and Intravascular Large B-Cell Lymphoma|CPS 386]].
- **[[Papilledema|Papilledema]] → [[Monro-Kellie Doctrine|three-compartment]] reasoning.** Converting a scary sign into a structured differential (brain/CSF/blood) is the move that organized the case — shared with [[CPS 269 - Headache and Leptomeningeal Lymphomatosis|CPS 269]] and [[CPS 220 - Drowsiness and Bickerstaff Brainstem Encephalitis|CPS 220]].
- **[[Base Rate|Base rates]] and setting.** In an endemic, resource-limited setting, a common parasitic disease outranks the exotic; the reasoning down-weighted CJD and cerebral malaria on tempo while keeping occult cancer alive — and the answer was the locally common one.
- **The differential-breadth miss — [[Multifocal Encephalopathy|many small lesions]].** The team framed the non-focal picture as *one* large lesion or a CSF-space process and never generated the "multiple small lesions" branch; naming that third pattern (shower of emboli, disseminated cysticerci) is the durable lesson.
- **[[Non-Convulsive Seizure|Anything episodic may be seizure]].** The hours-long confusional spells were reframed from ICP crises to discognitive non-convulsive seizures once imaging showed no hydrocephalus — with EEG as the arbiter and the humility that even experts mislabel spells.
- **[[Test Interpretation|Test interpretation]] as the pivot.** The CT's "circle with a dot" was pathognomonic, and reading the *stage* (vesicular = active) directly determined therapy — a case where imaging, not the exam, carried the diagnosis.

## Teaching Discussion

- When a chief concern lacks a schema, round to the nearest one — behavior/personality change becomes altered mental status, worked by localization × time course (frontal/limbic brain; subacute tempo) — and escalate to rapidly progressive dementia when a month of decline impairs daily function, remembering its differential is far broader than CJD.
- Convert papilledema into a three-compartment (Monro-Kellie) differential — brain, CSF, blood — and let a non-focal exam push toward a diffuse or CSF-space process (chronic meningitis, leptomeningeal carcinomatosis, a ventricular mass) rather than a single focal lesion, while keeping venous sinus thrombosis in mind for the right tempo.
- Recognize the "circle with a dot" (cyst plus eccentric scolex) as pathognomonic for neurocysticercosis, read its radiologic stage (vesicular → colloidal → granular-nodular → calcified; racemose in the subarachnoid space), and treat only active, non-calcified disease.
- Treat neurocysticercosis by cyst count and location, always with concurrent steroids: ≤2 cysts with albendazole, >2 with albendazole plus praziquantel, intraventricular cysts surgically, and calcified disease symptomatically — and give steroids alone (never antiparasitics) in the miliary neurocysticercotic encephalitis of children, where antiparasitics can cause fatal edema.
- Remember that neurocysticercosis usually causes seizures, headache, or hydrocephalus rather than cognitive decline, so an encephalopathic presentation should prompt a search for the encephalitic form or an alternative explanation.
- Treat anything episodic in neurology as possibly seizure — non-convulsive/discognitive seizures present as confusion, staring, or behavior change, EEG is the confirmatory test, and even skilled clinicians mislabel spells.
- Add "many small lesions" to the differential for a non-focal encephalopathy (a shower of emboli, disseminated infection or cysts), alongside a single strategic lesion and a diffuse toxic-metabolic process, so that a multifocal cause is not missed.
