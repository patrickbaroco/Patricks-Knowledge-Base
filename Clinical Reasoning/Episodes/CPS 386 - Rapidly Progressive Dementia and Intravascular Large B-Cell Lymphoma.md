## Summary

> A neurology Virtual Morning Report with neurologist **Aaron Berkowitz** and resident **Sebastian Green** presenting "confusion" — a case that becomes a masterclass in the differential for [[Rapidly Progressive Dementia|rapidly progressive dementia]]. A **77-year-old emeritus professor** with **two subcortical strokes and a central retinal artery occlusion in the prior 3–4 months** — all while anticoagulated on apixaban — has months of progressive [[Cortical vs Subcortical Dementia|subcortical]] cognitive and gait decline, incontinence, and 10 lb of weight loss. A mega-workup is relentlessly negative — MRI shows multifocal infarcts of varied ages without enhancement, MRA/TTE/TEE/PET-CT/hypercoagulable and autoimmune panels are all unremarkable, and an LP is bland (protein 101 but only 3 cells) — while the patient strokes every three days. ## Pearls

- **[[Dementia|Dementia]] is a syndrome, not a diagnosis, and [[Rapidly Progressive Dementia|rapidly progressive dementia]] is not all [[Creutzfeldt-Jakob Disease|CJD]].** RPD (subacute cognitive decline over weeks–months) has a real differential: atypically fast neurodegenerative disease, immune-mediated ([[Autoimmune Encephalitis|antibody-mediated]], [[Cerebral Amyloid Angiopathy|CAA-related inflammation]], "Hashimoto"/autoimmune encephalitis), ongoing strokes, malignancy, and toxic-metabolic causes. The Case Western prion-center series of "CJD that wasn't" was mostly atypically-rapid neurodegeneration and immune-mediated disease — and Berkowitz's clinical aphorism is that **the most common cause of RPD is that the history was wrong and it isn't actually that rapid.**
- **Recurrent strokes — especially *through* anticoagulation — are a [[Worst-Case Reasoning|red flag for occult malignancy]] or a hypercoagulable state.** Three strokes in three months breaks the base rate for embolic disease (even a high CHA₂DS₂-VASc yields a low daily risk). Work the [[Pump Pipes Plasma|pump–pipes–plasma]] schema (heart / arteries / blood), and when a patient strokes on a DOAC, resist the reflexive "DOAC failure" — a small stroke may be a **DOAC *success*** — and instead worry that "the blood is too thick": [[Trousseau Syndrome|Trousseau syndrome]] or, rarely, [[Intravascular Large B-Cell Lymphoma|intravascular lymphoma]].
- **[[Intravascular Large B-Cell Lymphoma|Intravascular large B-cell lymphoma]] lives inside the vessels — so it disrupts the brain but hides from the CSF.** CD20+ B cells fill small vessels (not the wall, not the meningeal space), producing recurrent strokes, progressive dementia, and systemic B-symptoms with a **characteristically bland CSF and often normal LDH/ESR** — the diagnosis is made by tissue (brain biopsy, or a random/cherry-angioma **skin biopsy**), and it responds to R-CHOP. CNS involvement occurs in ~40–75% of cases.
- **Distinguish [[Cortical vs Subcortical Dementia|cortical from subcortical]] cognitive patterns, and examine [[Attention|attention first]].** Psychomotor slowing, inattention, and slowed processing with relatively preserved naming point subcortical (white-matter networks), versus focal aphasia/amnesia/apraxia pointing cortical. Marty Samuels' "Russian dolls": attention is the outermost doll — an inattentive patient cannot be validly tested for memory or language, so a global "confusion" may be inattention rather than a focal cognitive loss.
- **[[Altered Mental Status|Confusion]] = [[Anatomical Approach|localization]] × [[Illness Tempo|time course]], gated medical/neurologic/psychiatric — and beware the two great over-diagnoses.** [[Normal Pressure Hydrocephalus|Normal pressure hydrocephalus]] (the wet/wacky/wobbly triad) and [[Primary CNS Vasculitis|primary CNS vasculitis]] are high-prevalence on the exam and low-prevalence in life; big ventricles are usually atrophy *ex vacuo*, and a vasculopathy on angiography is neither sensitive nor specific for vasculitis (which classically brings headache and an inflammatory CSF, and truly needs biopsy).
- **[[Brain Biopsy|Brain biopsy]] can beat empiric toxic therapy — and is safer than it sounds.** When the choice is between empiric cyclophosphamide for presumed vasculitis and a tissue diagnosis, the biopsy wins: "R-CHOP has more letters than C and P," so treating the wrong disease misses half the therapy. In skilled hands brain biopsy carries very low morbidity and near-nil mortality; here 4 of 5 samples were normal and the 5th made the diagnosis.

## Problem Representation

A 77-year-old previously high-functioning emeritus professor, anticoagulated on apixaban after two recent punctate subcortical strokes and a left central retinal artery occlusion, presents with two to three months of progressive, subcortical-pattern cognitive and functional decline — psychomotor slowing, inattention, impaired recent memory, worsening unsteady gait, and new incontinence — accompanied by roughly 10 lb of weight loss, without fever, headache, focal weakness, or psychiatric history. Exam shows bradycardia, cachexia, an awake-but-inattentive subcortical dementia with dysarthria, a right extensor plantar response, and a non-reactive left pupil attributable to the retinal occlusion. An extensive evaluation — brain MRI/MRA showing multifocal infarcts of varying ages without enhancement, normal TTE/TEE, negative PET-CT, negative hypercoagulable and autoimmune/paraneoplastic panels, and a bland CSF (protein 101 with only 3 cells) — is unrevealing as the patient continues to stroke every few days; a cerebral angiogram demonstrates a diffuse cerebral vasculopathy, and brain biopsy establishes intravascular large B-cell lymphoma, with clinical stabilization on R-CHOP.

## Case Narrative

A monthly Tuesday CP Solvers Neurology VMR, with **Aaron Berkowitz** teaching, UCLA resident **Sebastian Green** presenting, and A discussing. The chief concern is one word: **confusion**.

**Framing confusion before the history.** Berkowitz and A open on first principles. A offers the CP Solvers **[[MIST Mnemonic|MIST]]** schema (metabolic, infectious, structural, toxin) plus dementia, psychiatric disease, and seizure — but Berkowitz, as in his other AMS cases, argues that MIST and its cousins **remove the time course from the equation**, treating all causes as equal when [[Illness Tempo|tempo]] is decisive: sudden-onset confusion is a very different case from confusion evolving over days, weeks, months, or years. His first pass for [[Altered Mental Status|confusion/altered mental status]] is **medical, neurologic, or psychiatric** (sometimes more than one), with the honest caveat that most inpatient AMS consults are medical — the brain "bathed in bad humors" of sepsis, renal or hepatic failure — so the **signal-to-noise ratio for a neurologic cause is very low**, and the discipline is to not over-attribute (a sodium that is only mildly low, or a chronically-taken deliriogenic drug with stable organ function, cannot be blamed). He adds the recurring distinction: is "confusion" a **global inattentive state (delirium)**, or is it a **focal cognitive deficit** — [[Aphasia|aphasia]], amnesia, apraxia, abulia — misread as confusion?

**HPI.** Sebastian presents a **77-year-old man** with a pointed history: **two punctate subcortical strokes three months ago** and a **left central retinal artery occlusion (CRAO)** three to four months ago — all **while on apixaban** — plus migraines (on Botox), chronic kidney disease, and atorvastatin. A retired, recently emeritus professor, independent until this illness, he has declined over two to three months: markedly slow speech and thinking with frequent need for redirection, progressively slow and unsteady gait, and new incontinence (now in diapers). Remote memory is grossly intact; recent memory needs prompting. There is ~10 lb of weight loss, but **no** psychiatric history, hallucinations, new focal deficit, headache, seizure, fever, or new medications; he takes his medications as prescribed (aspirin + clopidogrel after the first strokes, switched empirically to apixaban after further events). He came in after a therapy appointment left him too fatigued to get out of the car.

**Reframing the syndrome.** Berkowitz walks A from "is this delirium or dementia?" to the correct frame: **[[Dementia|dementia]] is a syndrome** — cognitive decline sufficient to impair activities of daily living — **not a diagnosis**, and it has structural, vascular, paraneoplastic, and degenerative causes. Because this evolved over *months*, it is a **[[Rapidly Progressive Dementia|rapidly progressive dementia]] (RPD)**. Just as "dementia" wrongly conjures only Alzheimer's, "RPD" wrongly conjures only **[[Creutzfeldt-Jakob Disease|Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease]]** — a one-to-two-in-a-million diagnosis. Berkowitz invokes the **Case Western / National Prion Center series (~2011)** of brains sent as presumed CJD that were *not*: mostly atypically-rapid neurodegenerative disease (Alzheimer's, Lewy body, FTD, corticobasal degeneration), then immune-mediated disease (then-called "Hashimoto encephalopathy," now understood as [[Autoimmune Encephalitis|autoimmune encephalitis]]), toxic-metabolic, and malignancy — and his clinical rule that **the most common cause of RPD is an inaccurate history** (insidious decline mistaken for rapid). CJD, once truly underway, is devastatingly and unmistakably rapid; a patient who looks "too good for how bad the cognition is" argues against it.

**The unifying thread — ongoing strokes.** A names **[[Vascular Dementia|vascular dementia]]**, and Berkowitz refines it: not the textbook "stepwise decline" (uncommon in life) but either chronic insidious **microvascular ischemic disease** or a **strategic infarct** (a single hippocampal/PCA stroke causing amnesia, or an azygous-ACA infarct taking out both frontal lobes). The salient fact is **three strokes in three months** — which breaks the base rate (even a high CHA₂DS₂-VASc score yields a low *daily* risk). He works stroke etiology through the CP Solvers **[[Pump Pipes Plasma|pump–pipes–plasma]]** schema: **heart** (LV thrombus, atrial fibrillation, low EF, endocarditis, cardiac myxoma/fibroelastoma), **arteries** (carotid stenosis, [[Primary CNS Vasculitis|vasculitis]]/vasculopathy), and **blood** (hypercoagulable states). The blood branch is where the case bends: the weight loss and relentless strokes raise **[[Trousseau Syndrome|Trousseau syndrome]]** (with Marty Samuels' legend of Trousseau diagnosing his own gastric cancer from a migratory DVT) and the rare **[[Intravascular Large B-Cell Lymphoma|intravascular lymphoma]]**, diagnosable by cherry-angioma skin biopsy. Berkowitz drives home the meta-rule: **stroking through anticoagulation should worry you about cancer** — resist the reflexive "apixaban failure" ("maybe it's an apixaban *success*"). He also parks **[[Cerebral Amyloid Angiopathy|CAA-related inflammation]]** (microhemorrhages, asymmetric inflammatory white-matter signal, steroid-responsive) and, prompted by A, **[[Normal Pressure Hydrocephalus|NPH]]** — the wet/wacky/wobbly triad the patient technically has, but usually atrophy *ex vacuo* rather than true hydrocephalus — on the differential, alongside infectious mimics (meningovascular syphilis, HIV/HAND, fungal meningitis).

**The one test, and the exam.** Asked for a single test, A chooses **MRI brain with contrast** (to catch a leaky blood-brain barrier from inflammation or malignancy), plus a reversible-dementia serum screen (B12, thyroid, HIV, syphilis). The exam confirms rather than redirects: HR 53, BP 112/56, afebrile, **cachectic**; awake but not alert with **profound psychomotor retardation**; poor attention (cannot spell "world" backward or do serial 7s); slow, dysarthric but fluent speech with intact naming, following two- but not three-step commands; **recent-memory impairment** (1 of 3 at five minutes) with intact remote memory; a non-reactive left pupil and count-fingers vision from the CRAO; increased tone with decreased bulk but 5/5 strength on encouragement; a **right extensor plantar response** with a flat left; and a gait so unsteady it took three people and was deferred. Berkowitz reads this as a **[[Cortical vs Subcortical Dementia|subcortical]] pattern** — slowed processing with cortex "alive but slow," not focal cortical loss — and teaches Samuels' **[[Attention|"Russian dolls"]]**: attention is the outermost doll, so an inattentive patient cannot be validly assessed for memory or language. The right upgoing toe hints at an asymmetric left-sided process (or merely cervical stenosis), and the fixed pupil is retinal, not a third-nerve sign.

**The mega-workup.** Reversible-dementia labs are normal (thiamine 98, B12, TSH, HIV non-reactive, RPR non-reactive); **ESR 28, CRP unrecordably low**. **MRI/MRA** shows **multifocal tiny supra- and infratentorial infarcts** bilaterally, predominantly white matter with some cortical involvement, scattered confluent FLAIR change, and lesions of varying ages (some DWI-bright/ADC-dark, some normalized) — but **no contrast enhancement** and a normal MRA. The stroke hunt then comes up empty across the board: **TTE then TEE** normal (no LAA thrombus, negative bubble study for PFO, normal valves), repeat blood cultures negative, APLS and a full autoimmune panel negative, **whole-body PET-CT** with no abnormal FDG uptake, and a **bland LP** (opening pressure 18, <1 RBC, 3 WBC, glucose 61, **protein 101**, normal IgG index, negative oligoclonal bands, negative VZV IgG and VDRL, negative autoimmune/paraneoplastic and meningoencephalitis panels). Flow cytometry shows no monotypic B-cell population; cytology notes mildly reactive lymphocytes/monocytes. Berkowitz reads the isolated protein of 101 as nonspecific ([[Albuminocytologic Dissociation|albuminocytologic dissociation]] is not only Guillain-Barré) — a faint hint of inflammation with no cells to corroborate.

**Angiogram, biopsy, diagnosis.** As the patient **strokes roughly every three days** (a stroke code and new punctate infarcts each time), the team escalates to a **[[Cerebral Angiography|cerebral angiogram]]**, which shows **diffuse multifocal narrowing and dilatation across MCA, ACA, and PCA branches** — a **cerebral vasculopathy**, but not its cause. Berkowitz notes the limits: an angiographic "vasculopathy" is neither sensitive nor specific for [[Primary CNS Vasculitis|primary CNS vasculitis]] (his talk "how not to diagnose primary CNS vasculitis" — a diagnosis over-called on imaging that classically needs biopsy plus headache and inflammatory CSF). With heart and blood "out" and only "pipes" left — CNS vasculitis versus intravascular lymphoma — and given the weight loss and age, he predicts **[[Intravascular Large B-Cell Lymphoma|intravascular large B-cell lymphoma]]**. A **[[Brain Biopsy|brain biopsy]]** (cortical vein, dura, gray and white matter; 4 of 5 samples normal, positive on the 5th) confirms it: CD20+ B cells packed **within** small vessels — not the wall, not the meninges — which is exactly why the CSF stayed clean.

**Resolution and teaching.** IVLBCL has two phenotypes (a more neurologic "Western" and a more hematologic "Asian/Japanese" form), with CNS involvement in ~40–75% of cases, and is usually diagnosed late because patients are worked up as "strokes." Here even LDH, β2-microglobulin, and protein electrophoresis were unremarkable — the biopsy was the only signal. He was started on **R-CHOP**, stabilized (no further strokes on interval MRI, some weight regained) though with persistent cognitive and gait deficits. Berkowitz closes on two teaching points: the value of tissue over empiric toxic therapy ("R-CHOP has more letters than the C and P you'd give for vasculitis" — empiric cyclophosphamide would miss half the treatment), and the reassurance that **brain biopsy in skilled hands is low-risk** even when sampling tissue around tiny strokes rather than a discrete mass.

### Illness Scripts and Bias

- **[[Localization × Time Course|Localization × time course]] as the master script.** Localization is trivial (confusion → brain); the leverage is tempo — a *subacute* course reclassifies "confusion/dementia" as **rapidly progressive dementia** and unlocks a specific differential, the same Berkowitz frame that anchors [[CPS 153 - Intermittent Altered Mental Status and Non-Lesional Autoimmune Epilepsy|CPS 153]], [[CPS 359 - Altered Mental Status and Anti-NMDA Receptor Encephalitis|CPS 359]], and [[CPS 402 - Altered Mental Status and Disseminated Varicella-Zoster|CPS 402]].
- **[[Base Rate|Base-rate]] discipline against the availability of CJD.** "Rapidly progressive dementia" reflexively summons [[Creutzfeldt-Jakob Disease|CJD]], but the Case Western series and the "the history was wrong" aphorism reset the prior toward atypical neurodegeneration, immune-mediated disease, strokes, and malignancy — CJD is specifically *down*-weighted.
- **[[Worst-Case Reasoning|Worst-case reasoning]] on recurrent strokes.** Rather than accept "apixaban failure," the reasoning treats stroking-through-anticoagulation as a signal that "the blood is too thick," escalating the search to [[Trousseau Syndrome|Trousseau syndrome]] and [[Intravascular Large B-Cell Lymphoma|intravascular lymphoma]] — the dangerous, treatable causes you cannot afford to miss.
- **Schema-driven completeness — [[Pump Pipes Plasma|pump, pipes, plasma]].** The stroke differential is exhausted branch by branch (heart cleared by echo, blood by hypercoagulable panels, then pipes by angiogram), so when two branches are excluded the remaining branch (vasculopathy: itis vs lymphoma) is where the answer must lie.
- **[[Cortical vs Subcortical Dementia|Cortical vs subcortical]] pattern recognition and the [[Attention|attention-first]] exam.** Reading the deficit as subcortical (not focal cortical) points to a diffuse white-matter/vascular process; the Russian-dolls principle prevents over-interpreting "memory" or "language" findings in an inattentive patient.
- **[[Diagnostic Humility|Diagnostic humility]] and tissue over empiricism.** Berkowitz names the over-diagnosis traps ([[Normal Pressure Hydrocephalus|NPH]], [[Primary CNS Vasculitis|primary CNS vasculitis]]) and argues for a [[Brain Biopsy|brain biopsy]] to secure R-CHOP rather than reflexively treating presumed vasculitis with cyclophosphamide — comfortable pursuing certainty when the therapeutic stakes diverge sharply.

## Teaching Discussion

- Work confusion and altered mental status as localization × time course over a first gate of medical/neurologic/psychiatric; because most inpatient AMS is medical with a low signal-to-noise ratio for neurology, avoid over-attributing to trivial metabolic aberrations or chronic stable medications, and use tempo (not a cause-mnemonic like MIST) to select the not-to-miss neurologic diagnoses.
- Treat dementia as a syndrome (cognitive decline impairing ADLs), and when the course is subacute, call it rapidly progressive dementia and run its differential — atypically fast neurodegeneration, immune-mediated disease (autoimmune encephalitis, CAA-related inflammation), ongoing strokes, malignancy, toxic-metabolic — rather than defaulting to CJD; remember the most common "RPD" is an inaccurate history of a slower process.
- Separate cortical from subcortical cognitive patterns: psychomotor slowing, inattention, and slowed processing with preserved naming suggest a subcortical white-matter process, whereas focal aphasia, amnesia, or apraxia suggest cortical disease; and examine attention first (the Russian-dolls principle) because memory and language cannot be validly assessed in an inattentive patient.
- Regard recurrent strokes — particularly through anticoagulation — as a red flag for occult malignancy or a hypercoagulable state; resist "DOAC failure" reasoning (a small stroke may be a DOAC success) and pursue Trousseau syndrome and, rarely, intravascular lymphoma when the picture includes weight loss and relentless events.
- Structure the stroke etiology workup as pump–pipes–plasma: heart (echo/TEE for thrombus, PFO, valve mass), arteries (carotid/intracranial imaging, angiography for vasculopathy), and blood (hypercoagulable and malignancy evaluation, including whole-body imaging), and recognize that clearing two branches concentrates probability on the third.
- Recognize intravascular large B-cell lymphoma as a great mimic: intravascular CD20+ B cells cause recurrent strokes and rapidly progressive dementia with a characteristically bland CSF and often normal LDH/ESR, diagnosed by tissue (brain biopsy or random/cherry-angioma skin biopsy) and treated with R-CHOP, with CNS involvement in roughly 40–75% of cases.
- Beware the two over-diagnoses — normal pressure hydrocephalus (usually atrophy ex vacuo, not true hydrocephalus) and primary CNS vasculitis (over-called on angiography, which is neither sensitive nor specific; true vasculitis classically brings headache and inflammatory CSF and needs biopsy) — and weigh a low-morbidity brain biopsy against committing a patient to empiric toxic therapy for the wrong disease.
