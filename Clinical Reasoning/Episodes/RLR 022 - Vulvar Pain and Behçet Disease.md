> Reza presents to Rabih a woman with three days of rapidly progressive [[Vulvar Ulceration|vulvar pain, swelling, and ulceration]]. ## Pearls

- **Match the pace of the workup to the pace of the illness.** A complaint that developed over seconds or hours must be solved in seconds or hours; a days-long syndrome demands a days-long answer; a decade of symptoms grants time. This [[Illness Tempo|tempo rule]] also runs in reverse — a tempo of days is nearly incompatible with malignancy, so [[HPV-Related Squamous Cell Carcinoma|HPV-related vulvar cancer]] can be pruned early.
- **When new "peaks" appear, ask whether the chief complaint was ever the whole mountain.** Fever, headache, arthralgias, and a rash added to vulvar ulceration remove the illusion of focality and shift the [[Center of Gravity|center of gravity]] from a local gynecologic problem to a systemic inflammatory disease with a vulvar predominance — most of the diagnostic work is deciding which peaks are [[Signal versus Noise|signal and which are noise]].
- **Fever plus a perineal process makes [[Fournier Gangrene|Fournier gangrene]] the first thought, before anything esoteric.** Rabih explicitly catches himself reaching for a rare syndrome and resets to the acutely morbid, surgical diagnosis first — prioritize the can't-miss before indulging the interesting.
- **Painful, aggressive mucosal ulceration points to [[Primary HSV Infection|primary HSV]], not most other STIs — but a painful ulcer does not exclude [[Syphilis|syphilis]].** The classic syphilitic chancre is quiet and painless, yet STIs travel together and HSV–syphilis coinfection can make a painful lesion, so test regardless of the qualifier (and send an HIV test).
- **Recurrent oral *and* genital ulceration is [[Behçet Disease|Behçet disease]] until proven otherwise — and Behçet is a clinical diagnosis with no confirmatory test.** Supporting surface clues are an acneiform rash (often on the back), [[Pathergy|pathergy]], and [[Erythema Nodosum|erythema nodosum]]; confidence is then built by hunting the deeper organs it favors — eyes ([[Uveitis|uveitis]]), CNS, GI tract, and vasculature.
- **In an episodic illness, study the return to baseline as carefully as the flare.** Some relapsing diseases leave the patient a little sicker each cycle; this patient returns fully to baseline between episodes, and capturing that delta is what separates a benign relapsing syndrome from a progressive one.

## Problem Representation

A woman — this her third such episode over roughly 15 years — presents with three days of rapidly progressive vulvar pain, swelling, and ulceration accompanied by fever, a debilitating headache without encephalopathy, large-joint arthralgias, and a tender pustular rash across the chest; she is febrile to 39.2 °C, tachycardic, diaphoretic, and comfortable-appearing without meningismus or clinical arthritis, with exquisitely tender necrotic ulcerations of the labia majora on an edematous vulva, tender acneiform pustules confined to the chest (most prominent beneath a peeled EKG lead), and a neutrophil-predominant leukocytosis of ~22,000 with otherwise normal basic labs; HIV, syphilis, and HSV testing are negative, and a consultant elicits a history of recurrent, self-limited episodes of oral and genital ulceration with fever that resolve to baseline — the picture of a recurrent systemic inflammatory disease with mucocutaneous (oral and genital) predominance most consistent with Behçet disease, confirmed by a dramatic response to IV corticosteroids.

## Case Narrative

After Reza catches up on Rabih's 14-day service block and the newest CP Solvers family member — Rumi DX, a ten-week-old golden doodle — he offers a deliberately generous one-liner: a *young woman* with three days of rapidly progressive [[Vulvar Ulceration|vulvar pain, swelling, and ulceration]].

Rabih opens with metacognition. As a VA emergency provider, he sees very few young patients and very few women, so his guard goes up immediately: he cannot "glide," and his [[Base Rate|base rate]] for vulvar pain is skewed by what he has *read* rather than *seen*, which he consciously holds back. He notes that vulvar pain — like weakness prompting a neurologic exam — is a complaint he wants to *examine* before talking further. Most of all he is struck by the tempo, and articulates the rule Reza will single out as the episode's gem: you should aim to get to the bottom of a problem as fast as it developed — a decade of symptoms grants time, a few days does not, and seconds or hours demand an answer in kind. Reza calls this beautiful and generalizes it (PE, dissection, ACS demand speed; chronic abdominal pain, even from acute intermittent porphyria, grants time), later declaring that [[Illness Tempo|tempo]] "is truly queen."

Reza then broadens the picture: over the same three days she has had fevers, a debilitating headache without confusion or encephalopathy, large-joint arthralgias, and — one day before presenting — a tender pustular rash scattered across the chest. Rabih reflects on how the landscape has changed: the [[Center of Gravity|illusion of focality]] is removed. What looked like a single tall mountain of vulvar pain now has other peaks — headache, arthralgias, rash — and the task becomes deciding which to climb. Drawing on experience, he judges each ancillary peak only "plus-or-minus" productive: headaches accompany almost any acute illness, arthralgias (unlike arthritis) localize to nothing, and rashes are endlessly labeled nonspecific. The vulvar ulceration *must* be explained; the others may be corner pieces or may not belong to the puzzle at all. But the *combination* is informative — it shifts the center of gravity from a vulvar disorder to a systemic inflammatory disease with a vulvar predominance. Reza underscores the point: the hardest part of the work is distinguishing [[Signal versus Noise|signal from noise]] and framing the differential around the center of gravity.

The exam arrives in aliquots: T 39.2 °C, HR 110, normotensive, well-saturated, no clinical arthritis, uncomfortable and drenched in sweat, no nuchal rigidity. On genital exam, the labia majora are swollen and edematous with several severe, exquisitely tender ulcerations, some frankly necrotic; on the chest — and nowhere else — are tender, acne-like pustules. Labs show a neutrophil-predominant leukocytosis of ~22,000, otherwise unremarkable.

Rabih notes the tempo pressure has only risen, and anchors on the fundamental question — what causes inflammation? — via the "[[MAID Mnemonic|I MADE]]" schema: infection, malignancy, autoimmune, drugs. Drugs, though absent here, matter because several drug reactions hit the perineum ([[Stevens-Johnson Syndrome|SJS/TEN]], AGEP, fixed drug eruption). Malignancy — HPV-related squamous cell carcinoma — is real but the days-long tempo is "virtually incompatible" with it, so he prunes it. That leaves infection and autoimmunity. Among sexually transmitted infections, few produce aggressive *surface* ulceration: primary [[Syphilis|syphilis]] is classically quiet and painless, whereas [[Primary HSV Infection|primary HSV]] can be devastatingly painful (he recalls a small child in agony from HSV gingivostomatitis). Reaching briefly toward the autoimmune side — "one that starts with a B" — he stops himself, recognizing an error: *fever plus a perineal process* mandates thinking first about [[Fournier Gangrene|Fournier gangrene]]. He resets to prioritize the acutely morbid, surgical diagnosis and primary HSV before returning to the esoteric.

Reza adds Stu Ray's teaching point — a painful ulcer should not preclude syphilis testing, because STIs travel together and HSV–syphilis coinfection can render a chancre painful — and Rabih adds that an HIV test is mandatory. All three (HIV, syphilis, HSV serologies) return negative. Reza, in his first faculty year, had been anticipating a can't-miss multisystem infection — meningococcemia given the rash and headache — and was preparing an LP, until he consulted a subspecialist (later revealed as rheumatologist Dr. Alan Galber). That consultant obtained the pivotal additional history: the patient has had two prior similar episodes, is monogamous with her monogamous husband, has had *oral* ulcers alongside the vulvar lesions and fever that last a week or two and subside, and this is her third such presentation in 15 years — and the chest rash was most prominent beneath a peeled-off EKG sticker.

Rabih uses the moment to teach that eliciting history is itself a procedure: the consultant's questions flowed from a higher pretest probability for the specific diagnosis, not from a magical touch. The new history reframes the syndrome decisively — a *recurrent, purely episodic* systemic inflammatory disease with oral and genital ulceration that returns fully to baseline between flares. He stresses studying that [[Return to Baseline|return to baseline]]: some relapsing diseases leave the patient sicker each cycle, and capturing the delta matters. The recurrent oral–genital ulcer differential is short — recurrent STIs (excluded), hyper-IgD syndrome, glucagonoma (necrolytic migratory erythema) — but one diagnosis dominates: [[Behçet Disease|Behçet disease]]. It fits a young-to-middle-aged patient; it is worldwide (not merely "east of Turkey"), with Western-hemisphere disease skewing toward women and prominent GI involvement (Rabih has seen three Behçet patients presenting as IBD mimics, one with a perforation). The clinching surface clues are the marked systemic inflammation, the acneiform rash (typically on the back), and [[Pathergy|pathergy]] — the rash provoked under the EKG leads. He then maps how to build confidence beyond the surface (fevers, oral and genital ulcers, acneiform rash, pathergy, [[Erythema Nodosum|erythema nodosum]]) by probing the organs Behçet favors: the eyes ([[Uveitis|uveitis]]), the CNS ([[Neuro-Behçet|neuro-Behçet]] more than peripheral), the GI tract, and the vasculature (vasculitis being among its most morbid features). Because Behçet is a *clinical* diagnosis with no definitive test, he pauses to ask whether something simpler is "wearing a fancier coat today" — and concludes it is not.

Reza reveals the resolution: Dr. Galber told him not to do the LP, to stop the antibiotics, and to start IV steroids — reasoning that more than a week in, a woman looking this well without encephalopathy did not have bacterial meningitis. His larger diagnostic armamentarium let him stay calm where the anxious junior instinct was to "do more than less." The patient met clinical criteria for Behçet, responded dramatically to IV steroids, and was grateful to have a name rather than another "idiopathic" or "viral NOS" label; the case was later published in the *American Journal of Medicine*. Reza closes on medicine as a collaborative, interdependent effort — the key contribution can come from an attending, a student, a nurse, or a pharmacist — and the mic returns to Rabih to sign out.

### Illness Scripts and Bias

- **[[Illness Tempo|Tempo as discriminator]], run both directions.** Rabih's organizing rule — solve a problem as fast as it arose — sets the *pace* of the workup and simultaneously *prunes the differential*: a days-long tempo is nearly incompatible with malignancy. Tempo is used not as a vague descriptor but as an active Bayesian lever ("tempo is truly queen").
- **[[Base Rate|Base rate distorted by exposure]], named and corrected.** Rabih explicitly flags that his internal base rate for vulvar pain comes from reading, not from seeing these patients at a VA, and consciously discounts it. Recognizing that experience can be skewed — and saying so — is what keeps a low-frequency presentation from being force-fit to a familiar script.
- **[[Signal versus Noise|Signal versus noise]] via the center of gravity.** Rather than chase every new symptom, Rabih grades each "peak" (headache, arthralgias, rash) for productivity and lets the *combination* relocate the center of gravity from a local to a systemic process. The discipline is to explain the mandatory finding (vulvar ulceration) while holding ancillary findings loosely until they earn a place in the puzzle.
- **Prioritizing the can't-miss before the esoteric — a live self-correction.** Reaching for rare Behçet, Rabih interrupts himself: fever plus perineum means [[Fournier Gangrene|Fournier gangrene]] first. The teaching value is the visible reset — the acutely morbid, actionable diagnosis is secured before the intellectually satisfying one is explored.
- **Guarding against the seduction of a rare diagnosis.** Before committing to Behçet, Rabih asks whether "something simpler is wearing a fancier coat" — an Occam check that acknowledges how many filters (negative STI workup, recurrence, mucocutaneous pattern) had to be passed before esoterica became legitimate.
- **[[Premature Closure|Anchoring on the can't-miss infection]], relieved by expertise.** Reza, anxious and junior, anchored on meningococcemia and an LP; the expert's broader armamentarium and pattern recognition let him stay calm, defer the LP, stop antibiotics, and treat. Diagnostic confidence, not bravado, is what converts "do more than less" into the correct minimal action.
- **[[History-Taking as a Procedure|History-taking as a procedure]].** The consultant's decisive history flowed from a higher pretest probability that prompted the right questions — reframing "how did they get that history?" from magic to method, and modeling that the missing datum is often a question not yet asked.

## Teaching Discussion

- Set the pace of the evaluation to the pace of the illness: an abrupt, days-long syndrome must be pursued urgently, while a years-long one grants time — and the same tempo logic prunes the differential, since a days-long course is nearly incompatible with malignancy.
- Treat some complaints (vulvar pain, weakness) as reasons to examine before talking further, and be explicit when your internal base rate comes from reading rather than from experience so you can discount it deliberately.
- When ancillary "peaks" (headache, arthralgias, rash) accompany a localizing complaint, grade each for diagnostic productivity and let the combination relocate the center of gravity; most cognitive work in a multisystem presentation is separating signal from noise.
- Anchor an inflammatory presentation on infection, malignancy, autoimmunity, and drugs, then subtract categories on principled grounds — tempo for malignancy, absence of exposure for drugs — before spending effort on what remains.
- With fever and a perineal process, make Fournier gangrene the first diagnosis to exclude before entertaining rarer possibilities; among STIs, aggressive painful mucosal ulceration suggests primary HSV, but a painful ulcer does not exclude syphilis (STIs travel together; HSV–syphilis coinfection can be painful), and an HIV test is mandatory.
- In any relapsing illness, characterize the interval between flares: full return to baseline points toward a benign episodic syndrome, whereas incremental worsening between episodes points toward a progressive or destructive process.
- Put Behçet disease at the top for recurrent oral and genital ulceration; support it with an acneiform rash (often on the back), pathergy, and erythema nodosum, then hunt the deeper organs it favors — uveitis, CNS, GI tract, and vasculature — because Behçet is a clinical diagnosis with no confirmatory test.
- Before committing to a rare diagnosis, ask whether a simpler explanation is merely "wearing a fancier coat," and recognize that a broader diagnostic armamentarium reduces anxiety and enables the correct minimal action (deferring an LP, stopping antibiotics, starting steroids) rather than reflexive over-testing.
