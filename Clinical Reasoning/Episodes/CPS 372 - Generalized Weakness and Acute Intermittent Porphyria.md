## Summary

> [!summary] Scan Summary
> A Neurology VMR on **"generalized weakness"** that is a study in **[[Diagnostic Humility|productive not-knowing]]** and the payoff of **[[Round to the Nearest Schema|distilling a case to its step-1 stem]]** — the answer is **[[Acute Intermittent Porphyria|acute intermittent porphyria]]**. A **29-year-old woman** has **two months of progressive weakness** that **abruptly worsens over three days** (independent → wheelchair), spreading **left leg → right leg → both arms** ("around-the-clock"), with **tingling in all four limbs, new dysphagia, and urinary retention needing a catheter** — layered on **four months of relentless abdominal pain with 40-lb weight loss** and **new-onset seizures** (normal CT, on levetiracetam). Berkowitz first separates **[[Asthenia|systemic "weakness"]]** (anemia, thyroid, cancer — preserved power) from **neurologic weakness**, and frames true four-limb weakness in an awake patient as **"cervical spine or below"** — with **no sensory ⇒ muscle/NMJ**, **sensory present ⇒ cord or nerve**. He repeatedly models **[[Diagnostic Humility|"I don't know"]]**, and reaches for cognitive tools: **[[Unifying Diagnosis|lump the pieces in a young patient]]** (probably one disease, not three), **[[Round to the Nearest Schema|distill to a step-1 stem]]** ("acute neuropathy + abdominal pain — does that ring a bell?" → **[[Acute Intermittent Porphyria|porphyria]]**, raised early then set aside as "too subacute"), and **[[Framing Effect|swap the context]]** ("if this were 78, we'd say cancer + [[Paraneoplastic Neurologic Syndrome|paraneoplastic]]"). The **exam** localizes: **proximal > distal weakness, atrophy/wasting, hypotonia, global [[Areflexia|areflexia]], and sensory loss** (proprioception + pinprick discrimination) with **persistent tachycardia** — a **peripheral sensorimotor process with autonomic involvement**, out of muscle/NMJ (sensory) and out of the cord (areflexia). Clinically it **looks non-length-dependent / demyelinating** (proximal *and* distal, reflexes gone everywhere — the "**[[Axonal vs Demyelinating Neuropathy|paint-splatter]]**" picture vs the length-dependent "traffic-jam" axonal pattern), and the differential opens to **[[CIDP|CIDP]]**, **[[Nodopathy|nodo-/paranodopathies]]**, **[[Paraproteinemic Neuropathy|paraprotein]]**, **[[Vasculitic Neuropathy|vasculitic]]**, and toxic/metabolic causes. An exhaustive workup — B12, thiamine, SPEP/IFE, ganglioside/anti-MAG/neurofascin, ANA/ANCA, HIV/RPR/hepatitis, **heavy metals (the arsenic + abdominal-pain thought)**, whole-spine MRI — is **negative**, and the pivotal lesson lands on **[[Test Interpretation|the EMG overturning the clinical impression]]**: it shows a **reduced-amplitude, preserved-velocity, no-conduction-block, fibrillation-positive *axonal*** neuropathy, *not* demyelinating. Re-labeled a **subacute sensory-motor-autonomic axonal polyneuropathy**, the case returns to the step-1 stem: a **urine PBG of 12.4 (markedly elevated) with elevated ALA** and a confirmatory **[[HMBS Gene|HMBS]] mutation** clinch **[[Acute Intermittent Porphyria|AIP]]** — a disease that "can do anything," breaks the length rule (**axonal yet non-length-dependent, proximal- and motor-predominant**), and is remembered by the **[[Porphyria|five P's]]** (**P**olyneuropathy, abdominal **P**ain out of proportion, **P**sychiatric, **P**ort-wine urine on standing, **P**recipitants). She responded to **[[Hemin|hemin]]** and **[[Givosiran|givosiran]]**.

## Pearls

- **Separate [[Asthenia|systemic "weakness"]] from neurologic weakness first.** A patient can feel weak with *preserved power* from anemia, hypothyroidism, cancer, or infection ("we've all felt weak with the flu"). If it is true motor weakness, run the pathway — brain → brainstem → cord → root → nerve → **[[Neuromuscular Junction|NMJ]]** → muscle — and use associated features to narrow it.
- **True four-limb weakness in an awake patient is "cervical spine or below," and sensory involvement is the great divider.** A thoracic lesion would spare the arms; **no sensory symptoms ⇒ [[Myopathy|muscle]] or [[Neuromuscular Junction|NMJ]]**, **sensory present ⇒ cord or nerve**, and **[[Areflexia|areflexia]]** pushes you *out of the cord and into the peripheral nerve/root*.
- **When many things pile up in a *young* patient, [[Unifying Diagnosis|lump]] — and when stumped, distill to a step-1 stem.** Youth favors one disease over several; and stripping a baroque case down to "**[[Acute Intermittent Porphyria|acute neuropathy + abdominal pain]]**" surfaces the schema the detail was hiding. Berkowitz also **[[Framing Effect|swaps the context]]** ("if this were 78, I'd say cancer + [[Paraneoplastic Neurologic Syndrome|paraneoplastic]]") to test hypotheses.
- **[[Axonal vs Demyelinating Neuropathy|Length-dependent (axonal) vs non-length-dependent (demyelinating)]] — read it off the pattern, but let the EMG have the last word.** Axonal = length-dependent (feet-first; the "traffic-jam" on the longest axons; toxic/metabolic — diabetes, B12). Demyelinating = non-length-dependent (proximal *and* distal, reflexes gone everywhere — "throw a can of **[[Axonal vs Demyelinating Neuropathy|paint]]** at the chalk outline"). This case *looked* demyelinating and was **axonal on EMG**.
- **The EMG is where clinical phenotype can be *overturned*, not just confirmed.** A convincingly non-length-dependent, proximal-predominant picture returned **reduced amplitudes with preserved velocities, no conduction block, and fibrillations/positive sharp waves — axonal**. Amplitude = axons, velocity = myelin, **[[Conduction Block|conduction block]] = acquired demyelination**.
- **[[Acute Intermittent Porphyria|Acute intermittent porphyria]] "can do anything" — central, peripheral, sensory, motor, autonomic — and it *breaks the rules*.** Its neuropathy is **axonal yet often non-length-dependent, proximal- and motor-predominant**, and the course can be **subacute-to-chronic**, not just acute. Remember the **[[Porphyria|five P's]]**: **P**olyneuropathy, abdominal **P**ain (out of proportion to exam), **P**sychiatric change, **P**ort-wine urine (darkens on standing), **P**recipitants (drugs, alcohol).
- **Confirm porphyria with urinary [[Porphobilinogen|PBG]] and [[Aminolevulinic Acid|ALA]] (then genetics), and treat the pathway.** Elevated urine **PBG/ALA** with an **[[HMBS Gene|HMBS]]** (PBG-deaminase) mutation makes AIP; **[[Hemin|hemin]]** infusions downregulate ALA synthase, and **[[Givosiran|givosiran]]** (an ALAS1 siRNA) prevents precursor buildup — and choose anticonvulsants that don't precipitate attacks.

## Problem Representation

A 29-year-old woman presents with **two months of progressive weakness that abruptly worsened over three days** — from independent ambulation to wheelchair transfers — evolving **left leg → right leg → both arms**, with **paresthesias in all four limbs, two days of dysphagia, and urinary retention requiring catheterization**, on a background of **four months of severe, relentless abdominal pain with ~40 lb of weight loss** and **new-onset generalized seizures** (normal head CT, controlled on levetiracetam), plus chronic opioid therapy for the pain. Examination shows **persistent tachycardia**, diffuse abdominal tenderness, and a **peripheral sensorimotor syndrome**: **proximal-greater-than-distal weakness (1–3/5), moderate symmetric atrophy/wasting, hypotonia, global areflexia**, and **sensory loss** (impaired proprioception and pinprick discrimination in all limbs) with intact mental status and cranial nerves. The localization is a **peripheral polyneuropathy with autonomic involvement** (out of muscle/NMJ by the sensory loss, out of the cord by areflexia), which *clinically* appears **non-length-dependent/demyelinating** but which **nerve conduction studies reclassify as axonal** (reduced motor and sensory amplitudes, preserved velocities, no conduction block, with fibrillations and positive sharp waves on EMG). A comprehensive nutritional, toxic (including heavy metals), infectious, and immune-mediated workup and whole-neuraxis MRI are unrevealing; **elevated urinary porphobilinogen (PBG 12.4) and δ-aminolevulinic acid (ALA)** with a confirmatory **HMBS mutation** establish **acute intermittent porphyria** with a **subacute sensory-motor-autonomic axonal polyneuropathy** (unifying the weakness, abdominal pain, seizures, dysautonomia, and weight loss), treated successfully with **hemin** and **givosiran**.

## Case Narrative

Sebastian Green presents **"generalized weakness,"** and Berkowitz has "A" (Aye) open on the two words. She distinguishes **[[Asthenia|subjective weakness with preserved power]]** (anemia, hypothyroidism, cancer, inflammation) from true motor weakness along the pathway (brain → brainstem → cord → roots → nerves → **[[Neuromuscular Junction|NMJ]]** → muscle), noting that truly *generalized, four-limb* weakness implies **bilateral lesions or a generalized process**. Berkowitz endorses it and refines: if the patient is awake and telling the story, true four-limb weakness is **"cervical spine or below"** (a thoracic cord lesion would spare the arms), and probability favors the **peripheral** compartment — **no sensory ⇒ muscle/NMJ; sensory present ⇒ cord or nerve**. They agree on the history targets: **tempo, proximal vs distal, anything above the neck** (chewing/swallowing/eye movements), **sensory involvement**, and **bowel/bladder**.

The **history** is deliberately overwhelming: subacute weakness with an **[[Around-the-Clock Pattern|around-the-clock]]** spread (leg → leg → arms), tingling, dysphagia, urinary retention (Foley in the ED), and then **four months of relentless abdominal pain, 40-lb weight loss, and new seizures**. Berkowitz openly models **[[Diagnostic Humility|not knowing]]** ("I have no idea what's going on here") and reaches for tools. On localization, he notes the around-the-clock spread is rare — seen in **[[ALS|ALS]]** (but this is too fast and has urinary/sensory features) and in the **[[Cervicomedullary Junction|cervicomedullary junction]]** (where arm and leg corticospinal fibers decussate at slightly different points) — before returning to basics: the tingling and urinary retention keep **cord and nerve** in play and push out muscle/NMJ. On synthesis, he applies **[[Unifying Diagnosis|parsimony for a young patient]]** (multiple contemporaneous problems are probably *one* disease, unlike the acute-on-chronic mix of the elderly), and twice uses the **[[Round to the Nearest Schema|"distill to a step-1 stem"]]** move — "**acute neuropathy + abdominal pain**, does that ring a bell?" naming **[[Acute Intermittent Porphyria|acute intermittent porphyria]]** early, then setting it aside as seemingly too subacute — and the **[[Framing Effect|context-swap]]** ("if this were a 78-year-old we'd think cancer + [[Paraneoplastic Neurologic Syndrome|paraneoplastic]]"). Even a simplified "young woman with multifocal lesions → **[[Multiple Sclerosis|MS]]**/**[[NMO|NMO]]**" is entertained and discarded (too fulminant for MS, too slow over two months for an NMO relapse).

The **exam** finally gives a localization. Vitals show **persistent tachycardia** (116–130); the abdomen is diffusely tender. The neuro exam is normal in mental status and cranial nerves (a mild subtle hypophonia/tremulous voice) but reveals **moderate symmetric wasting, hypotonia, and a proximal > distal weakness** (deltoids/hips 1/5 → wrists/ankles 3/5) that **does not fatigue**, **impaired proprioception and pinprick discrimination** in all limbs, and **areflexia throughout** with mute plantar responses. Aye localizes to the **peripheral nerve**: sensory *and* motor (so not muscle/NMJ), **areflexic** (so out of the cord), no upper-motor-neuron signs. Berkowitz presses the teaching point that **proximal-predominance would ordinarily suggest [[Myopathy|myopathy]]**, but the sensory loss and areflexia exclude muscle — so this is a **polyneuropathy**, to be classified before building a differential.

He then delivers the episode's neuromuscular core — **[[Axonal vs Demyelinating Neuropathy|length-dependent vs non-length-dependent]]**. **Axonal** neuropathies are **length-dependent** (the longest lumbar-to-toe axons fail first — the "traffic-jam on the longest commute"; toxic/metabolic, e.g. diabetes and B12), starting in the feet and only reaching the hands when the deficit climbs to mid-shin. **Demyelinating** neuropathies are **non-length-dependent** ("throw a can of **paint** at the chalk outline" — short and long fibers together, reflexes lost everywhere), though still often distal-predominant because long fibers carry the most myelin. Because this patient is **proximal *and* distal with global areflexia**, Aye reads it as **non-length-dependent / demyelinating**, and — with the tachycardia — Berkowitz packages a **subacute sensory-motor-autonomic demyelinating polyneuropathy**, differential **[[CIDP|CIDP]]** (though the dysautonomia is atypical), **[[Nodopathy|nodo-/paranodopathies]]** (neurofascin, contactin), **[[Paraproteinemic Neuropathy|paraprotein]]**/**[[Amyloidosis|amyloid]]**, **[[Paraneoplastic Neurologic Syndrome|paraneoplastic]]**, immune-mediated (occult rheumatologic), and **toxic/metabolic** causes.

The **workup** is exhaustive and negative: normal CBC/chemistries (a mildly transaminitic ALT 301/AST 137), **B12 >4000** (supplements), normal folate/thiamine/B6/vitamin E/MMA, **negative heavy metals** (the **[[Arsenic Poisoning|arsenic]]**-plus-abdominal-pain and **[[Nitrous Oxide|nitrous]]** "dental assistant" threads), normal CK/TSH, negative HIV/RPR/hepatitis, negative SPEP/immunofixation, ganglioside/anti-MAG/neurofascin, ANA/ANCA, and **normal brain and whole-spine MRI**. Berkowitz invokes his refrain that **neurology has only four tools — MRI, CSF, EEG, EMG** — and predicts the study "may just say demyelinating." Instead, the **[[EMG|nerve conduction study]] overturns the phenotype**: **reduced sensory and motor amplitudes with preserved velocities, no [[Conduction Block|conduction block]], and fibrillations/positive sharp waves** — an **axonal**, not demyelinating, neuropathy. He draws the meta-lesson: the EMG's power is exactly this — a case that *sounds* non-length-dependent demyelinating can be **axonal**, and the label must change (now a **subacute sensory-motor-autonomic axonal polyneuropathy**).

Re-anchored on **axonal + abdominal pain**, Berkowitz circles back to the step-1 stem — **could this be porphyria after all?** — and floats **[[Vasculitic Neuropathy|vasculitic neuropathy]]** with mesenteric ischemia as an alternative unifier. Sebastian reveals the answer: a **urine [[Porphobilinogen|porphobilinogen]] of 12.4 (markedly elevated)** with elevated **[[Aminolevulinic Acid|ALA]]**, and a confirmatory **[[HMBS Gene|HMBS]]** (PBG-deaminase) mutation — **[[Acute Intermittent Porphyria|acute intermittent porphyria]]** complicated by an **axonal sensory-motor polyneuropathy**. He teaches it: autosomal-dominant **[[Heme Biosynthesis|heme-synthesis]]** defect, buildup of neurotoxic **ALA** (a GABA-receptor interactor) and PBG, and a disease that "can do anything" across the neuraxis. The **[[Porphyria|five P's]]** all fit or nearly fit — **P**olyneuropathy, abdominal **P**ain out of proportion to exam, **P**sychiatric (subtle here), **P**ort-wine urine that **darkened on standing** (she had noticed maroon urine), and **P**recipitants (drugs/alcohol) — and the two "rule-breaking" features are the ones that fooled everyone: the neuropathy is **axonal yet non-length-dependent and proximal/motor-predominant**, and the course was **subacute**, not classically acute. She responded to **[[Hemin|hemin]]** infusions (downregulating ALA synthase) and **[[Givosiran|givosiran]]** (an **[[ALAS1|ALAS1]]** siRNA), completed rehab, and returned to work. Berkowitz closes on the reasoning arc: the answer was hiding in the **step-1 stem** the whole time, and rare diseases seen only once are recognized by holding an **[[Illness Script|illness script]]** — "in case I ever see it, it looks like this."

- **[[Diagnostic Humility|Diagnostic humility]] as method.** Berkowitz repeatedly says "I don't know," and *uses* the not-knowing — narrating the tools he reaches for rather than forcing a premature answer.
- **[[Unifying Diagnosis|Parsimony in the young]].** Contemporaneous weakness + abdominal pain + seizures + weight loss in a 29-year-old is probably **one** disease — the lump that ultimately pays off with porphyria.
- **[[Round to the Nearest Schema|Distill to a step-1 stem]].** Stripping detail to "**acute neuropathy + abdominal pain**" surfaces **porphyria**; the **[[Framing Effect|context-swap]]** ("if 78 → cancer + paraneoplastic") stress-tests hypotheses.
- **[[Axonal vs Demyelinating Neuropathy|Length-dependent vs non-length-dependent]].** The traffic-jam (axonal, feet-first) vs paint-splatter (demyelinating, proximal+distal) images — and the trap that this looked demyelinating but was axonal.
- **[[Test Interpretation|The EMG can overturn the clinical impression]].** Amplitude = axons, velocity = myelin, **[[Conduction Block|conduction block]] = acquired demyelination**; the study reclassified the neuropathy and redirected the differential.
- **[[Illness Script|Illness scripts]] for rare disease.** **[[Acute Intermittent Porphyria|AIP]]** (five P's; axonal, non-length-dependent, motor-predominant, rule-breaking), **[[CIDP|CIDP]]**/**[[Nodopathy|nodopathies]]**, **[[Paraproteinemic Neuropathy|paraprotein]]**/**[[Amyloidosis|amyloid]]**, **[[Vasculitic Neuropathy|vasculitic]]** (mesenteric ischemia unifier), **[[Paraneoplastic Neurologic Syndrome|paraneoplastic]]**, and the toxic mimics (**[[Arsenic Poisoning|arsenic]]**, **[[Nitrous Oxide|nitrous]]**).

## Teaching Discussion

- Distinguish systemic asthenia (weakness with preserved power from anemia, thyroid disease, cancer, or infection) from true neurologic weakness before localizing, then run the motor pathway from brain to muscle and use sensory involvement, reflexes, and bowel/bladder to narrow it.
- Localize true four-limb weakness in an awake patient to the cervical cord or below, remembering that no sensory involvement points to muscle or neuromuscular junction, sensory involvement points to cord or nerve, and global areflexia moves you out of the cord into the peripheral nerve or root.
- Lump aggressively in young patients (multiple simultaneous problems usually share one cause) and, when stumped, distill the case to a step-1 stem — here "acute neuropathy plus abdominal pain" points straight at porphyria — and swap the patient's age or context to test which hypotheses survive.
- Classify every neuropathy as length-dependent (axonal, feet-first, toxic/metabolic) or non-length-dependent (demyelinating, proximal and distal with reflexes lost everywhere), but treat the clinical impression as provisional, because nerve conduction studies can reclassify a convincingly demyelinating picture as axonal.
- Read the EMG as the arbiter of neuropathy type: reduced amplitudes with preserved velocities and fibrillations indicate axonal loss, slowed velocities indicate demyelination, and conduction block indicates acquired demyelination — and let the study, not the bedside impression, direct the etiologic workup.
- Recognize acute intermittent porphyria as a great neurologic mimic that breaks the rules — an axonal but often non-length-dependent, proximal- and motor-predominant polyneuropathy with autonomic features, subacute course, abdominal pain out of proportion to exam, seizures, and port-wine urine on standing — and confirm it with urinary PBG and ALA plus HMBS genetics, treating with hemin and givosiran while avoiding porphyrinogenic drugs.
