> A **format-bending, management-and-communication** episode: Rabih plays the **ED physician signing out two patients** to Reza as the **admitting medicine resident**, in real time, and deliberately leaves one case as a **cliffhanger for RLR 055**. It opens with a real-world **spaced-repetition** win — Reza recounts applying **RLR 049** at the bedside (a hyperacute pancreatitis, **lipase >5,000**, that "looked alcoholic" but on rising transaminases and MRCP proved **gallstone** → same-admission cholecystectomy, anticipatory insulin, early fluids). **Case 1 (resolved):** an **older man (60s–70s), out of care**, with **~3 weeks of progressive confusion, imbalance, and gait failure** — the combination of **encephalopathy plus ataxia** flags **primary CNS disease**, not a benign gait disorder. **CT/MRI: multiple hemorrhagic, rim-enhancing masses at the gray-white junction** (largest 3–4 cm, mass effect, **no diffusion restriction**), radiology favoring **cancer**. The team runs the **brain-mass differential** (neoplasm — solid *and* lymphoma — infection, demyelinating, subacute infarct), notes that **hemorrhage + vascular distribution** prioritizes **metastasis** via the **[[MRCT mnemonic|MRCT]]** bleeders (melanoma, renal, choriocarcinoma, thyroid) while remembering **base rate favors lung**. A **skin exam is negative**; a **rescan** (an outside CT was invisible in the record system) reveals a **new 2 cm lung mass, thoracic adenopathy, and a 6 cm liver mass** — with a **normal chest CT just one month earlier**. A **liver biopsy returns small cell lung cancer**; the **breakneck tempo** was the tell. Being **extensive-stage**, management is **palliative** — whole-brain radiation then systemic chemotherapy — and Reza models a textbook **[[breaking bad news|SPIKES-style]] conversation** ("I'm the water; I take the shape of the patient's glass"). Interleaved is **Case 2 (to be continued):** a woman in her 60s, **hours after bilateral TMJ reconstruction with the jaw wired shut**, who becomes **agitated, rigid, febrile (38.8), tachycardic, hypertensive, with CK 3,500 and leukocytosis** — a picture that reads as **[[neuroleptic malignant syndrome]]** vs **serotonin syndrome** (dantrolene on the table) — then suffers a **witnessed macro-aspiration and desaturation**, triggering a **rapid response** (sit upright, maximal O₂, low threshold to call a code and intubate for hypoxemic failure) and transfer to the **ICU**. Her diagnosis is held for "next time" — RLR 055 turned out to be a different three-case episode, but **[[RLR 056 - One Life, So Many Dreams — Post-Operative Rigidity and Propofol-Induced Neuroexcitatory Syndrome|RLR 056]] finally resolves this cliffhanger: propofol-induced neuroexcitatory syndrome**. Threaded throughout: **tempo dictates the urgency of diagnosis**, **don't block admissions / trust the sign-out**, and **management and communication are core clinical-reasoning skills**.

**Pearls**

- **Encephalopathy + ataxia = a CNS red flag.** Gait instability *alone* has benign explanations (weakness, sensory loss, cerebellar disease, NPH); gait instability **plus subacute progressive confusion** points to **primary CNS disease** (cerebellar or cortical involvement) and demands imaging.
- **The brain-mass differential:** **neoplasm** (solid metastasis/primary *and* liquid — **lymphoma**), **infection** (abscess, tuberculoma, fungal, toxoplasmosis), **demyelinating** (tumefactive), and **subacute infarct** (can look mass-like). **Hemorrhage in a vascular distribution** reprioritizes toward **metastasis**.
- **Hemorrhagic metastases — [[MRCT mnemonic|MRCT]], but base rate wins.** **M**elanoma, **R**enal, **C**horiocarcinoma, **T**hyroid have the highest *propensity* to bleed, yet **lung** — being so common — is still the **most likely** hemorrhagic met. A non-MRCT primary does **not** exclude a hemorrhagic solid cancer.
- **Diagnose the sensitive site indirectly.** When lesions sit in the **brain**, don't biopsy the brain first — look **systemically**: a **PET-CT** and a **head-to-toe skin exam** (for melanoma) are the cheap, high-yield first moves ("examine every square inch").
- **Tempo dictates the urgency of the diagnosis.** Three weeks of confusion is a **subacute** problem; a post-op patient who is agitated, febrile, and rigid within **two hours** is an **emergency**. Triage by *tempo*, not by which chart you opened first.
- **A cancer that appears in a month is small cell until proven otherwise.** A **normal chest CT one month prior** that becomes a **2 cm lung mass with nodal, hepatic, and brain spread** is defined by its **velocity** — classic **[[small cell lung cancer]]** (aggressive, but chemo/radiation-sensitive). **Extensive-stage → palliative intent.**
- **Post-operative rigidity + hyperthermia + autonomic instability + CK elevation** = **[[neuroleptic malignant syndrome]]** (anesthesia can unmask home antipsychotic metabolism) vs **serotonin syndrome**; **malignant hyperthermia** usually strikes **during** anesthesia. Recognize the triad, review the med list, and know **dantrolene**.
- **Aspiration rapid response, in order:** sit the patient **upright**, give **maximal O₂** (open the non-rebreather fully — don't titrate from 2 L), **continuous pulse-ox and telemetry**, and a **low threshold to call a code** and **intubate for hypoxemic respiratory failure**. Intubating for **altered mental status alone** has weak supporting evidence.
- **Systems pearls:** **trust the person signing out** (someone saw the patient and is worried — so should you) and **do not block admissions** at time zero; you can always discharge tomorrow. And **management + communication are clinical-reasoning skills** — breaking bad news well (**SPIKES**: assess understanding, ask permission, give a warning shot, respond to emotion, offer partnership) is as teachable as a differential.

**Problem Representation**
An older man with three weeks of progressive encephalopathy and gait failure, found to have multiple hemorrhagic gray-white-junction brain masses and — on rescan — a new lung mass, adenopathy, and liver mass that developed within a month, diagnosed as extensive-stage small cell lung cancer with brain metastases (managed palliatively) — presented alongside an unresolved post-operative hyperthermic-rigidity case held for the next episode.

## Case Narrative

The episode is a **role-play**: Rabih is the **ED physician**, Reza the **admitting medicine resident**, and the teaching is as much about **triage, management, and communication** as diagnosis. It opens with Reza's **spaced-repetition** story — a direct payoff of **RLR 049**. He admitted a man with **sudden epigastric pain and a lipase north of 5,000**; the reflex (alcohol history, transaminitis) was "alcoholic pancreatitis," but the **hyperacute tempo** ("gallstone/obstructive until proven otherwise") plus **rising ALT/AST** pushed him to an **MRCP**, which found a **stone in the cystic duct** poised to obstruct. Applying the episode's lessons, he arranged a **same-admission cholecystectomy** (mild edematous, non-necrotizing pancreatitis — otherwise ~30% recur), **anticipated endocrine insufficiency** (started basal insulin), and pushed **early aggressive fluids**. It is offered as proof that "management reasoning" is learnable and life-changing.

Before the cases, a **systems interlude**: as a VA **Medical Officer of the Day**, Reza's philosophy is to **trust the person signing out** — "someone has seen this patient and is worried, so I should be too" — and to **never block an admission** at time zero (you can discharge tomorrow; blocking is how patients get hurt). His intake ritual is lean: chief problem, what was done (fluids/antibiotics), vitals (for placement), then the labs.

**Case 1.** An **older man (60s–70s), out of care for a year**, is brought in by his son for **~3 weeks of increasing confusion, imbalance, and "not feeling right"** — leaning to one side, legs failing, thinking slowed. Reza immediately flags the **constellation**: this is **not** isolated gait instability (which could be weakness, sensory, cerebellar, or NPH) but **encephalopathy plus ataxia** — a **CNS red flag** for a **primary brain process** (cerebellar or cortical). The **CT** shows **multiple hemorrhagic masses in a vascular distribution**; radiology is concerned for **hemorrhagic metastases**. Reza teaches the discipline of **stepping back before the hemorrhage detail**: **what causes brain masses?** — **neoplasm** (solid; and **liquid**, i.e., **lymphoma**), **infection** (abscess, **tuberculoma**, fungal, **toxoplasmosis**), **demyelinating**, and **subacute infarct**. Here, **hemorrhage + vascular territory** prioritizes **metastasis**, and both hosts recall — from **Berkowitz's case the prior week (RLR 053)** — the **[[MRCT mnemonic|MRCT]]** bleeders (**melanoma, renal, choriocarcinoma, thyroid**). The **MRI** confirms **hemorrhagic rim-enhancing gray-white-junction lesions** (largest 3–4 cm) with **mass effect** and **no diffusion restriction**, "strongly favoring cancer."

Reza turns to **management of raised ICP**: bed **upright**, **osmotic therapy** (mannitol), **steroids** (dexamethasone for peritumoral edema), and — if mass effect is severe — an **external ventricular drain**, all in concert with **neurosurgery**, the goal being to prevent **herniation**. Neurosurgery starts **dexamethasone** (pausing only over the classic worry that steroids can **reduce lymphoma yield** — but, as Reza notes, if a patient is herniating you treat anyway, like giving epinephrine for suspected anaphylaxis). Then the search for the **primary**: because the **brain is a sensitive biopsy site**, ask **"can I diagnose without biopsying the brain?"** → a **PET-CT** and a **meticulous skin exam** (for melanoma). The **skin exam is negative** ("every square inch"). A wrinkle: an **outside CT** existed but was **invisible in the record-sharing system**, so they **rescan** — revealing a **new 2 cm lung mass, thoracic lymphadenopathy, and a 6 cm liver mass**, against a **completely normal chest CT one month earlier**. Reza reasons that the **brain is the end of the journey**, not the origin; between lung and liver, **lung → brain** is far more likely the primary, and the **explosive one-month tempo** and pattern (adenopathy, brain and liver spread) point to **small cell**. A **liver biopsy confirms small cell lung cancer of pulmonary origin**; because it is **extensive-stage**, intent is **palliative** — **whole-brain radiation** (mapped that Monday) followed by **systemic chemotherapy**.

Reza then models the **communication** the episode is really about — a **SPIKES-style** conversation once dexamethasone has cleared the patient's sensorium enough for capacity. He **assesses understanding** ("do you have a sense of what's going on?"), **asks permission** ("would you like me to explain what I know?"), delivers a **warning shot**, names the finding plainly (masses in the brain concerning for cancer; a lung mass as the likely origin; tissue needed to be definitive), **responds to emotion** ("Does this mean I'm going to die?" — validated, not deflected), **gently declines to assign blame** when the patient laments his smoking, and closes with **partnership and autonomy** ("you're not alone; the choice will always be yours"). His framing metaphor: **"I'm the water; I take the shape of the patient's glass"** — some want every detail, some defer to family, some want nothing.

**Case 2 (to be continued).** Interleaved by acuity is a woman in her **60s**, **two hours after bilateral TMJ reconstruction with her jaw wired shut**, who — after an uncomplicated **propofol/ketamine** anesthetic and extubation — becomes **agitated, writhing, and stiff**, with **T 38.8, HR 120 (sinus), BP 190s/110s, CK 3,500, WBC 14**, unresponsive to lorazepam and haloperidol. Reza reads the **triad of rigidity + hyperthermia + CK elevation with autonomic hyperactivity** as **[[neuroleptic malignant syndrome]]** vs **serotonin syndrome** (anesthesia can interfere with a home antipsychotic's metabolism and precipitate NMS; **malignant hyperthermia** usually erupts **intra-operatively**), reviews the med list, and readies **dantrolene**. She then has a **witnessed macro-aspiration with desaturation**, prompting a **rapid response** that Reza runs by protocol — **upright, maximal O₂ via non-rebreather, continuous pulse-ox/telemetry, low threshold to call a code blue and intubate** for hypoxemic respiratory failure (noting the **weak evidence for intubating AMS alone**). She stabilizes on **high-flow (20 L)** with **bilateral interstitial infiltrates** (aspiration pneumonitis) and goes to the **ICU** — her diagnosis and course **deliberately withheld for RLR 055**. The **wire-shut jaw** looms as an unspoken airway hazard. Rabih closes by reflecting on how RLR has shifted him toward the **"day-to-day realities of medicine"** — management and communication — over rare diagnoses.

### Illness Scripts and Bias

This episode reframes **clinical reasoning as a three-legged stool — diagnosis, management, and communication** — and spends most of its energy on the latter two. The **diagnostic** thread is a deliberate **spaced-repetition** of RLR 053: the same *brain-mass → hemorrhagic-met → MRCT vs base-rate → hunt-the-primary* engine, but here the twist is **kinetic** — a cancer that **materialized in a month** is diagnostic of **small cell** by **velocity alone**, and the "invisible outside CT" is a real-world systems trap that nearly hid the tempo. The **management** thread teaches **tempo-based triage** (a two-hour post-op crisis outranks a three-week subacute one) and **ICP escalation logic** (upright → osmotic → steroid → EVD, neurosurgery-guided), plus the **steroid-vs-lymphoma-yield** tension resolved by **acuity** (treat herniation now). The **communication** thread is the heart of the episode: a **[[breaking bad news|SPIKES]]** exemplar in which Reza **calibrates disclosure to the patient's readiness** ("I take the shape of the glass"), **normalizes fear**, **refuses to moralize about smoking**, and anchors on **partnership and autonomy**. The **systems** teaching — **trust the sign-out, don't block admissions** — guards against the **ED-vs-medicine friction** that harms patients. **Case 2** is a masterclass in **recognizing a hyperacute post-anesthetic emergency** (NMS/serotonin syndrome) and executing a **rapid response**, and its **cliffhanger** structure is itself a teaching device — modeling the reality that inpatient stories resolve across shifts and settings.

## Teaching Discussion

**Gait plus confusion localizes to the brain.** Isolated gait instability has a broad, often benign differential (deconditioning, neuropathy/myelopathy, cerebellar disease, normal-pressure hydrocephalus). But **gait failure with subacute, progressive encephalopathy** is a **structural CNS red flag** — think cerebellar or cortical involvement — and mandates imaging (**CT first**, but proceed to **MRI even if CT is normal**, since small lesions hide on CT).

**A structured brain-mass differential.** **Neoplasm** — metastatic or primary **solid** tumors, and **liquid** tumors (**CNS/systemic lymphoma**). **Infection** — pyogenic **abscess** (restricts on DWI), **tuberculoma**, **fungal**, **toxoplasmosis** (HIV). **Inflammatory/demyelinating** — tumefactive MS, neurosarcoid. **Vascular** — a **subacute infarct** that enhances and mimics a mass. The radiologist's descriptors reprioritize the list: **DWI restriction** (abscess), **ring completeness**, **gray-white location** (metastasis/emboli), and **hemorrhage**.

**Hemorrhagic metastases and MRCT.** Highest bleeding *propensity*: **[[MRCT mnemonic|MRCT]]** — **melanoma, renal cell, choriocarcinoma, thyroid** — but the **most common** hemorrhagic met is **lung**, because lung metastasizes so frequently; even "low-propensity" tumors bleed a meaningful fraction of the time. So **absence of an MRCT primary does not exclude a solid cancer**, and the workup should hunt broadly.

**Diagnosing a sensitive site indirectly.** When lesions are in the **brain** (a hazardous biopsy target), first ask **"can I make the diagnosis elsewhere?"** A **PET-CT** localizes a systemic primary, and a **complete skin exam** can reveal an occult **melanoma** for free. Only when non-invasive/peripheral avenues fail does one accept the risk of a **brain biopsy**.

**Small cell lung cancer.** A **neuroendocrine** carcinoma, almost always in **smokers**, defined by **rapid growth and early widespread metastasis** (bone, liver, brain, marrow) and by **paraneoplastic** syndromes (SIADH, Lambert-Eaton, ectopic ACTH). Staged simply as **limited** (one hemithorax, encompassable in a radiation field) vs **extensive**. It is initially **very chemo- and radiosensitive** (platinum-etoposide ± immunotherapy) but relapses; **extensive-stage disease is treated with palliative intent**. **Prophylactic/therapeutic cranial irradiation** has a role given the brain's affinity for SCLC. The **tell** in this case was **kinetic** — normal imaging a month earlier.

**Raised ICP from brain metastases.** Escalate stepwise: **head-of-bed up**, **osmotic therapy** (mannitol, hypertonic saline), **corticosteroids** (dexamethasone reduces **vasogenic edema** around tumors) — and, for obstructive hydrocephalus or refractory mass effect, **CSF diversion (EVD)** — all with **neurosurgery**. The feared endpoint is **herniation**. The **steroid-before-biopsy** caveat (lymized yield in lymphoma) yields to **acuity**: a herniating patient is treated first.

**Neuroleptic malignant syndrome vs serotonin syndrome vs malignant hyperthermia.** All can present with **hyperthermia, rigidity, autonomic instability, and CK elevation**. **NMS** (dopamine-blocking agents; anesthesia can unmask a home antipsychotic) evolves over **days** with **"lead-pipe" rigidity** and **hyporeflexia**; **serotonin syndrome** (serotonergic agents) is faster, with **clonus, hyperreflexia, and myoclonus**; **malignant hyperthermia** erupts **intra-operatively** with volatile anesthetics/succinylcholine. Management is **stop the offender, aggressive cooling and supportive care**, benzodiazepines, and — for severe rigidity/hyperthermia — **dantrolene** (and **bromocriptine** for NMS; **cyproheptadine** for serotonin syndrome).

**The aspiration rapid response.** For a witnessed aspiration with desaturation: **sit the patient upright**, deliver **maximal FiO₂** immediately (**non-rebreather wide open**, not a slow titration), ensure **continuous pulse-ox and telemetry**, and hold a **low threshold to call a code and intubate for hypoxemic respiratory failure**. Distinguish **aspiration pneumonitis** (chemical injury from gastric acid — often self-limited, antibiotics not automatically indicated) from later **aspiration pneumonia**. Note the weak evidence for intubating **altered mental status alone** in a patient who is oxygenating and protecting reasonably.

**Communication and systems as clinical skills.** **Breaking bad news** follows a learnable structure — **[[breaking bad news|SPIKES]]**: **S**etting, **P**erception (what does the patient understand?), **I**nvitation (how much do they want to know?), **K**nowledge (a warning shot, then plain language), **E**mpathy (name and validate emotion), **S**trategy/Summary (next steps, partnership, autonomy). And the **ED–medicine handoff** works best on **trust**: assume the referring clinician's concern is warranted, **don't block admissions** at intake, and gather only what you need (problem, interventions, vitals, labs).
