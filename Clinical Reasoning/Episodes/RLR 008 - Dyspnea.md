## Summary

> [!summary] Scan Summary
> A 50-year-old woman with long-standing diffuse [[Systemic Sclerosis|systemic sclerosis]] and previously well-controlled [[Hypertension|hypertension]] presents with ten days of dyspnea on exertion, lower-extremity edema, and falling urine output, now with a blood pressure of 180/96 against a baseline in the 130s. The host reflex is to run toward [[Scleroderma Renal Crisis|scleroderma renal crisis]] as the can't-miss diagnosis of her background disease, the same way one runs toward the can't-miss causes of chest pain — a sound reasoning move even though SRC ultimately proves *not* to be the answer here. But Rabih narrates the landmines that accumulate against it: SRC almost always strikes within the first five years (she is ten years in), it is a *vascular*-compartment lesion that should show minimal proteinuria, and this patient has nephrotic-range proteinuria (a 24-hour collection of ~5 g). Empiric captopril fails to move her pressure, urine output, or dyspnea, breaking the anchor. A mild leukopenia and low-normal platelets — cytopenias that scleroderma leaves alone but lupus does not — reframe the case toward an autoimmune "cousin." An ANA of 1:1280 and a kidney biopsy land the answer: stage IV [[Lupus Nephritis|lupus nephritis]], treated with [[Cyclophosphamide|cyclophosphamide]].

## Pearls

- **In diffuse [[Systemic Sclerosis|systemic sclerosis]] with new hypertension and volume overload, run toward [[Scleroderma Renal Crisis|scleroderma renal crisis]] the way you run toward the can't-miss causes of chest pain — then look for landmines.** SRC is morbid and treatable, so a single inconsistency should not talk you out of it; but you pursue it "with one eye" on the features that would say you are being misled.
- **The landmines against SRC here were the time course, the urinalysis, and the treatment response.** SRC almost invariably occurs within the first five years of diagnosis (she is ~10 years in); it is a lesion of the *vascular* renal compartment and so should show minimal-to-no proteinuria, yet she has ~5 g/24h; and failure to respond to titrated [[Captopril|captopril]] is a third strike. Any one alone is soft; together they demand an alternative.
- **An [[ACE Inhibitor|ACE inhibitor]] treats scleroderma renal crisis but does not prevent it — keep it in your back pocket.** Being on [[Losartan|losartan]] (or any ARB) does not prevent SRC, and in a patient at risk you deliberately *avoid* prophylactic ACE inhibition so the drug is available as treatment when crisis strikes. ACE inhibitors transformed SRC from a disease that killed most patients within a year into one where many even come off dialysis.
- **The most common cause of nephrotic-*range* proteinuria is diabetes; the most common cause of overt nephrotic *syndrome* is not.** Diabetes rarely produces the full syndrome, so the label "nephrotic syndrome" ([[Proteinuria|proteinuria]] plus [[Hypoalbuminemia|hypoalbuminemia]]) is a useful gate that reorders the differential toward its systemic causes.
- **Cytopenias are the tell that separates lupus from its autoimmune cousins.** [[Rheumatoid Arthritis|Rheumatoid arthritis]], scleroderma, [[Sjögren Syndrome|Sjögren]], and IgG4 disease typically leave the CBC untouched; [[Systemic Lupus Erythematosus|lupus]] produces leukopenia and thrombocytopenia — so a mild leukopenia in a proteinuric patient is worth going back to the chart to confirm.
- **[[BNP]] and [[Troponin|troponin]] can rise from the kidney, not the heart.** Nephrogenic cardiac stretch and downtrending troponin make intrinsic cardiac disease hard to claim; the symmetry-breaker between [[Congestive Nephropathy|congestive nephropathy]] and a primary renal lesion is the response to diuresis and, here, the markedly abnormal urinalysis pointing intrinsic-kidney.

## Problem Representation

A 50-year-old woman with diffuse systemic sclerosis (ILD, digital vasculopathy, contractures) on immunosuppression, plus chronic hypertension and hypothyroidism, presents with ten days of exertional dyspnea, orthopnea, lower-extremity edema, and decreasing urine output; she is hypertensive to 180/96 from a 130s baseline, volume-overloaded (JVP 11 cm, bibasilar crackles, pitting edema), with a bump in creatinine (1.0 → 1.5), nephrotic-range proteinuria (3+ on UA, ~5 g/24h), mild leukopenia and low-normal platelets, and a modestly elevated BNP with downtrending troponin — a picture initially framed as presumptive scleroderma renal crisis but ultimately proven to be stage IV lupus nephritis.

## Case Narrative

Reza presents a modified morning-report case: a 50-year-old woman arriving with ten days of dyspnea on exertion, lower-extremity edema, and decreased urine output. She has orthopnea but no PND, and — notably — no weight gain or loss, no fevers, night sweats, or chills. Her background is dominated by diffuse [[Systemic Sclerosis|systemic sclerosis]] diagnosed roughly a decade earlier by diffuse skin thickening extending proximally, complicated by Raynaud phenomenon, interstitial lung disease, digital contractures, and remnants of distal vasculopathy with prior ulceration and ischemia. She is on several immunosuppressants (including MMF) for her ILD, plus [[Losartan|losartan]] and amlodipine for hypertension, gabapentin, and levothyroxine for hypothyroidism. On exam her blood pressure is 180/96 against a usual 130s, heart rate 90; JVP is 11 cm at 45 degrees, there is pitting lower-extremity edema, diffuse sclerodermatous skin changes extending proximally, and fine bibasilar crackles.

Rabih's first move is not to reach for a diagnosis but to organize the flood of data. He splits the case into a **[[Background–Foreground Framing|background]]** (the conditions she already carries) and a **foreground** (the summary of what is happening now), keeping every data point but rendering the less central ones in "smaller font" rather than erasing them. He identifies the center of the foreground by asking which symptoms are both diagnostically productive and morbid — and any thoracic symptom, dyspnea included, satisfies both, so exertional dyspnea takes center stage. Its modifiers (orthopnea, edema, non-inflammatory course, absent weight change) push him toward [[Heart Failure|heart failure]] and, more broadly, a [[Volume Overload|volume overload]] schema whose usual company is the heart, the liver, and the kidney. He notes the tensions: orthopnea fits heart failure but also ascites splinting the diaphragm; the *absence* of weight gain is atypical for heart failure but fits the muscle wasting of kidney and liver disease; and hypertensive cardiomyopathy usually presents with *normalized* pressures, so hypertension in the setting of volume overload should refocus attention on the kidney.

Then he lets the background override the foreground. Even though he can build a cardiac case — systemic sclerosis overlaps with [[Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension|pulmonary arterial hypertension]], [[Restrictive Cardiomyopathy|restrictive cardiomyopathy]], [[Constrictive Pericarditis|constrictive pericarditis]], and ILD-driven right heart failure — none of those is as emergent as [[Scleroderma Renal Crisis|scleroderma renal crisis]], the vasculopathy that chokes off the kidney and presents exactly as diffuse-scleroderma volume overload with a rising blood pressure. He commits to running toward it the way one runs toward the can't-miss causes of chest pain, while keeping "one eye" on the single big inconsistency he already sees: SRC overwhelmingly strikes within the first five years, and she is a decade into a previously stable diagnosis.

Reza then loads more data. The CBC shows platelets 120 and hemoglobin 8.6, both at baseline, with a white count reported as normal; TSH is 14 with a normal free T4 (an elevation he pre-empts as poor levothyroxine absorption in gut edema rather than the cause of her edema). Creatinine has risen from 1.0 to 1.5 with normal potassium and bicarbonate; LFTs are normal; chest x-ray shows no new process; BNP is 1200; high-sensitivity troponin is 18 and downtrends to ~6. The urinalysis shows specific gravity 1015, trace blood, 3 RBCs, no white cells, and **3+ protein**.

Rabih rambles through the synthesis. The elevated [[BNP]] is a weak signal because nephrogenic cardiac stretch — pressure transmitted from the kidney — dilates chambers and raises BNP even in structurally fine hearts, and the downtrending [[Troponin|troponin]] adds little. This is the classic [[Congestive Nephropathy|congestive nephropathy]] versus primary-renal fork, whose most powerful clue is the response to diuresis; the symmetry-breaker is evidence of *intrinsic* disease, and the marked proteinuria points intrinsic-kidney. He asks for a serum albumin to walk the journey from proteinuria → nephrotic-range → nephrotic syndrome; Reza has a 24-hour collection of ~5 g but with the caveat that low urine creatinine (from the AKI) inflates the ratio. Either way, the markedly abnormal UA plus scleroderma's own prioritization of the kidney makes intrinsic renal disease the place to sit.

Now the proteinuria itself becomes the landmine. SRC lives in the *vascular* compartment and characteristically shows minimal-to-no proteinuria; this degree of protein signals a *glomerular* lesion, nudging the case toward a nephrotic phenotype (supported by the near-absent RBCs, though one UA is not definitive). Rabih raises two ways to be fooled: an occult diabetic glomerulopathy from prior prednisone exposure layered onto SRC, or an autoimmune "cousin" — lupus or Sjögren-driven cryoglobulinemia — traveling alongside the scleroderma. He recites the systemic causes of nephrotic syndrome (HIV, hepatitis B and C, lupus, IgG4 disease, sarcoidosis, Sjögren, amyloidosis) and flags the data already in hand: the low-ish platelets and the white count deserve a second look, because lupus, unlike scleroderma, tweaks the CBC. His practical plan: treat presumptively for SRC with an ACE inhibitor while pursuing nephrotic syndrome as an independent schema.

Reza pushes on three points. Hypertension is a poor explanation for *this much* proteinuria (hypertensive nephrosclerosis is typically minimally proteinuric, and the very existence of hypertension-caused CKD is debated); the concentrated urine could artifactually inflate protein but only in the "retrospectoscope," not going forward; and prednisone is feared as an SRC *precipitant* yet still used cautiously in scleroderma. Prompted by Rabih's reflection, Reza rechecks the count and corrects the record — she in fact has a **mild leukopenia**.

The case resolves in increments. Presumptive SRC is diagnosed, rheumatology is consulted, and titrated [[Captopril|captopril]] is started — but after several days her urine output, blood pressure, and dyspnea are all unchanged. The team puts on the brakes and consults nephrology. An ANA returns at **1:1280** with negative hepatitis serologies; a kidney biopsy is performed. Rabih, now "not guilty" on scleroderma renal crisis given the failed captopril, the proteinuria, and the time course, argues the leukopenia is the scary clue and that lupus nephritis needs a biopsy for both diagnosis and staging. The biopsy shows **stage IV [[Lupus Nephritis|lupus nephritis]]**, and she is started on [[Cyclophosphamide|cyclophosphamide]].

### Illness Scripts and Bias

- **Anchoring on the can't-miss diagnosis — deployed correctly, then disciplined.** The hosts anchor on [[Scleroderma Renal Crisis|scleroderma renal crisis]] by design, importing the chest-pain habit of leading with the morbid, treatable diagnosis of the patient's background disease. The skill on display is not avoiding the anchor but *holding it lightly* — pursuing it "with one eye" on disconfirming features, so that accumulating landmines (time course, proteinuria, treatment failure) can dislodge it before it becomes [[Premature Closure|premature closure]]. Morning report itself anchored: most of the original discussion was spent on SRC.
- **[[Framing Effect|Framing]] by font size and by background vs foreground.** Rabih's [[Background–Foreground Framing|large-font/small-font]] technique is a deliberate anti-flattening move: refusing to give every data point equal attention while refusing to erase any, so the center (exertional dyspnea → volume overload → kidney) organizes the rest. The background (systemic sclerosis) is what reframes an ambiguous volume-overload foreground into a kidney-first problem.
- **Base-rate reasoning as the primary disconfirmer.** The "<5 years from diagnosis" rule for SRC functions like "[[Giant Cell Arteritis|GCA]] above 50, [[Takayasu Arteritis|Takayasu]] below 40" — an epidemiologic prior strong enough to demote a diagnosis the clinical picture otherwise fits. Reza frames the lesson explicitly: this fact is written in the "same font" as everything else in a textbook, and only a reader trained in *how to learn medicine* flags it as load-bearing.
- **Compartment thinking as an illness-script filter.** Localizing intrinsic renal disease to glomerular / tubular / interstitial / *vascular* lets the marked proteinuria act as a discriminator: SRC is a vascular lesion (minimal proteinuria), so nephrotic-range protein points elsewhere. The [[Urinalysis Schema|UA schema]] converts a single lab line into a compartment localization.
- **"[[Diseases Travel Together|Diseases travel together]]."** The move from scleroderma to its autoimmune cousins (lupus, Sjögren, cryoglobulinemia) is an illness-script-linking heuristic — and the cytopenias are the concrete clue that operationalizes it, since scleroderma, RA, Sjögren, and IgG4 spare the CBC while lupus does not.
- **The [[Cognitive Autopsy|cognitive autopsy]].** After the lupus nephritis diagnosis, the team returns to the whiteboard to re-examine every clue that had been rationalized away — the losartan (does not prevent SRC), the 3+ protein (over-soothed by the concentrated urine and background hypertension), the five-year rule (missed because it read like every other sentence), and the leukopenia (obscured by MMF). The retrospective review is the reasoning artifact of the episode.

## Teaching Discussion

- The organizing technique is Rabih's background/foreground split with variable "font size" — hold every data point but weight them, and find the center through diagnostically productive *and* morbid features. Thoracic symptoms almost always seize center stage on both counts.
- Scleroderma vocabulary matters: *scleroderma* is skin sclerosis (which can be localized, e.g., morphea); *systemic sclerosis* is the systemic disease, subdivided into limited and diffuse by how far skin involvement extends proximally, each with distinct antibodies and visceral signatures. Diffuse disease carries the heavier visceral burden — ILD, PAH, restrictive cardiomyopathy, constrictive pericarditis, and renal crisis.
- Scleroderma renal crisis is a **vascular**-compartment disease: new/accelerated hypertension with rapidly rising creatinine and oliguria, typically within five years of diagnosis, and characteristically **minimal proteinuria**. Marked proteinuria should make you question the diagnosis; failure to respond to an ACE inhibitor should make you abandon it.
- The [[ACE Inhibitor|ACE inhibitor]] point is high-yield and counterintuitive: it is *treatment*, not *prophylaxis*. Losartan does not prevent SRC, ACE inhibitors do not prevent it, and giving one prophylactically to an at-risk patient may blunt your ability to recognize and treat crisis — so it is reserved. Historically SRC killed most patients within a year; ACE inhibitors dramatically improved survival, and transplant is deferred in the first six months because many dialysis-dependent patients recover renal function.
- The nephrotic distinction is worth carrying: diabetes is the most common cause of nephrotic-*range* proteinuria but rarely causes the full *syndrome*, so requiring hypoalbuminemia (true nephrotic syndrome) reorders the differential toward systemic causes (HIV, hepatitis B/C, lupus, IgG4, sarcoidosis, Sjögren, amyloidosis).
- Cytopenias discriminate lupus from its cousins: leukopenia and thrombocytopenia point to lupus, whereas scleroderma, RA, Sjögren, and IgG4 usually leave the count alone — though concurrent MMF (and other marrow-suppressing immunosuppressants) can muddy the read, as it did here.
- Interpreting labs against the host: an elevated BNP and even a mild troponin bump can be nephrogenic; the concentrated urine can artifactually inflate proteinuria; and a positive ANA is expected in systemic sclerosis, so it is weak confirmation on its own. Each value has to be read against this specific patient, not its reference range.
