## Pearls

- **One word can rewrite the whole differential: [[Illness Tempo|recurrent]].** Three days of nausea/vomiting/diarrhea reads as gastroenteritis; the *same* illness five or six times since age 13 excludes acute foodborne/viral infection and forces the inflammatory framework (infection, cancer, autoimmune, drug, endocrinopathy). Duration and periodicity do enormous diagnostic work before any other data.
- **Build the [[Center of Gravity|center of gravity]] as symptoms accumulate.** "Nausea" localizes nowhere; adding vomiting leans GI; adding diarrhea runs to the GI tract — while keeping a systemic mimic (e.g., thyrotoxicosis, whose center of gravity is the blood) in the back pocket.
- **Work colitis through the four I's: [[Colitis|Infection, IBD, Infiltrative, Ischemia]].** Recurrent, waxing-waning colitis with ileal involvement and rectal sparing in a young patient points to **[[Inflammatory Bowel Disease|Crohn's]]**; infiltrative (eosinophilic, lymphoma) is argued down by the decade-long relapsing course, and ischemia doesn't fit the age.
- **Exclude infection before you immunosuppress IBD — even when it won't explain the chronicity.** Infection can *trigger* a flare, so send C. diff, stool cultures, and NAAT/multiplex PCR, plus ESR/CRP; **stool lactoferrin is the "leukocyte esterase" of the gut** (it flags white cells) and calprotectin's value depends on pretest probability. Some infections favor the ileum (Yersinia, TB).
- **[[Pathergy|Pathergy]] is a quiet but powerful clue.** An inflammatory reaction *out of proportion* at a site of minor penetration (an IV site, EKG-lead contact) suggests pathergy and should trigger targeted questions for **[[Behçet's Disease|Behçet's]]**: oral ulcers, genital ulcers, ocular disease (HLA-B51 supports but the diagnosis is clinical). Behçet's mimics IBD "to a T," including ileal colitis with deep ulcers and neutrophilic infiltrate.
- **[[Base Rate|Epidemiology shapes autoimmune disease and cancer, not only infection]].** Travel/ancestry isn't just an infection filter: Behçet's is endemic in Japan (highest on the northern island), and HTLV-associated lymphoma clusters there too. Local experts practice **context-specific reasoning**, sniffing out Behçet's the way others reach for community-acquired pneumonia.
- **Keep "autoimmune" broader than "rheumatologic."** The [[Diagnostic Momentum|reflex]] to equate autoimmune with lupus/RA/scleroderma buries **immune-mediated** GI disease (IBD, Behçet's) and the "immune-mediated NOS" bucket (sarcoid). Build the schema so non-rheumatologic immune disease can't fall off the list.

## Problem Representation

A 23-year-old woman from Hokkaido presents with three days of severe nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea, mildly dehydrated (afebrile, mildly tachycardic, normotensive) — and, crucially, this is her fifth or sixth identical episode since age 13. Labs show a neutrophilic leukocytosis (15k), normocytic anemia (Hgb 10), thrombocytosis (497k), normal liver enzymes, a mild AKI, and a hypokalemic non-gap acidosis consistent with diarrhea; CT shows pancolitis and small-bowel enterocolitis with enhancement most prominent in the ileum and splenic flexure and mild reactive lymphadenopathy. Stool infectious testing (C. diff, cultures, multiplex PCR) is negative, and while a colonoscopy is pending she develops an erythematous patch at her antecubital IV site (a pathergy reaction), which prompts history revealing recurrent oral and genital ulcers. Endoscopy/colonoscopy shows deep ulcers with a neutrophilic infiltrate, establishing Behçet's disease (with A20 haploinsufficiency considered given onset at ~11), with a dramatic response to colchicine within three days.

## Case Narrative

Rabih presents live from Japan: a 23-year-old woman with three days of severe nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea. Reza layers a [[Young Patient Schema|young-patient]] backdrop borrowed from a Rebecca Berger / CP Solvers case — "bad luck, bad genes, and risky behavior" (accidents; genes manifesting in the 2nd–3rd decade; risk exposure) — but sets the [[Center of Gravity|center of gravity]] in the GI tract as the symptoms stack: nausea localizes nowhere, vomiting leans GI, diarrhea runs to the gut (with a systemic mimic like thyrotoxicosis held in reserve). At time zero he favors a **foodborne toxin or viral gastroenteritis** and declines more data until he's ready.

The next aliquot changes the frame with one word. The vitals show mild dehydration, but the history is that she has had **five or six identical bouts since age 13** — she's "here for another round." Reza declares this transformative: adding **recurrent** rules out acute foodborne/viral infection and reframes the illness through the inflammatory lens (infection, cancer, autoimmune, drug, endocrinopathy). Most infections fall away (even chronic GI colonizers like *T. whipplei* don't relapse this way), so he prioritizes an **endocrinopathy / neuroendocrine tumor**, an **exposure**, or an **autoimmune** process — noting that rheumatologic disease rarely centers on the GI tract.

Labs and imaging sharpen it: a **neutrophilic leukocytosis (15k)**, **normocytic anemia (Hgb 10)**, **thrombocytosis (497k)**, normal liver enzymes, mild AKI, and a **hypokalemic non-gap acidosis** (diarrheal), with CT showing **pancolitis and small-bowel enterocolitis**, enhancement most prominent in the **ileum** (and splenic flexure), and mild reactive lymphadenopathy. Reza jumps off the **colitis** finding and runs the **four I's — [[Colitis|Infection, IBD, Infiltrative, Ischemia]]**. Infection can't explain the relapses; infiltrative disease (eosinophilic infiltration, lymphoma) is argued down by a decade of waxing-waning symptoms since 13; ischemia doesn't fit. He lands on **[[Inflammatory Bowel Disease|IBD]]**, favoring **Crohn's** given ileal involvement and rectal sparing, and notes the anemia, thrombocytosis, diarrhea, and pancolitis all fit. But he invokes the discipline: **before immunosuppressing, exclude infection**, which can *trigger* a flare — send C. diff, stool cultures, and multiplex/NAAT PCR, plus ESR/CRP. He teaches the stool markers (**lactoferrin = the "leukocyte esterase" of the gut**, flagging white cells; **calprotectin** weighted by pretest probability) and would move to **colonoscopy with biopsy**, mentioning ileum-loving infections (**Yersinia**, TB) as caveats.

Stool infectious testing (biofire PCR) returns **negative**, and colonoscopy is slated for the next day. Then the **soft clue**: on reassessment the resident notes a **new erythematous patch at the right antecubital IV site**. Reza names it — an inflammatory reaction *out of proportion* at a site of minor penetration is **[[Pathergy|pathergy]]** (also seen at EKG-lead sites) — and pivots to **[[Behçet's Disease|Behçet's disease]]**, a great IBD mimic (ileal colitis, extra-intestinal features). He directs the targeted history — **oral ulcers, genital ulcers, ocular disease** (with HLA-B51 as a supportive haplotype, though the diagnosis is clinical, and Rabih's website figure showing the frequency of each finding). The history surfaces **many undisclosed recurrent oral and genital ulcers**, and colonoscopy shows **deep ulcers with a neutrophilic infiltrate** consistent with Behçet's; **colchicine** produces dramatic improvement within three days.

The Japanese hosts add the epidemiologic and clinical-reasoning coda. Behçet's has **one of the highest incidences in the world in Hokkaido**, so local clinicians reason about it the way others reason about community-acquired pneumonia — **context-specific clinical reasoning** that prioritizes locally common diagnoses. Given onset at ~11 (unusually young), they even consider **[[A20 Haploinsufficiency|A20 haploinsufficiency]]**, a heritable Behçet-like autoinflammatory disease (testing wouldn't change management). Rabih generalizes the deeper lesson: **epidemiology influences autoimmune disease and cancer, not just infection** — Behçet's regionally, and HTLV-associated lymphoma in Japan — so travel/ancestry should widen more than the infectious differential. The closing [[Cognitive Autopsy|autopsies]]: Rabih on the power of the word **recurrent** and the readiness to pull a patient off a diagnostic "track" when a soft clue appears; Reza (via Harry Hollander's framework — infection, malignancy, autoimmune, and immune-mediated NOS like sarcoid) on his own trap of equating **"autoimmune" with "rheumatologic,"** which nearly buried IBD/Behçet's, and the fix of building a schema that keeps **non-rheumatologic immune-mediated disease** on the list.

### Illness Scripts and Bias

- **[[Illness Tempo|Tempo/recurrence as the pivot]].** The single most powerful move is recognizing that "recurrent since 13" converts a benign gastroenteritis into a chronic inflammatory problem — periodicity and duration reshape the differential before labs.
- **[[Center of Gravity|Center of gravity, built incrementally]].** Reza refuses to over-commit on "nausea" and lets the accumulating symptoms (then the CT colitis) define the anatomic/organ focus, keeping a systemic mimic in reserve.
- **[[Schema-Based Reasoning|Nested schemas]].** The young-patient schema, the inflammatory framework (infection/cancer/autoimmune/drug/endocrinopathy), and the colitis four-I's stack to convert a broad problem into ordered forks.
- **[[Diagnostic Momentum|Riding a track, ready to jump off]].** "IBD until proven otherwise" is the correct base-rate track — and the discipline is staying alert enough to a soft clue (the pathergy patch) to leave it. The bias lesson is that being on a track is fine *if* you remain removable.
- **Broadening "autoimmune" beyond rheumatologic (schema completeness).** Reza's [[Cognitive Autopsy|autopsy]] names the trap of jumping to lupus/RA/scleroderma and missing immune-mediated GI disease; the fix (Hollander's infection/malignancy/autoimmune/immune-NOS) keeps IBD and Behçet's on the list.
- **[[Base Rate|Epidemiology beyond infection]].** Local prevalence reweights *autoimmune* and *malignant* priors, not just infectious ones — context-specific reasoning that made Behçet's a near-reflex for the Hokkaido clinicians.

## Teaching Discussion

- Let periodicity reset the differential: a recurrent, relapsing illness excludes acute infection and moves you into the inflammatory framework (infection, cancer, autoimmune, drug, endocrinopathy) even when the acute picture looks like gastroenteritis.
- Build the center of gravity as symptoms accumulate rather than over-committing on a single nonspecific complaint, while holding a systemic mimic (e.g., thyrotoxicosis) in reserve.
- Work colitis through Infection, IBD, Infiltrative, and Ischemia; ileal involvement with rectal sparing in a young patient favors Crohn's, and a decade of relapses argues against infiltrative disease.
- Exclude infection before immunosuppressing IBD (infection can trigger a flare): send C. diff, stool cultures, and multiplex PCR, use stool lactoferrin as the gut's leukocyte-esterase and calprotectin by pretest probability, and remember ileum-favoring infections (Yersinia, TB).
- Recognize pathergy — a disproportionate inflammatory reaction at a minor penetration site (IV, EKG leads) — as a prompt to screen for Behçet's with questions about oral ulcers, genital ulcers, and ocular disease; Behçet's mimics IBD, including ileal colitis with deep ulcers and neutrophilic infiltrate.
- Let epidemiology widen the autoimmune and malignant differential, not just the infectious one: Behçet's is endemic in Japan (highest on the northern island) and HTLV-lymphoma clusters there — an example of context-specific reasoning.
- Keep "autoimmune" broader than "rheumatologic": immune-mediated GI disease (IBD, Behçet's) and immune-mediated-NOS (sarcoid) must stay on the checklist so a reflex toward lupus/RA/scleroderma doesn't bury them.
- Stay ready to leave a correct base-rate track (IBD) when a soft clue (pathergy, undisclosed ulcers) appears — being on a track is fine if you remain removable.
