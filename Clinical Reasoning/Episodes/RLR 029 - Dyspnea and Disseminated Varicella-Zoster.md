## Summary

> [!summary] Scan Summary
> Rabih's final Patreon case, and the one he calls the most humbling of his career: a 74-year-old man who had deliberately kept out of the medical system for decades arrives with a week of vague chest, right-shoulder, and right-upper-quadrant discomfort and the sense his heart is racing. He is in new [[Atrial Flutter]] at 150 with a normal blood pressure, mildly [[Pleuritic Chest Pain|pleuritic]], and has flat neck veins, clear lungs, and mild RUQ tenderness. Reza works both problems as [[Primary vs Secondary Disease|primary versus secondary]] and hunts for a trigger. Labs show a sodium of 125 that proves hypo-osmolar, with a urine osm over 600 and — obtained later — a urine sodium of 130 and a serum uric acid of 1: [[SIADH|inappropriate ADH]] with aldosterone off. A CTPA and pan-CT are clean, the BNP is 250, and Reza refuses to explain a sodium of 125 by clinically silent heart failure, citing the [[Law of Proportionality|law of proportionality]] and declining to diurese; Rabih, anchored on flutter with congestive hepatopathy, [[Anchoring Bias|diureses anyway]] and the patient fails to respond. Cortisol, TSH, meds, and toxins are unremarkable, leaving [[SIADH]] with the cause still hidden — until the next morning, when the man erupts in a head-to-belly [[Vesicular Rash|vesicular rash]] and his pleuritic pain worsens. A bedside [[Tzanck Smear|cytology]] shows multinucleated giant cells; VZV PCR confirms [[Varicella Zoster Virus]]. Thoracic zoster is a bigger driver of SIADH than Legionella, and its neuritic involvement explains the CT-negative pleuritic and RUQ pain. Whether this is [[Disseminated Zoster|disseminated zoster]] or [[Primary Varicella|primary varicella]] in an under-vaccinated host is never resolved — a case that hid its most specific card until last.

## Pearls

- **Break both the arrhythmia and the hyponatremia into [[Primary vs Secondary Disease|primary versus secondary]], and hunt for a trigger.** New [[Atrial Flutter|atrial flutter]] and new [[SIADH]] each demand a secondary driver — screen with [[MIST Mnemonic|MIST]] (metabolic, infectious, toxin), checking TSH, caffeine/energy drinks, occult infection, and organ perfusion. The payoff of the case is that a *single* secondary cause drove both problems at once.
- **A normal blood pressure in a fast tachyarrhythmia does not exclude cardiogenic shock.** A rate of 150 should itself suggest flutter with 2:1 conduction; a preserved pressure may reflect cardiac reserve, but a failing heart can be in shock at that rate with cold extremities as the only tell — so read the JVP, the lungs, and the peripheral perfusion before being reassured, and be wary of rate-controlling a rhythm that may be compensatory.
- **Respect the [[Law of Proportionality|law of proportionality]] — a sodium of 125 is out of proportion to clinically silent heart failure.** That degree of hyponatremia lives in *florid, advanced* heart failure as a poor-prognostic marker, not in a patient with flat neck veins, clear lungs, and no edema. An elevated BNP earns an echocardiogram, not reflexive diuresis; here the diuretic trial failed, and the number that did not fit the picture was the one telling the truth.
- **The SIADH algorithm reads two urine values in sequence.** A high urine osm proves ADH is on (excluding psychogenic polydipsia and beer potomania); a high urine sodium proves aldosterone is *off* (excluding low-effective-volume causes) — together, inappropriate ADH, which narrows to five diagnoses: [[Adrenal Insufficiency|adrenal insufficiency]], hypothyroidism, [[Cerebral Salt Wasting|cerebral salt wasting]], thiazides, and [[SIADH]]. A [[Serum Uric Acid|serum uric acid]] under 4 supports SIADH — but interpret the urine sodium only off diuretics and without ATN or renal failure.
- **Once SIADH is confirmed it is almost always secondary — hunt pain, medications, infection, and occult malignancy.** The classic infectious trigger is pneumonia (especially [[Legionella|Legionella]]), and the classic tumor is a [[Small Cell Lung Cancer|small-cell/neuroendocrine]] cancer of the lung — but **thoracic zoster is a more potent SIADH trigger than Legionella**, and belongs on the list.
- **Rank findings by specificity and weight the most specific one, even when it arrives last.** Flutter and SIADH each carry broad differentials; a diffuse [[Vesicular Rash|vesicular rash]] does not. Occult SIADH may not stay occult if you admit and watch — this diagnosis "played its most specific card last," and was likely not gettable before the rash appeared.

## Problem Representation

A 74-year-old man who had deliberately avoided health care for decades — leaving a blank chart and an unknown baseline — presents with a week of vague chest, right-shoulder, and right-upper-quadrant discomfort and weeks to months of palpitations. He is in new atrial flutter at 150 with a normal blood pressure, normal oxygen saturation, mildly pleuritic chest pain, flat JVP, clear lungs, and mild RUQ tenderness without other exam abnormality. Labs show a sodium of 125 that is proportionally hypo-osmolar with a urine osm over 600, a later urine sodium of 130, and a serum uric acid of 1, with normal CBC, TSH, liver enzymes, morning cortisol, and a clear chest radiograph; a CT pulmonary angiogram, CT abdomen/pelvis, and BNP (250) do not explain the picture, and a diuretic trial fails. The evolving diagnosis is SIADH of initially occult cause, resolved the next day when he develops a diffuse head-to-abdomen vesicular eruption on an erythematous base with worsening pleuritic pain — a Tzanck-positive, PCR-confirmed varicella-zoster infection (disseminated zoster versus primary varicella left undetermined) driving both the SIADH and the neuritic chest and abdominal pain.

## Case Narrative

Recorded as the last Patreon episode before the move to their website, Reza frames this as the most humbling case he has ever managed. The patient is a 74-year-old man, immediately kind and warm, who had made a deliberate decision to spend his life away from health care — strong feelings about the medical system, a blank chart, and years of caring for himself his own way. The last week forced him in: vague chest discomfort, right-upper-quadrant discomfort, and right-shoulder discomfort, plus a sense that his heart had been racing for some weeks to months.

Reza opens on the danger of the blank chart: the absence of a recorded past medical history does not mean the absence of *disease* — undiagnosed hypertension, hyperlipidemia, or an unknown immune status could all be shaping the foreground problem. He wants vitals first, a low threshold for an [[Electrocardiogram|EKG]] given "racing heart plus chest discomfort," and — because multiple chest complaints are hard to localize — early attention to the [[Aortic Dissection|aorta]] as a do-not-miss cause of pain in several places at once. The monitor shows a heart rate of 150 with a normal blood pressure and oxygen saturation; the chest pain is [[Pleuritic Chest Pain|pleuritic]]; the EKG shows [[Atrial Flutter|atrial flutter]] with no priors.

Reza turns the rate itself into a teaching point: a heart rate of exactly 150 suggests flutter with 2:1 conduction even before the tracing. A normal blood pressure at that rate may signal cardiac reserve — but he cautions that a normal or high pressure does *not* exclude cardiogenic shock, recalling patients in shock from a tachyarrhythmia whose only clue was cold extremities over a poorly functioning heart, so the JVP, lung, and perfusion exam are crucial. He then frames the arrhythmia as [[Primary vs Secondary Disease|primary versus secondary]]: aging alone raises the risk of atrial arrhythmias, but flutter is often the surface signature of something beneath it, so he would look for triggers with a [[MIST Mnemonic|MIST]]-style sweep — metabolic (a TSH for thyrotoxicosis), toxic (caffeine, energy drinks), infectious (a pneumonia irritating the pericardium or diaphragm) — while checking liver and kidney function and urine output for end-organ effects. The exam is reassuring: flat JVP, clear lungs, tachycardic without murmurs, and only mild RUQ tenderness. Rabih deliberately does *not* rate-control him, reasoning the flutter might be an appropriate compensation for an underlying process he has not yet found.

The labs return: a normal CBC, a sodium of 125, a urine osm (inferred from the UA, later confirmed) over 600, normal liver enzymes, a normal TSH, and a clear chest radiograph. Reza builds the hyponatremia schema. A serum osm confirms the sodium is *proportionally* low — true hypo-osmolar [[Hyponatremia|hyponatremia]]. A urine osm over 600 means ADH is on, excluding psychogenic polydipsia and beer potomania. The next step is a urine sodium: low would indicate a low-effective-volume state (heart, liver, kidney, or hypovolemia, with aldosterone on), while high would open a narrow differential. He flags a key mismatch up front — a sodium of 125 should *not* cause atrial flutter, and should not cause RUQ tenderness; hyponatremia's manifestations are mainly neuropsychiatric — so he keeps a small, separate circle open for the RUQ pain (a dislodged stone or early congestion the labs have not yet shown) and a large circle for the flutter, sensing they must connect but unable to link them until the urine sodium arrives.

Rabih, meanwhile, has cast a wide net: a CT pulmonary angiogram for new flutter with pleuritic pain, a CT abdomen/pelvis to "tuck in the belly" for the RUQ pain, and a BNP he almost forgot. The CT chest and CT abdomen/pelvis are stone-cold normal; the BNP is 250. Reza notes the BMI of ~27–28 (adipose metabolizes BNP, lowering levels, so this value is meaningful) and considers whether new-onset heart failure could explain *everything* — a dilated atrium precipitating flutter, dyspnea, RUQ congestion, and hyponatremia. He rejects it on the [[Law of Proportionality|law of proportionality]]: a sodium of 125 belongs to *florid, advanced* heart failure — the volume-overloaded patients for whom the sodium is a poor-prognostic marker — not to a man with flat neck veins, clear lungs, and no edema. The disconnect (like a UA with large hemoglobin but only a few red cells) means the elevated BNP earns an echocardiogram, not diuresis. He would not diurese, would image the atria, and would wait for the urine sodium; the clean CTPA also excludes PE and a large dissection, and there is no pericarditis, pneumonia, or pneumothorax to pin the pleurisy on.

Rabih confesses he diuresed the patient anyway — anchored on flutter with an elevated BNP and hyponatremia — and the man did not respond to furosemide. Crucially, the urine sodium had been drawn *before* the diuretic: it was 130 (high), with a serum uric acid of 1, no medications, no toxins, a normal morning cortisol, and a normal pan-scan. A COVID swab was initially declined; Reza's non-judgmental negotiation of hospital policy (respecting the patient while explaining the rules) let the man stay, and that relationship would matter later.

Reza closes the electrolyte reasoning. With the urine sodium valid (off diuretics, no renal insufficiency, no ATN), ADH is on (high urine osm) and aldosterone is off (high urine sodium) — inappropriate ADH, which activates just five possibilities: [[Adrenal Insufficiency|adrenal insufficiency]] (cortisol normal, no supportive clinical features), hypothyroidism (TSH normal), [[Cerebral Salt Wasting|cerebral salt wasting]] (no intracranial insult, no salt-wasting diuresis), thiazides (no medications, and uric acid would run high), and [[SIADH]]. The [[Serum Uric Acid|serum uric acid]] of 1 seals it: SIADH lowers urate both by dilution and by an increased fractional excretion, running under 4. He then works the secondary-cause hunt SIADH demands — pain, medications (psychotropics), infection (pneumonia, especially [[Legionella|Legionella]]), and occult cancer — noting that the perineoplastic tumors are the [[Small Cell Lung Cancer|small-cell/neuroendocrine]] cancers that live in the lung, so a negative CT deserves a second look for a nodule, a smoking history, and possibly a [[PET-CT|PET-CT]]. For management he lays out the [[SIADH]] ladder: confirm that urine (Na + K) is less than serum sodium so fluid restriction can work, restrict fluid (not just water) to ~1 L, then reach not for salt tabs but for [[Urea for Hyponatremia|urea powder]] (15 g, which rebuilds the medullary gradient and excretes free water), with furosemide (a hypotonic, nephrogenic-DI-mimicking urine) as a further option.

Then the twist. Reza happened to be both the ED physician and, that week, the ward attending; the patient was admitted overnight and became a holdover on his own team. On rounds the group could not source the SIADH in a non-smoker with no risk factors — until the resident, sent to re-examine him, found a head, chest, back, and abdominal [[Vesicular Rash|vesicular eruption]] on an erythematous base, with the pleuritic pain now worse. Twelve hours earlier Reza had examined that same chest and belly and seen nothing.

Reza's rash schema narrows fast. A diffuse vesicular eruption is an "inside job," not contact dermatitis, and the morphology points to a herpesvirus: [[Varicella Zoster Virus|varicella-zoster]] in its two flavors — chickenpox or shingles — with disseminated zoster in a seemingly immunocompetent host prompting an HIV test; HSV fits poorly; AGEP is more pustular and needs an antibiotic exposure he lacks; and a paraneoplastic eruption is a distant possibility. He would swab a vesicle for VZV, recheck liver chemistries for visceral dissemination, and repeat a chest radiograph. He names the meta-lesson: of the three findings, the flutter is non-specific and the SIADH narrows to a list, but the [[Vesicular Rash|vesicular rash]] has few causes — the diagnosis had hidden itself by revealing its most specific card last.

The team recognized varicella-zoster: an abrupt eruption over head, chest, abdomen, and back with *nothing* on the extremities, and pleuritic and abdominal pain they now read as neuritic involvement of the virus. Checking the literature, they found thoracic zoster to be a *more* prominent SIADH trigger than Legionella — enough that they updated their SIADH schema. Dermatology's bedside [[Tzanck Smear|cytology]] showed multinucleated giant cells with viral inclusions classic for zoster; IV acyclovir brought improvement over three days; HIV was negative and VZV was PCR-proven. What remained unresolved was *which* VZV syndrome: abrupt [[Disseminated Zoster|disseminated zoster]] with no findable immunodeficiency, or [[Primary Varicella|primary varicella]] (chickenpox) in a man with limited or unknown prior exposure/vaccination. Reza leaned toward zoster on the basis of his age and the degree of neuropathic pain, while the abrupt torso-predominant onset, the absence of fever, and lesions not in separate stages of evolution muddied the call. A full immune workup — HIV, IgG subclasses, a chest film for thymoma, cortisol, medication review, CD4 and T-lymphocyte subsets — was normal.

Reza's reflections: the SIADH was a powerful clue, but the case was likely not gettable before the rash; still, CT-negative pleuritic chest pain with SIADH will now make him think of zoster, because pleuritic pain can be a *chest-wall* (neuritic or bony) problem as much as a pleural one, and zoster belongs on the pleuritic-chest-pain schema. He recalls the [[Diagnostic Humility|humbling]] moment of having examined a bare chest and belly half a day before it was covered in vesicles — one of the most topsy-turvy diagnostic journeys of his career, and a companion to his earlier EBV-cerebritis case where a herpesvirus hid, then declared itself.

### Illness Scripts and Bias

- **[[Anchoring Bias|Anchoring]] on heart failure — named and lived in real time.** Reza's headline move is refusing to explain a sodium of 125 by clinically silent heart failure; Rabih, anchored on flutter plus an elevated BNP plus hyponatremia, diuresed and the patient did not respond. The episode is unusually candid that the anchor was seductive precisely because heart failure could account for *every* finding — the temptation of a unifying story is what had to be resisted.
- **[[Law of Proportionality|The law of proportionality]] as an anti-anchor.** The discipline that broke the heart-failure anchor was weighing the *magnitude* of one finding against the rest: a sodium of 125 is a marker of florid, end-stage volume overload, not of a euvolemic-appearing man, so the number that did not fit the clinical picture (the sodium), not the number that did (the BNP), was the one to trust. The parallel drawn is a UA with large hemoglobin but few red cells — read the disproportion, not the single positive.
- **[[Primary vs Secondary Disease|Primary-versus-secondary]] as the organizing spine.** Both the flutter and the SIADH are worked as surface signatures demanding a hidden driver, with SIADH held to an even higher standard of secondary-cause hunting than the arrhythmia. The satisfying twist is that one secondary cause — VZV — sat beneath both, rewarding the refusal to treat either problem as idiopathic.
- **[[Test Interpretation|Reading the urine chemistries as a two-step lock]].** Urine osm and urine sodium are not a bundle but a sequence: osm establishes ADH is on, sodium establishes aldosterone is off, and only their conjunction yields *inappropriate* ADH — with the explicit caveats that a urine sodium is uninterpretable on diuretics or in ATN, and that a serum uric acid under 4 is confirmatory physiology, not a throwaway.
- **[[Tempo-Based Reasoning|Tempo and the specificity of the last card]].** The most discriminating finding appeared last, and only because the patient was admitted and re-examined; "occult SIADH may not stay occult if you watch." Ranking findings by specificity (rash >> SIADH >> flutter) and letting the illness evolve over hours did what no single cross-section could — a lesson in serial observation over one-shot workup.
- **[[Diagnostic Humility|Humility and the relational layer]].** The case ends genuinely unresolved (disseminated zoster vs primary varicella), and Reza treats "we don't know" as honest rather than a failure; separately, the non-judgmental handling of the patient's vaccine and COVID-swab hesitancy preserved a therapeutic alliance that mattered — and the unknown vaccination history is itself the datum that keeps the VZV subtype undetermined.

## Teaching Discussion

- Meet a new atrial arrhythmia by asking whether it is primary or secondary, and sweep for triggers (MIST: metabolic, infectious, toxin) with a TSH, a caffeine/stimulant history, and a search for occult infection — while remembering that a rate near 150 is flutter with 2:1 until proven otherwise, and that a normal blood pressure does not rule out cardiogenic shock when reserve is masking it.
- Build the hyponatremia diagnosis in order: confirm hypotonicity with a serum osm, read the urine osm to establish that ADH is on (excluding polydipsia and beer potomania), then read the urine sodium to establish that aldosterone is off — the combination is inappropriate ADH, whose five causes are adrenal insufficiency, hypothyroidism, cerebral salt wasting, thiazides, and SIADH, with a serum uric acid under 4 favoring SIADH; interpret the urine sodium only off diuretics and without ATN or renal failure.
- Let the law of proportionality govern a value that does not fit: a sodium of 125 implies florid, advanced heart failure, so in a patient with flat neck veins and clear lungs an elevated BNP should trigger an echocardiogram rather than empiric diuresis — the disproportionate finding, not the convenient one, is usually the honest signal.
- After confirming SIADH, treat it as almost always secondary and hunt pain, medications (especially psychotropics), infection, and occult malignancy — recalling that pneumonia (notably Legionella) and small-cell/neuroendocrine lung cancer are classic, but that thoracic zoster is an even stronger SIADH trigger and belongs explicitly on the schema.
- Manage SIADH by first checking that urine (sodium + potassium) is less than serum sodium so fluid restriction can succeed, then restricting total fluid (not water alone) to about a liter, adding urea powder (which rebuilds the medullary gradient to excrete free water) in preference to salt tabs, and reserving a loop diuretic to generate a hypotonic, nephrogenic-DI-like urine.
- Narrow a diffuse vesicular rash as an "inside job": varicella-zoster (chickenpox versus shingles) leads, with HSV and antibiotic-associated AGEP less likely and a paraneoplastic eruption a distant option; swab a vesicle for VZV, send a bedside Tzanck for multinucleated giant cells, and in disseminated zoster without an obvious cause, test for HIV and screen the immune system (IgG subclasses, CD4 and T-cell subsets, a chest film for thymoma, cortisol, a medication review).
- Read pleuritic chest pain as potentially a chest-wall problem — neuritic or bony — not only a pleural one, and add zoster to the differential for CT-negative pleuritic pain, especially alongside SIADH, since the rash of zoster can lag its pain and neuritis; the reasonable honest endpoint here — disseminated zoster versus primary varicella in an under-vaccinated host — is left undetermined because it changes the expected complications.
