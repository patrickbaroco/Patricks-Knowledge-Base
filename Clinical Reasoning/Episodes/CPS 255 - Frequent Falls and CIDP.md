## Summary

> [!summary] Scan Summary
> A Neurology VMR on **"[[Falls|frequent falls]]"** that becomes a clinic on **[[Peripheral Neuropathy|peripheral neuropathy]]**. Berkowitz opens with his signature move: **[[Gait Disorder|gait is the "symphony"]]** of the whole nervous system (the seven-part exam tested last), so falls could be **weakness, sensory loss, [[Ataxia|ataxia]], [[Parkinsonism|parkinsonism]], or vision** — *or* not gait at all but **[[Syncope|syncope]]** from autonomic dysfunction/polypharmacy. The patient is a **72-year-old woman** with a week of worsening falls (now multiple daily) and, on ROS, **numbness and tingling that began in the *fingers* two months ago**, spread across both hands, and only a week ago reached the feet. The **[[Stocking-Glove Distribution|stocking-glove]]** pattern drives a **[[Unifying Diagnosis|parsimonious]] [[Anatomical Approach|localization]]**: not cortex (would need many lesions), not brainstem (no cranial-nerve signs), not cord (no bowel/bladder, why spare the proximal limb?), not painful roots — **the peripheral nerves**. The episode's spine is the **[[Axonal vs Demyelinating Neuropathy|axonal-vs-demyelinating]] branch point**: **axonal** neuropathies are **length-dependent** (longest axons first → *feet* first; toxic/metabolic — diabetes, B12, chemo), while **demyelinating** ones are **non-length-dependent** (hands and feet together, reflexes lost throughout; inflammatory). Because the **hands were affected *before* the feet**, this is **demyelinating** — and the **subacute (months) tempo** is too slow for **[[Guillain-Barré Syndrome|GBS]]**, too fast for **[[Charcot-Marie-Tooth|CMT]]**, pointing at an inflammatory cause. Exam confirms a **distal sensorimotor polyneuropathy with global [[Areflexia|areflexia]]**. The first-pass neuropathy labs (**A1C, B12, and — the commonly-forgotten third — [[SPEP|SPEP with immunofixation]]**) and a paraneoplastic/imaging screen are negative; **[[Nerve Conduction Studies|nerve conduction studies]]** ("an extension of the physical exam") show a **demyelinating sensorimotor polyneuropathy with prolonged distal motor latencies**, confirming **[[CIDP|chronic inflammatory demyelinating polyradiculoneuropathy]]** — the "chronic cousin of GBS." She was treated with **[[IVIG|IVIG]]**, then steroids (stabilized, not improved; steroid hyperglycemia), with **[[Rituximab|rituximab]]** under consideration. Teaching coda: **the history made the diagnosis** (hands-before-feet settled *demyelinating* before any test), and CIDP is both real and **frequently *mis*diagnosed** (equivocal EMGs in pain syndromes → inappropriate immunosuppression).

## Pearls

- **[[Gait Disorder|Gait is the "symphony"]] of the nervous system — so decode "[[Falls|falls]]" broadly, and consider it may not be gait at all.** Walking integrates vision, cognition, [[Basal Ganglia|basal-ganglia]] pattern-generation, strength, sensation/proprioception, and cerebellar coordination; a faller could have any of these — or the "falls" could be **[[Syncope|syncope]]** (autonomic failure, polypharmacy, orthostasis), a different schema entirely.
- **A [[Stocking-Glove Distribution|stocking-glove]] sensory pattern [[Unifying Diagnosis|parsimoniously]] localizes to the [[Peripheral Neuropathy|peripheral nerve]].** Bilateral distal hands *and* feet can't be one cortical or brainstem lesion (no cranial-nerve signs), isn't cord (no bowel/bladder, and why spare proximal?), and isn't painful roots — the [[Anatomical Approach|anatomy]] forces a **polyneuropathy**, especially when symmetric and distal-predominant.
- **The most powerful first branch point in neuropathy is [[Axonal vs Demyelinating Neuropathy|axonal vs demyelinating]] — read it off the *pattern*.** **Axonal** = **length-dependent** (longest axons fail first, so *feet before hands*; think toxic/metabolic — **[[Diabetic Neuropathy|diabetes]]**, **[[B12 Deficiency|B12]]**, chemotherapy). **Demyelinating** = **non-length-dependent** (short and long fibers together, reflexes lost throughout; think inflammatory). Here the **hands came first**, which is *non-length-dependent* → **demyelinating**.
- **[[Illness Tempo|Tempo]] narrows the demyelinating differential.** Days-to-weeks (nadir 2–4 weeks) = **[[Guillain-Barré Syndrome|GBS/AIDP]]**; years = **[[Charcot-Marie-Tooth|CMT]]**; **subacute (months)** — too slow for GBS, too fast for CMT — points to **inflammatory ([[CIDP|CIDP]])** and **[[Paraneoplastic Neurologic Syndromes|paraneoplastic]]** causes.
- **The neuropathy first-pass labs are A1C, B12, and — the one people forget — [[SPEP|SPEP with immunofixation]].** Paraprotein neuropathies (myeloma, MGUS, Waldenström, **[[POEMS Syndrome|POEMS]]**, amyloid) are rare-but-don't-miss, and **SPEP alone is inadequate — order immunofixation** (a monoclonal band can be missed otherwise). Second pass: rheumatologic panel, HIV, TSH, Lyme.
- **[[Nerve Conduction Studies|Nerve conduction studies]] are an *extension of the physical exam* — velocity vs amplitude.** Demyelination slows **conduction velocity** (and prolongs distal latencies); axonal loss drops **amplitude**. In suspected [[CIDP|CIDP]], an **[[Lumbar Puncture|LP]]** shows **[[Albuminocytologic Dissociation|albuminocytologic dissociation]]** (the roots traverse the CSF — the "P" is polyradiculo*neuropathy*), and remember CIDP is **frequently misdiagnosed** from equivocal EMGs, committing patients to needless immunosuppression.

## Problem Representation

A 72-year-old woman with diet-controlled type 2 diabetes presents with one week of rapidly worsening falls (now multiple per day) on a background of two months of sensory symptoms that began as numbness and tingling in the **fingers**, spread across both hands, and only a week ago reached the feet — a stocking-glove distribution that localizes to the peripheral nerves. Examination shows a **distal sensorimotor polyneuropathy** (distal-predominant weakness, decreased distal sensation and proprioception, preserved coordination) with **global areflexia**. Because the **hands were involved before the feet**, the pattern is **non-length-dependent**, favoring a **demyelinating** rather than axonal (length-dependent, toxic/metabolic) process; the **subacute** months-long tempo is too slow for GBS and too fast for a hereditary neuropathy, pointing to an inflammatory cause. A first-pass and expanded serologic workup (A1C, B12, SPEP with immunofixation, anti-MAG, paraneoplastic/imaging screen) is unrevealing, and nerve conduction studies confirm a **demyelinating sensorimotor polyneuropathy with prolonged distal motor latencies**. The diagnosis is **chronic inflammatory demyelinating polyradiculoneuropathy (CIDP)**, managed with IVIG, then corticosteroids, with rituximab under consideration.

## Case Narrative

Rafa presents a one-word chief concern — **"[[Falls|frequent falls]]"** — and Berkowitz frames the approach with his familiar teaching that **[[Gait Disorder|gait is the "symphony"]] of the nervous system**: the seven-part neurologic exam (mental status, cranial nerves, motor, sensory, reflexes, cerebellum, gait) tests gait *last* because walking requires all the other instruments playing together — vision, cognition, [[Basal Ganglia|basal-ganglia]] movement-patterning, strength, sensation/proprioception, and cerebellar coordination. So a faller could be weak, sensory-impaired, ataxic, parkinsonian, or visually impaired. Alice widens it further: some **atypical [[Parkinsonism|parkinsonian]] syndromes** cause autonomic failure, so the "falls" might actually be **[[Syncope|syncope]]** — a different schema (episodic loss of consciousness) altogether. Yasmin adds the age/risk framing (falls rise sharply over 65; ask about number, circumstances, vision, cognition, orthostasis, **polypharmacy**, vitamin D). Berkowitz gathers these into the questions he wants answered — *are the falls trips, faints, or something else; by day or night (night/shower = a real-world [[Romberg Sign|Romberg]]); at home or outside; with weakness, numbness, pain, or incoordination* — and flags parkinsonian features to probe.

The **history** sharpens it. A **72-year-old woman**, previously fully independent and **never a faller**, has fallen repeatedly for a week, now multiple times daily, losing balance during ordinary activities like stepping backward. On review of systems, she reveals **numbness and tingling that began in the *fingers* two months ago**, progressed to involve **all fingers of both hands**, and only **a week ago** reached the **feet**. Yasmin's reflex is **[[B12 Deficiency|B12 deficiency]]** and a plan to test [[Romberg Sign|Romberg]] for sensory vs cerebellar ataxia; Alice reads a **[[Stocking-Glove Distribution|glove-and-stocking]] [[Peripheral Neuropathy|neuropathy]]** (diabetes, toxins, alcohol, B12) and — astutely — flags the *hands-first* detail as "particular."

Berkowitz builds the **[[Anatomical Approach|localization]]** deliberately, first reviewing the somatosensory pathway (peripheral nerve → **[[Dorsal Root Ganglion|dorsal root ganglion]]** → dorsal root → dorsal column [vibration/proprioception, crossing in the lower medulla] vs **[[Spinothalamic Tract|spinothalamic]]** [pain/temperature, crossing on entry] → VPL thalamus → somatosensory cortex). Then he applies **[[Unifying Diagnosis|parsimony]]**: could one lesion explain bilateral distal hands *and* feet? Not **cortex** (would require several separate lesions), not **brainstem** (no cranial-nerve deficits), not **spinal cord** (no bowel/bladder involvement, and why would it spare the *proximal* limb?), and not **[[Radiculopathy|nerve roots]]** (usually painful/radicular). A **symmetric, distal-predominant** sensory (± motor) problem localizes to **peripheral nerve** — a **polyneuropathy** — and *not* the neuromuscular junction or muscle (which spare sensation).

Now the episode's spine: the **[[Axonal vs Demyelinating Neuropathy|axonal-vs-demyelinating]] branch point**. Alice notes some neuropathies start in the *hands*, tied to **axonal vs demyelinating** physiology, and Berkowitz develops it with two images:
- **Axonal** neuropathies are **length-dependent** — the longest axons (lumbar cord → toe) are most vulnerable to a toxin or metabolic insult ("a 20-mile commute suffers more in a traffic jam than a 1-mile one"; the clearest example is **microtubule-poisoning chemotherapies** — taxanes, platins, vinca — that stall axonal transport). So patients start in the **feet**, and the hands aren't involved until the deficit climbs to the shin. Axonal → think **toxic/metabolic** (**[[Diabetic Neuropathy|diabetes]]** — the commonest cause in high-income countries — **[[B12 Deficiency|B12]]**, chemo).
- **Demyelinating** neuropathies attack the **myelin everywhere** ("throw a can of paint at your chalk outline — it goes all over"), so they are **non-length-dependent**: short and long fibers, upper and lower limbs affected *together*, with **reflexes lost throughout** (not just distally). Longer fibers still carry the most myelin, so a distal predominance can remain, but the presentation is generalized.

Because the **hands were affected before the feet**, this is **non-length-dependent → demyelinating** — which reframes the differential toward **inflammatory** causes and away from the diabetic/B12 axonal pattern. **[[Illness Tempo|Tempo]]** narrows it further: the **months-long** course is **too slow for [[Guillain-Barré Syndrome|GBS/AIDP]]** (days, nadir at 2–4 weeks) and **too fast for [[Charcot-Marie-Tooth|CMT]]** (years) — a **subacute** window that raises **[[CIDP|CIDP]]** and **[[Paraneoplastic Neurologic Syndromes|paraneoplastic]]** neuropathy (B12's subacute combined degeneration is *axonal*, so it fits less well).

The **exam** confirms the localization: cranial nerves intact; **5/5 in large proximal groups but distal weakness** (dorsal interossei 4/5, abductor pollicis brevis 3/5, tibialis anterior and toe muscles 4/5); **absent reflexes** at biceps, patellae, and ankles (trace triceps); **decreased distal sensation and proprioception**; and **normal finger-nose with no ataxia**. This is a **distal sensorimotor polyneuropathy with global [[Areflexia|areflexia]]** — the peripheral-nerve localization proven, and (with hands-before-feet) still favoring **demyelinating**. Alice raises **[[Paraneoplastic Neurologic Syndromes|paraneoplastic]]** disease (**[[Anti-Hu Antibody|anti-Hu]]**, associated with lung/breast and multiple neurologic syndromes in a 72-year-old) and the reflex loss reminiscent of GBS; Berkowitz keeps **[[CIDP|CIDP]]** (the chronic cousin of GBS, with sensory/motor/sensorimotor variants) as the lead, noting a **[[Sensory Ganglionopathy|sensory ganglionopathy]]** is excluded by the *motor* involvement.

He then teaches the **[[Test Interpretation|workup]]**: the AAN's **first-pass neuropathy labs** are **A1C/glucose, B12 (with MMA), and — the one people forget — [[SPEP|SPEP with immunofixation]]** (screening the paraprotein neuropathies: myeloma, MGUS, Waldenström, **[[POEMS Syndrome|POEMS]]**, amyloid), with the caveat that **SPEP alone is inadequate — you must add immunofixation**. The **[[Base Rate|base-rate]] reality**: in years of practice the SPEP has caught an occult myeloma "maybe once," but it's a don't-miss. Second pass: rheumatologic panel (lupus, Sjögren, sarcoid), HIV, TSH, Lyme, syphilis (the last two rarely causing neuropathy). If serum tests are unrevealing in a progressing patient: **[[Nerve Conduction Studies|EMG/nerve conduction studies]]** ("an extension of the physical exam"), where **demyelination slows conduction velocity** (and prolongs distal latencies) while **axonal loss lowers amplitude** (temporal dispersion muddies severe cases); a **[[Lumbar Puncture|lumbar puncture]]** for **[[Albuminocytologic Dissociation|albuminocytologic dissociation]]** (the roots traverse the CSF — the "P" of CIDP/AIDP is *polyradiculoneuropathy*) and antinodal antibodies; and, last, a **nerve biopsy**.

The data return: **CBC, CMP, CK, TSH, B12 normal; [[Anti-MAG Neuropathy|anti-MAG]] negative; SPEP negative; CT head and chest normal** — and finally **nerve conduction studies showing a demyelinating sensorimotor polyneuropathy with prolonged distal motor latencies**. As Alice notes, the NCS *proves* nothing new — it **confirms** the demyelinating neuropathy the history and exam already established. The diagnosis is **[[CIDP|CIDP]]** (with a nod to the distal **[[DADS|DADS]]** variant and its anti-MAG association, here negative). She was treated with **[[IVIG|IVIG]]** (no improvement, continued slow progression), then **high-dose steroids** (stabilized without significant improvement, complicated by steroid **hyperglycemia**), with **[[Rituximab|rituximab]]** now under consideration.

Berkowitz closes on the meta-lessons. This case shows **the power of the history** — the sequence "**hands before feet**" settled *demyelinating* before a single test. CIDP is real but **[[Diagnostic Momentum|frequently misdiagnosed]]**: patients with diffuse pain syndromes get labeled from **equivocal EMG findings** and committed to inappropriate immunosuppression, so the updated criteria lean heavily on rigorous NCS parameters, and firm diagnosis ideally documents **CSF inflammation**. Maria's scribe summary retraces the arc: gait as afference/integration/efference (and the syncope alternative), localization to nerve by parsimony, the **axonal (length-dependent, toxic/metabolic) vs demyelinating (non-length-dependent, inflammatory)** split resolved by *hands-first*, tempo (acute = GBS, subacute/chronic = CIDP and cousins), and the first-pass A1C/B12/SPEP.

### Illness Scripts and Bias

- **[[Schema-Based Reasoning|Schema]]: gait as a symphony, and the syncope alternative.** Falls activate a whole-nervous-system differential (vision → cognition → basal ganglia → strength → sensation → cerebellum) *and* a parallel **[[Syncope|syncope]]** schema — the reminder that "falls" may not be a gait problem at all.
- **[[Unifying Diagnosis|Parsimony]] + [[Anatomical Approach|localization]].** "One lesion to explain everything" excludes cortex/brainstem/cord/root for a **[[Stocking-Glove Distribution|stocking-glove]]** pattern and lands on **peripheral nerve** — the localization-first discipline of these VMRs.
- **[[Axonal vs Demyelinating Neuropathy|Axonal vs demyelinating]] — the decisive branch point.** **Length-dependent** (feet-first, toxic/metabolic) vs **non-length-dependent** (hands-and-feet-together, inflammatory), taught with the traffic-jam and paint-on-the-chalk-outline images; **hands-first** here settles *demyelinating* from the history alone.
- **[[Illness Tempo|Tempo]] as discriminator.** Acute = **[[Guillain-Barré Syndrome|GBS]]**, subacute = **[[CIDP|CIDP]]**/paraneoplastic, chronic = **[[Charcot-Marie-Tooth|CMT]]** — the localization × time-course engine applied to neuropathy.
- **[[Test Interpretation|Test interpretation]].** The **A1C/B12/[[SPEP|SPEP-with-immunofixation]]** first pass (SPEP alone inadequate); **[[Nerve Conduction Studies|NCS]]** velocity (demyelinating) vs amplitude (axonal) read as an *extension of the exam*; **[[Albuminocytologic Dissociation|albuminocytologic dissociation]]** on LP (roots in the CSF); the principle that a confirmatory test *confirms* rather than *makes* the diagnosis.
- **[[Base Rate|Base rate]] and don't-miss.** Diabetes is the commonest neuropathy but the pattern is wrong here; the SPEP is almost never positive yet catches the occasional myeloma — probability weighed against morbidity.
- **[[Diagnostic Momentum|Diagnostic momentum]] / misdiagnosis.** CIDP is **frequently over-diagnosed** from equivocal EMGs in pain syndromes, committing patients to inappropriate immunosuppression — hence rigorous NCS criteria and documented CSF inflammation before committing.
- **[[Illness Script|Illness scripts]].** [[CIDP|CIDP]] (chronic cousin of GBS; sensory/motor/sensorimotor and distal **[[DADS|DADS]]** variants), [[Guillain-Barré Syndrome|GBS]], [[Diabetic Neuropathy|diabetic]] (axonal, feet-first), [[Paraneoplastic Neurologic Syndromes|paraneoplastic]] (anti-Hu), and **[[Sensory Ganglionopathy|ganglionopathy]]** (excluded by motor involvement).

## Teaching Discussion

- Decode "falls" as a whole-nervous-system problem — vision, cognition, basal-ganglia pattern-generation, strength, sensation, and cerebellar coordination all feed gait — and always ask whether the falls are actually syncope from autonomic failure, polypharmacy, or orthostasis.
- Localize a stocking-glove sensory (± motor) pattern to peripheral nerve by parsimony: bilateral distal hands and feet cannot be one cortical or brainstem lesion, isn't cord (no bowel/bladder, spares the proximal limb), and isn't painful roots.
- Make axonal-vs-demyelinating the first branch point: length-dependent axonal neuropathies start in the feet and are usually toxic/metabolic (diabetes, B12, chemotherapy), while non-length-dependent demyelinating neuropathies affect hands and feet together with global areflexia and are usually inflammatory — so hands-before-feet points to demyelinating.
- Use tempo to narrow the demyelinating differential: days-to-weeks is GBS, years is CMT, and a subacute months-long course points to inflammatory CIDP and paraneoplastic causes.
- Send the first-pass neuropathy labs — A1C, B12 (with MMA), and SPEP with immunofixation (not SPEP alone) — then expand to a rheumatologic panel, HIV, TSH, and Lyme; screen for an occult paraprotein and, in older patients, paraneoplastic disease.
- Read nerve conduction studies as an extension of the exam: demyelination slows conduction velocity and prolongs distal latencies, axonal loss lowers amplitude; confirm CIDP with albuminocytologic dissociation on LP (the roots traverse the CSF) and treat with IVIG, corticosteroids, plasma exchange, or rituximab.
- Guard against over-diagnosing CIDP: equivocal EMG findings in diffuse pain syndromes are commonly mislabeled, so anchor the diagnosis on rigorous nerve-conduction criteria and documented inflammation before committing a patient to immunosuppression.
