## Pearls

- **A low blood pressure is a number until you reconcile it with the clinical picture and the patient's baseline.** A cachectic elder, a cirrhotic with **[[Splanchnic Vasodilation|splanchnic vasodilation]]**, or an **[[HFrEF|EF of 5%]]** may *live* at a "shock" pressure — so before unleashing a schema, ask whether the number **matches**, check the **baseline** (here a fall from 150s–160s is real), recheck it, and gauge perfusion (mentation, urine, skin, lactate); keep a **low threshold to give fluid** for genuinely significant hypotension — *except* when the picture is cardiogenic.
- **In heart failure there are three realms of hypotension, all the same number but distinguished by volume status.** **[[Arterial Hypotension with Venous Hypertension|Hypotension with venous hypertension]]** (volume-overloaded) is **[[Cardiogenic Shock|cardiogenic shock]] — the worst**; **asymptomatic hypotension with normal volume** is *good* (the low pressure helps the heart heal → continue **[[Guideline-Directed Medical Therapy|GDMT]]**); and **[[Over-Diuresis|hypotension from over-diuresis]]** (hypovolemic) is *not-so-bad* (give volume, cut the diuretic). The **[[Volume Status|volume exam]] (JVP)** is the differentiator.
- **Arterial hypotension *with* venous hypertension is the most anxiety-provoking hypotension — check the JVP first.** An engorged **[[Jugular Venous Pressure|JVP]]** converts "low BP" into a pump problem; **tachypnea with hypoxemia** (unlike the compensatory tachypnea of lactic acidosis, which spares oxygenation) signals **[[Pulmonary Edema|pulmonary edema]]**, and a **[[Lactate|lactate]] of 6** — a tissue-perfusion surrogate — with AKI and transaminases in the thousands (**[[Ischemic Hepatitis|ischemic hepatitis]]**) frames **cardiogenic shock**.
- **Cardiogenic shock inverts the usual hypotension reflex — do not give fluid.** Decongest and support with **[[LMNOP|LMNOP]]** (upright posture, positive pressure, Lasix, nitro/afterload reduction, oxygen — *not* morphine, and correcting hypoxemia to blunt the catecholamine surge that further weakens the heart), add **[[Dobutamine|inotropy]]** for a failing pump, dose the loop diuretic (~20× the creatinine), and escalate to **[[Afterload Reduction|afterload reduction]]** or **[[Ultrafiltration|ultrafiltration]]** in a monitored setting when diuresis fails.
- **GDMT for HFrEF is four pillars, each with a mortality benefit — start them inpatient, because they rarely get started later.** **[[ARNI|ARNI]]** (preferred over ACE/ARB), a **[[Beta Blocker|beta-blocker]]** (metoprolol succinate/carvedilol, uptitrated), an **[[Mineralocorticoid Receptor Antagonist|MRA]]**, and an **[[SGLT2 Inhibitor|SGLT2 inhibitor]]** — begun even at low doses one at a time — whereas **[[Loop Diuretic|Lasix]]** is symptomatic only; and **[[Over-Diuresis|"GDMT over-success"]]** (a recovering EF plus the diuretic effect of ARNI/MRA/SGLT2i) can render the loop diuretic redundant and cause over-diuresis.
- **Don't dismiss a vulnerable patient's pathology as "just the substance."** Every new **[[HFrEF|HFrEF]]** deserves an **[[Ischemic Evaluation|ischemic evaluation]]** (two-thirds of HFrEF is ischemic), and reflexively blaming alcohol (or methamphetamine) risks **[[Don't Blame the Substance|substandard care]]** — as in the pulmonary-hypertension case blamed on meth that was systemic sclerosis; ask **[[Contribution Not Dichotomy|"how much is each factor contributing?"]]**, not "is it this or that?"

## Problem Representation

A middle-aged man with severe alcohol use disorder (and prior severe withdrawal requiring ICU care, prompting care avoidance) is referred from clinic for a blood pressure of 86/62 against a baseline of 150s–160s, with a month of progressive exertional intolerance, orthopnea, 20 pounds of weight gain, and lower-extremity edema. In the emergency department he appears ill — tachypneic (22), hypoxemic (SpO₂ 90%), with an engorged jugular venous pressure, bibasilar crackles, a distended abdomen, and marked edema — with a new potassium of 5.5 and creatinine of 2 (both normal a month earlier), AST 1500/ALT 900 with a mildly elevated bilirubin and INR, an NT-proBNP of ~10,000, a lactate of 6, and a chest radiograph showing cardiomegaly with interstitial edema. Point-of-care ultrasound reveals severe biventricular dysfunction with an ejection fraction of roughly 5% and a plethoric IVC, and coronary angiography excludes ischemic disease, establishing cardiogenic shock from an alcohol-related dilated cardiomyopathy with ischemic hepatitis and cardiorenal AKI. He recovers with decongestion (dobutamine, diuresis, then nitrate afterload reduction) and is initiated on four-pillar guideline-directed medical therapy, but re-presents twice with the same 80s/60s blood pressure in different volume states — asymptomatic euvolemic hypotension (continue therapy) and, after further weight loss and orthostasis, hypovolemic hypotension with AKI from over-diuresis (give volume and stop the loop diuretic) — the case's teaching frame being the three volume-defined realms of hypotension in heart failure.

## Case Narrative

*A three-part heart-failure case (ED → ICU → floor → discharge → back to ED), presented by Rabih and discussed by Reza, built to teach the volume-defined realms of hypotension.*

**Part 1 — cardiogenic shock.** A patient is sent from an upstairs clinic to the ED for a BP of **86/62**. Reza — referencing a recent RLR on chronic hypotension — starts not with a schema but with **reconciliation**: a low pressure is a *number* that may be normal for a cachectic elder, a cirrhotic with **[[Splanchnic Vasodilation|splanchnic vasodilation]]**, or an **[[HFrEF|EF of 5%]]**, so he asks whether it **matches the picture** and the **baseline**, would recheck it, gauge perfusion (lightheadedness, urine, skin), and keep a **low threshold for fluid** — even in heart failure — *pending* the exam. The exam changes everything: the patient looks sick, is **tachypneic (20–22)**, **[[Hypoxemia|SpO₂ 90%]]**, with an **engorged [[Jugular Venous Pressure|JVP]]**, **bibasilar crackles**, a distended abdomen, and marked edema, after a month of decline, 20-lb gain, orthopnea, and severe dyspnea, delayed by **alcohol use** and fear of withdrawal; the **baseline BP is 150s–160s** (so this is real). Reza recognizes **[[Arterial Hypotension with Venous Hypertension|arterial hypotension with venous hypertension]]** — the most ominous hypotension — and reasons the **tachypnea with hypoxemia** (unlike the compensation of lactic acidosis, which spares O₂) points to **[[Pulmonary Edema|pulmonary edema]]** and a widened A–a gradient. Now worried about **[[Cardiogenic Shock|cardiogenic shock]]**, he flips the reflex: *do not give fluid* — decongest. Invoking Gurpreet's **[[LMNOP|LMNOP]]** (Lasix, Morphine [omit], Nitro, Oxygen, Positive pressure/Posture), he keeps the patient **upright on [[BiPAP|BiPAP]]** (correcting hypoxemia to blunt the catecholamine surge), gives **[[Loop Diuretic|IV Lasix]]** (~20× the creatinine), secures IV access, and stands **ready for early pressors**.

Data confirm shock: **EKG** normal sinus, troponin normal, **K 5.5 / Cr 2** (both new), **CXR** cardiomegaly + interstitial edema; then **AST 1500 / ALT 900** with high bilirubin/INR (**[[Ischemic Hepatitis|ischemic hepatitis]]** from hypoperfusion/congestion), **NT-proBNP ~10,000**, and **[[Lactate|lactate 6]]** — a tissue-perfusion surrogate that, like one schistocyte for MAHA, frames the whole picture as **cardiogenic shock**. Lasix 60 yields only 300–400 cc (a **[[Lasix Challenge|failed diuretic challenge]]**), raising **[[Ultrafiltration|ultrafiltration]]**. **[[Cardiogenic Shock|Cardiology]]** (a rarity in this ED outside STEMI) does POCUS: **severe biventricular dysfunction, EF ~5%, plethoric IVC**. In the ICU he is decongested with **[[Dobutamine|dobutamine]]** (inotropy) plus a Lasix drip; his pressure is too low for early **[[Afterload Reduction|afterload reduction]]**, so **[[Afterload Reduction|nitro]]** is added on day 2 once he urinates and his pressure rises. A **cath excludes ischemia** → **[[Alcoholic Cardiomyopathy|alcohol-related cardiomyopathy]]**, and he is started on **[[Guideline-Directed Medical Therapy|GDMT]]**.

Reza teaches the **four pillars** (guideline-, not goal-, directed): **A** — **[[ARNI|ARNI]]** (preferred over ACE/ARB) plus an **[[Mineralocorticoid Receptor Antagonist|MRA]]**; **B** — a **[[Beta Blocker|beta-blocker]]** (metoprolol **succinate**/carvedilol, uptitrated — start low but *start*, because it won't be started outpatient); **C** — an **[[SGLT2 Inhibitor|SGLT2 inhibitor]]** (empagliflozin) — all with **mortality benefit**, whereas **[[Loop Diuretic|Lasix]]** is symptomatic only. He would add them **one at a time**, defer to cardiology on dosing, and stresses the **[[Ischemic Evaluation|ischemic evaluation]]** (two-thirds of HFrEF is ischemic; CT-FFR emerging) — and the bias lesson: **[[Don't Blame the Substance|don't blame everything on alcohol]]** (recalling the pulmonary-hypertension case blamed on meth that proved to be systemic sclerosis), asking **[[Contribution Not Dichotomy|how much each factor contributes]]** rather than "this or that."

**Part 2 — asymptomatic euvolemic hypotension.** Recovered and on the floor (beta-blocker, entresto, spironolactone, empagliflozin), his BP reads **80/61** and a page goes out. With the context now different — no signs of shock — Reza goes to the **lowest-hanging fruit (the meds)** and the **[[Volume Status|volume exam]]**: he'd hold the GDMT if needed and check the **[[Jugular Venous Pressure|JVP]]**. The pressure rechecks at **96**, the patient feels great ("why are three doctors here?"), and he is euvolemic — **asymptomatic hypotension**, the *good* realm, where a low-ish pressure **helps the heart heal**. He is discharged (to alcohol rehab; he stops drinking) on **GDMT + Lasix**.

**Part 3 — hypovolemia from over-diuresis.** He returns to the ED ten days later — **no alcohol ×10 days, adherent to GDMT** — because his home cuff read **81/62**. This time he has been **dizzy/lightheaded for days**, has **lost 7 pounds in a week**, is **profoundly orthostatic**, "**dry as bone**," with a **new AKI** (creatinine risen again, but from the *opposite* direction). This is **[[Over-Diuresis|hypovolemic hypotension from over-diuresis]]** — the third realm — treated with **500 cc of fluid** and **stopping the Lasix**. The mechanism is **[[Guideline-Directed Medical Therapy|GDMT "over-success"]]**: a recovering EF plus the **diuretic/natriuretic effect of ARNI, MRA, and SGLT2i** did the loop diuretic's job, so **Lasix became redundant**.

**The theme.** Rabih crystallizes the arc: **three realms of hypotension**, the *same* 80s/60s number each time, differentiated by **[[Volume Status|volume status]]** — **[[Arterial Hypotension with Venous Hypertension|hypotension with venous hypertension]]** (volume-overloaded → the ICU/cardiogenic-shock realm, *diurese*), **asymptomatic euvolemic hypotension** (the discharge realm, a helpful low pressure), and **[[Over-Diuresis|hypovolemic hypotension]]** (the over-diuresis realm, *give volume*). The differentiator is always the **volume exam**, and the ideal target is **normovolemia with a low-ish pressure that lets the heart heal**. His one change: he wishes they'd told the patient to **hold the Lasix for a >2-lb loss**. Both hosts close on the dual lesson — the elegance of **GDMT** (and its over-success) and the imperative **[[Don't Blame the Substance|not to dismiss a vulnerable patient's disease as "just the substance."]]**

### Illness Scripts and Bias

- **Reconcile the number ([[Base Rate|base rate]]/[[Test Interpretation|test interpretation]]).** A low pressure is data to interpret against **baseline** and **perfusion**, not a schema to fire — but never dismissed, given how central perfusion is.
- **[[Three Realms of Hypotension|Three realms of hypotension]] by [[Volume Status|volume status]].** The organizing insight: identical numbers, opposite management, decided by the **[[Jugular Venous Pressure|volume exam]]** — venous-hypertensive (diurese), euvolemic-asymptomatic (continue), hypovolemic (give volume).
- **[[Arterial Hypotension with Venous Hypertension|Arterial hypotension + venous hypertension]] = [[Cardiogenic Shock|cardiogenic shock]].** Recognized by the JVP; **hypoxemia** (not just tachypnea), **[[Lactate|lactate]]**, **[[Ischemic Hepatitis|ischemic hepatitis]]**, and AKI corroborate — and the reflex to give fluid is **inverted**.
- **[[LMNOP|LMNOP]] and [[Anatomical Approach|hemodynamic reasoning]].** Upright + positive pressure + diuresis + afterload reduction + oxygen (blunting the catecholamine surge), with **[[Dobutamine|inotropy]]** for the pump and **[[Ultrafiltration|ultrafiltration]]** for diuretic failure.
- **[[Guideline-Directed Medical Therapy|GDMT]] and its over-success.** Four mortality-reducing pillars started inpatient (one at a time); **[[Loop Diuretic|Lasix]]** is symptomatic; recovered EF + the diuretic effect of **[[ARNI|ARNI]]/[[Mineralocorticoid Receptor Antagonist|MRA]]/[[SGLT2 Inhibitor|SGLT2i]]** cause **[[Over-Diuresis|over-diuresis]]**.
- **Anti-anchoring on the substance ([[Diagnostic Humility|humility]]).** Every new **[[HFrEF|HFrEF]]** gets an **[[Ischemic Evaluation|ischemic evaluation]]**, and "**[[Don't Blame the Substance|it's just the alcohol]]**" is a bias that harms vulnerable patients — reframed as **[[Contribution Not Dichotomy|contribution, not dichotomy]]**.

## Teaching Discussion

- Treat a low blood pressure as a number to reconcile with the clinical picture and the patient's baseline before applying a schema, recognizing that some patients live at a "shock" pressure, while keeping a low threshold to give fluid for genuinely significant hypotension except when the picture is cardiogenic.
- Organize hypotension in heart failure into three volume-defined realms — hypotension with venous hypertension (cardiogenic shock, the worst), asymptomatic euvolemic hypotension (favorable, a low pressure aiding recovery), and hypovolemic hypotension from over-diuresis — and use the volume exam, especially the JVP, as the differentiator.
- Recognize arterial hypotension with venous hypertension as the most dangerous hypotension, corroborated by hypoxemic tachypnea (pulmonary edema), an elevated lactate, ischemic hepatitis, and cardiorenal AKI, and invert the usual reflex by decongesting rather than giving fluid.
- Manage cardiogenic pulmonary edema with LMNOP (upright posture, positive pressure, loop diuretic dosed to renal function, nitrate afterload reduction, and oxygen to blunt catecholamine surge, omitting morphine), add inotropy for a failing pump, and escalate to afterload reduction or ultrafiltration in a monitored setting when diuresis fails.
- Start the four pillars of guideline-directed medical therapy inpatient — ARNI (preferred over ACE/ARB), a beta-blocker, a mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist, and an SGLT2 inhibitor — even at low doses one at a time, because each reduces mortality and they are rarely initiated afterward, while remembering that loop diuretics are symptomatic only.
- Anticipate GDMT "over-success": a recovering ejection fraction combined with the diuretic effect of ARNI, MRA, and SGLT2 inhibitors can render the loop diuretic redundant and cause hypovolemia, so counsel patients to hold the diuretic for a rapid weight loss and manage over-diuresis with volume and diuretic reduction.
- Evaluate every new HFrEF for ischemia (the cause in roughly two-thirds) and resist attributing cardiomyopathy or other pathology to a substance such as alcohol or methamphetamine, asking how much each factor contributes rather than forcing a single cause, to avoid substandard care in vulnerable patients.
