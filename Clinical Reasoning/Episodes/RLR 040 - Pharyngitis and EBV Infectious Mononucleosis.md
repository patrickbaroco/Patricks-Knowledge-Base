## Pearls

- **Pharyngitis is an *-itis* — reframe it as inflammation and the whole [[I MADE Mnemonic|"I MADE"]] differential opens.** [[Pharyngitis|Sore throat]] can be Infectious, Malignant ([[Lymphoma|lymphoma]]), Autoimmune ([[Adult-Onset Still's Disease|Still's disease]]), Drug ([[DRESS Syndrome|drug-induced hypersensitivity]] or agranulocytosis), or Endocrine ([[Thyroiditis|thyroiditis]]) — but the *practical* engine is a **tiered infectious approach**: Tier 1 non-specific viruses / COVID / influenza vs pyogenic [[Group A Streptococcus|Group A strep]] and oral flora; Tier 2 the mononucleosis viruses ([[Epstein-Barr Virus|EBV]], [[Cytomegalovirus|CMV]], acute [[Acute HIV|HIV]]) plus bacterial STIs.
- **Failure to improve after ~48 hours of a β-lactam is itself a data point — it demotes uncomplicated strep and promotes Tier 2.** Uncomplicated [[Group A Streptococcus|GAS]] should defervesce quickly; persistence means either a *complication* (pyogenic, or toxin-mediated like [[Scarlet Fever|scarlet fever]]) or the wrong tier entirely. Here it was the signal to think mononucleosis.
- **A morbilliform rash after an aminopenicillin in a mono patient is a classic phenomenon — not a penicillin allergy.** The [[Aminopenicillin Rash in EBV|amoxicillin/ampicillin rash of EBV]] is the textbook clue that raises mono; a key reason to *nail the diagnosis* is to avoid mislabeling the patient with a lifelong [[Aminopenicillins|β-lactam]] "allergy" they don't have.
- **When pretest probability is high, a negative screening test barely moves the posterior.** The [[Monospot|monospot / heterophile]] is only ~50% sensitive in week one and ~90% in week two; against a story of pharyngitis + tender node + night-sweats/fever + post-amoxicillin rash, a negative result leaves the mono hypothesis nearly intact ([[Bayesian Reasoning|Bayesian]] reasoning), and specific [[EBV Serologies|EBV serologies]] settle it.
- **[[Base Rate|Base rate]] and phenotype separate the mono viruses.** [[Epstein-Barr Virus|EBV]] vastly outnumbers [[Cytomegalovirus|CMV]], and CMV tends to cause *less severe* pharyngitis — so both prevalence and the severity of the sore throat point to EBV; the isolated [[Lymphocytosis|lymphocytosis]] fits, and youth (<50) plus acute onset make [[Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia|CLL]]/[[Lymphoma|lymphoma]] much less likely.
- **The worth of a test is not only "will it change management" — clarity is its own indication.** The [[Value of a Diagnosis|value of a diagnosis]] is naming the disease and its expected course; here a benign confirmation ("I never thought I'd be so happy with a positive") ends weeks of dread about lymphoma, HIV, or lupus in a man who had watched a relative die young — and [[Collective Reasoning|running the case by a trusted colleague]] is how the reasoning stayed honest.

## Problem Representation

A previously healthy man in his late 30s, no antibiotics in over a decade, with three days of sore throat, drenching night sweats, and nightly fevers to 39°C responsive to acetaminophen. Urgent care documents a Centor-positive exam (no cough, tender anterior cervical lymphadenopathy, fever, tonsillar swelling that becomes exudative) and treats empirically, first with azithromycin and then aminopenicillins (amoxicillin, then amoxicillin-clavulanate) without rapid antigen or culture confirmation. He fails to defervesce over ~48 hours and, after roughly six to seven days of aminopenicillin, develops a diffuse erythematous maculopapular rash of the chest, back, and arms, later spreading to the face with a malar distribution and mild facial swelling. Workup shows an isolated lymphocytosis (WBC ~11,000 with 65–70% lymphocytes, normal hemoglobin and platelets) with negative COVID, RSV, influenza, rapid and PCR streptococcal testing, and HIV, and an initially negative monospot. Against a high pretest probability the leading diagnosis is EBV infectious mononucleosis (favored over CMV by base rate and pharyngitis severity), with a superimposed drug-induced hypersensitivity to amoxicillin as a low-probability second diagnosis and leukemia/lymphoma held as a remote tier — subsequently confirmed as EBV mononucleosis by positive VCA IgM and IgG.

## Case Narrative

The framing is the point of this episode as much as the medicine. It is RLR's first *mobile* recording: Reza catches Rabih on his commute, and the two agree to capture, live, the kind of "microdose" curbside they trade all the time — a still-unfolding case with *no answer yet*, brought precisely because "group thinking is better than any individual thinking." Reza even invites the audience to post their own reasoning on the site. The patient is a childhood friend in his late 30s, previously healthy, who last took antibiotics over a decade ago.

For three days he has had a [[Pharyngitis|sore throat]] with unprompted, drenching [[Night Sweats|night sweats]] ("I wake up in the middle of the night and I literally have to change my shirt") and nightly fevers to 39°C that respond to acetaminophen. Urgent care evaluates him and computes a [[Centor Score|Centor score]]: no cough, a tender anterior [[Cervical Lymphadenopathy|cervical lymph node]] ("half the size of a nickel"), fever, and swollen tonsils — no exudates at first, though exudates later appear. They prescribe azithromycin. Reza, phoned for a curbside, recalculates the Centor himself and puts the friend around a **50% probability** of strep or another bacterial cause (strep variants, or [[Fusobacterium|Fusobacterium]]); he steers him off azithromycin toward [[Aminopenicillins|amoxicillin]], ten days, twice daily. Then comes the episode's most charming move: because no rapid antigen or throat culture was obtained, Reza *runs the case by Dr. Centor himself* — the author of the score — who would simply treat empirically with about seven days of amoxicillin. The friend takes amoxicillin.

Two days later he is still febrile with night sweats and goes to the emergency department, where — he reports — no blood tests are drawn; he is switched from amoxicillin to **[[Aminopenicillins|amoxicillin-clavulanate]]** (Augmentin) and told to wait. About three days into the Augmentin, now roughly six to seven days of aminopenicillin total, he develops a **rash** — diffuse erythematous macules and papules over the chest, back, and arms. This is Reza's first deliberate pause.

Rabih, "brief" only by his own standard, delivers the schema. Pharyngitis fascinates him precisely because it is an *-itis*: an inflammatory problem whose spectrum is all-encompassing, so that every branch of the [[I MADE Mnemonic|"I MADE" mnemonic]] can present as a sore throat — **I**nfection (saved for last because most important), **M**alignancy (namely [[Lymphoma|lymphoma]]), **A**utoimmune (namely [[Adult-Onset Still's Disease|Still's disease]]), **D**rug ([[DRESS Syndrome|drug-induced hypersensitivity syndrome]] and drug-induced agranulocytosis), and **E**ndocrine ([[Thyroiditis|thyroiditis]]). Practically, infections dominate, and he tiers them. **Tier 1**: the non-specific viruses (the vast majority), with special attention to COVID, influenza, and — in unvaccinated populations — measles; against the common pyogenic bacteria, [[Group A Streptococcus|Group A strep]] most famous because most common, plus other Lancefield groups and oral flora. He then makes the case's pivotal inference: **failure to improve on 48 hours of amoxicillin argues against uncomplicated GAS**, which should resolve expediently unless there is a pyogenic or toxin-mediated complication (like [[Scarlet Fever|scarlet fever]], which itself can cause a rash) — so he escalates to **Tier 2**: the mononucleosis viruses **[[Epstein-Barr Virus|EBV]]** (most common, and the one that "classically develops a rash after amoxicillin"), **[[Cytomegalovirus|CMV]]**, and acute **[[Acute HIV|HIV]]**, alongside the bacterial STIs (gonorrhea, chlamydia, syphilis). The rash, he notes, specifically prioritizes **scarlet fever, EBV, or acute HIV**.

Reza had reasoned identically at the bedside-by-phone. Once the rash appeared he told his friend to *stop the antibiotics* — about seven days was ample for uncomplicated strep — but insisted on a proper workup **before blaming the amoxicillin**. He thought mono most likely. He considered [[DRESS Syndrome|DRESS]] and set it aside: the reaction was too early, the last drug exposure was over a decade ago, and there was **no mucosal involvement** (he specifically asked about eyes, oral ulcers beyond the tonsillar exudate, and penile or anal involvement) — a purely cutaneous process. Back at the ED for the rash, Reza's recommended panel was a [[Complete Blood Count|CBC with differential]] (for [[Lymphocytosis|lymphocytosis]]), [[Liver Chemistries|liver chemistries]] (AST/ALT), the [[Monospot|monospot]] and EBV [[EBV Serologies|viral capsid antigen]], CMV, HIV, and a viral swab. Results: COVID negative (tested three or four times), RSV negative, influenza negative, rapid strep negative, PCR strep negative, HIV negative; CBC with normal hemoglobin, WBC ~11,000 with **65–70% lymphocytes** and normal platelets — an **isolated lymphocytosis**. The ED declined to send CMV, liver chemistries, or the VCA, and deferred to primary care. They are now ~9 days from symptom onset.

Rabih calls this "a very compelling case for mononucleosis." The differential of [[Lymphocytosis|lymphocytosis]] includes mono and less common infections such as pertussis; its most alarming member is a lymphoproliferative process like [[Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia|CLL]] — but age under 50 shrinks that considerably, and the abrupt, acute constellation argues against an indolent process masquerading as acute. The very-good-but-imperfect negative HIV test leaves **EBV vs CMV**. Knowing which matters, he says, mostly to **clarify the amoxicillin "allergy" and keep it from being recorded as a true allergy**; beyond that it is somewhat academic. [[Base Rate|Base rate]] (EBV ≫ CMV) and phenotype (CMV causes *less severe* pharyngitis) both favor **EBV**. On testing, he flags the [[Monospot|monospot]]'s sensitivity — ~50% in week one, ~90% in week two — so even now a negative carries a ~10% miss; he would lean hard on EBV and, for a well-known patient, might simply "write it out," expecting improvement in about another week.

Prompted by Reza — who admits he lacks a robust illness script for it — Rabih sketches **[[Scarlet Fever|scarlet fever]]**: the most common cutaneous complication of Group A strep, a characteristically **sandpaper** rash (rough to the palpating hand), far more common in children, appearing *syn-pharyngitically* (during the pharyngitic episode) and one that should *not persist despite optimal antibiotics* — so, here, much less likely, and the lymphocytosis argues further against it.

Then the case turns again. The **[[Monospot|monospot returns negative]]** — but with such a high pretest probability, Reza reasons, that hardly dissuades him; he also flags the honest alternative that the rash may be unrelated to the antibiotic and simply the illness running its natural course. It is nearly **day 14**; the friend has another fever and a *new* facial rash — a **[[Malar Rash|malar distribution with nasolabial sparing]]**, with a subjective sense of facial swelling. This raises the [[Hickam's Dictum|worst case of *two* diagnoses]]: mononucleosis (EBV or CMV) **plus** superimposed [[DRESS Syndrome|drug-induced hypersensitivity]] from amoxicillin — exceedingly low probability, but the facial rash/swelling plus a lymphocytosis (which DRESS can also produce) earns it a little more space. Asked what he would actually order, Rabih gives the tiered answer: stop the amoxicillin; check [[Liver Chemistries|liver enzymes]] and a renal panel (to exclude significant hepatitis or nephritis); send **CMV IgM** and **advanced [[EBV Serologies|EBV serologies]]** for diagnostic clarity; and watch closely. The island-minimum is simply to stop the drug and observe; the high-resource standard adds the enzymes, kidney assessment, and serologies. Reza adds an LDH and complement ("superbly paranoid," screening the photosensitive-looking rash), sets a Friday primary-care appointment, and gives clear return precautions — shortness of breath or chest pain back to an ED, but a *different* one.

Reza then turns the lens on himself (Rabih's prompt: "what reps did you get?"). What he treasures is applying the [[Centor Score|Centor score]] and then *talking to Centor*; the antibiotic-choice teaching (why not azithromycin for strep); and a live, honestly-held tiered differential — **Tier 1 mono (EBV/CMV), Tier 2 autoimmune** (less likely in a man), **Tier 3 leukemia/lymphoma**, where he wants someone to *look at the cells* (lymphocytes vs blasts) even though the count is only mildly elevated. Rabih is hopeful the illness will "come to an abrupt arrest very soon."

The coda is a second mini-recording. The friend's results return: **EBV VCA IgM and IgG both positive → infectious mononucleosis due to EBV, confirmed.** Rabih's reflection is the teaching spine: the *relief* at diagnosing an "incredibly benign condition" shows that the **[[Value of a Diagnosis|value of a test]]** is not only whether it changes management — clarity on *what is going on* and *what to expect* is itself of profound importance. As Reza puts it, no one could have disproven lymphoma, HIV, or lupus in a man with weeks of rash, night sweats, and fevers and a family history of early death; the named diagnosis is what ends the dread. "I never thought I'd be so happy with a positive." The friend is fatigued but reassured, the rash slowly fading, and Reza plans a follow-up video on recognizing mono earlier and on counseling — contagiousness, return to work, and the complications to watch.

### Illness Scripts and Bias

- **[[Schema-Based Reasoning|Schema]]: pharyngitis as inflammation.** Rabih's [[I MADE Mnemonic|"I MADE"]] frame (Infection, Malignancy, Autoimmune, Drug, Endocrine) prevents a common complaint from collapsing prematurely to "strep vs not," and the **tiered infectious sub-schema** (Tier 1 non-specific viral / COVID-flu / pyogenic bacteria → Tier 2 mono viruses + STIs) gives an ordered escalation path when Tier 1 fails.
- **[[Test Interpretation|Test interpretation]] as the through-line.** The [[Centor Score|Centor score]] is used as designed — and then pressure-tested by consulting its *author* — while the [[Monospot|monospot]]'s week-dependent sensitivity, the imperfect HIV screen, and the distinction between rapid antigen and PCR strep are all handled as probabilities, not verdicts.
- **[[Bayesian Reasoning|Bayesian]] handling of a negative test.** A high pretest probability (classic mono story + post-amoxicillin rash) means a negative monospot leaves the posterior high; the correct next step is specific [[EBV Serologies|serology]], not abandonment of the hypothesis — the antidote to treating a screening test as a rule-out.
- **[[Base Rate|Base rate]] + phenotype to split look-alikes.** EBV over CMV (prevalence and more-severe pharyngitis); youth and acuity over CLL/lymphoma for the lymphocytosis — base rate doing the discriminating work once the syndrome is fixed.
- **[[Hickam's Dictum|Hickam's dictum]] — the "two diagnoses" worst case.** The late malar rash and facial swelling force explicit consideration that the patient carries *both* mono and a drug-induced hypersensitivity; naming the low-probability second diagnosis (rather than forcing everything into one) is the [[Unifying Diagnosis|Occam]]-vs-Hickam tension applied in real time.
- **Illness-script gaps, owned.** Reza names his thin [[Scarlet Fever|scarlet fever]] script; Rabih fills it (sandpaper, syn-pharyngitic, pediatric-predominant, should not persist on antibiotics) — modeling that a missing script is a prompt to consult, not to guess.
- **The aminopenicillin-rash pitfall.** The morbilliform eruption is a *reason to diagnose mono*, and diagnosing it **de-labels** a spurious lifelong β-lactam allergy — a management consequence hidden inside a "benign" answer.
- **[[Value of a Diagnosis|Reasons to test beyond changing management]], and [[Collective Reasoning|collective cognition]].** The closing insight reframes testing: clarity and natural history are legitimate indications, and the whole episode is an argument for the *curbside network* — "group thinking is better than any individual thinking" — as a reasoning instrument.

## Teaching Discussion

- Treat pharyngitis as inflammation and carry the "I MADE" differential (Infection, Malignancy, Autoimmune, Drug, Endocrine), then run the practical infectious tiers: Tier 1 non-specific viruses / COVID / influenza vs pyogenic Group A strep; Tier 2 the mononucleosis viruses (EBV, CMV, acute HIV) and bacterial STIs. Escalate tiers when the common causes don't explain the course.
- Read "failure to improve on a β-lactam" as information. Uncomplicated Group A strep defervesces quickly; persistence means a complication (pyogenic or toxin-mediated) or the wrong tier — a prompt to reconsider mononucleosis and its cousins rather than to broaden antibiotics reflexively.
- Recognize the aminopenicillin rash of EBV as a diagnostic clue, not an allergy. A morbilliform eruption days into amoxicillin/ampicillin in a mono patient should raise EBV; confirming mono prevents a lifelong, incorrect penicillin-allergy label.
- Interpret the monospot by the calendar and the pretest probability. Heterophile sensitivity is ~50% in week one and ~90% in week two; against a strong clinical story a negative result should not overturn the diagnosis — send specific EBV serologies (VCA IgM/IgG, EBNA) for clarity.
- Use base rate and phenotype to separate the mono viruses and to rank the dangerous mimics: EBV over CMV (commoner, more severe pharyngitis), and age <50 plus acute onset to push CLL/lymphoma down — while still planning to examine the lymphocyte morphology (lymphocytes vs blasts) if the illness persists.
- Hold Hickam's dictum ready when new features don't fit. A late malar rash with facial swelling justifies naming a second, low-probability diagnosis (superimposed drug-induced hypersensitivity), checking liver enzymes and a renal panel, and stopping the culprit drug rather than forcing one unifying label.
- Remember why we test. A diagnosis is worth pursuing for clarity and prognosis, not only because it changes management; a benign, named answer can be profoundly reassuring — and a trusted curbside network is a legitimate tool for keeping the reasoning honest.
