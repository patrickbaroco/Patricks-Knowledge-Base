## Pearls

- **When a patient doesn't know why they're there: vitals, a few questions, then find the sender.** Someone flagged them for a reason; track down the caregiver/referring source. Meanwhile, screen the vitals and a brief history for red flags. ([[RLR 065 - Orthopnea and Compressive Goiter|RLR 065]])
- **Rest is not exertion — a comfortable patient at rest can hide cardiopulmonary disease.** [[Dyspnea|Dyspnea]] is hard to judge in someone sitting in a wheelchair; challenge the system (walk them) or catch them in the provoking state. Here the diagnosis only appeared when he was **laid flat**. ([[RLR 065 - Orthopnea and Compressive Goiter|RLR 065]])
- **[[Orthopnea|Orthopnea]] has a differential — it is not synonymous with heart failure.** [[Base Rate|Base rate]] is left-heart failure (and **PND is the more cardiac-specific symptom**), but orthopnea also comes from raised **intrathoracic** pressure (COPD), raised **intra-abdominal** pressure (ascites splinting the diaphragm), and **diaphragm/neuromuscular** disease along the cervical-cord → phrenic-nerve → muscle axis. Prioritize the heart, then pivot if JVP/edema/lungs are clean. ([[RLR 065 - Orthopnea and Compressive Goiter|RLR 065]])
- **Absent congestion signs do not exclude heart failure.** ~30% of chronic HF patients lack peripheral edema (lymphatics compensate), crackles can be absent, and JVP assessment is operator- and patient-dependent — so a clean exam lowers but doesn't erase HF. ([[RLR 065 - Orthopnea and Compressive Goiter|RLR 065]])
- **The *timing* of a cough localizes it: instant-on-lying-down favors aspiration/airway over reflux or HF.** A cough that fires the moment the patient is supine points to an airway/aspiration trigger; reflux and heart-failure cough are slower to build. ([[RLR 065 - Orthopnea and Compressive Goiter|RLR 065]])
- **Work external tracheal (hollow-viscus) compression by the [[Mass Lesion Schema|lumen / wall / outside]] schema, then the mass categories.** Borrowing Lindsay Sipley's biliary-obstruction framework and Jack Penner's [[Mediastinal Mass|mediastinal-mass]] figure: distinguish intraluminal, mural, and **extrinsic** compression, then sort an extrinsic mass into **abnormal growth of normal tissue** (e.g., [[Goiter|goiter]]), **infection** (abscess), or **cancer** (thyroid, lymphoma, other). A chronic course with no systemic signs points **benign**. ([[RLR 065 - Orthopnea and Compressive Goiter|RLR 065]])
- **Put the specific history on the imaging request.** Ordering a CXR for "shortness of breath" under-primes the radiologist; the specific story (positional cough) is what surfaced the abnormal trachea on re-read. The history was, genuinely, the diagnosis. ([[RLR 065 - Orthopnea and Compressive Goiter|RLR 065]])

## Problem Representation

An older male veteran with hypertension and hyperlipidemia (on lisinopril and a statin), wheelchair-bound for chronic back pain, presents at his caregiver's urging with six months of dyspnea (worse over two weeks); he is well-appearing and comfortable at rest with entirely normal vital signs. When laid supine he develops visible orthopnea and a violent, immediate cough with a stridorous quality, while the cardiac and pulmonary exam shows no JVP elevation, no peripheral edema, and clear lungs; EKG, BNP, and the initial chest x-ray read are normal. A specifically-informed re-read of the chest x-ray reveals an abnormal, focally narrowed trachea, and CT demonstrates external tracheal compression from a massive, non-palpable, euthyroid goiter — a benign positional airway obstruction, managed with upright positioning pending endocrine-surgical debulking.

## Case Narrative

Rabih, freshly back from Japan, presents a well-appearing older veteran wheeled in by his caregiver, wearing a cowboy hat, who cheerfully says he has no idea why he's there and feels fine. Reza's approach to the **patient who doesn't know why they came**: look at the vitals first, ask a few general questions, and — if no red flags — **find who sent him**, because someone flagged a reason. The caregiver is parking; in the meantime the vitals are all normal and the man is in no distress. Prompted, he reports his caregiver noticed he "can't quite catch his breath" sometimes; a glance at the CPRS cover sheet shows hypertension, hyperlipidemia, lisinopril, statin, and a vitamin. The one useful HPI detail: **six months** of dyspnea, worse the last couple of weeks.

Reza frames it as a basic older-adult [[Dyspnea|dyspnea]] workup. He flags the key limitation — **dyspnea is hard to assess in a seated, resting patient**; you must challenge the cardiopulmonary system (walk them) to unmask it. The **[[Illness Tempo|six-month tempo]]** is reassuring against the hyperacute vascular killers (PE, ACS, dissection). By [[Base Rate|base rate]], ~95% of dyspnea is heart or lung, so he wants an **EKG, chest x-ray**, and a focused exam (lungs, JVP, legs), plus the category he says he and Rabih always forget — **[[Anemia|anemia]]**. (An aside: the man walked from the parking lot fine; the wheelchair is for chronic back pain.)

The pivot is a **[[Return to the Bedside|bedside maneuver]]**. To gown and reposition him, the team lays the bed flat — and the moment he's supine he becomes markedly short of breath. Now facing **visible [[Orthopnea|orthopnea]]**, Reza reaches for the orthopnea schema he attributes to the Rezigh twins (Alec and Austin): orthopnea is *not* automatically cardiac. **PND is the more cardiac-specific symptom**; orthopnea, while most often left-heart failure, also arises from raised **intrathoracic** pressure (COPD), raised **intra-abdominal** pressure (ascites splinting the diaphragm), and **diaphragm/neuromuscular** disease along the cervical-cord → phrenic-nerve → diaphragm-muscle axis. So he prioritizes the left heart (JVP, edema, nocturia, weight trend) but is ready to pivot. On exam, though, there is **no edema, no JVP even up close, and clear lungs** — and he correctly cautions that this does not exclude HF (≈30% of chronic HF lacks edema; lymphatics clear pulmonary fluid; JVP is unreliable), keeping HF at number one while noting the data have shifted.

The **caregiver** returns and supplies the case-cracking history: for two months, **every time he lies down** he gets short of breath and coughs — a **violent, hacking, immediate cough**. Reza seizes on the **timing**: a cough that fires *instantly* on lying supine prioritizes an **airway/aspiration** trigger over reflux or HF (both slower), recalling a Hopkins pulmonologist's teaching that the latency of the cough distinguishes reflux vs HF vs aspiration. He orders CXR, BNP, BMP, and CBC. **BNP is normal; the official CXR read is normal.** But Rabih — who admits he unhelpfully wrote only "shortness of breath" on the order — spots an **abnormal trachea** himself (normal caliber, then narrowed, then widening) and calls the radiologist, who agrees on re-read. The lesson lands: the **specific history on the requisition** is what surfaces the finding, and being primed by the *cough* is what made both of them scrutinize the airway (Reza had even inspected the EKG for COPD signs — low limb-lead-I and precordial-V6 voltages).

CT is the obvious next step to characterize tissue, and it shows **external compression of the trachea** (confirmed by a positional, stridorous sound on supine auscultation; the scan itself was hard to obtain because he coughed lying flat). Reza works the **[[Mass Lesion Schema|extrinsic-compression differential]]**, explicitly borrowing **Lindsay Sipley's** hollow-viscus/biliary-obstruction schema (is the problem in the **lumen**, the **wall**, or **outside** compressing?) and **Jack Penner's** [[Mediastinal Mass|mediastinal-mass]] figure. Reasoning that the trachea is a relatively dense structure requiring a substantive mass, and that the process is **endogenous** (not an inhaled foreign body in the lumen), he sorts extrinsic masses into **abnormal growth of normal tissue**, **infection** (abscess — argued down by six months without any inflammatory signal), and **cancer** (thyroid, lymphoma without B symptoms, other solid tumors — argued down by the chronic, indolent, systemically-silent course). His bet: a **benign** mass causing pure mass effect.

The reveal: a **massive [[Goiter|goiter]]**, growing substernally so that it was **completely non-palpable**, pushing the trachea to the right — with a **normal TSH and T4** (euthyroid). The man, relieved, insists "I told you it was nothing." Management is **upright positioning** pending **endocrine surgery** for debulking. In the [[Cognitive Autopsy|debrief]], Reza is proud precisely because goiter is off his hospitalist radar — he reached it by **[[Analogical Reasoning|analogy]]** (Lindsay's mass schema, the "abnormal growth of normal tissue" category, regional anatomy) rather than reflex, and links it to other **positional** physiology (an atrial mass/PFO shunting when supine — platypnea-orthodeoxia). Both close on how the **caregiver's history changed everything**: without it, the man would have been discharged and the diagnosis lost.

## Reasoning Breakdown

### Bayesian Updates

| Data point | Hypothesis raised/adjusted | Direction of update |
|---|---|---|
| Well-appearing, normal vitals, comfortable at rest | Acute cardiopulmonary emergency | Lowered (but rest ≠ exertion) |
| 6 months dyspnea, worse 2 weeks | Hyperacute vascular (PE/ACS/dissection) | Lowered (tempo reassuring) |
| Older, HTN/HLD, dyspnea | Heart failure (base-rate first) | Raised |
| Laid flat → visible orthopnea | Left heart failure (orthopnea schema) | Raised, then broadened |
| No JVP, no edema, clear lungs | Congestive heart failure | Lowered (not excluded) |
| Normal BNP; normal official CXR read | Heart failure | Lowered further |
| Violent cough *instantly* on lying supine | Aspiration / airway process | Raised |
| Re-read CXR: focally abnormal trachea | Airway lesion | Strongly raised |
| CT: external tracheal compression | Extrinsic mass (lumen/wall/outside) | Confirmed extrinsic |
| No systemic/inflammatory signs; chronic indolent course | Infection (abscess); aggressive cancer | Lowered → benign mass |
| Non-palpable mass; normal TSH/T4 | Substernal euthyroid goiter | Confirmed |

### Illness Scripts and Bias

- **[[Schema-Based Reasoning|Schemas that resist the reflex]].** The orthopnea schema (cardiac / intrathoracic / intra-abdominal / diaphragm-neuromuscular) and the extrinsic-mass schema (lumen/wall/outside; abnormal-growth vs infection vs cancer) are what let Reza move past "orthopnea = heart failure" and past "mass = cancer."
- **[[Base Rate|Base rate held, then updated]].** He anchors on HF (correctly, by base rate) and explicitly refuses to let a clean exam or normal BNP prematurely exclude it — while staying alert enough to pivot when the airway data arrive.
- **[[Return to the Bedside|The provoking maneuver]].** The diagnosis was invisible at rest and only declared itself supine; reproducing the physiology (and later tolerating a hard, cough-interrupted CT) was the decisive act. Rest is not exertion.
- **[[Analogical Reasoning|Analogy did the heavy lifting]].** Goiter was off the hospitalist radar; it was reached by transporting Lindsay Sipley's hollow-viscus schema and Jack Penner's mediastinal-mass figure to the trachea, plus the "abnormal growth of normal tissue" mass category — a model of cross-domain [[Anatomical Approach|anatomical]] reasoning.
- **The history as the test.** A single caregiver detail (violent cough the instant he lies down) reweighted the entire case toward the airway and, via a specifically-worded re-read request, surfaced the finding — a reminder that the requisition's history changes what the radiologist sees.
- **[[Illness Tempo|Tempo → benignity]].** Six months of positional symptoms without systemic features argues against infection and aggressive malignancy and toward a slow, benign, purely mechanical process.

## Teaching Discussion

- For the patient who doesn't know why they came, check vitals and a brief history for red flags, then track down the person who sent them; the reason is usually with the caregiver or referring source.
- Remember that a patient comfortable at rest may still have significant cardiopulmonary disease; assess dyspnea under provocation (exertion or the provoking position) rather than at rest alone.
- Treat orthopnea as having a differential — left heart failure by base rate, but also raised intrathoracic pressure (COPD), raised intra-abdominal pressure (ascites), and diaphragm/neuromuscular disease — and recall that PND is the more cardiac-specific symptom.
- Do not exclude heart failure on a clean exam: roughly a third of chronic HF patients lack peripheral edema, crackles may be absent, and JVP is operator-dependent.
- Localize a cough by its timing: an immediate cough on lying supine favors an airway or aspiration process over reflux or heart failure.
- Work external tracheal (and any hollow-viscus) compression by the lumen/wall/outside schema, then sort an extrinsic mass into abnormal growth of normal tissue, infection, or cancer — and let a chronic, systemically-silent course point toward a benign cause.
- Put the specific clinical history on imaging requisitions; the right context changes what the radiologist looks for and reports.
- Keep positional physiology in mind (orthopnea, platypnea-orthodeoxia, positional shunts and compressions) as a distinct diagnostic lens.
