## Pearls

- **Elevated [[Aminotransferases|AST/ALT]] almost always means the liver by [[Base Rate|base rate]], but pause for the extrahepatic sources — muscle, heart, and red cells — before you commit.** ALT (the one with the "L") is more liver-specific *and* has a longer half-life, so as an injury ages the ALT overtakes the AST and lingers; an AST higher than ALT, or a remote muscle/cardiac/hemolytic event, keeps you out of the liver. Reach for a hemoglobin, and consider a CK and cardiac review of systems, to shut the extrahepatic door.
- **[[Illness Tempo|Tempo]] and dynamism separate parenchymal liver injury from "plumbing."** Up to half of acute liver injury is a plumbing problem — either drainage (a stone that lodges then pops open, transiently spiking transaminases) or inflow (a perfusion drop causing [[Ischemic Hepatitis|ischemic hepatitis]]). Plumbing *bounces and dances*; a flat, persistently elevated line over days points into the [[Acute Liver Injury|parenchyma]] itself — though the base rate is high enough that a right-upper-quadrant [[Right Upper Quadrant Ultrasound|ultrasound with Doppler]] is still worth it.
- **A [[Sinus Tachycardia|sinus tachycardia]] collapses to three buckets: impending/actual shock, sympathetic toxicity, or fever — and you assume shock until proven otherwise.** Shock is hypovolemic, distributive, cardiogenic, or obstructive; sympathetic toxicity is exogenous (cocaine, caffeine, albuterol, benzodiazepine withdrawal, serotonin syndrome) or endogenous (pain/anxiety, pheochromocytoma, hyperthyroidism, dysautonomia). This reframes a "40-item differential" into two or three moves.
- **Build a [[Liver Disease in Pregnancy|liver-in-pregnancy schema]] and let trimester weight it.** Branch it into disease *modified* by pregnancy (flaring or unmasked [[Autoimmune Hepatitis|autoimmune hepatitis]], reactivated hepatitis B/C), disease *unique* to pregnancy ([[Acute Fatty Liver of Pregnancy|AFLP]], [[Intrahepatic Cholestasis of Pregnancy|ICP]], [[HELLP Syndrome|HELLP]] and [[Preeclampsia|preeclampsia]]), and the single most common association — estrogen-driven gallstones. The morbid, hypertension-linked entities (AFLP, HELLP, preeclampsia) cluster in the third trimester, which makes them unlikely at 15 weeks.
- **[[Intrahepatic Cholestasis of Pregnancy|ICP]] does not read like the word "cholestasis": pruritus (classically palms and soles) with prominently elevated *transaminases*, not the alkaline-phosphatase-and-bilirubin signature you expect.** The confirmatory test is the serum total bile acid level — here 109 against a reference near 8 — which can take over a week to return, and a history of ICP in a prior pregnancy is a powerful prior. Treatment is [[Ursodeoxycholic Acid|ursodiol]] with obstetric co-management.
- **Outside your domain, name the gap and lower your threshold to ask.** Reza treats a pregnant patient as caring for two people, runs even a cough syrup past a pharmacist, and calls the obstetrician early — recognizing that a consultant's minutes can outperform hours of UpToDate, and that a curious, non-hierarchical posture makes the conversation better. Knowing what you don't know beats not knowing it.

## Problem Representation

A 15-week-pregnant woman (G3P2, two prior uncomplicated vaginal deliveries) with a history of hypertension — on amlodipine and aspirin for preeclampsia prophylaxis — presents with cough and is admitted with mild-to-moderate COVID-19 not requiring oxygen. A comprehensive metabolic panel shows a hepatocellular pattern (ALT 538, AST 275, alkaline phosphatase 180, total bilirubin 1.0, albumin 3.9, total protein 8.3) that persists flatly over a five-day hospitalization; CK, troponin, INR, platelets, and hemoglobin are normal, and a transient day-one leukocytosis (13,000) resolves. She is afebrile with a low-grade sinus tachycardia, a soft, mildly distended, non-tender abdomen without stigmata of cirrhosis or jaundice, a productive cough, CT-confirmed multifocal consolidations from COVID-19, and pruritus of the hands and feet. Viral hepatitis serologies are non-reactive (CMV IgG positive, reflecting past exposure), and a right-upper-quadrant ultrasound with Doppler shows no vascular abnormality, no biliary dilation or stone, and an incidental absent gallbladder (prior cholecystectomy). A markedly elevated serum total bile acid level (109; reference ~<8) establishes intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy, corroborated by the same diagnosis in her prior pregnancy; she is treated and discharged well.

## Case Narrative

After the customary palindrome banter ("Reza loves Robbie / Robbie loves Reza"), Rabih's news that he has become a trail runner heading for the North Bay, and a brief reflection from Reza on the January 6th Capitol attack, Reza sets up a deliberately inverted case: rather than opening with the age and sex, he will hand Rabih the abnormal lab first and work backward — a "Benjamin Button," reverse presentation whose rationale he promises to reveal once they reach the patient's age. The seed is a single CMP drawn in the emergency department: ALT 538, AST 275, alkaline phosphatase 180, total bilirubin 1.0, albumin 3.9, total protein 8.3.

Rabih begins by resisting the reflex to call these "liver enzymes." The [[Aminotransferases|aminotransferases]] (AST and ALT) are, in his framing, enzymes crucial to many tissues — primarily the liver, but also muscle, heart, and red blood cells — so their elevation announces only that *some* cell is damaged, and the diagnostic question is which. He localizes toward the liver on two grounds: the ALT (the enzyme "with the L") is more liver-specific than the AST, and the [[Base Rate|base rate]] favors the liver among the candidate tissues. Ordinarily that pull is reinforced when the alkaline phosphatase and bilirubin are also up; here they are essentially normal, so he must keep asking whether an extrahepatic source is in play. Two clues would push harder toward the liver — an ALT above the AST (present) and elevations in alkaline phosphatase and bilirubin (absent) — but even the reassuring ALT-over-AST pattern carries a caveat: because the ALT has a *long half-life*, a remote injury to muscle, heart, or red cells weeks ago could have started with a higher AST that has since been overtaken by a lingering ALT. He therefore wants a hemoglobin and would consider a CK and a cardiac review of systems, while keeping the focus on the liver. He then adds the [[Illness Tempo|tempo]] argument that this is almost certainly acute-to-subacute: sustaining transaminases this high for a chronic period is difficult (cirrhotics rarely run this high without an acute-on-chronic insult), and prolonged severe injury would have dragged down the bilirubin and alkaline phosphatase. The albumin (half-life ~21 days) is a weak, non-specific clue that something has been brewing — but at 3.9 it is normal here.

Reza teases the "acute-to-subacute" hedge — a 66% chance of being right when the only other option is chronic — and Rabih confesses he actually favors acute. Reza then supplies more data while still withholding the demographics: the CK and troponin are normal, and the INR, albumin, platelets, and hemoglobin are all normal; a day-one leukocytosis of 13,000 normalized; and, importantly, the AST and ALT remained *flatly* elevated across the five-day hospitalization. On exam she has a low-grade tachycardia with normal oxygenation and work of breathing, an audible cough, a soft and only mildly distended non-tender abdomen without right-upper-quadrant pain, no fever, no jaundice, and no stigmata of cirrhosis.

Rabih reads the new data in sequence. The normal CK, troponin, hemoglobin, and platelet count confirm the disease is in the liver and, with no thrombocytopenia, push toward an acute rather than chronic process. He then broadens the [[Acute Liver Injury|acute liver injury]] schema to emphasize "plumbing" alongside infections and toxins: drainage plumbing (an acute stone that lodges and pops open the hepatocytes before any cholestatic alkaline-phosphatase/bilirubin pattern develops) and inflow plumbing (a perfusion drop producing [[Ischemic Hepatitis|ischemic hepatitis]]). The kinetics adjudicate: plumbing problems are intermittent — the stone blocks and dislodges, perfusion is compromised then restored — so a *bouncing and dancing* transaminase pattern suggests plumbing, whereas a flat, persistently elevated line suggests the parenchyma. Up to half of acute liver injury is a plumbing issue, so despite the lack of fluctuation he would still obtain a [[Right Upper Quadrant Ultrasound|right-upper-quadrant ultrasound with Doppler]]; the odds, though, favor a parenchymal process.

He then addresses the two incidental findings. The [[Sinus Tachycardia|sinus tachycardia]] he handles with a reflection he says came to him on a long run: rather than a differential of forty or fifty items, a sinus tachycardia is really just impending/actual *shock* (hypovolemic, distributive, cardiogenic, or obstructive), *sympathetic toxicity*, or *fever*. Sympathetic toxicity is exogenous — a drug that drives catecholamines (cocaine), a withdrawal state that unmasks them (benzodiazepines), caffeine, albuterol, or an idiosyncratic reaction such as serotonin syndrome — or endogenous — acute pain or anxiety, pheochromocytoma, hyperthyroidism, or a dysautonomia. She is afebrile, so he pivots to assume shock physiology until proven otherwise and will watch whether the heart rate resolves with fluids or reflects admission anxiety. The [[Abdominal Distention|abdominal distention]] he reduces to an accumulation of solid, liquid, or gas: tympany for gas is detectable, ascites less reliably so; the overwhelmingly common cause is benign adipose tissue, the other non-pathologic cause is pregnancy, and organomegaly or a mass is possible but often turns out to be noise. Finally, the cough: the base rate of cough is high enough that it may be a chronic, unrelated finding, or it may localize the case as a liver-plus-lung problem — to be decided by whether evidence builds that the cough represents significant, acute respiratory pathology, with COVID looming as the obvious candidate.

Reza then delivers the reveal: she is a 15-week-pregnant woman, young, with two prior uncomplicated vaginal deliveries. Her right-upper-quadrant ultrasound with Doppler shows no vascular abnormality, no biliary dilation, and no common-bile-duct stone — and, as an incidental surprise never volunteered in the history, an absent gallbladder from a prior cholecystectomy. A CT pulmonary angiogram shows multifocal consolidations, and a COVID-19 nucleic acid amplification test is positive; she is admitted with mild-to-moderate COVID-19 not requiring oxygen, yet with these persistently abnormal liver chemistries. She has no nausea, vomiting, or (initially reported) itch.

Rabih integrates: the cough and CT findings are explained by COVID-19, and the ultrasound excludes a plumbing problem. Confronting the pregnancy, he first flags the temptation of a lung-plus-liver overlap — a genuinely fascinating category (TB, Q fever, Legionella can travel from lung to liver) — but then, in an explicit act of [[Problem Refinement|problem refinement]], tosses the lung out: the lung is COVID and does not explain the liver, so the authentic question is *liver alone versus liver-plus-pregnancy*. He lays out a [[Liver Disease in Pregnancy|liver-in-pregnancy schema]]. Pregnancy can *modify* pre-existing disease — its immunologic shifts can flare or remit autoimmune conditions and can reactivate or worsen viral hepatitis — so he considers unmasked or flaring [[Autoimmune Hepatitis|autoimmune hepatitis]] and hepatitis B/C, prioritized by the pregnancy. Pregnancy also brings *unique* diseases: [[Acute Fatty Liver of Pregnancy|acute fatty liver of pregnancy]] (a devastating, near-alcoholic-hepatitis-like accumulation of fat with liver failure and portal hypertension, typically third-trimester), [[Intrahepatic Cholestasis of Pregnancy|intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy]] (intense pruritus), and — prompted by Reza's mid-discussion correction that she in fact has itching of the hands and feet — [[HELLP Syndrome|HELLP]] (hemolysis, elevated liver enzymes, low platelets; ~26 weeks and beyond). The single most common association, he notes, is the estrogenic effect on the gallbladder producing gallstones. Liver alone, by contrast, would return him to infectious and toxin-mediated hepatitis (with an acetaminophen level) and to metabolic causes such as [[Wilson Disease|Wilson disease]]. He asks whether COVID itself could explain the transaminases — plausibly, though the height of the elevation prompts a search for another cause — and whether COVID *therapy* could (Reza confirms [[Remdesivir|remdesivir]] can cause acute liver injury), noting she was not yet on treatment.

Reza then narrates the real workup. Viral serologies were non-reactive for hepatitis A, B, and C; CMV IgG was positive, reflecting remote exposure rather than a compatible mononucleosis syndrome. Recognizing the limits of his obstetric knowledge — "my n for taking care of pregnant patients is two," and now framing the case as caring for two people, mother prioritized — he set a very low threshold to involve obstetrics, and in the interim consulted UpToDate, which recommended exactly what Rabih proposed. He first excluded the emergencies (AFLP, HELLP, preeclampsia/eclampsia), which the obstetrician noted occur later in pregnancy and which were argued against by the absence of hypertension, proteinuria, and hemolysis; she was already on aspirin (with amlodipine) for preeclampsia prophylaxis given her hypertension. He learned that intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy — despite the word "cholestasis" — can elevate the AST and ALT prominently rather than showing the alkaline-phosphatase/bilirubin signature he associated with the term. On the obstetrician's suggestion he sent a serum total bile acid level (which he had not known could be ordered, and which took over a week to return); against a reference near 8, hers was 109. When he spoke with the patient again, she recalled having been diagnosed with intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy in a prior pregnancy and treated for it. The diagnosis was intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy; she was discharged and doing well, with Reza planning to confirm management with obstetrics. He closes by naming the reason for the reversed presentation: he wanted Rabih first in a comfortable domain, then pushed into an unfamiliar one to model the discomfort — and the heightened caution and humility — that pregnancy demanded of him. Rabih agrees that his own lack of knowledge visibly surfaced (the audible pause when he began reaching for esoteric lung-liver diagnoses), and reflects that the trigger to call for help matters less than the manner: approaching the consultant as a curious, enthusiastic learner, rather than through a hierarchy of deference, makes the exchange better for everyone and can compress hours of solo reading into a far higher-quality conversation.

### Illness Scripts and Bias

- **[[Base Rate|Base-rate reasoning]] applied with a discipline against over-anchoring.** Rabih uses the base rate to localize transaminases to the liver, but he refuses to let it foreclose the extrahepatic sources — muscle, heart, red cells — until CK, troponin, and hemoglobin come back normal. The pearl is base rate as a *starting posture* that still demands disconfirmation, not a stopping rule; the long ALT half-life is exactly the kind of detail that keeps a remote extrahepatic injury alive despite an otherwise "liver-looking" pattern.
- **[[Illness Tempo|Tempo]] and dynamism as the discriminator between parenchyma and plumbing.** The flat five-day course, read against the intermittent physiology of stones and perfusion, is what moves Rabih from "plumbing" to "parenchymal." This is [[Bayesian Reasoning|Bayesian]] use of the *shape* of a trend rather than a single value — a bouncing enzyme is a different hypothesis than a plateau.
- **[[Schema-Based Reasoning|Schema over recall]] for high-entropy findings.** Faced with two potentially overwhelming problems — sinus tachycardia and abdominal distention — Rabih deploys compact schemas (shock/sympathetic-toxicity/fever; solid/liquid/gas) that convert a "40-item differential" into a few moves. The liver-in-pregnancy schema (modified-by-pregnancy vs unique-to-pregnancy vs gallstones) is the frame that ultimately holds the answer.
- **[[Problem Refinement|Problem refinement]] — authentically discarding the lung.** The most instructive cognitive move is Rabih's decision to *drop* the lung: he entertains the seductive lung-plus-liver overlap (TB, Q fever, Legionella), then recognizes that COVID fully explains the lung and not the liver, and reframes to "liver alone vs liver-plus-pregnancy." Resisting the pull to build one unifying story from every finding ("true, true, and unrelated") is what keeps the reasoning honest.
- **[[Diagnostic Humility|Diagnostic humility]] as the episode's thesis.** Reza's explicit "n of 2," his framing of the pregnant patient as an "n of 2" in a second sense (two patients), his running every medication past a pharmacist, and his low threshold to call obstetrics are humility operationalized. Both hosts note that naming a knowledge gap and acting on it — a phone call as a curious learner — can outperform hours of solo reading, and Rabih's audible pause when reaching for esoterica is offered as a real-time signal that one should call for help.
- **Anchoring on a word, corrected.** Reza's initial expectation that "cholestasis" must mean an alkaline-phosphatase/bilirubin pattern nearly mismatched ICP to the hepatocellular picture; learning that ICP can prominently raise transaminases is the semantic-anchor correction that let the diagnosis land.

## Teaching Discussion

- When AST/ALT are elevated, localize to the liver by base rate but deliberately keep muscle, heart, and red cells in view; use the ALT's greater liver specificity and long half-life, and a hemoglobin (± CK, troponin, cardiac review of systems), to close the extrahepatic door rather than assuming it shut.
- Interpret transaminases by their trend, not a single value: a flat, persistent elevation points to the parenchyma, while a bouncing pattern suggests intermittent "plumbing" (a lodging-then-dislodging stone or transient hypoperfusion causing ischemic hepatitis). Because up to half of acute liver injury is a plumbing problem, still image the right upper quadrant with Doppler even when the pattern looks parenchymal.
- Collapse a sinus tachycardia into shock (hypovolemic, distributive, cardiogenic, obstructive), sympathetic toxicity (exogenous or endogenous), or fever — and, absent fever, treat it as shock physiology until proven otherwise. Reduce abdominal distention to solid, liquid, or gas, remembering that adipose tissue and pregnancy are the common non-pathologic causes.
- Refine the problem before expanding it: a common finding with a high base rate (a cough) may be true, true, and unrelated. When one organ's abnormality is fully explained by a separate process, drop it from the unifying hypothesis rather than forcing a single diagnosis to cover everything.
- Carry an explicit liver-in-pregnancy schema — disease modified by pregnancy (autoimmune and viral hepatitis), disease unique to pregnancy (AFLP, ICP, HELLP/preeclampsia), and the most common association (estrogen-driven gallstones) — and let gestational age weight it, since AFLP, HELLP, and preeclampsia cluster in the third trimester.
- Do not let the word "cholestasis" mislead you: intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy presents with pruritus (classically palms and soles) and can raise the transaminases prominently rather than producing the expected alkaline-phosphatase/bilirubin pattern. Confirm it with a serum total bile acid level (which may take over a week to result), and treat a prior-pregnancy history of ICP as a strong prior; management is ursodiol with obstetric co-management.
- Practice diagnostic humility structurally when you leave your domain: treat a pregnant patient as two patients, run even minor medications past a pharmacist, and lower your threshold to consult early. A consultant approached as a collaborator — not through a hierarchy of deference — can compress hours of reading into a better decision, and an audible pause when you reach for esoterica is itself a cue to pick up the phone.
