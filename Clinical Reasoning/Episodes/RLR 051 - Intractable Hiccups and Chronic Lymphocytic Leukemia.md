Labs show a **WBC of 15k that is 90% lymphocytes** with an **LDH ~400**; the quiet clincher is the **old chart** — lymphocytes have outnumbered neutrophils on smears for **~5 years**, reframing the count from "reactive" to **chronic/clonal**. A **CT** reveals **massive splenomegaly** with a **wedge-shaped splenic infarct abutting the diaphragm**, and **flow cytometry** confirms **[[chronic lymphocytic leukemia|CLL]]**. The unifying mechanism: an enlarging spleen **outgrew its blood supply** (infarct → LDH) and **mechanically irritated the diaphragm**, driving **diaphragmatic myoclonus** — the hiccups. He was **treated for the CLL** because the **symptomatic, infarcting spleen** is itself an indication. **Pearls**

- **Hiccups are a reflex, so reason like it's a reflex.** The arc is **afferent** (vagus, phrenic, sympathetic chain) → a medullary **"hiccup center"** → **efferent phrenic nerve → diaphragm**. Anything that irritates any limb of that arc can hiccup — which is why the differential is huge and why you *split it by mechanism* rather than memorize a list.
- **Use [[analogical reasoning]] for "final common pathway" symptoms.** Hiccups behave like **[[cough]]**, **[[syncope]]**, or **[[altered mental status]]**: one output, many inputs. Borrow the same scaffold every time — **central vs peripheral/structural vs toxic-metabolic** — and the intimidating symptom becomes a solvable one.
- **Tempo defines the hiccup workup.** A **bout (<48 h)** is benign and needs nothing. **Persistent (>48 h)** or **intractable (>1 month)** hiccups — especially **debilitating** ones that bring someone to the **ED** — are red flags that earn a structured search for a **structural or metabolic** driver.
- **Diaphragm-irritating disease lives in the left/right upper quadrant and lower chest.** Subphrenic abscess, **[[splenomegaly]]**, hepatomegaly, gastric distension, pericarditis, and lower-lobe/pleural processes all sit against the diaphragm — so **new LUQ tenderness** in a hiccuping patient is not incidental, it's a **localizer**.
- **Read the *prior* differentials before you call a lymphocytosis reactive.** A **WBC 15k, 90% lymphocytes** could be acute viral — but **five years of lymphocyte-predominant smears** converts "reactive" into **chronic/clonal**. Chronicity on old labs is one of the cheapest, highest-yield tests you already own.
- **The lymphocytosis buckets:** **reactive/viral** (EBV/CMV mononucleosis — *atypical* lymphs, younger host, acute), **drug/other reactive** (pertussis, **[[DRESS syndrome|DRESS]]**), and **clonal/malignant** (**[[chronic lymphocytic leukemia|CLL]]**/SLL — mature small lymphs, smudge cells, longstanding). **Flow cytometry** adjudicates.
- **[[chronic lymphocytic leukemia|CLL]] is a diagnosis of the peripheral blood.** A **monoclonal B-cell population** (CD5+, CD19+, CD23+, light-chain-restricted) of **≥5,000/µL** on **flow cytometry** defines it — no marrow required. Fewer clonal cells without cytopenias/adenopathy is **monoclonal B-cell lymphocytosis**.
- **A massive spleen can outgrow its own blood supply.** Progressive splenomegaly → **wedge-shaped [[splenic infarct]]** (pleuritic LUQ pain, **elevated LDH**), and a spleen pressing on the diaphragm can trigger **hiccups**. In CLL, **symptomatic/infarcting splenomegaly** is itself a **treatment indication**.

**Problem Representation**
A man in his late 60s with seven days of persistent, debilitating hiccups, new left-upper-quadrant tenderness, and a longstanding (multi-year) lymphocyte-predominant leukocytosis, found to have massive splenomegaly with a splenic infarct due to chronic lymphocytic leukemia — the enlarged, infarcting spleen irritating the diaphragm to produce the hiccups.

## Case Narrative

Rabih — presenting from his emergency-department shifts at the VA — brings **Reza** ("Profrez," discussant today) a man in his **late 60s** whose chief complaint is, of all things, **hiccups**: not a passing spell but **seven consecutive days** of them, continuous enough to interrupt sleep, eating, and conversation, and finally driving him to the **ED**. Reza's first instinct is to refuse to treat "hiccups" as trivial and instead to treat it as a **symptom with a physiology** — a **reflex arc**. He sketches the arc aloud: **afferent** input from the **vagus and phrenic nerves** (and the sympathetic chain) travels to a poorly-localized **"hiccup center" in the medulla/brainstem**, which fires down the **phrenic nerve** to produce an abrupt **diaphragmatic contraction** truncated by glottic closure. Because *any* irritation along that arc can generate a hiccup, the differential is enormous — and Reza's move is explicitly **[[analogical reasoning]]**: hiccups belong to the same family as **[[cough]]**, **[[syncope]]**, and **[[altered mental status]]** — **"final common pathway" symptoms** with one output and many inputs, best approached with a **reusable scaffold** rather than a memorized list.

That scaffold splits the causes by **mechanism**: **central** (a brainstem lesion — stroke, mass, demyelination — irritating the hiccup center), **peripheral/structural** (anything mechanically irritating the **vagus or phrenic** along their course, or the **diaphragm** itself — GERD and gastric distension, mediastinal or esophageal disease, pericarditis/MI, and the **subdiaphragmatic** culprits: subphrenic abscess, **[[splenomegaly]]**, hepatomegaly), and **toxic-metabolic** (uremia, hyponatremia, hypocalcemia, alcohol, and the classic iatrogenic offenders — **steroids and benzodiazepines**). Before spending on any of it, Reza applies **tempo**: hiccups are graded by **duration** — a **bout (<48 h)** is benign and self-limited, **persistent** hiccups exceed **48 hours**, and **intractable** hiccups exceed a **month**. This man's **week** of debilitating, ED-worthy hiccups lands him squarely in the **worrisome, workup-earning** category, where a **structural or metabolic** driver must be actively sought.

The **exam** is the pivot. Neurologically the patient is **pristine** — no focal deficit, no brainstem signs, no cranial-nerve findings to suggest a central cause — which quietly **down-weights the CNS bucket**. But there is one abnormality: **new tenderness in the left upper quadrant**. Reza treats this not as incidental but as a **localizer** — the LUQ sits directly against the **left hemidiaphragm**, exactly the territory where a diaphragm-irritating process would live. The differential's center of gravity shifts **below the diaphragm**.

The **labs** deepen the story. The **CXR is clear** (no lower-lobe/pleural process, no mediastinal mass). The **CBC**, however, shows a **WBC of ~15,000 that is 90% lymphocytes** — an **absolute lymphocytosis** — with an **LDH around 400**. Reza runs the **lymphocytosis buckets**: a young patient with **atypical lymphocytes** would suggest **reactive/viral** disease (**EBV/CMV mononucleosis**); a recent drug exposure might raise **[[DRESS syndrome|DRESS]]** or pertussis; but a **mature lymphocytosis in an older adult** demands ruling out a **clonal/malignant** process — **[[chronic lymphocytic leukemia|CLL]]** or SLL. The decisive, almost-free test is the **old chart**: prior smears show **lymphocytes outnumbering neutrophils for roughly five years**. That **chronicity** reclassifies the count from "reactive to something acute" to a **longstanding clonal lymphoproliferation** — and, tellingly, a process that has had **years to enlarge lymphoid organs**.

The **CT** closes the loop: **massive splenomegaly** with a **wedge-shaped splenic infarct** pressing against the **diaphragm**, alongside **small, diffuse lymphadenopathy**. **Flow cytometry** confirms a **monoclonal B-cell population** consistent with **[[chronic lymphocytic leukemia|CLL]]**. The mechanism now assembles into a single line: the **CLL** drove **progressive splenomegaly**; the spleen **outgrew its blood supply** and **infarcted** (the wedge lesion, the pleuritic LUQ tenderness, the **elevated LDH**); and the enlarged, infarcting spleen **mechanically irritated the left hemidiaphragm**, producing **diaphragmatic myoclonus** — the **hiccups**. The patient was **treated for his CLL**, not because his blood counts alone crossed a threshold, but because a **symptomatic, infarcting spleen** is itself an **indication to treat**. Matt closes the episode with the wrap-up. (Mike Najat is name-checked in the banter but was not on the episode.)

### Illness Scripts and Bias

The episode is a clinic in **not dismissing a "small" symptom**. The reflexive script for hiccups is "benign, self-limited, reassure" — an **availability/base-rate trap** that is correct for the **<48 h bout** and dangerous for the **week-long** version. Reza defuses it by refusing to reason from the *label* ("hiccups") and instead reasoning from the **mechanism** (a reflex arc), which forces a differential rather than a shrug. The **[[analogical reasoning]]** move — treating hiccups like **cough** or **[[altered mental status]]**, other "one-output-many-inputs" symptoms — is the generative engine: it imports a **ready-made, mechanism-based scaffold** (central / structural / toxic-metabolic) and guarantees breadth without brainstorming from scratch. **Tempo** is again a first-class variable, deciding *how hard to look* before a single test is ordered. The **anti-anchoring** discipline shows twice: the **pristine neuro exam** is allowed to quietly *subtract* the tempting "brainstem" story rather than be ignored, and the **LUQ tenderness** — the lone positive — is treated as a **signal to localize**, not noise. The subtlest teaching point is **using the old chart**: the same WBC of 15k reads as *reactive* if you see it once and *clonal* if you see its five-year trajectory — a reminder that **prior data reframe present data**, and that the cheapest way to avoid over-calling a reactive lymphocytosis is to look backward before looking outward.

## Teaching Discussion

**Treat "final common pathway" symptoms as reflexes with a differential.** Hiccups (**singultus**) are a **myoclonic reflex**: afferents in the **vagus, phrenic, and sympathetic chain** feed a poorly-localized **medullary "hiccup center,"** whose efferent limb is the **phrenic nerve** driving an abrupt diaphragmatic contraction cut short by glottic closure. Because the arc is long and richly innervated, causes are legion — but they are **splittable by mechanism**, and that is the whole trick. The same reasoning applies to **[[cough]]**, **[[syncope]]**, and **[[altered mental status]]**: name the reflex/pathway, then walk its anatomy.

**Split hiccups three ways.** **Central** — a lesion irritating the brainstem hiccup center (stroke, tumor, demyelination, infection); look for **other neurologic signs**. **Peripheral/structural** — irritation anywhere along the **vagus or phrenic** or of the **diaphragm** itself: GERD and gastric distension, esophageal/mediastinal disease, pericarditis and inferior MI, and the **subdiaphragmatic** cluster (subphrenic abscess, **[[splenomegaly]]**, hepatomegaly). **Toxic-metabolic** — uremia, hyponatremia, hypocalcemia, hyperglycemia, alcohol, and the iatrogenic classics **steroids and benzodiazepines**. A clean neuro exam **subtracts** the first bucket; **upper-abdominal** findings point to the second.

**Grade by duration, and let tempo set aggressiveness.** A **bout/acute** attack (**<48 h**) is benign and needs no workup. **Persistent** hiccups (**>48 h**) and **intractable** hiccups (**>1 month**) — and any that are **debilitating** enough to reach the ED — warrant a **structured evaluation**: metabolic panel (renal function, sodium, calcium, glucose), **CXR**, targeted imaging along the phrenic/vagal course (**CT chest/abdomen** when a structural driver is suspected), and **brain/brainstem MRI** if central signs are present. Tempo tells you *how far down that list to go*.

**Work up a lymphocytosis by chronicity and clonality.** An **absolute lymphocytosis** sorts into **reactive** (viral **mononucleosis** — EBV/CMV, with *atypical* lymphs, usually younger and acute; pertussis; **[[DRESS syndrome|DRESS]]**) versus **clonal/malignant** (**[[chronic lymphocytic leukemia|CLL]]/SLL**, with small mature lymphocytes and **smudge cells**). Two cheap discriminators: the **prior differentials** (a *years-long* lymphocyte predominance is chronic/clonal, not reactive) and **flow cytometry**. In an older adult with a **stable, mature lymphocytosis**, think **CLL** first.

**CLL is diagnosed in the blood.** Per **iwCLL**, **≥5,000/µL clonal B-lymphocytes** in the peripheral blood — a **monoclonal population** that is characteristically **CD5+, CD19+, CD23+** with **dim surface light-chain restriction** on **flow cytometry** — establishes the diagnosis; **no bone marrow biopsy is required**. Fewer than 5,000/µL clonal cells *without* cytopenias, adenopathy, or organomegaly is **monoclonal B-cell lymphocytosis (MBL)**, a precursor state. Staging (Rai/Binet) and prognosis (IGHV, FISH for del(17p)/TP53) follow, but the *diagnosis* is a peripheral-blood one.

**Massive spleens infarct — and can hiccup.** As a spleen enlarges (here from CLL infiltration), it can **outgrow its arterial supply**, producing **wedge-shaped infarcts** that cause **pleuritic LUQ pain** and **elevated LDH**. A spleen large enough to abut and irritate the **left hemidiaphragm** can drive **diaphragmatic myoclonus** — the mechanism of this patient's hiccups. Crucially, **symptomatic or progressive splenomegaly** (and its complications, like infarction) is a recognized **iwCLL indication to treat**, independent of the absolute lymphocyte count: this patient was treated **because of the spleen**, not the number.
