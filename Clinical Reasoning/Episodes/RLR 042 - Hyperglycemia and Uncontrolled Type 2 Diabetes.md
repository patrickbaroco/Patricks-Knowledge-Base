## Pearls

- **Split hyperglycemia into *consequence* then *cause*, and let [[Base Rate|base rate]] tier the causes.** First exclude the emergencies ([[Diabetic Ketoacidosis|DKA]], [[Hyperosmolar Hyperglycemic State|HHS]]); then, for cause, Tier 1 is [[Medication Nonadherence|nonadherence]], dietary indiscretion, and [[Infection|infection]] (the overwhelming majority), Tier 2 is an [[Cushing Syndrome|endocrinopathy]] or a [[Pancreatic Cancer|pancreatic process]] — reached "almost never," and only after Tier 1 comes up empty.
- **A "new metallic taste + epigastric pain + rapidly rising A1c" can seduce you toward [[Pancreatic Cancer|pancreatic cancer]] — name the pull.** New-onset or abruptly worsening diabetes *is* a recognized herald of pancreatic cancer, and a revered mentor's aphorism makes the script vivid ([[Availability Heuristic|availability]]); the discipline is to keep it a *low-pretest* hypothesis until the common, reversible drivers are excluded, not to let it recruit every subsequent finding.
- **[[C-Peptide|C-peptide]] is cleared by the kidney and is falsely elevated in [[Acute Kidney Injury|renal failure]] — don't read a high value as "the pancreas is fine."** Insulin is cleared hepatically but C-peptide renally, so with an AKI a C-peptide "just above range" may badly overstate β-cell output — the same [[Test Interpretation|interpretation trap]] as a high PTHrP or a high-normal PTH against hypercalcemia. Repeat it once the creatinine normalizes (here it fell appropriately, and the glucose was 211).
- **When imaging surprises you, talk to radiology before you build a cancer around it.** Too-numerous-to-count hepatic lesions on CT *and* MRI turned out to be **[[Motion Artifact|motion artifact]]** — IR found nothing on ultrasound. A confident "there is nothing there" is more trustworthy than "we saw something but the patient was moving"; the anchored cancer narrative nearly survived its own disproof.
- **Treat the AKI and the acidosis as *downstream* of the glucose.** [[Hyperglycemia|Hyperglycemia]] → [[Osmotic Diuresis|glycosuria and osmotic diuresis]] → hypovolemia → [[Acute Kidney Injury|prerenal AKI]] and a [[Lactic Acidosis|lactic]] anion gap; a few liters of fluid normalized the bicarbonate, creatinine, and gap **without insulin**, confirming the vicious cycle rather than a primary renal or acid-base disease.
- **The negative million-dollar workup *is* the diagnosis — and the patient pays for the journey.** [[Diagnostic Humility|Bread-and-butter]] uncontrolled type 2 diabetes, with the hip pain only [[Trochanteric Bursitis|trochanteric bursitis]], was the answer; the closing audience question ("how does the *patient* feel?") is the real teaching — told he "likely had cancer," then untold, then re-tested, he returned days later overwhelmed, his pain still unexplained.

## Problem Representation

An 87-year-old man with type 2 diabetes (A1c 6.8 earlier in the year), hypertension, coronary artery disease, GERD, and BPH — adherent to metformin — presents to primary care with a few weeks of worsening chronic left-hip and low-back pain, poor appetite, and malaise, and is found to have marked hyperglycemia (glucose 640, later >700) with glucosuria but no ketosis, a creatinine risen to 2.2 from a baseline of 1.1, a mild lactic anion-gap acidosis, and an A1c that has doubled to 13.6 over eight months despite documented metformin adherence and 14 pounds of weight loss. He has no fever, leukocytosis, DKA, or hyperosmolar coma; the problem is the *cause* of an abrupt loss of glycemic control in a previously well-controlled older man, with an open question of what the hip pain represents. An anchoring concern for pancreatic malignancy (metallic taste, epigastric pain, rapid A1c rise, an initially "elevated" C-peptide, and reported liver lesions) is progressively dismantled: the C-peptide was falsely elevated by renal failure and normalized appropriately once the creatinine corrected, the hepatic lesions proved to be motion artifact, and islet-cell/GAD antibodies, tumor markers, and a whole-body PET-CT were all negative, with the glucose improving to 211 on fluids and education alone. The working diagnosis is reversible, hyperosmolar-spectrum worsening of type 2 diabetes (ketosis-prone/pancreatic-stunning physiology entertained but not formally invoked), the hip pain attributed to trochanteric bursitis.

## Case Narrative

This is an RLR episode staged as a **live Medicine Grand Rounds** — Elizabeth Eberley introduces the hosts, there are no slides ("we're trying to stay true to clinical reasoning"), Rabih is the blinded discussant, and a ~60-person audience will be pulled into the discussion. Reza, presenting off a just-finished night shift, promises "bread and butter," not esoterica, and transforms the room into the podcast.

The patient is an **87-year-old man** seen for **routine primary-care follow-up**, reporting worsening **chronic left-hip and low-back pain** over a few weeks with "I just don't feel well" and poor appetite. The review of systems is otherwise negative (no fever, chills, night sweats, dysuria, diarrhea, dyspnea, chest pain). His history: hypertension, CAD, GERD, BPH, and **type 2 diabetes with an A1c of 6.8** earlier that year; medications include amlodipine, lisinopril, metoprolol, atorvastatin, finasteride, tamsulosin, and **metformin**. Exam is unremarkable but for **left-hip tenderness**. The PCP plans to continue medications and follow up in three months — but orders labs, which is itself the hinge. The labs: TSH normal; UA with **glucose >500, no ketones**, bland sediment; chemistry with **glucose 640**, **creatinine 2.2 (baseline 1.1)**, bicarbonate 21, sodium 131, anion gap 15; normal LFTs; normal CBC (Hgb 12.7, platelets 157); and a pending A1c.

Rabih models the analysis. The chronic hip/back pain is a **camouflaging** condition, and "the reason the patient is seeking care is always a big part of the analysis" — this is an acute flare of a perceived chronic problem, represented as **[[Low Back Pain|low-back and hip discomfort]]**. Why did the PCP draw blood at all, when most such flares don't get labs? Because of **soft [[Back Pain Red Flags|red flags]]**: for [[Low Back Pain|back pain]], one separates the ~5% of specific secondary causes from the ~95% of non-specific back pain by looking for (1) systemic features (fever, nausea, anything "outside the back") and (2) local alarm signs (the most specific being **neurologic compromise** — autonomic and motor, not mild sensory). Here two things pushed deeper: a flare approaching the **~6-week** threshold (about three weeks and climbing) and the **anorexia/malaise** that aren't classic for a musculoskeletal exacerbation (though they can be side effects of back-pain medications). The fundamental finding Rabih extracts from all the data is **[[Hyperglycemia|hyperglycemia without ketosis]]**.

He schematizes the new hyperglycemia. Out of the blue, it usually has two simple, potentially unrelated causes: **[[Medication Nonadherence|medication adherence]]/dietary discretion**, or **progression of the underlying disease**. The **[[Acute Kidney Injury|AKI]]** doesn't jump out because it's readily explained by the degree of hyperglycemia ([[Osmotic Diuresis|osmotic diuresis]] → hypovolemia). He invokes the "**I**'s" of hyperglycemia — **[[Infection|infection]]**, **[[Inflammation|non-infectious inflammation]]** (hyperthyroidism, pancreatitis), and **nonadherence** — while warning not to be "falsely distracted" into the back pain: a *distant* inflammatory process could be driving the glucose while the back pain is coincident chronic noise. His summary: chase the hyperglycemia, understand its cause, and don't lock onto the back pain. Reza praises the moves — anchoring in [[Base Rate|base rate]] (back pain common; 90% type 2, 5–10% type 1 diabetes), searching for red flags, refusing to fixate on the AKI in favor of the more severe abnormality, and treating the back pain as possible [[Center of Gravity|noise rather than signal]].

The PCP sends the patient to the **ED**. There he reaffirms metformin adherence (the pharmacy confirms regular refills) but now adds a week of **decreased intake, dry lips, a metallic taste, and mild epigastric discomfort**, and on probing, **new nocturia (3–4×/night)** and **weight loss** — 14 pounds over four months (200 → 186; BMI 36). Vitals are normal; exam again only left-hip tenderness. A **VBG**: pH 7.27, PCO2 47, calculated bicarbonate 21.5, **anion gap 18, glucose >700, lactate 2.7**; a respiratory viral panel is negative. The **A1c has doubled — 6.7 eight months ago, now 13.6**. The resident asks: does he need IV insulin?

Rabih calls it classic polydipsia/polyuria and declines to over-invest in the **[[Metallic Taste|metallic taste]]** (renal failure, drugs like metronidazole, or simple dry mouth). The fundamental question: why has the diabetes gone "wildly out of control" despite weight loss and metformin adherence? The **[[Lactic Acidosis|lactate]] of 2.7** makes [[Metformin-Associated Lactic Acidosis|metformin toxicity]] less likely (and one would stop metformin anyway). He splits the differential into an **acute reversible trigger** (the "I"s — occult infection in that MSK area, [[Inflammation|inflammation]] like hyperthyroidism, mesenteric ischemia, occult steroid/supplement use) versus **worsening of the underlying disease absent a trigger**: **[[Ketosis-Prone Diabetes|ketosis-prone diabetes]]** (transient β-cell "stunning," classically older men, needing temporary insulin), a **[[Pancreatic Cancer|mass-forming pancreatic process]]**, or rarely **[[Insulin Autoantibodies|insulin antibodies]]** — plus a hard look at the medication list. He would characterize the hip pain (intra-articular vs greater trochanter vs referred SI joint; inflammatory/infectious/malignant) and remember diabetic causes of leg/hip pain — **[[Diabetic Amyotrophy|diabetic radiculoplexus neuropathy]]** and **[[Diabetic Myonecrosis|diabetic myonecrosis]]**. Management: aggressive fluids and **IV insulin** (5–10 units, insulin-naïve, AKI, trend requirements); with no ketosis and no altered sensorium, he meets neither [[Diabetic Ketoacidosis|DKA]] nor [[Hyperosmolar Hyperglycemic State|HHS]] criteria, so "some fluids, a little insulin," and the real questions are the hip and the medications — and, if unrevealing, **the status of the pancreas**.

Reza reveals the physiology: the patient got **two liters of LR and no insulin**, after which the **bicarbonate normalized, the creatinine improved, and the anion gap fell to 10**. The chain: hyperglycemia → glycosuria → [[Osmotic Diuresis|osmotic diuresis]] → hypovolemia → AKI and a lactic gap → all reversed by volume. But *why* did the A1c double? Here Reza names his own anchor: mentor **Lawrence Tierney** taught that a **new metallic taste can signal [[Pancreatic Cancer|pancreatic cancer]]**, and the triad of metallic taste, epigastric pain, and a rapidly rising A1c had him convinced. Rabih, pulling up his **[[Hyperglycemia|approach-to-diabetes]] figures**, lays out the map: hyperglycemia → (1) cause and (2) consequence; no DKA/HHS, so it's cause — either worsening **[[Insulin Resistance|insulin resistance]]** (steroids, [[Cushing Syndrome|Cushing]], HIV, hep C) or a mis-labeled diagnosis hiding an **[[Insulin Deficiency|insulin-deficiency]] syndrome** ([[Type 1 Diabetes|autoimmune type 1]], a pancreatic process such as cancer or hemochromatosis, or **[[Ketosis-Prone Diabetes|ketosis-prone diabetes]]**). Base rate: the answer is usually stopped meds, diet, or an obvious infection — none overt here — "so we've got to think about the pancreas."

The labs deepen the pull. Repeat values: after fluids the anion gap normalized, glucose still 532; Hgb 11.3 (baseline; earlier 12.7 was hemoconcentration), platelets 105 (baseline 120–130); **[[C-Peptide|C-peptide]] 3.91** (range 0.8–3.85) drawn when glucose was 640; a beta-hydroxybutyrate ordered but never resulted. Rabih reasons live about the C-peptide: the pancreas *is* secreting, but is it secreting **enough** for this degree of hyperglycemia and presumed insulin resistance? — the same logic as a "normal" PTH against a high calcium. He flags a personal knowledge gap and would lean on endocrinology, warning against being "lulled" that a high value means "done." **Dr. Michelle Jeffrey**, an endocrinologist in the audience, supplies the pearl: this is pancreatic cancer or chronic pancreatitis until proven otherwise, *and* she avoids ordering C-peptide/insulin in significant renal impairment because it can be **falsely elevated**. Reza makes the mechanism explicit — **[[C-Peptide|C-peptide]], the connecting peptide of pro-insulin, is cleared by the kidney** (insulin is cleared by the liver), so an **[[Acute Kidney Injury|AKI]] falsely elevates it** — the same trap as the earlier **[[Test Interpretation|PTHrP-that-was-high-with-no-cancer]]** case. His read of "pancreas working, so not insulin-deficient" had rested on a spuriously high number.

A rheumatologist, **Dr. Rockmas**, presses the other loose thread — *has the patient actually been examined?* Reza's [[Return to the Bedside|hip exam]]: painless range of motion, **tenderness on lateral palpation over the greater trochanter**, and mild discomfort bearing weight — coded as **[[Trochanteric Bursitis|trochanteric bursitis]]**, NOS. Endocrinology, echoing Dr. Jeffrey, recommends a pancreatic-protocol CT. Ordered at night without the arterial phase, the CT is a limited look at the pancreas but reports **"multiple, too numerous to count, hypoenhancing lesions"** across both hepatic lobes (largest 1.8 cm) with morphology "suggesting chronic liver disease"; hip films show a possible old pelvic fracture, and MRI shows no osseous hip abnormality. Rabih pauses — "why are we entering esoterica again?" — and teaches the **[[Liver Mass|liver-lesion]]** approach agnostic to this patient: assume malignant until radiology says otherwise; [[Hepatocellular Carcinoma|HCC]] versus **[[Liver Metastasis|metastasis]]** turns on two variables — background **[[Cirrhosis|cirrhosis]]** (most HCC arises in it) and **lesion count**; no overt cirrhosis plus innumerable lesions ⇒ metastatic disease, commonly from **lung or GI tract**, and the move is an **image-guided biopsy of the metastatic site** (confirms and stages at once). Everything, Reza notes, is "lining up" — metallic taste, epigastric pain, rocketing A1c, liver lesions, the portal route from a pancreatic primary.

Then the case turns. The radiologist recommends **gadolinium MRI**; the MRA is **severely limited by motion artifact** but read as scattered foci "corresponding" to the CT. **IR arrives to biopsy — and cannot identify a single lesion on ultrasound.** Re-reviewing the MRI, they see nothing; the study is uninterpretable from motion. **There is nothing there.** Rabih has never seen numerous lesions on both CT and MRI turn out to be **[[Motion Artifact|motion artifact]]**, and draws the lesson: talk to radiology, and a confident "nothing there" beats "we saw something but the patient was moving." The negatives then cascade: **islet-cell and [[GAD Antibody|GAD]] antibodies negative**; a **repeat [[C-Peptide|C-peptide]] of 2.46 with glucose 211 and a normalized creatinine** — appropriate insulin production; **[[Tumor Markers|tumor markers]] (CEA, CA19-9, CA125, AFP) all normal**; and a **whole-body [[PET-CT|PET-CT]] with zero tracer uptake anywhere**.

Rabih's reflection reframes the payoff: the workup was appropriate (there was too much to sit on something sinister), but the quietly decisive datum is that **the glucose is now 211** — under much better control, matching a more appropriate A1c — *without* specific treatment of any underlying disease. The case is therefore about **how to analyze worsening hyperglycemia** (nonadherence, diet, meds, endocrinopathy, pancreas), with base rate saying most are reversible; whether to formally invoke **[[Ketosis-Prone Diabetes|ketosis-prone diabetes]] / [[Pancreatic Stunning|pancreatic stunning]]** (older men, a touch of ketosis, relative insulin insufficiency) is "an artificial discussion." Prognosis is excellent given the negative PET and the improving glucose. Reza's team and endocrinology ultimately called it **type 2 diabetes** — never any true ketosis (bicarbonate never very low; the gap was lactate; it corrected on fluids alone), with glucose on the **[[Hyperosmolar Hyperglycemic State|hyperosmolar-hyperglycemic]] spectrum** typical of insulin-resistant disease — planning interval liver imaging, though no pancreatic lesion was ever seen.

The hosts close on **how they learn** (the GlassHealth digital notebook of schemas/scripts/cases; reflection and figure-making; the RLR post-case reflection videos, referencing their recent **acute EBV/mononucleosis** case). But the last word belongs to the audience. **Dr. Hussein Danani** returns the exercise to the human: after all the corrected metabolic derangements, *how does the patient feel about the hip and back pain that brought him in?* Reza answers honestly — the patient was **frustrated and overwhelmed**, told he "likely had cancer," then untold, then re-tested; he **returned days after discharge**, unable to cope and needing insulin education, his pain still unexplained. It was not a pleasant journey — the [[Diagnostic Humility|humbling]] counterweight to a satisfying diagnostic chase.

### Illness Scripts and Bias

- **[[Base Rate|Base rate]] as the spine — tier the causes of hyperglycemia and resist esoterica.** Ninety percent type 2; Tier 1 (nonadherence, diet, infection) dominates, Tier 2 (endocrinopathy, pancreatic process) is reached "almost never." The episode's arc is the pull *into* Tier 2 and the base-rate discipline that ultimately pulls back out.
- **[[Schema-Based Reasoning|Schema]]: cause vs consequence, then resistance vs deficiency.** [[Hyperglycemia|Hyperglycemia]] is first triaged for a consequence ([[Diabetic Ketoacidosis|DKA]]/[[Hyperosmolar Hyperglycemic State|HHS]]) and then for cause — worsening [[Insulin Resistance|resistance]] (steroids, [[Cushing Syndrome|Cushing]]) versus new [[Insulin Deficiency|deficiency]] (autoimmune, [[Ketosis-Prone Diabetes|ketosis-prone]], pancreatic) — the "I"s organizing the reversible triggers.
- **[[Center of Gravity|Center of gravity]] and signal-vs-noise.** The [[Acute Kidney Injury|AKI]] is deliberately demoted (explained by osmotic diuresis) and the chronic back pain treated as possible *noise*, concentrating cognitive energy on the [[Hyperglycemia|hyperglycemia]] — while explicitly hedging that a distant process could drive the glucose with the pain coincident.
- **[[Test Interpretation|Test interpretation]] — the renally-cleared [[C-Peptide|C-peptide]].** A value "just above range" is not "the pancreas is fine" when there is an [[Acute Kidney Injury|AKI]]: C-peptide is cleared by the kidney (insulin by the liver), so renal failure falsely elevates it — the identical trap as a high PTHrP or a high-normal PTH against hypercalcemia. The repeat, drawn at normal creatinine, was appropriate.
- **[[Anchoring Bias|Anchoring]] and [[Availability Heuristic|availability]] via a mentor's aphorism.** Lawrence Tierney's "metallic taste → pancreatic cancer," plus a genuinely worrisome triad, built a pretest probability strong enough that the artifactual liver lesions were absorbed as confirmation; Reza names the pull rather than hiding it — the metacognitive antidote.
- **Talk to radiology — [[Motion Artifact|artifact]] masquerading as metastases.** Numerous lesions on *both* CT and MRI were motion artifact; a clean ultrasound and a confident "nothing there" outrank "we saw something but explained it away." The anchored narrative nearly outlived its disproof.
- **[[Return to the Bedside|Return to the bedside]] and [[Diagnostic Humility|humility]].** A rheumatologist's prompt forced the actual hip exam ([[Trochanteric Bursitis|trochanteric bursitis]]), and the closing patient-perspective question reframed a satisfying chase as an over-medicalized, frightening ordeal — the negative workup being the diagnosis, at real cost to the patient.

## Teaching Discussion

- Analyze new or worsening hyperglycemia in two steps: rule out a consequence (DKA, HHS) first, then pursue the cause, tiering by base rate — nonadherence, dietary indiscretion, and infection first; an endocrinopathy or pancreatic process only after those are excluded.
- Keep new-onset/abruptly worsening diabetes on the radar as a possible herald of pancreatic cancer, but hold it as a low-pretest hypothesis; don't let a vivid mentor aphorism (metallic taste) or a strong-sounding triad recruit every subsequent finding into a malignancy narrative.
- Interpret C-peptide against renal function: it is renally cleared and falsely elevated in AKI, so a "high" value does not exclude relative insulin insufficiency — repeat it once creatinine normalizes, and generalize the trap (PTHrP, PTH-against-calcium) to any renally handled analyte.
- Treat AKI and a lactic anion-gap acidosis in severe hyperglycemia as downstream of osmotic diuresis and hypovolemia; expect fluids alone to correct the gap, bicarbonate, and creatinine, and reserve early aggressive IV insulin decisions to the presence or absence of DKA/HHS.
- When imaging conflicts with the clinical trajectory or with a subsequent study, talk to radiology before committing — innumerable "lesions" on CT and MRI can be motion artifact, and a clean confirmatory study plus a confident "nothing there" should override an anchored cancer story.
- Work up back/hip pain through its red-flag filter (duration beyond ~6 weeks, systemic features, and especially neurologic — autonomic/motor — compromise), and actually examine the joint; here range-of-motion was painless while lateral trochanteric palpation reproduced the pain, yielding trochanteric bursitis.
- Remember diabetic causes of proximal leg pain (diabetic radiculoplexus neuropathy/amyotrophy, diabetic myonecrosis), and keep ketosis-prone diabetes ("pancreatic stunning," older men) in mind for dramatic, reversible loss of control — while recognizing that formally naming it may be an artificial exercise when the glucose improves on its own.
- Close the loop on the patient's experience: a negative exhaustive workup is a legitimate answer, but being told "likely cancer" and then untold is its own harm — plan for reassurance, education (here, insulin teaching), and follow-up so the diagnostic journey doesn't become the disease.
