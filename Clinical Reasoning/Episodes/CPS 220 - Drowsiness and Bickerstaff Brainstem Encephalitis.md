## Summary

> [!summary] Scan Summary
> A neurology Virtual Morning Report with neurologist **Aaron Berkowitz** on the deliberately vague chief concern of **increased drowsiness** — a case that turns entirely on **[[Problem Representation|problem representation]]**. A previously healthy **13-year-old girl**, a week after a flu-like illness, develops headache, blurry vision, and sleepiness that crescendos over two days into confusion, slurred speech, unsteadiness, and a fluctuating depressed level of consciousness; naloxone at an outside hospital does nothing. Berkowitz first splits "drowsiness" into a **sleep disorder vs an impairment of [[Level vs Content of Consciousness|arousal]]**, then runs [[Altered Mental Status|altered consciousness]] through his master equation — differential = [[Anatomical Approach|localization]] × [[Illness Tempo|time course]] over a medical/neurologic gate — while the resident **Mathieu** brings the ED coma algorithm (ABCs, glucose, opioid toxidrome, [[Coma Exam|coma exam]], CT). The **headache + blurry vision + altered arousal** triad reads as **raised [[Intracranial Pressure|intracranial pressure]]** (Monro-Kellie, [[False Localizing Sign|false-localizing]] sixth-nerve palsy, [[Cerebral Venous Sinus Thrombosis|CVST]]), and the age-plus-post-viral context makes Berkowitz anchor on **[[ADEM|ADEM]]** / [[MOGAD|anti-MOG]] disease. The exam shows a lethargic girl with **complete ophthalmoplegia, sluggish reactive pupils, no papilledema, and lower-limb hyporeflexia** — and a normal CT, **normal MRI**, and an LP with **albuminocytologic dissociation** (very high protein, near-acellular) with negative infectious studies; EEG shows only diffuse slowing. She improves on steroids and IVIG, waking with **external ophthalmoplegia, ataxia, and persistent hyporeflexia**. The pivot is the resident **Mattias**, who reframes ophthalmoplegia + areflexia + ataxia as **[[Miller Fisher Syndrome|Miller Fisher syndrome]]** — and Berkowitz completes it: Miller Fisher *plus* altered consciousness is **[[Bickerstaff Brainstem Encephalitis|Bickerstaff brainstem encephalitis]]**, confirmed by a positive **[[Anti-GQ1b Antibody Syndrome|anti-GQ1b antibody]]** — a syndrome nested inside the [[Guillain-Barré Syndrome|Guillain-Barré]] spectrum.

## Pearls

- **"Drowsiness" first splits two ways, then localizes.** Decide whether this is a **sleep disorder** (narcolepsy, obstructive sleep apnea — which can masquerade as dementia, headache, or inattention) or an **impairment of [[Level vs Content of Consciousness|arousal]]**. Impaired arousal localizes to the bilateral hemispheres, the bilateral thalami, or the brainstem **[[Reticular Activating System|reticular activating system]]** (upper pons/midbrain → thalami → cortex) — or to *systemic* physiology (hypoxia, hypercarbia, anemia, toxic-metabolic). Then apply localization × time course.
- **In the ED, altered consciousness is an algorithm before it is a differential.** ABCs, then the two instantly reversible misses — **glucose** and an **opioid toxidrome** (a naloxone trial) — then a structured **[[Coma Exam|coma exam]]** to separate structural from non-structural causes, then labs and CT. Here naloxone did nothing, removing the easy answer.
- **[[Headache|Headache]] + visual change + depressed arousal is the raised-[[Intracranial Pressure|intracranial-pressure]] triad.** ICP is set by the Monro-Kellie compartments (brain ~80%, CSF ~10%, blood ~10%); the pressure-volume curve is forgiving until it is suddenly not. Watch for a **[[False Localizing Sign|false-localizing]] sixth-nerve palsy** (long intracranial course over the clivus) and [[Papilledema|papilledema]] — though papilledema takes days to appear, so its absence never excludes raised ICP. Causes sit in the brain (mass, hemorrhage, inflammation), the CSF (communicating vs non-communicating hydrocephalus), or the venous system (**[[Cerebral Venous Sinus Thrombosis|CVST]]**).
- **Use the [[Oculocephalic Reflex|oculocephalic reflex]] to tell true ophthalmoplegia from coma.** A patient who cannot move the eyes may have a cranial-nerve/brainstem lesion *or* simply be too obtunded to look around; holding the eyes open and turning the head shows whether CN VIII is talking to III/IV/VI through the MLF. Complete ophthalmoplegia with headache and visual change would instead raise **[[Cavernous Sinus Thrombosis|cavernous sinus thrombosis]]**.
- **[[Problem Representation|Problem representation]] is the whole case — and teams build better ones.** Fixating on the altered mental status, Berkowitz missed the pattern until the resident collapsed ophthalmoplegia + areflexia + ataxia into **[[Miller Fisher Syndrome|Miller Fisher syndrome]]**; adding the altered consciousness that "didn't fit" yields **[[Bickerstaff Brainstem Encephalitis|Bickerstaff brainstem encephalitis]]**. The right one-line summary selects the diagnosis; what you choose to *leave out* is as powerful as what you keep.
- **[[Anti-GQ1b Antibody Syndrome|Anti-GQ1b]] unifies a nested spectrum, and CSF may be the only clue.** [[Guillain-Barré Syndrome|Guillain-Barré]], [[Miller Fisher Syndrome|Miller Fisher]], and [[Bickerstaff Brainstem Encephalitis|Bickerstaff]] overlap along the anti-GQ1b axis, and "every rule of Guillain-Barré has an exception." **[[Albuminocytologic Dissociation|Albuminocytologic dissociation]]** (high protein, few cells) is a non-specific marker of immune-mediated nerve/root disease — and here, with a normal MRI, it was nearly the only abnormal finding.

## Problem Representation

A previously healthy 13-year-old girl, one week after a flu-like illness, presents with two days of headache, blurry vision, and progressive drowsiness evolving into confusion, slurred speech, unsteadiness, and a fluctuating depressed level of consciousness unresponsive to naloxone. Her exam shows lethargy with preserved arousal to voice, complete horizontal and vertical gaze limitation with sluggishly reactive isochoric pupils, no papilledema, intact gag, spontaneous movement of all limbs, and lower-limb hyporeflexia, with a level of consciousness oscillating enough to warrant intubation. CT and MRI are normal, CSF shows albuminocytologic dissociation with negative infectious studies, and EEG shows non-specific diffuse slowing; she improves with methylprednisolone and IVIG, awakening with external ophthalmoplegia, ataxia, and persistent hyporeflexia. The unifying representation — ophthalmoplegia, areflexia, and ataxia (Miller Fisher syndrome) plus altered consciousness — is Bickerstaff brainstem encephalitis, an anti-GQ1b antibody syndrome on the Guillain-Barré spectrum, confirmed by a positive anti-GQ1b antibody.

## Case Narrative

A CP Solvers Neurology VMR with **Aaron Berkowitz** teaching, **Maria** presenting, and two discussants at different training stages — **Mattias** (about to begin neurology residency) and **Mathieu** (bringing an emergency-medicine lens). The chief concern is two vague words: **increased drowsiness**.

**Framing "drowsiness."** Berkowitz first forces the split that organizes everything: is this a **sleep disorder** (narcolepsy, obstructive sleep apnea — which can counterfeit dementia, headache disorders, and daytime inattention) or an **impairment of [[Level vs Content of Consciousness|arousal]]**? Mattias localizes the latter along the **[[Reticular Activating System|reticular activating system]]** — upper pons/lower midbrain projecting to the bilateral thalami and then the bilateral hemispheres — so impaired consciousness means a bihemispheric, bithalamic, or brainstem process, *or* a systemic one (hypoxia, hypercarbia, anemia, toxic-metabolic). Over that he lays [[Localization × Time Course|localization × time course]], subdividing tempo into hyperacute, acute, subacute, and chronic, and preferring a **medical-vs-neurologic** first gate to a cause-mnemonic like [[MIST Mnemonic|MIST]]. Mathieu supplies the **ED coma algorithm**: ABCs, then the two instantly reversible misses — **glucose** and an **opioid toxidrome** — then a structured **[[Coma Exam|coma exam]]** to separate structural from non-structural causes, then labs and CT.

**HPI.** Maria reveals a **13-year-old girl** transferred for unresponsiveness — an age that surprises the room. The day before, she came home from school with **headache, blurry vision, and sleepiness**; her mother gave Tylenol and she napped, then woke **tripping over things and babbling incomprehensibly, looking "drunk or still asleep,"** complained again of blurry vision, and went back to bed. She slept through the entire day; the next morning she was very hard to rouse, confused, and dysarthric, prompting the ED visit. **Naloxone at the outside hospital did nothing.** She was previously healthy, had **"the flu" the prior week**, and — poignant context — her father had recently died of a gunshot wound, and the family had just moved to a neighborhood where, her mother worried, drug use was common.

**Building the differential.** Berkowitz sets the age aside to read the pure syndrome: **headache + blurry vision + altered arousal = raised [[Intracranial Pressure|intracranial pressure]]**. He teaches the Monro-Kellie compartments (brain ~80%, CSF ~10%, venous blood ~10%) and the steep pressure-volume curve, the **[[False Localizing Sign|false-localizing]] sixth-nerve palsy** (its long intracranial course over the clivus), and [[Papilledema|papilledema]] (which takes days, so its absence excludes nothing). Causes live in the brain (mass, hemorrhage, fulminant demyelination, infection), the CSF (communicating vs non-communicating hydrocephalus), or the venous system — the last being Mathieu's astute exception, **[[Cerebral Venous Sinus Thrombosis|cerebral venous sinus thrombosis]]**, the one "vascular" cause that crescendos rather than strikes instantly. Then he restores the context — a 13-year-old with a recent viral prodrome — and **anchors on [[ADEM|acute disseminated encephalomyelitis]]** (the post-infectious CNS "cousin of Guillain-Barré," commoner in children, often **[[MOGAD|anti-MOG]]**-associated within the [[NMO Spectrum Disorder|NMO/MOG]] spectrum), while keeping [[HSV Encephalitis|HSV]] and other encephalitides, a possible **intoxication** (unknown neighborhood drugs), and **acetaminophen hepatotoxicity** (correcting himself that Reye syndrome is *aspirin*) on the board.

**The exam.** Vitals normal. She is lethargic but opens her eyes spontaneously and to voice, does not answer, and is disoriented to time, place, and person. Her eyes open spontaneously but **cannot move horizontally or vertically**; pupils are **isochoric, normal-sized, with a sluggish but present light reflex**; there is **no papilledema**, an intact gag, and no facial palsy. She does not follow commands but moves all four limbs spontaneously, with **lower-limb hyporeflexia**; her consciousness oscillates between stupor and lethargy, prompting intubation for airway protection. Mattias flags the crucial ambiguity — was the gaze limitation **true ophthalmoplegia** or merely coma? — noting that **[[Oculocephalic Reflex|oculocephalics]]** (untested) would have distinguished them, and that the waxing-waning course raises **[[Non-Convulsive Status Epilepticus|non-convulsive status]]**. Berkowitz reads the exam as non-localizing and reassuring (reactive symmetric pupils, no anisocoria), teaching that acute CNS insults can present with hyporeflexia before upper-motor-neuron signs emerge, and that complete ophthalmoplegia with headache would instead point at **[[Cavernous Sinus Thrombosis|cavernous sinus thrombosis]]**.

**The data that took everything off the table.** The **drug panel and BMP are normal, the CT is normal, and — strikingly — the MRI is normal.** The LP shows a **normal opening pressure, near-acellular fluid, adequate glucose, and a very high protein** — **[[Albuminocytologic Dissociation|albuminocytologic dissociation]]** — with negative Gram stain, cultures, and viral panel; the **EEG shows diffuse slowing without seizures**. Antibodies are sent (with a long turnaround), and she is empirically pulsed with **methylprednisolone and IVIG**. Roughly a week later she is weaned from the ventilator and awake, oriented, but speaking with difficulty and now showing **external ophthalmoplegia, generalized weakness, a broad-based gait, persistent hyporeflexia, and cerebellar signs** (finger-nose and heel-knee ataxia, dysdiadochokinesia). Berkowitz treats the normal MRI as decisive — it removes ADEM and mass lesions — and free-associates through his zebras (**[[Cysticercotic Encephalitis|cysticercotic encephalitis]]** in young girls; **[[Post-Infectious Cerebellitis|post-infectious cerebellitis]]**, often post-VZV; mitochondrial PEO/Kearns-Sayre, excluded by the steroid response) while conceding the only hard signal is the CSF protein.

**The pivot — problem representation.** [[Anti-NMDA Receptor Encephalitis|Anti-NMDA]] is too fast and lacks the prodrome; the pelvic imaging is normal. Then **Mattias reframes the case**: ophthalmoplegia + hyporeflexia + ataxia + albuminocytologic dissociation, taken *in isolation*, is **[[Miller Fisher Syndrome|Miller Fisher syndrome]]** (anti-GQ1b) — the altered mental status is the only thing that doesn't fit. Berkowitz completes the synthesis he had missed by fixating on the encephalopathy: **Miller Fisher plus altered consciousness is [[Bickerstaff Brainstem Encephalitis|Bickerstaff brainstem encephalitis]]** — invoking his mentor Alan Ropper's out-of-print Guillain-Barré monograph and the debate (Ropper's skepticism vs the Japanese pathologic evidence) over whether Bickerstaff is truly CNS Guillain-Barré or ICU delirium. Maria confirms it: after an extensive literature search to justify an expensive antibody (prompted by an UpToDate line on ophthalmoplegia "plus syndromes"), the **anti-GQ1b returned positive**, and the final diagnosis is **Bickerstaff brainstem encephalitis** — "a syndrome inside a syndrome inside a syndrome," nested within both the Guillain-Barré and ADEM spectra. Valeria's teaching points note that reflexes can be brisk *or* absent across the spectrum (Bickerstaff, Bickerstaff + GBS overlap), underscoring how porous these categories remain.

### Illness Scripts and Bias

- **[[Problem Representation|Problem representation]] as the pivot.** The case is a live demonstration that the diagnosis is selected by the one-line summary: "encephalopathic child, post-viral" pointed at ADEM, while "ophthalmoplegia + areflexia + ataxia (+ altered consciousness)" pointed at [[Bickerstaff Brainstem Encephalitis|Bickerstaff]] — and choosing what to *exclude* from the representation was as decisive as what to include.
- **[[Localization × Time Course|Localization × time course]] and the level/content split.** Fixing arousal to the RAS/thalami/hemispheres and reading a *subacute post-infectious* tempo kept the differential in the immune-mediated lane, the same Berkowitz frame anchoring [[CPS 153 - Intermittent Altered Mental Status and Non-Lesional Autoimmune Epilepsy|CPS 153]], [[CPS 269 - Headache and Leptomeningeal Lymphomatosis|CPS 269]], [[CPS 359 - Altered Mental Status and Anti-NMDA Receptor Encephalitis|CPS 359]], [[CPS 386 - Rapidly Progressive Dementia and Intravascular Large B-Cell Lymphoma|CPS 386]], and [[CPS 402 - Altered Mental Status and Disseminated Varicella-Zoster|CPS 402]].
- **[[Anchoring Bias|Anchoring]], named and then broken by the team.** Berkowitz fixated on the altered mental status and on ADEM; the resident's reframe to Miller Fisher was the datum he had discounted (the hyporeflexia), showing how a second reasoner supplies the representation the first cannot see.
- **[[Test Interpretation|Test interpretation]] driving Bayesian moves.** A **normal MRI** actively lowered ADEM/mass/CVST rather than merely reassuring; **[[Albuminocytologic Dissociation|albuminocytologic dissociation]]** was read as non-specific immune-mediated nerve/root inflammation; the **[[Oculocephalic Reflex|oculocephalic reflex]]** was flagged as the missing test to disambiguate ophthalmoplegia from coma; and the cost of anti-GQ1b prompted a literature-guided, targeted order rather than a shotgun panel.
- **[[Diagnostic Humility|Diagnostic humility]] and treating under uncertainty.** With antibodies pending for weeks, the team treated empirically (steroids, IVIG) for a presumed immune-mediated process, tolerated a "post-infectious something" label, and acknowledged the genuine nosologic debate over whether Bickerstaff is a distinct entity — comfortable acting before certainty.
- **The nested-spectrum script.** [[Anti-GQ1b Antibody Syndrome|Anti-GQ1b]] ties [[Guillain-Barré Syndrome|GBS]], [[Miller Fisher Syndrome|Miller Fisher]], and Bickerstaff into overlapping phenotypes, reinforcing that "every rule of Guillain-Barré has an exception" and that reflexes, sphincter, and CNS involvement all vary across the axis.

## Teaching Discussion

- Parse "drowsiness" before localizing it: distinguish a sleep disorder (narcolepsy, OSA, insomnia) from an impairment of arousal, and remember arousal localizes to the bilateral hemispheres, the thalami, or the brainstem reticular activating system — or to systemic physiology — then apply localization × time course over a medical-vs-neurologic gate.
- In the emergency setting, treat altered consciousness as an algorithm first: ABCs, correct the instantly reversible misses (glucose, an opioid toxidrome/naloxone trial), perform a structured coma exam to separate structural from non-structural causes, and image early.
- Read headache plus visual change plus depressed arousal as raised intracranial pressure; localize it to the Monro-Kellie compartments (brain, CSF, blood), watch for a false-localizing sixth-nerve palsy, and recall that absent papilledema does not exclude raised ICP and that CVST is the venous cause that crescendos.
- Use the oculocephalic reflex to distinguish true ophthalmoplegia (a cranial-nerve/brainstem or neuromuscular process) from a comatose patient who simply is not looking around, and let complete ophthalmoplegia with headache raise cavernous sinus thrombosis.
- Let problem representation drive the diagnosis: collapsing ophthalmoplegia, areflexia, and ataxia into Miller Fisher syndrome — and adding the altered consciousness that "doesn't fit" to reach Bickerstaff brainstem encephalitis — shows that the right one-line summary, including what you deliberately leave out, selects the answer, and that a team builds a better representation than one mind.
- Interpret a normal MRI as information that actively removes ADEM, mass lesions, and CVST, and read albuminocytologic dissociation as a non-specific marker of immune-mediated nerve/root disease (not only Guillain-Barré); when a confirmatory antibody is expensive, use the clinical pattern and the literature to order the right one (anti-GQ1b) rather than a broad panel.
- Situate Bickerstaff brainstem encephalitis within the anti-GQ1b spectrum alongside Guillain-Barré and Miller Fisher, expect overlap (reflexes may be depressed or brisk, CNS involvement is debated), and treat empirically with immunotherapy (IVIG, steroids) while the diagnosis is being secured.
