## Summary

> [!summary] Scan Summary
> A Neurology VMR on **"right hand weakness for 3–4 months"** that is a **[[Anatomical Approach|peripheral-localization]] masterclass**, ending on the rare **[[Hirayama Disease|Hirayama disease]]** (juvenile distal-upper-limb monomelic amyotrophy). A **19-year-old woman** has **painless, purely motor** wasting and weakness of the **right hand** — first dorsal interosseous atrophy, trouble holding a pen, a finger "getting stuck" in her pocket — with **no sensory symptoms and no neck pain**. Berkowitz frames **[[Isolated Hand Weakness|isolated hand weakness]]** as almost always **peripheral or a tiny central lesion**: the **[[Hand Knob|hand-knob]]** motor cortex (an "omega" bump, the huge-handed homunculus) can give a **[[Pseudo-Radial Pattern|pseudo-radial]]** cortical hand — but the [[Corona Radiata|corona radiata]]/capsule would drag in face and arm, and the [[Spinal Cord|cord]] can't isolate hand fibers of the [[Corticospinal Tract|corticospinal tract]] without leg signs. He teaches the peripheral engine — **learn five nerves of the arm cold**: **[[Musculocutaneous Nerve|musculocutaneous]]** (biceps, "make a muscle"), **[[Axillary Nerve|axillary]]** (deltoid) — both too short to lesion in isolation, so an isolated deficit means *proximal/root/plexus* — then **[[Radial Nerve|radial]]** ("**BEST**" = brachioradialis, extensors, supinator, triceps), **[[Median Nerve|median]]**, and **[[Ulnar Nerve|ulnar]]**. The hand splits on a **middle-finger line**: **ulnar = intrinsic hand muscles** (interossei — PAD/DAB, adductor pollicis, hypothenar), **median = APB/thenar**, with the long-finger flexors divided; **[[Carpal Tunnel Syndrome|median compresses at the wrist]]** (sparing forearm-innervated long flexors), **[[Cubital Tunnel Syndrome|ulnar at the elbow]]** (worse — takes the medial long flexors too), and ulnar **sensation is only distal to the wrist** (the medial forearm is the **[[Medial Antebrachial Cutaneous Nerve|MABC]]**, off the medial cord). The catch: exam shows **ulnar (interossei) + partial radial (wrist extension) + median (APB) — but *incompletely***, which **can't be three partial nerve lesions**, so localization moves **proximal** (plexus/root) — and then the **left hand is involved too**. The decisive reframe is that the picture is **pure motor, atrophic, areflexia-sparing, and bilateral-asymmetric**, which (once **[[Anchoring Bias|the early ulnar anchor is dropped]]**) opens the **[[Motor Neuron Disease|motor-neuron-disease]]** door: **[[ALS|ALS]]** (the **[[Split Hand|split hand]]** — APB + FDI wasting, hypothenar spared), **[[Hirayama Disease|Hirayama]]**, **[[Multifocal Motor Neuropathy|MMN]]** (anti-GM1, conduction block, CIDP-cousin), and **[[Parsonage-Turner Syndrome|Parsonage-Turner]]** (usually painful, AIN "OK-sign"). **[[EMG|EMG/NCS]]** — "an extension of the exam" — shows **low CMAP amplitudes with preserved velocities and no conduction block (axonal)** plus **active denervation and reduced recruitment in C7–C8–T1 muscles**; supine cervical MRI is normal. The answer is delivered by the key maneuver: a **[[Flexion Cervical MRI|flexion cervical MRI]]** showing **ventral displacement of the posterior dura (C5–C7) compressing the anterior cord/anterior horn cells** — a **pre-ganglionic** localization with focal chronic denervation, *not* diffuse MND — i.e. **Hirayama disease** (Berkowitz and the presenter had each seen exactly one prior case; CP Solvers has now covered it twice).

## Pearls

- **[[Isolated Hand Weakness|Isolated hand weakness]] is peripheral or a *tiny* central lesion — rarely anything in between.** The [[Corona Radiata|corona radiata]]/internal capsule packs hand, face, and leg fibers together (you'd get more than a hand), and the [[Spinal Cord|cervical cord]] can't isolate the hand fibers of the [[Corticospinal Tract|corticospinal tract]] without leg signs. A small **[[Hand Knob|hand-knob]]** cortical stroke (the pre-central "omega") *can* mimic a peripheral hand — a **[[Pseudo-Radial Pattern|pseudo-radial]]** pattern, since pyramidal weakness preferentially hits extensors.
- **Learn five arm nerves cold, and let the exam tell you nerve vs root vs plexus vs central.** **[[Musculocutaneous Nerve|Musculocutaneous]]** (biceps) and **[[Axillary Nerve|axillary]]** (deltoid) are so short that an *isolated* lesion is rare — an isolated deficit there means think **proximal/root/plexus**. **[[Radial Nerve|Radial]] = "BEST"** (brachioradialis, extensors, supinator, triceps). Deficits that don't map to one nerve → look for a shared **root**; deficits spanning several nerves → **plexus** or **multiple nerves** or **central**.
- **Split the hand on a middle-finger line: [[Ulnar Nerve|ulnar]] does the intrinsics, [[Median Nerve|median]] does the thenar.** Ulnar = interossei (**PAD**/**DAB**), adductor pollicis, hypothenar; median = **APB** (thenar). **[[Carpal Tunnel Syndrome|Median at the wrist]]** spares the forearm-innervated long finger flexors (weak long flexors = a *rare* forearm median lesion); **[[Cubital Tunnel Syndrome|ulnar at the elbow]]** (commoner than Guyon's canal) is more disabling because it also takes the medial long flexors.
- **Use sensory anatomy to set the proximal-distal boundary.** The **[[Ulnar Nerve|ulnar]] sensory territory is only distal to the wrist**; medial-forearm numbness means you're proximal (medial cord / **[[Medial Antebrachial Cutaneous Nerve|MABC]]** / C8), not in the ulnar nerve. Palmar cutaneous branches bypass the carpal/Guyon canals, so wrist-level compression **spares the palm base** — sparing that *begins just distal to the wrist*.
- **A [[Motor Neuron Disease|pure-motor]], atrophic, painless, sensory-sparing hand with preserved reflexes points at the anterior horn — think motor neuron disease.** Beyond **[[ALS|ALS]]** (watch for the **[[Split Hand|split hand]]**: APB + first-dorsal-interosseous wasting with a *spared hypothenar*), remember **[[Hirayama Disease|Hirayama disease]]**, **[[Multifocal Motor Neuropathy|MMN]]** (anti-GM1, conduction block), and **[[Parsonage-Turner Syndrome|Parsonage-Turner]]** (usually painful, AIN "OK-sign," proximal/scapular).
- **[[EMG|EMG/NCS]] is "an extension of the exam": amplitude = axons, velocity = myelin.** Low **CMAP/SNAP amplitudes** with **preserved conduction velocity and no [[Conduction Block|conduction block]]** = **axonal**; focal slowing/**conduction block** = demyelinating (the signature of MMN/CIDP). **[[F-wave|F-waves]]** interrogate the proximal segment. Here: axonal loss with active denervation in **C7–C8–T1** muscles.
- **[[Hirayama Disease|Hirayama disease]] hides on a normal supine MRI — you have to image in flexion.** Juvenile, distal-upper-limb, often oblique **forearm amyotrophy**; a **[[Flexion Cervical MRI|flexion cervical MRI]]** reveals **ventral displacement of the posterior dural sac** compressing the **anterior horn cells**, and the **focal, chronic, pre-ganglionic** denervation (with normal reflexes) distinguishes it from diffuse ALS/SMA.

## Problem Representation

A healthy 19-year-old woman presents with **three to four months of painless, purely motor weakness and wasting of the right hand** — first dorsal interosseous atrophy, difficulty holding a pen, and a finger catching in her pocket (impaired adduction) — **without any sensory symptoms, pain, or neck pain**, and without leg, bulbar, or upper-motor-neuron complaints. Examination shows **normal proximal arm, leg, cranial-nerve, and reflex findings** but a **right-hand deficit that crosses single-nerve territories**: intrinsic-hand (ulnar) weakness with interosseous and hypothenar atrophy, partial wrist-extension (radial) weakness, thumb-abduction (median/APB) weakness, and distal finger-flexion weakness — with **preserved reflexes, no sensory loss**, and **mild contralateral (left) hand involvement**. Because the deficits do not fit one nerve, are **bilateral, pure motor, atrophic, and areflexia-sparing**, localization moves from a single peripheral nerve to the **anterior horn / motor neuron**. **Nerve conduction studies show low motor amplitudes with preserved velocities and no conduction block (axonal), normal sensory responses**, and **EMG shows active denervation with reduced recruitment in C7–C8–T1 muscles**; a supine cervical MRI is normal. A **flexion cervical MRI** demonstrates **ventral displacement of the posterior dural sac from C5 to C7 with anterior cord compression and myelopathic signal**, establishing a **pre-ganglionic, focal, chronic** process — **Hirayama disease** (juvenile monomelic/distal-upper-extremity amyotrophy), rather than a diffuse motor neuron disease such as ALS or SMA.

## Case Narrative

Aikant presents a 19-year-old woman with **right hand weakness for 3–4 months**, and Berkowitz asks Valeria to localize. She runs the motor pathway from **[[Motor Cortex|motor cortex]]** → **[[Corona Radiata|corona radiata]]** → **[[Corticospinal Tract|corticospinal tract]]** → cord → root → **[[Brachial Plexus|plexus]]** → nerve, reasoning that a purely hand deficit sits most likely in the **distal periphery** (radial/median/ulnar or their roots) and that she'd want the precise pattern of finger movements. Berkowitz endorses the framework and sharpens it: for **[[Isolated Hand Weakness|truly isolated hand weakness]]**, the top localizations are **peripheral nerve** (common compressive lesions — median at the wrist, ulnar at the elbow) or a **small central lesion**. He teaches the **[[Hand Knob|hand-knob]]** — the "omega" bump on the pre-central gyrus where the homunculus's oversized hand lives — a small stroke there can produce **[[Pseudo-Radial Pattern|pseudo-radial]]** isolated hand weakness. The **[[Corona Radiata|corona radiata]]** would drag in face/arm/leg, and the **[[Spinal Cord|cervical cord]]** can't isolate hand fibers without leg signs (a hemicord pushing only medial C8 fibers is unconvincing) — so **brain (small) or periphery** leads, pending exam and the 3–4-month tempo.

The **history** favors periphery and pure motor: distal-onset **atrophy** (first dorsal interosseous), trouble **holding a pen**, and a finger **stuck in a pocket** — with **no numbness, paresthesia, pain, or neck pain**, and no left-hand, systemic, or autonomic symptoms. Valeria initially localizes to the **[[Ulnar Nerve|ulnar nerve]]** (interossei + fifth-finger function), wondering about a slow compressive lesion at **[[Guyon Canal|Guyon's canal]]**, and floats a "locked hand" myopathy — which Berkowitz gently redirects (**[[Myotonic Dystrophy|myotonic dystrophy]]** grip myotonia is genetic, not mitochondrial, and different from the *weakness*-based pocket sign here).

Berkowitz then delivers the episode's core **[[Schema-Based Reasoning|schema]]** — **know five arm nerves cold**, and let the exam sort nerve from root from plexus from central. **[[Musculocutaneous Nerve|Musculocutaneous]]** (biceps — "make a muscle") and **[[Axillary Nerve|axillary]]** (deltoid, teres minor) are so short that an *isolated* lesion is rare, so a deficit there flags a **proximal/root/plexus** problem. **[[Radial Nerve|Radial]] = "BEST"**: brachioradialis, extensors, supinator, triceps (with brachioradialis the flexion exception, C6 but radial — a lever to separate it from musculocutaneous C6 biceps). The hand is the hard part: draw a **line down the middle finger** — **[[Ulnar Nerve|ulnar]]** does the **intrinsics** (interossei via **PAD/DAB**, adductor pollicis, hypothenar), **[[Median Nerve|median]]** does the **thenar** (**APB**), and the **long finger flexors** divide (FDS all median; FDP median+ulnar; lumbricals 1–2 median, 3–4 ulnar). He layers on the **compression anatomy**: **[[Carpal Tunnel Syndrome|median at the wrist]]** spares the forearm-innervated long flexors (weak long flexors ⇒ rare forearm median lesion), while **[[Cubital Tunnel Syndrome|ulnar at the elbow]]** (commoner than Guyon's) is more disabling; and the **sensory** clincher — **ulnar sensation is only distal to the wrist** (the **[[Medial Antebrachial Cutaneous Nerve|medial antebrachial cutaneous nerve]]** off the medial cord does the medial forearm), and palmar cutaneous branches bypass the canals, so wrist compression spares the palm base. He also teaches the **root axis**: each root feeds several nerves and each nerve carries several roots, so muscles that don't fit one nerve should be checked against a **shared root** (interossei ≈ **C8–T1**).

The **exam** breaks the single-nerve hypothesis. Beyond a normal proximal, leg, cranial-nerve, and reflex exam, the **right hand** shows **first-dorsal-interosseous + hypothenar atrophy**, **very weak finger abduction/adduction and thumb abduction** (ulnar intrinsics + median APB), **mildly weak wrist extension** (radial), and **distal finger-flexion weakness** — plus **mild left-hand weakness**, **normal reflexes throughout**, and **normal sensation**. Valeria recognizes this can't be one nerve, nor even three *partial* nerve lesions, and — cued by Berkowitz — confronts the **bilaterality**: two symmetric plexus or cortical lesions "missing the heart and lungs" (Marty Samuels's "lesser organs") is implausible. The reframe lands on the **[[Anatomical Approach|localization principle]]**: a **pure-motor, atrophic, painless, sensory-sparing, reflex-sparing, bilateral** hand syndrome points at the **[[Anterior Horn Cell|anterior horn]]** — and Berkowitz names the early **[[Anchoring Bias|anchoring]]** on a focal ulnar lesion that had to be undone.

He runs the **[[Motor Neuron Disease|motor-neuron-disease]] and mimics** differential: **[[ALS|ALS]]** (worrying but possible; watch for the **[[Split Hand|split hand]]** — APB + first-dorsal-interosseous wasting with a *relatively spared hypothenar*), **[[Hirayama Disease|Hirayama disease]]** (juvenile, distal-upper-limb, reflex-sparing, from cervical flexion-related anterior-cord compression — normal supine MRI, abnormal flexion MRI, often striking **forearm amyotrophy**), **[[Multifocal Motor Neuropathy|multifocal motor neuropathy]]** (a pure-motor, distal-UE, **conduction-block**, anti-GM1 CIDP-cousin), and **[[Parsonage-Turner Syndrome|Parsonage-Turner]]** (immune brachial neuritis — usually severe shoulder/axilla pain then weakness, favoring proximal plexus and the **[[Anterior Interosseous Nerve|anterior interosseous nerve]]** "OK-sign," here against it given the painlessness).

Asked for **one test**, Valeria chooses **[[EMG|EMG/nerve conduction studies]]**, and Berkowitz frames it as **"an extension of the neurologic exam"** — a way to interrogate muscles not obviously weak and pin the localization. The study shows **low motor amplitudes (CMAPs) with preserved velocities and no [[Conduction Block|conduction block]]** and **normal sensory responses** — an **axonal** picture — plus **active denervation (fibrillations) and reduced recruitment (single fast-firing polyphasic units) in C7–C8–T1 muscles**; the **supine cervical MRI is normal**. Berkowitz reads the physiology (amplitude = axons, velocity = myelin), notes MMN's absent conduction block here, and — cued that the *supine* MRI was normal — predicts **Hirayama**. Aikant confirms: a **[[Flexion Cervical MRI|flexion cervical MRI]]** shows **ventral displacement of the posterior dura from C5 to C7 with anterior cord compression, mid-cord thinning, and myelopathic signal**, and multilevel subluxation on flexion. The **focal, chronic, pre-ganglionic** denervation of C7–C8–T1 with **normal reflexes** and **no upper-motor-neuron signs** argues *against* diffuse ALS/SMA and *for* **[[Hirayama Disease|Hirayama disease]]** — a diagnosis each of them had seen only once, and CP Solvers's second case.

### Illness Scripts and Bias

- **[[Anatomical Approach|Localization]] is the whole case.** Isolated hand → periphery or small central; the **[[Hand Knob|hand-knob]]** pseudo-radial cortical mimic; the cord's inability to isolate hand fibers; and the move from single nerve → root → plexus → **[[Anterior Horn Cell|anterior horn]]** as the data force it.
- **[[Schema-Based Reasoning|Schema]]: five arm nerves + the median/ulnar hand split + root axis.** **[[Musculocutaneous Nerve|Musculocutaneous]]**/**[[Axillary Nerve|axillary]]** (short → isolated lesion rare), **[[Radial Nerve|radial]]** "BEST," **[[Median Nerve|median]]** (APB/thenar, carpal tunnel), **[[Ulnar Nerve|ulnar]]** (intrinsics, cubital tunnel), and the "doesn't-fit-one-nerve → shared root (C8–T1)" logic.
- **[[Anchoring Bias|Anchoring]] and its correction.** The early, reasonable **ulnar** anchor had to be dropped once the exam crossed nerve territories and went bilateral — Berkowitz names it explicitly ("we got anchored early on a focal peripheral localization").
- **[[Test Interpretation|Test interpretation]].** **[[EMG|EMG]] as an extension of the exam**; **amplitude = axons, velocity = myelin**; **[[Conduction Block|conduction block]]** as the MMN/demyelinating signature (absent here); **[[F-wave|F-waves]]** for the proximal segment; and the pivotal **[[Flexion Cervical MRI|flexion MRI]]** when the supine study is normal.
- **[[Base Rate|Base rate]] vs the zebra.** Common compressive neuropathies (median/ulnar) top the pre-test list, but the pure-motor, bilateral, atrophic pattern forces a rare **[[Hirayama Disease|Hirayama]]** diagnosis — the discipline of following the syndrome over the base rate once red flags accumulate.
- **[[Illness Tempo|Tempo]].** A **subacute-to-chronic** (months), slowly progressive, painless course fits an indolent anterior-horn process and argues against acute plexitis.
- **[[Illness Script|Illness scripts]].** **[[Hirayama Disease|Hirayama disease]]** (juvenile flexion-myelopathy monomelic amyotrophy), **[[ALS|ALS]]** / **[[Split Hand|split hand]]**, **[[Multifocal Motor Neuropathy|MMN]]** (anti-GM1, conduction block), **[[Parsonage-Turner Syndrome|Parsonage-Turner]]** (painful, AIN, proximal), **[[Carpal Tunnel Syndrome|median]]**/**[[Cubital Tunnel Syndrome|ulnar]]** entrapments, **[[Brachial Plexopathy|brachial plexopathy]]**, and the **[[Hand Knob|hand-knob]]** cortical stroke.

## Teaching Discussion

- Treat isolated hand weakness as peripheral or a small central lesion, because the corona radiata and internal capsule bundle hand, face, and leg fibers together and the cervical cord cannot isolate hand corticospinal fibers without leg signs — but keep a hand-knob cortical stroke (pseudo-radial pattern) on the list.
- Memorize five arm nerves — musculocutaneous, axillary, radial ("BEST"), median, and ulnar — with the muscles and roots each serves, so that a deficit either maps to one nerve, points to a shared root when it does not, or signals plexus or central disease when it spans several nerves.
- Split the hand on a middle-finger line: the ulnar nerve drives the intrinsic hand muscles (interossei, adductor pollicis, hypothenar) and the median nerve the thenar (abductor pollicis brevis); median compression at the wrist spares the forearm-innervated long finger flexors, while ulnar compression at the elbow is more disabling and also takes the medial long flexors.
- Use sensory anatomy to fix the proximal-distal boundary: ulnar sensation is only distal to the wrist, so medial-forearm numbness implies a lesion at the medial cord or C8 (via the medial antebrachial cutaneous nerve), not the ulnar nerve itself.
- Suspect motor neuron disease when a hand is purely motor, atrophic, painless, sensory-sparing, and bilateral with preserved reflexes — think ALS (with its split hand), Hirayama disease, multifocal motor neuropathy (conduction block, anti-GM1), and Parsonage-Turner (usually painful and proximal) — and read the EMG as an extension of the exam, with amplitude reflecting axons and velocity reflecting myelin.
- Diagnose Hirayama disease by imaging in flexion: a normal supine cervical MRI does not exclude it, and a flexion study reveals ventral displacement of the posterior dura with anterior-cord/anterior-horn compression, while the focal, chronic, pre-ganglionic denervation and normal reflexes distinguish it from diffuse ALS or SMA.
- Guard against anchoring: an early, reasonable single-nerve (ulnar) localization must yield when the exam crosses nerve territories and becomes bilateral — following the syndrome over the first impression is what reopens the correct differential.
