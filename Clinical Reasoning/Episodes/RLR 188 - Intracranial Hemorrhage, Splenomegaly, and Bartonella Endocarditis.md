> Rabih presents a case he picked up off the overnight holdover list — a man in his 50s with essentially no medical history except heavy alcohol use, transferred to the SF VA for **[[Pancytopenia|pancytopenia]] of unclear etiology**. The story opens with abrupt severe headache and a combined subarachnoid + intraparenchymal **[[Intracranial Hemorrhage|hemorrhage]]** from a small **[[Distal Cerebral Aneurysm|distal cerebral aneurysm]]** (coiled), incidental pancytopenia (WBC ~2, Hgb 7–8, platelets 70–80k), **massive [[Splenomegaly|splenomegaly]]**, a normal echo/EKG, and a *normal* bone marrow. Reza refuses to accept "pancytopenia" as a monolith — he wants the **grade of each lineage, the timeline, and whether there is inflammation** — and he flags the aneurysm's *distal* location as atypical for a berry aneurysm, invoking Aaron Berkowitz's pearl that a distal vessel argues for a **[[Mycotic Aneurysm|hematogenously seeded]]** process. He resists blaming the [[Thrombocytopenia|thrombocytopenia]] for the bleed (platelets >50k rarely bleed spontaneously; a sequestering spleen still mobilizes platelets) and runs the **[[Massive Splenomegaly|massive-splenomegaly schema]]** — excluding [[Portal Hypertension|portal hypertension]] (alcohol but no cirrhotic stigmata) and homing in on a **[[Reticuloendothelial System|RES]]-tropic, vessel-seeking** organism that could unify spleen, vessel, and marrow. Over three weeks the case turns twice: markedly elevated ESR/CRP (>100) with **negative ANA, positive RF, positive p-[[ANCA|ANCA]], and low C3/C4**, then a rising creatinine and a urine sediment of dysmorphic RBCs and casts — a **[[Glomerulonephritis|glomerulonephritis]]**. Reza reads the **[[Hypocomplementemia|low complement]]** as the tell: this is *immune-complex*, not pauci-immune, disease, so the positive [[ANCA|MPO/p-ANCA]] and RF are likely **[[Test Interpretation|false-positive noise]]** from an over-active immune system — pushing toward an infectious mimic rather than a primary vasculitis. The **[[Unifying Diagnosis|unifying]]** answer is a **[[Culture-Negative Endocarditis|culture-negative endocarditis]]** whose endovascular + RES tropism explains everything: of the vast granuloma differential, only **[[Bartonella]], [[Brucellosis|Brucella]], and [[Q Fever|Coxiella]]** seed the endocardium. A stray cat, heavy alcohol (the classic dissemination risk), and sky-high **[[Bartonella Endocarditis|Bartonella henselae]]** titers seal it — a disease so reliably a **[[ANCA Vasculitis Mimics|vasculitis/GN mimic]]** that rheumatologists reflex "MPO-positive + hypocomplementemic = Bartonella until proven otherwise."

## Pearls

- **Do not accept "pancytopenia" as one thing — grade each lineage and add timeline and inflammation.** A CBC labeled pancytopenic may really be a *severe anemia with mild bicytopenia*, which changes the entire approach; before deploying any schema, ask the actual counts (here WBC ~2, Hgb 7–8, platelets 70–80k — proportional, mild-to-moderate), how fast it evolved, and whether the patient is inflamed. [[Pancytopenia|Pancytopenia]] with a **normal marrow** shifts the question to peripheral destruction/sequestration or something the marrow isn't showing.
- **A *distal* cerebral aneurysm is not a garden-variety berry aneurysm — its location argues for a hematogenously seeded process.** Berry aneurysms sit proximally at the circle of Willis; a distal, cortical aneurysm should raise **[[Mycotic Aneurysm|mycotic (infective) aneurysm]]** (or, per Berkowitz's teaching, seeded TB). Always ask the two separate questions — *why is there an aneurysm* and *why did it bleed* — and don't let a platelet count above 50k (which rarely bleeds spontaneously, and which a sequestering spleen can still mobilize) satisfy the second question.
- **Work massive splenomegaly through a schema, starting with portal hypertension.** Cirrhotic stigmata are specific but insensitive, so use imaging (no nodular liver, ascites, or varices here) to exclude **[[Portal Hypertension|portal hypertension]]** despite the alcohol history; then move through infiltration (amyloid), **granulomatous** infection and malignancy (lymphoma), viruses (HIV/EBV/CMV), and leishmaniasis — and recognize when the true target is a **[[Reticuloendothelial System|RES]]-seeking** organism rather than a primary splenic disease.
- **Low complement reclassifies a glomerulonephritis as immune-complex — not pauci-immune — disease, and that discordance unmasks a mimic.** Primary ANCA-associated vasculitis is **pauci-immune with normal complement**; a positive p-[[ANCA|ANCA]]/MPO alongside **low C3 and C4** is internally contradictory and should prompt a hunt for an **[[ANCA Vasculitis Mimics|ANCA mimic]]**. Confirm a positive p-ANCA with the specific **[[ANCA|MPO and PR3]]** assays and remember that **[[Rheumatoid Factor|rheumatoid factor]]** turns positive whenever the immune system is busy — neither antibody should be over-weighted.
- **When one process must explain everything, look for a bug with dual endovascular + RES tropism.** The seeded distal aneurysm, the massive spleen, the cytopenias, and the immune-complex GN are best read as "true, true, and *related*" through a **[[Unifying Diagnosis|single mechanism]]**; something in the *blood* that lodges in *vessels* and the *reticuloendothelial system* is far more parsimonious than parallel rheumatologic, hematologic, and neurologic diseases.
- **Culture-negative endocarditis narrows a huge granuloma differential to three endovascular-tropic organisms: Bartonella, Brucella, and Coxiella.** TB, histoplasma, sporothrix, and NTM seed the lung/RES but *not* the endocardium; of the three that do, cat exposure and **heavy alcohol use** (the best-described risk factor for *dissemination*) make **[[Bartonella Endocarditis|Bartonella henselae]]** the answer — a textbook **[[ANCA Vasculitis Mimics|mimic]]** producing false-positive RF, false-positive MPO-ANCA, and hypocomplementemic GN. The rheumatology reflex: MPO-positive + hypocomplementemic ⇒ **[[Bartonella]] until proven otherwise**.

## Problem Representation

A man in his 50s with no significant past medical history except alcohol use disorder presents with abrupt severe headache and is found to have a combined subarachnoid and intraparenchymal hemorrhage arising from a small distal cerebral aneurysm (coiled), together with incidental deep pancytopenia (WBC ~2,000, hemoglobin 7–8 g/dL, platelets 70,000–80,000), massive splenomegaly, and — on two separate biopsies — a normal bone marrow, with a normal transthoracic echo and EKG. Over roughly three weeks the picture evolves: a transesophageal echo shows a small aortic-valve lesion read as a benign variant; nutritional labs, LDH, and peripheral smear are unrevealing; and markedly elevated ESR and CRP (>100 each) are accompanied by a negative ANA, positive rheumatoid factor, negative anti-CCP, positive p-ANCA, and low C3 and C4, while a rising creatinine (0.7→1.4), new hypertension, and a nephritic urine sediment (hematuria, proteinuria, dysmorphic red cells, and casts) signal a hypocomplementemic glomerulonephritis. The unifying process is a culture-negative (Bartonella henselae) endocarditis — acquired from a stray cat and disseminated in the setting of heavy alcohol use — whose endovascular and reticuloendothelial tropism produces the mycotic distal aneurysm and hemorrhage, the massive spleen and cytopenias, and an immune-complex glomerulonephritis with false-positive rheumatoid factor and MPO/p-ANCA, confirmed by extremely high Bartonella serologies.

## Case Narrative

Rabih explains how he came to know the case: he was not the treating physician but scans the overnight **holdover email** of admissions, and at a VA that is "90% bread and butter," a middle-aged man **transferred from an outside hospital for [[Pancytopenia|pancytopenia]] of unclear etiology** stood out enough that he copied the name onto his follow-up list. The patient is in his 50s with essentially no medical history and no medications; the only relevant social history is **significant alcohol use**.

Reza opens by refusing to swallow the label "pancytopenia" whole. Citing a CPC by Dr. Aird that reframed a "pancytopenia" as a *severe anemia with mild bicytopenia*, he insists on the **grade of each lineage** before any approach — and names his three first questions: **what are the actual cell counts, what is the timeline, and is there inflammation**. Rabih tells the story as it unfolded rather than as a clean problem representation: the patient presented with **abrupt severe headache** and was found to have a **combined subarachnoid and intraparenchymal [[Intracranial Hemorrhage|hemorrhage]]** from a small **[[Distal Cerebral Aneurysm|distal aneurysm]]** on CTA, which was coiled. During that admission he was incidentally noted to be pancytopenic (no prior CBCs), with **WBC in the 2s, hemoglobin 7–8, and platelets in the 70s–80s**; a transthoracic echo and EKG were normal, imaging revealed **massive splenomegaly**, and a **bone marrow biopsy was normal**. Unable to explain the cytopenias, the outside hospital transferred him to his home VA.

Reza works the intracranial bleed structurally. The subarachnoid space envelops brain and cord; most aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage announces itself as the "worst headache of life." The key move is the aneurysm's **location**: berry aneurysms cluster proximally, so a **distal, cortical** aneurysm is atypical and points away from the usual culprits (genetic predisposition, atherosclerosis, connective-tissue disease) toward an **embolic or hematogenously seeded** source — he recalls a case with Aaron Berkowitz in which a strikingly **distal** aneurysm turned out to be seeded **[[Mycotic Aneurysm|TB]]**. He then separates two questions that must be answered independently: **why does this patient have an aneurysm, and why did it bleed?** He explicitly declines to blame the **[[Thrombocytopenia|thrombocytopenia]]**: a platelet count above 50,000 should not bleed spontaneously, and even a sequestering spleen can mobilize platelets, so the bleed still demands its own explanation. The massive spleen becomes the third pillar — Reza notes that *mild* splenomegaly cannot be blamed for cytopenias, but **massive** splenomegaly plausibly can, which is a real clinical-reasoning step rather than a hand-wave. His instinct: **true, true, and related through a common mechanism** — something **vessel-tropic and hematogenous** linking spleen, marrow, and the seeded aneurysm.

He deploys the **[[Massive Splenomegaly|splenomegaly schema]]**. First, **[[Portal Hypertension|portal hypertension]]**: alcohol is a classic cause, but the stigmata of cirrhosis are specific, not sensitive, and imaging shows no nodular liver, ascites, or other signs — so he sets portal hypertension aside. Next, infiltration (e.g., AL amyloid — which can involve vessels and bleed, but the pattern doesn't fit), then **granulomatous** processes split into infection (TB, endemic mycoses, Q fever) and malignancy (lymphoma, primary splenic lymphoma seeding a vessel), then viruses (HIV, EBV, CMV) and leishmaniasis. What he keeps returning to is a **[[Reticuloendothelial System|RES]]-seeking organism that also reaches vessels**. His next steps: neurology on board, **blood cultures**, continuous telemetry, an **echo with bubble study**, and escalating vascular imaging (CTA → MRA → conventional angiography) to hunt for a target.

Rabih confirms diagnostic certainty took **three weeks**, underscoring the complexity, and notes the patient was on the service of the legendary clinician Gurpreet Dhaliwal. Neurology reviewed the outside data (no additional aneurysms), worried about a **cardioembolic** source given the distal aneurysm, and arranged a **transesophageal echo**, which showed a small **aortic-valve** finding read as a **benign variant**. Hematology's workup was unrevealing: normal **nutritional labs** (B12, folate, copper, vitamin C), an **LDH** of only 300–400, and a **normal peripheral smear**; the spleen was again enormous without cirrhosis, and a **repeat bone marrow biopsy was again normal**. Reza, still circling an infiltrative-or-infectious spleen with something on the valve, suggests a **PET-CT** (to gauge splenic FDG avidity and interrogate the valve) and asks the pivotal question — **is there inflammation?** He also names the entities that can make a spleen huge *and* seed a valve: **culture-negative endocarditis** from organisms like **TB or Coxiella (Q fever)**.

The tempo picks up. With the case stalling, **rheumatology** is consulted because the **ESR and CRP are both over 100**. The autoimmune panel returns a **negative ANA**, a **positive rheumatoid factor**, a **negative anti-CCP**, a **positive p-[[ANCA|ANCA]]**, and **low C3 and C4**. Meanwhile the **creatinine rises from 0.7 to 1.4**, the patient becomes **hypertensive**, and nephrology is consulted. Reza cautions against premature excitement over the serologies: a positive p-ANCA is a **screen** that must be confirmed with the specific **MPO and PR3** assays, and a positive **[[Rheumatoid Factor|RF]]** can appear whenever the immune system is active. Crucially, he reads the **[[Hypocomplementemia|very low complement]]** as evidence of an **immune-complex** process — pauci-immune ANCA vasculitis runs with *normal* complement — so a syndrome of aneurysm + massive spleen + inflammation is far easier to explain through **infection** (seeding the valve, the spleen, and the kidney) or **cancer** (marantic endocarditis, paraneoplastic GN) than through a primary rheumatologic disease, and he reminds the audience that massive splenomegaly is itself hard to attribute to rheumatologic disease (Felty, Still, sarcoid being the rare exceptions).

Nephrology, convinced by **serial urinalyses showing hematuria, proteinuria, dysmorphic red cells, and casts**, is poised to biopsy a **[[Glomerulonephritis|glomerulonephritis]]**. Rabih delivers the final problem representation: a 54-year-old man with **three parallel processes** — an **aneurysm that bled**, **massive splenomegaly with cytopenias**, and a **glomerulonephritis with hypocomplementemia** — decorated by clues (alcohol use, a "benign" aortic-valve finding, a positive RF, a positive MPO, an active urine sediment). Rheumatology's assessment is that the picture is "concerning but **not rheumatological**," recommending a broad **non-rheumatologic** search. Reza reasons that the GN is real (dysmorphic reds and casts don't lie) but is likely a **[[Immune Complex Glomerulonephritis|secondary immune-complex]]** consequence — possibly an **MPGN pattern** — of a systemic process, while the true pathology lives in the **spleen and the seeded vessel**. He predicts a **hematogenous, uncommon infection** and, prompted by asking about housing and animals, learns the patient **cares for a stray cat**. That unlocks the **[[Bartonella|Bartonella]]** script: cats transmit **B. henselae** (scratch/lick), *Bartonella* is the leading cause of **[[Culture-Negative Endocarditis|culture-negative endocarditis]]** among the granulomatous, endovascular-tropic organisms, and it can produce **splenomegaly**. His next step: **Bartonella henselae serology** and empiric doxycycline coverage.

The **Bartonella titers return sky-high** (~1:1000+), confirming **Bartonella henselae endocarditis**. Rabih supplies the epidemiology: beyond cat exposure, disseminated Bartonella endocarditis strikes disproportionately **older men who drink heavily** — for reasons unknown, **alcohol use disorder is the best-described risk factor for dissemination** — matching the patient exactly. He crystallizes the schema Reza rebuilt from first principles: many organisms reach the **[[Reticuloendothelial System|RES]]** (mycobacteria, histoplasma, Coxiella, Brucella), but only three of the granuloma-causing infections have the **endovascular tropism** to cause **culture-negative endocarditis** — **[[Bartonella]], [[Brucellosis|Brucella]], and [[Q Fever|Coxiella]]** (TB, histo, sporothrix, and NTM do **not**) — with Bartonella dominating in the US and Q fever in southern France. He closes on the case's teaching heart, citing the rheumatologist (Brett Dietz), who published a *Clinical Problem Solvers*/NEJM case of exactly this pattern: **Bartonella as a masquerader** of ANCA vasculitis, so reliably that a positive **MPO with hypocomplementemia** should read as **Bartonella until proven otherwise**. Rabih reflects that he passively absorbed the case for weeks and *never* thought of Bartonella, whereas Reza reached it by **deliberately slowing down** — a live demonstration of the power of effortful, hypothesis-driven **[[Dual Process Reasoning|analytical reasoning]]** over passive pattern absorption.

### Illness Scripts and Bias

- **Refusing the label ([[Schema-Based Reasoning|schema-based reasoning]]).** Reza will not run a "pancytopenia" schema until he knows the *grade* of each lineage, the timeline, and the inflammatory state — the Aird CPC reframing (pancytopenia → severe anemia with mild bicytopenia) as a guard against approaching a mislabeled problem.
- **Location as a discriminator ([[Distal Cerebral Aneurysm|distal aneurysm]] → [[Mycotic Aneurysm|mycotic]]).** The single most productive observation is that a **distal** cerebral aneurysm is not a berry aneurysm; anatomy redirects the differential toward a hematogenously seeded (infective) process, echoing the Berkowitz "distal = seeded" pearl.
- **Separating linked questions.** *Why an aneurysm* and *why did it bleed* are handled independently, and the **[[Thrombocytopenia|thrombocytopenia]]** is explicitly refused as a lazy answer for the bleed (platelets >50k, mobilizable from a sequestering spleen).
- **The complement tell ([[Test Interpretation|test interpretation]]).** **[[Hypocomplementemia|Low C3/C4]]** converts a would-be pauci-immune **[[ANCA|ANCA]]** vasculitis into an **[[Immune Complex Glomerulonephritis|immune-complex]]** process — an internal contradiction that unmasks an **[[ANCA Vasculitis Mimics|ANCA mimic]]**. Positive p-ANCA and **[[Rheumatoid Factor|RF]]** are treated as screening/nonspecific signals of an active immune system, to be confirmed (MPO/PR3) rather than anchored upon ([[Anchoring Bias|anchoring bias]] resisted).
- **Parsimony under pressure ([[Unifying Diagnosis|unifying diagnosis]]).** Rather than three diseases, Reza insists on one **vessel- and [[Reticuloendothelial System|RES]]-tropic** organism to explain the seeded aneurysm, the massive spleen/cytopenias, and the immune-complex GN — "true, true, and related."
- **Building the [[Culture-Negative Endocarditis|culture-negative endocarditis]] schema from the granuloma schema.** Of ~30 granuloma-causing infections, only **[[Bartonella]], [[Brucellosis|Brucella]], and [[Q Fever|Coxiella]]** carry endovascular tropism — a schema-within-a-schema that, combined with the **cat** and the **alcohol**, lands on Bartonella.
- **[[Illness Script|Illness script]] with a dissemination gate.** Bartonella henselae is usually a *localized* mucocutaneous illness (scratch, lymphadenopathy); systemic **endocarditis** requires a dissemination risk factor, and **alcohol use disorder** is the best-described one — the host context is part of the script.
- **[[Dual Process Reasoning|System 2 over System 1]], and [[Illness Tempo|tempo]]/[[Diagnostic Humility|humility]].** Rabih passively absorbed the case for weeks and never reached Bartonella; Reza got there by deliberately slowing down and generating hypotheses. Three weeks to certainty underscores that some illnesses must be given **time to declare** — and that effortful reasoning, not pattern recognition, closed the gap.

## Teaching Discussion

- Before applying any cytopenia framework, quantify the problem: grade the depth of anemia, leukopenia, and thrombocytopenia separately, establish the timeline, and judge whether the patient is inflamed — a "pancytopenia" is often really a dominant single-lineage cytopenia that demands a different approach.
- Read aneurysm location as data: proximal berry aneurysms are the common intrinsic type, whereas a distal, cortical aneurysm should raise a hematogenously seeded (mycotic) process, and every aneurysmal bleed deserves two separate explanations — why the aneurysm exists and why it ruptured.
- Resist attributing a hemorrhage to thrombocytopenia when platelets exceed ~50,000, and remember that even a large, sequestering spleen can mobilize platelets, so the bleed still requires its own mechanism.
- Approach massive splenomegaly through a deliberate schema — portal hypertension first (using imaging, since cirrhotic stigmata are insensitive), then infiltration, granulomatous infection and malignancy, viruses, and leishmaniasis — and recognize when the unifying culprit is a reticuloendothelial-tropic organism rather than a primary splenic disease.
- Let complement levels reclassify a glomerulonephritis: low C3/C4 indicate immune-complex disease and are discordant with pauci-immune ANCA vasculitis, so a positive p-ANCA or MPO in a hypocomplementemic patient should trigger a search for an ANCA mimic rather than a diagnosis of primary vasculitis.
- Interpret a positive p-ANCA as a screen to be confirmed with MPO and PR3, and treat a positive rheumatoid factor as a nonspecific marker of an active immune system — neither should anchor the diagnosis.
- When a seeded aneurysm, massive splenomegaly, cytopenias, and glomerulonephritis coexist, favor a single hematogenous organism with both endovascular and reticuloendothelial tropism over multiple concurrent diseases.
- Narrow culture-negative endocarditis from the broad granuloma differential to the three endovascular-tropic infections — Bartonella, Brucella, and Coxiella (Q fever) — and use exposure history (cats for Bartonella henselae) and dissemination risk (heavy alcohol use) to choose among them.
- Anticipate that Bartonella endocarditis mimics ANCA-associated vasculitis with false-positive rheumatoid factor and MPO/p-ANCA and a hypocomplementemic glomerulonephritis: MPO-positivity with low complement is Bartonella until proven otherwise, confirmed by serology, and treated rather than biopsied and immunosuppressed.

- A **mycotic aneurysm** card — distal/cortical location, infective endocarditis as source, imaging escalation (CTA→MRA→angiography), and the two questions (why an aneurysm, why did it bleed).
