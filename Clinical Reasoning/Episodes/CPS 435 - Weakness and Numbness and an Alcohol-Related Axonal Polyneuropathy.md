## Summary

> [!summary] Scan Summary
> A Neurology VMR on **"weakness and numbness"** that is really a clinic on **[[Peripheral Neuropathy|neuropathy red flags]]** and — like [[CPS 438 - Bilateral Leg Weakness and an Unresolved Immune-Mediated Neuropathy|CPS 438]] the month before — ends on an explicit **[[Occam's Razor|Occam]] → [[Hickam's Dictum|Hickam]]** pivot. A **41-year-old woman** has **3 months of progressive, distal-onset numbness and difficulty walking**, pins-and-needles in feet then hands, unhelped by pregabalin — carrying outside labels of **[[POTS|POTS]]** and **[[Mast Cell Activation Syndrome|MCAS]]** and a "negative" prior MRI/neuro workup. Berkowitz frames the two-symptom localization: weakness can sit anywhere on the neuraxis (brain → brainstem → cord → root → nerve → [[Neuromuscular Junction|NMJ]] → muscle), numbness anywhere *except* NMJ and muscle — so **weakness + numbness together lifts NMJ and muscle off** (unless two problems), and a **symmetric, distal, positive-sensory** picture points squarely at the **[[Peripheral Neuropathy|peripheral nerve]]**. The discussion's spine is the **[[Neuropathy Red Flags|garden-variety vs "behaving-badly"]] distinction**: ~995/1000 neuropathies are **distal, symmetric, sensory, chronic (DSSC)** and need only the **[[Distal Symmetric Polyneuropathy Workup|AAN first-pass]] — A1C, B12, and SPEP with immunofixation, *stop*** ([[Test Interpretation|no TSH, ANA, RPR, B6, or heavy metals]] on the first pass; syphilis doesn't cause peripheral neuropathy, ANA lands you at 1:80 "off to the races," arsenic is high from seafood). Violate **any** of the four (here: **subacute tempo + apparent autonomic involvement**) and the workup opens up. The differential runs through **[[CIDP|CIDP]]** and its **[[Nodopathy|nodal/paranodal antibody]]** carve-outs (autonomic involvement is atypical for CIDP), **[[Amyloidosis|amyloid]]** (neuropathy + heart), **[[Connective Tissue Disease|CTD]]**-associated ([[Sjögren Syndrome|Sjögren]], lupus), and — the eventual answer — **[[Nutritional Neuropathy|nutritional/toxic]]** causes. The exam (emaciation, poor dentition, dry scaling shins, bruising, distal sensory loss, absent ankle jerks, mild proximal weakness) screams **malnutrition**, and Berkowitz reads the reflexes to call it **[[Axonal vs Demyelinating Neuropathy|axonal, length-dependent]]** despite the proximal weakness. Labs seal it by **[[Return to the Bedside|returning to the history]]**: **A1C 4.9** (strikingly low), **MCV 103**, **folate 2.5**, **AST:ALT ~2:1** — prompting a re-ask that (with the husband's collateral) reveals **daily drinking and poor intake**; **[[Phosphatidylethanol|PEth]] 654** confirms heavy alcohol use, and EMG shows a **length-dependent, sensory-greater-than-motor axonal polyneuropathy**. Diagnosis: **alcohol-related axonal polyneuropathy** with a nutritional component (thiamine repletion started empirically — serum thiamine is unreliable). The coda names the reasoning trap directly: the team had tried to **lump** the POTS/tachycardia/orthostasis into one unifying neuropathy (**"Occam's razor bias"**), but the autonomic story fragments — sinus tachycardia from **[[Alcohol Withdrawal|withdrawal]]**/holiday heart, orthostasis from **dehydration/deconditioning** — so **Hickam's dictum** ("as many diseases as they please") fits better than a forced single diagnosis.

## Pearls

- **Weakness *and* numbness together lifts the [[Neuromuscular Junction|neuromuscular junction]] and muscle off the list** (both spare sensation) — unless the patient has two problems. Weakness alone runs the whole neuraxis; numbness runs it too *except* NMJ/muscle. A **symmetric, distal, positive-sensory** (pins-and-needles) pattern then points at the **[[Peripheral Neuropathy|peripheral nerve]]**; unilateral-with-face suggests brain, and a dissociated hemicord sensory level suggests **[[Brown-Séquard Syndrome|Brown-Séquard]]**.
- **Learn the "garden-variety" neuropathy so you can spot the one [[Neuropathy Red Flags|behaving badly]].** ~995/1000 are **distal, symmetric, sensory, chronic (DSSC)**. Violate *any* one — **acute/subacute tempo, motor or autonomic involvement, marked asymmetry (mononeuritis multiplex), or non-length-dependent/proximal spread** — and you are licensed to expand the workup. Here the tempo (subacute) and the autonomic story are the red flags.
- **The [[Distal Symmetric Polyneuropathy Workup|AAN first-pass neuropathy workup]] is just three tests: A1C (or glucose tolerance), B12, and [[SPEP|SPEP with immunofixation]] — then stop.** ([[Test Interpretation|The England/AAN guideline.]]) Diabetes and B12 are common (metformin and PPIs impair B12 absorption; nitrous causes functional deficiency); the SPEP catches the rare paraproteinemic neuropathy. **TSH, ANA, RPR, B6, HIV, heavy metals are second-pass at best** — and reflexive panels backfire (ANA → 1:80 "off to the races"; arsenic runs high from seafood; **syphilis does not cause a peripheral neuropathy**).
- **[[Axonal vs Demyelinating Neuropathy|Axonal = length-dependent]] (feet-first, toxic/metabolic/nutritional); demyelinating = non-length-dependent** (hands and feet together, reflexes lost *everywhere* even beyond symptomatic zones). A bedside gauge: align the arm along the leg — fingertips reach ~mid-shin, so a deficit only "to the knee" should not yet reach the forearm. Preserved upper-limb reflexes here argue **length-dependent axonal**, favoring a nutritional/toxic cause.
- **Think nutritional/toxic when the systemic exam shows malnutrition — and remember thiamine's lab lies.** **[[Alcohol-Related Neuropathy|Alcohol]]** (directly neurotoxic *and* via deficiency), **[[Thiamine Deficiency|B1]]** (dry beriberi = GBS-like neuropathy; wet = heart failure), **[[B12 Deficiency|B12]]**/**[[Copper Deficiency|copper]]** (subacute combined degeneration; copper needs hyperzincemia), **[[Pellagra|niacin]]**, **[[Scurvy|vitamin C]]**, and **[[ANAN|acute nutritional axonal neuropathy]]**. **Serum thiamine doesn't reflect brain/nerve stores — treat empirically**, don't wait on the level.
- **[[Return to the Bedside|Return to the history]] when the labs whisper alcohol.** **A1C 4.9** (implausibly low), **MCV 103**, **isolated low folate**, and **AST:ALT ~2:1** are a metabolic fingerprint of occult alcohol use and poor intake; a targeted re-ask plus **collateral from the husband** unmasked daily drinking, and **[[Phosphatidylethanol|PEth]] 654** confirmed it.
- **Beware "[[Occam's Razor|Occam's razor bias]]" — sometimes you must invoke [[Hickam's Dictum|Hickam]].** The instinct (drilled since step 1) to make *every* finding fit one diagnosis nearly forced the POTS/tachycardia/orthostasis into the neuropathy. In fact it fragments: **sensorimotor axonal neuropathy** (alcohol/nutrition) + **sinus tachycardia** (withdrawal/holiday heart) + **orthostasis** (dehydration, deconditioning) — three problems, not one.

## Problem Representation

A 41-year-old woman with outside diagnoses of POTS (on nadolol) and mast cell activation syndrome (on cetirizine) presents with **three months of insidiously progressive, distal-onset numbness and difficulty walking**, with pins-and-needles spreading from feet to hands, unrelieved by therapeutic pregabalin, and a prior "negative" neurologic workup including MRI. She has heavy environmental/animal exposures (farm, chickens, pigs, dogs, 1950s house), vapes, uses occasional Delta-8, and denies preceding infection, bowel/bladder symptoms, or GI complaints. Examination shows **emaciation, poor dentition, dry scaling shins, and bruising from coordination-related falls**, with **distal-predominant sensory loss** (reduced pinprick to below the knees and to the distal forearms), **absent ankle jerks with preserved upper-limb reflexes**, and **mild proximal (hip-flexion) weakness** without ataxia — a **length-dependent axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy** on a background of apparent malnutrition. Labs reveal a strikingly **low A1C (4.9)**, **macrocytosis (MCV 103)**, **isolated low folate (2.5)**, low magnesium, and **AST:ALT ~2:1** with otherwise normal B1/B6/B12/vitamin D and negative heavy metals, HIV, RPR, Borrelia, inflammatory markers, CSF (mildly elevated protein, no oligoclonal bands), and MRI. A history re-taken with collateral discloses **daily alcohol use and poor intake**, and **PEth is 654** (heavy use); EMG confirms a **length-dependent, sensory > motor, axonal polyneuropathy**. The diagnosis is **alcohol-related axonal polyneuropathy** with a nutritional component, treated with empiric thiamine and multivitamin repletion — the dysautonomia best explained not by one unifying process but by withdrawal-related tachycardia plus deconditioning/dehydration orthostasis.

## Case Narrative

Sarah opens with only **"weakness and numbness"** in a 41-year-old woman, and Berkowitz asks Vijay and Sebastian to localize each. Vijay runs both symptoms from cortex to peripheral nerve, keeping **[[Illness Tempo|time course]]** central; Sebastian notes that the two together, without a devastating central presentation, make **[[Peripheral Neuropathy|peripheral neuropathy]]** the front-runner. Berkowitz draws the map explicitly: **weakness** localizes anywhere (brain, brainstem, cord, roots, nerves, NMJ, muscle), **numbness** anywhere *except* NMJ and muscle — so hearing **both** lets you **take NMJ and muscle off** (unless two problems). Weakness-plus-numbness in the **brain** would usually carry more (face involvement, mental-status change); in the **[[Spinal Cord|cord]]** it is common, and a hemicord **[[Brown-Séquard Syndrome|Brown-Séquard]]** gives dissociated sensory findings ± bowel/bladder (small [[Multiple Sclerosis|MS]] lesions may spare them, unlike the full-cord transverse myelitis of **[[NMO|NMO]]**/**[[MOG Antibody Disease|MOG]]**); **symmetric** distal disease points to nerve or cord. With just age, sex, and two symptoms, **MS** and — if ascending — **[[Guillain-Barré Syndrome|GBS]]** come to mind, but tempo and distribution will decide.

The history sharpens the localization: **three months of progressive, distal-onset** numbness and gait difficulty, **positive sensory symptoms** (pins-and-needles) in feet and hands, pregabalin without benefit, and a prior negative MRI — plus the outside **POTS** and **MCAS** labels and heavy exposure history. Sebastian localizes to the **peripheral nerves with autonomic involvement** and offers infectious (farm), inflammatory (**CIDP**/variants), and metabolic/nutritional (**B12**, copper, zinc) buckets, flagging that COVID-vaccine–associated small-fiber neuropathies exist and that this "inflammatory individual" nudges him toward autoimmunity. Prompted to define **[[CIDP|CIDP]]**, he gives the chronic-GBS demyelinating polyradiculoneuropathy framing; Berkowitz uses the exchange to teach that **CIDP is a syndrome diagnosed by clinical + neurophysiologic criteria, not a biomarker**, that its **[[Nodopathy|nodal/paranodal antibody]]** carve-outs (neurofascin, contactin) are more severe and IVIG-refractory, and — the key point — **autonomic dysfunction is atypical for CIDP**, which starts to argue against it here. Vijay layers on **[[Amyloidosis|amyloid]]** (autonomic + neuropathy), toxic/metabolic causes, and **[[Connective Tissue Disease|connective-tissue]]** disease (**[[Sjögren Syndrome|Sjögren]]**, lupus) given a woman of this age.

Berkowitz consolidates: this is a **subacute sensory-motor-autonomic neuropathy**, and he teaches the **[[Neuropathy Red Flags|garden-variety vs behaving-badly]]** framework. About **995/1000** neuropathies are **distal, symmetric, sensory, chronic (DSSC)** — for those, the **[[Distal Symmetric Polyneuropathy Workup|AAN first-pass workup is only three tests: A1C, B12, and SPEP with immunofixation]]**, then stop. He and Sebastian contrast this with the reflexive "fill-the-cart" panels (B6, TSH, ANA, RPR, HIV) taught elsewhere, and Berkowitz prunes them: **ANA** yields 1:80 red herrings, **heavy metals** false-positive (arsenic from seafood — "stop eating fish and recheck"), and **syphilis does not cause a peripheral neuropathy** (tabes is near-extinct). But **any** DSSC violation — **acute/subacute tempo, motor or autonomic involvement, marked asymmetry (mononeuritis multiplex), or non-length-dependent/proximal spread** — licenses the full second-pass hunt for autoimmunity, exotic toxins, and nutritional deficiency. This neuropathy is already "behaving badly" (subacute, autonomic), so the field is open.

The **exam** reframes the case from the exotic to the mundane. Beyond mild hypertension, the striking findings are **systemic**: **poor dentition, dry scaling shins, bruising from falls, emaciation with low muscle mass**. The neuro exam shows **normal cranial nerves and mental status**, **5/5 strength except 4/5 hip flexion/extension**, **normal fine finger movement, no drift**, **reflexes 2+ upper / 1+ patellar / absent ankle with mute plantar responses**, and **reduced pinprick to below the knees and to the distal forearms** with intact coordination. Vijay and Sebastian converge on **malnutrition** — Sebastian reading the dentition (vitamin C), dry skin (niacin), and bruising (vitamin K) as a **globally deficient** patient, and even naming **[[Leprosy|leprosy]]** (the world's leading neuropathy cause) given the rash. Berkowitz names the group's **[[Availability Heuristic|"morning-report bias"]]** — reaching for CIDP variants and amyloid before the common nutritional causes — and works the **[[Axonal vs Demyelinating Neuropathy|length-dependence]]** question: the below-knee-plus-forearm sensory loss and the mild proximal weakness *could* suggest non-length-dependent/demyelinating disease, but the **preserved upper-limb reflexes** (a non-length-dependent process tends to knock reflexes out everywhere) and his read of the sensory exam's subjectivity (the **"[[Do-It-Again Sign|do-it-again sign]]"**) lead him to call this **length-dependent axonal** with superimposed deconditioning — a pattern that fits a **nutritional** cause, which is usually axonal.

He runs the **[[Nutritional Neuropathy|nutritional differential]]**: **B12** (usually subacute combined degeneration — cord — but can hit nerve; functional deficiency from nitrous/"whippets"), **[[Copper Deficiency|copper]]** (B12-mimic, requires hyperzincemia — the classic denture-cream CPC), **[[Thiamine Deficiency|thiamine]]** (**dry beriberi** = GBS-like neuropathy; **wet beriberi** = heart failure; Wernicke's), **[[Pellagra|niacin]]**, **[[Scurvy|vitamin C]]** (Anthony Rosen's legendary ICU-scurvy diagnosis), and the debated **[[ANAN|ANAN]]** (acute nutritional axonal neuropathy). He flags the **thiamine lab caveat** — serum levels don't reflect brain/nerve stores, so **treat empirically** — and the recurring double-diagnosis motif (a UCSF case of **CIDP** that became bedbound and *then* developed thiamine deficiency). His plan: the AAN first pass plus targeted nutritional labs, **start supplementation**, and defer heavy metals given their false-positive rate.

The **labs** point home by **[[Return to the Bedside|forcing a return to the history]]**: **CBC normal but MCV 103**, **AST 79 / ALT 42 (~2:1)**, **folate 2.5 (low)**, **magnesium 1.4 (low)**, while **B1, B6, B12, vitamin D, lead/heavy metals** are normal, **A1C is 4.9** (the lowest Berkowitz has seen), and drug/alcohol screens on admission are negative. The **AST:ALT ratio and macrocytosis** prompt the team (as they did Berkowitz) to **re-ask about alcohol** — and, with the **husband's collateral**, she discloses **daily drinking and grazing rather than eating**. A sent **[[Phosphatidylethanol|phosphatidylethanol]] (PEth) of 654** confirms heavy use; HIV, RPR, Borrelia, ESR/CRP, TSH, CSF (protein 50, no oligoclonal bands, negative BioFire and autoimmune panel), and MRI brain/spine are all unrevealing; **EMG shows a length-dependent, sensory > motor, axonal polyneuropathy**. The diagnosis is **alcohol-related axonal polyneuropathy** with a likely nutritional contribution, managed with **empiric thiamine and multivitamin**.

Berkowitz closes on the reasoning meta-lesson, and it mirrors [[CPS 438 - Bilateral Leg Weakness and an Unresolved Immune-Mediated Neuropathy|the prior month's case]]: they had tried to make the **POTS/tachycardia/orthostasis** part of one unifying neuropathy — the step-1-trained reflex to make every finding fit — which he calls **"[[Occam's Razor|Occam's razor bias]]."** Here the honest formulation invokes **[[Hickam's Dictum|Hickam's dictum]]**: a **sensorimotor axonal neuropathy** from alcohol/nutrition, **sinus tachycardia** from withdrawal (or "holiday heart"), and **orthostasis** from dehydration and deconditioning (standing for the first time in weeks) — several independent problems, not one elegant diagnosis. The outside **POTS** and **MCAS** labels were, in the end, part of the red-herring thicket (the farm, the animals, the 1950s house) that the case deliberately laid down.

## Teaching Discussion

- Localize weakness plus numbness by first removing what cannot fit: both symptoms together exclude the neuromuscular junction and muscle (which spare sensation) unless there are two problems, and a symmetric, distal, positive-sensory pattern points to the peripheral nerve rather than the brain (usually more, with face involvement) or cord (look for a dissociated Brown-Séquard level and bowel/bladder).
- Anchor on the garden-variety neuropathy — distal, symmetric, sensory, chronic — so you can recognize the one behaving badly: acute or subacute tempo, motor or autonomic involvement, marked asymmetry, or non-length-dependent/proximal spread each justify expanding beyond the first pass.
- Send the AAN first-pass workup and stop there for a garden-variety neuropathy: A1C or a glucose tolerance test, vitamin B12, and SPEP with immunofixation — and resist reflexively adding TSH, ANA, RPR, B6, HIV, or heavy metals, which are second-pass at best and generate false-positive rabbit holes.
- Distinguish axonal (length-dependent, feet-first, toxic/metabolic/nutritional) from demyelinating (non-length-dependent, hands and feet together, reflexes lost everywhere) at the bedside — using reflex distribution and the arm-to-leg length gauge — because axonal length-dependent disease reframes the search toward nutritional and toxic causes.
- Pursue nutritional and alcohol causes when the systemic exam shows malnutrition, and treat thiamine empirically because serum levels do not reflect brain or nerve stores; remember alcohol is both directly neurotoxic and a cause of multiple deficiencies, and that low A1C, macrocytosis, isolated low folate, and an AST:ALT ratio near 2:1 are clues to occult heavy drinking.
- Return to the history — and get collateral — when the labs contradict the story: a re-ask with the patient's husband and a phosphatidylethanol level converted a "negative outside workup" and inherited POTS/MCAS labels into a clear alcohol-related diagnosis.
- Beware Occam's razor bias: the reflex to unify every finding can be wrong, and Hickam's dictum ("a patient can have as many diseases as they please") better fits a picture where an axonal neuropathy, withdrawal-related tachycardia, and deconditioning orthostasis are three separate problems rather than one.
