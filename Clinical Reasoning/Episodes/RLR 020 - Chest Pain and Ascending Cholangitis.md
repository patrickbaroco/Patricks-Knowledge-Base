## Summary

> [!summary] Scan Summary
> A well-appearing 76-year-old man with insulin-dependent diabetes and atrial fibrillation on apixaban walks into the VA ED reporting a single, self-resolved episode of midsternal [[Chest Pain|chest pain]] — a fist on the sternum, gone by arrival. EKG, point-of-care [[Troponin|troponin]], and chest x-ray are unremarkable, but the first-pass labs carry a quiet abnormality: a mild hepatocellular transaminitis (AST 130, ALT 100) with a barely-elevated bilirubin. Reza runs the chest pain through the [[4+2+2 Chest Pain Schema|4+2+2 schema]] and uses *tempo* — a transient episode that resolved on its own — to retire pneumothorax, tamponade, takotsubo, and esophageal rupture/impaction, leaving [[Acute Coronary Syndrome|ACS]] and [[Pulmonary Embolism|PE]] (the latter dampened by therapeutic anticoagulation). Crucially, he refuses to abandon the thorax *or* the liver, running both diagnostic paths in parallel. Four hours in, the case detonates: fever, confusion, and tachypnea (flagged by a first-day nurse), a white count climbing 13 → 22, and liver enzymes that *double*. A repeat EKG, troponin, CT chest, and blood gas stay clean; a CT abdomen shows only nonspecific gallbladder-wall thickening with *no* ductal dilation. The break comes not from a test but from an abdominal exam Rabih had deferred — exquisite [[Murphy's Sign|right-upper-quadrant tenderness]] — prompting an [[MRCP|MRCP]] that reveals [[Cholelithiasis|gallstones]] and a stone in the common bile duct. The diagnosis is [[Ascending Cholangitis|ascending cholangitis]] from [[Choledocholithiasis|choledocholithiasis]]; the patient is drained by [[ERCP]] ("pus better") after antibiotics. The teaching spine: upper-abdominal/biliary disease can masquerade as chest pain, an AST/ALT bump can be the *first* sign of biliary obstruction before dilation or jaundice appear, and the diagnostician's job is to run multiple 12-mile jogs at once and let the unproductive ones fall away.

## Pearls

- **Use the [[4+2+2 Chest Pain Schema|4+2+2 schema]] to name the killers first, then let *tempo* prune it.** Four cardiac ([[Acute Coronary Syndrome|ACS]], [[Aortic Dissection|dissection]], [[Cardiac Tamponade|tamponade]], [[Takotsubo Cardiomyopathy|takotsubo]]), two pulmonary ([[Pulmonary Embolism|PE]], [[Pneumothorax|pneumothorax]]) plus a shout-out to severe [[Pneumonia|pneumonia]], two esophageal ([[Esophageal Rupture|rupture]], [[Esophageal Food Impaction|impaction]]). A single episode that *resolved on its own* argues hard against pneumothorax, tamponade, takotsubo, and esophageal catastrophe — but a transient PE can still insult and settle, so it survives.
- **Therapeutic anticoagulation is itself Bayesian evidence.** A compliant patient already on apixaban for AFib is receiving what would be the *treatment* for a PE, which lowers PE's pretest-to-posttest odds before any imaging is ordered.
- **Read AST/ALT as the first time-point of a trajectory, and remember the muscle.** An AST-predominant transaminitis is most often alcohol (the 2:1 ratio need not be met), but extrahepatic sources — [[Myocardial Infarction|MI]], [[Rhabdomyolysis|rhabdomyolysis]], [[Hemolysis|hemolysis]] — mimic "liver" enzymes, so a CK belongs in the workup when the story is chest pain.
- **A mildly-thickened gallbladder wall with abnormal liver enzymes is rarely isolated [[Cholecystitis|cholecystitis]].** Uncomplicated cholecystitis barely moves the LFTs; when they rise, suspect a complication — [[Choledocholithiasis|choledocholithiasis]] or [[Ascending Cholangitis|cholangitis]] — or a different primary process driving both the wall edema and the transaminitis.
- **The absence of biliary dilation does not exclude an obstructing stone, and an AST/ALT rise can precede the ALP and bilirubin.** Stones are missed on ultrasound and CT; imaging sensitivity climbs US → CT → MRCP → EUS → ERCP, and choledocholithiasis is one of the most common causes of transaminitis in the VA population.
- **[[Ascending Cholangitis|Cholangitis]] is a life-threatening cause of jaundice that behaves like an abscess in the biliary tree — it needs drainage, not just antibiotics.** It can take a comfortable, well-appearing person and put them in the ICU within hours; jaundice is often not visible until the bilirubin exceeds ~3.

## Problem Representation

A 76-year-old man with insulin-dependent diabetes and atrial fibrillation on apixaban presents with a single transient, self-resolved episode of midsternal chest pain and appears entirely well, with a normal EKG, negative point-of-care troponin, and unremarkable chest x-ray but a mild hepatocellular transaminitis (AST 130, ALT 100, ALP 140, total bilirubin 1.2/direct 0.6) on first-pass labs; over the next few hours he develops fever, altered mental status, and tachypnea with a rising leukocytosis (13 → 22) and rapidly doubling liver enzymes (AST/ALT into the 300s, ALP into the 200s, bilirubin 2, now direct-predominant), while a repeat EKG, troponin, contrast CT of the chest, COVID PCR, and blood gas remain unrevealing and a CT abdomen shows only nonspecific gallbladder-wall thickening without ductal dilation — ultimately, marked right-upper-quadrant tenderness prompts an MRCP demonstrating cholelithiasis and a common-bile-duct stone, diagnostic of ascending cholangitis from choledocholithiasis.

## Case Narrative

Rabih presents a case he warns will require a sledgehammer. It is noon on a good day in the VA ED, and the triage board simply reads *chest pain*. In an environment where chest pain is so algorithmic that the EKG precedes the patient, Rabih walks in to find a healthy-appearing man with normal vitals, sitting comfortably. The patient — knowledgeable, a two-decade regular — explains that despite being asked about chest pain at every visit for years and always denying it, *this* morning he had one episode of pain "right here in the middle of my chest." It is gone now, but the very fact that he came in because the question had been posed to him so many times is, Reza notes, itself telling: the patient has framed his own chest pain as significant, and that framing deserves weight. The patient is 76.

Reza deploys the schema he credits to Rabih — the [[4+2+2 Chest Pain Schema|4+2+2 approach]]: four cardiac causes ([[Acute Coronary Syndrome|ACS]], [[Aortic Dissection|aortic dissection]], [[Cardiac Tamponade|tamponade]], [[Takotsubo Cardiomyopathy|takotsubo]]), two pulmonary ([[Pulmonary Embolism|PE]], [[Pneumothorax|pneumothorax]], with a third shout-out to severe [[Pneumonia|pneumonia]]), and two esophageal ([[Esophageal Rupture|rupture]], [[Esophageal Food Impaction|impaction]]) — and insists on clearing the life-threatening causes before any anatomic differential of benign disease. He then uses the *tempo* — a transient episode that resolved without intervention — as a filter: a pneumothorax would not vanish so cleanly, tamponade and takotsubo would not be transient and would carry other findings, and esophageal rupture or impaction is simply not in this shape. A PE, however, can cause its insult and settle, so it survives; and ACS fits the description of an acute anginal episode. He resolves to study the background before deploying his non-life-threatening (anatomic) schema.

The data return in real life. The EKG is normal sinus; a point-of-care troponin is negative within a minute or two. The VA problem list stares back with three items: insulin-dependent diabetes, atrial fibrillation on apixaban. First-pass labs show a chest x-ray without acute pathology, a white count of 13 with normal hemoglobin and platelets, a normal metabolic panel/creatinine/glucose, normal albumin and INR, normal urinalysis, and a normal BNP — but AST 130, ALT 100, alkaline phosphatase 140, and a total bilirubin of 1.2 with a direct of 0.6.

Reza reads the background methodically. Diabetes is a coronary risk factor, noted. Compliance with apixaban lowers the probability of PE, since the patient is already anticoagulated. The clean chest x-ray argues against bony or structural pathology. The mild leukocytosis suggests some bodily stress — and, he recalls, leukocytosis was used as a marker of ACS before troponin existed. The transaminitis is the pivot he keeps circling: is it part of the presentation or true-true-and-unrelated? Absent priors, he assumes it is new and part of the syndrome. Because AST (130) exceeds ALT (100) and AST is more ubiquitous across tissues, he runs the extrahepatic differential of "liver" enzymes — [[Myocardial Infarction|MI]], [[Rhabdomyolysis|rhabdomyolysis]], [[Hemolysis|hemolysis]] (less likely with a normal hemoglobin) — and would send a CK. He then dances back toward the thorax: repeat troponin, and a CK/CK-MB to see what is driving the AST/ALT. If the enzymes are truly hepatic, alcohol is the commonest cause, with viral hepatitides (hep C, hep B) behind it. Rabih notes, to Reza's delight, that at the VA the bilirubin is automatically fractionated — no separate order required.

Then the case turns. A first-day nurse named Bella comes to Rabih, worried: the patient is now febrile to 38.3, increasingly confused, tachypneic, and simply not who he was thirty minutes ago. Reza applauds the instinct — nurses spend far more time at the bedside and are often steps ahead, and missed nursing concern is itself a route to [[Diagnostic Error|diagnostic error]]. The fever recasts the case as one of *inflammation*: with a leukocytosis and an acute tempo, he leans toward infection over malignancy or autoimmunity and lets the symptoms localize the source. The confusion, arising after a chest complaint, does not suggest a primary CNS infection; the tachypnea could reflect inflammation, metabolic acidosis, or true pulmonary pathology. Framing the patient now as an elderly, immunocompromised diabetic with transient chest pain, fever, altered mentation, and a hepatocellular pattern, he asks for a CT chest (for x-ray-negative pneumonia, pleural, parenchymal, or mediastinal disease) extended through the abdomen and pelvis to hunt for something like a hepatic abscess.

The workup stays frustratingly clean: repeat EKG without ischemia, repeat troponin negative, COVID PCR negative, a contrast CT chest that is completely normal, a normal pH, and no lactic acidosis on VBG. But the repeat labs escalate: the white count is 22, up from 13, and the liver enzymes have *doubled* — AST and ALT into the 300s, ALP into the 200s, bilirubin now 2 and mostly direct. The CT abdomen/pelvis shows only mild gallbladder-wall thickening, no biliary ductal dilation, and no other intra-abdominal pathology.

Reza declares the one thing he is sure of: the patient is acutely, progressively inflamed, with the signals being transient chest pain, tachypnea, and a worsening hepatocellular transaminitis alongside nonspecific gallbladder-wall thickening. He reasons that this cannot be isolated [[Cholecystitis|acute cholecystitis]], because cholecystitis alone does not derange the liver enzymes unless complicated — by [[Choledocholithiasis|choledocholithiasis]] or another process. The wall thickening, he suspects, is a *consequence* of the primary problem. He raises [[Ascending Cholangitis|cholangitis]] as a life-threatening cause of jaundice, notes that jaundice is usually not seen until bilirubin exceeds 3, and — critically — that the absence of ductal dilation does not exclude obstruction, because imaging sensitivity climbs from ultrasound to CT to MRCP to EUS to ERCP, and an extrahepatic obstruction can announce itself with an AST/ALT rise *before* the ALP and bilirubin move. He walks the [[Anatomical Approach|hepatic anatomy]] — biliary obstruction, hypotension/ischemic hepatitis, [[Budd-Chiari Syndrome|Budd-Chiari]] (usually with ascites, checkable by asking the radiologist about hepatic-vein patency), then the parenchyma (viral A/B/C, CMV/HSV in the immunocompromised, drugs and toxins including Tylenol and mushrooms, and less common autoimmune hepatitis, PBC, PSC, Wilson's) — and lands on next steps of viral serologies, a careful OTC-medication history, and a CK.

The resolution is a physical exam, not a test. Rabih confesses he had *not* felt the belly initially — as he does not for most chest-pain patients — and only performed a thorough abdominal exam once the patient deteriorated. The right upper quadrant was exquisitely, dramatically tender. That single finding escalated the case and prompted an [[MRCP]], which showed multiple stones in the gallbladder and a stone in the common bile duct. The diagnosis is [[Ascending Cholangitis|ascending cholangitis]] from choledocholithiasis. Reza starts ertapenem (covering gram-negatives and anaerobes) or ceftriaxone plus metronidazole and calls gastroenterology: cholangitis behaves like an abscess in the biliary system and must be drained. The patient undergoes [[ERCP]] — "pus better" — and the kidney injury and confusion are all explained by the biliary tract infection.

In reflection, both hosts sit with how a patient who reported chest pain with a fist to the sternum, appeared totally well, and had negative cardiac workups ended up in the ICU for cholangitis four hours later. Rabih is disarmingly honest that he does not know what he would do differently — most chest pain is ACS, PE, or nothing, and not everyone with chest pain "needs" liver enzymes — but concedes that it was those initial LFTs that were the clue, and that upper-abdominal pathology can refer as chest pain. Reza recounts a recent case of his own: a young patient with RUQ pain and an AST/ALT in the 700–800s, no biliary dilation on ultrasound or CT, whom he was convinced had intrahepatic (viral/autoimmune) disease — until the enzymes down-trended over days and revealed a transient biliary obstruction. He credits a study by Tony Brew showing choledocholithiasis is among the most common causes of transaminitis in the VA, and a schema by team member Lindsay Shipley on extrahepatic obstruction and the sensitivity of the various imaging modalities.

## Reasoning Breakdown

### Bayesian Updates

| Data point | Hypothesis raised/adjusted | Direction of update |
|---|---|---|
| Single transient episode, self-resolved | Pneumothorax, tamponade, takotsubo, esophageal rupture/impaction | Lowered (tempo retires them) |
| Same transient tempo | PE (can insult then settle) | Retained |
| Patient's own emphasis on the episode | Chest pain as clinically significant | Raised (weighted up) |
| Compliant on apixaban for AFib | Pulmonary embolism | Lowered (already anticoagulated) |
| Diabetes | Coronary artery disease | Raised (risk factor) |
| Normal EKG, negative troponin ×2 | ACS | Lowered |
| Mild leukocytosis (13) | Bodily stress; pre-troponin marker of ACS | Nonspecific, noted |
| AST 130 > ALT 100 | Alcohol; extrahepatic (MI, rhabdo, hemolysis) | New problem; prompts CK |
| Fever 38.3, confusion, tachypnea | Acute inflammation → infection | Raised (recasts case) |
| WBC 22 (up from 13), LFTs doubled | Progressive acute process | Escalated |
| Normal CT chest, COVID neg, normal pH/no lactate | Pneumonia, pulmonary source, metabolic acidosis | Lowered |
| Gallbladder-wall thickening, *no* ductal dilation | Isolated cholecystitis | Lowered (LFTs too deranged) |
| Worsening hepatocellular pattern, direct bili rising | Biliary obstruction / cholangitis | Raised (dilation absence not exclusionary) |
| Marked RUQ tenderness on deferred exam | Biliary pathology | Pivot finding |
| MRCP: cholelithiasis + CBD stone | Choledocholithiasis → ascending cholangitis | Confirmed |
| ERCP yields pus | Cholangitis | Confirmed (drained) |

### Illness Scripts and Bias

- **[[Anchoring Bias|Anchoring]] on the presenting complaint, actively resisted.** The gravitational pull of a chest-pain chief complaint — reinforced by a chest-pain-algorithmic ED — could have kept the entire workup thoracic. Reza repeatedly "dances back" to the troponin and CK even as the data pull him toward the liver, but never lets the chest pain *own* the case; both hosts name this parallel traversal, rather than committing early, as the crux skill.
- **[[Parallel Reasoning|Parallel diagnostic paths]] over premature commitment.** Rabih's closing image — running two 12-mile jogs at once (chest pain *and* liver enzymes) and hoping one falls away as unproductive — is the episode's meta-lesson. The diagnostic path is not one road but several traversed simultaneously; here the liver-enzyme path reached the destination while the cardiac path fell off.
- **[[Framing Effect|Framing]] by the patient's own emphasis.** Reza explicitly up-weights the chest pain because the patient — a 20-year regular who had always denied chest pain — chose to present *this* episode. The patient's framing of his symptom as a departure from baseline is treated as legitimate signal, not discounted as worried-well.
- **[[Availability Heuristic|Availability]] and the AST/ALT reflex.** Reza's stored association that "liver enzymes" have extrahepatic sources (MI, rhabdo, hemolysis) is availability working correctly — but it also keeps him tethered to the thorax slightly longer than the biliary answer warranted; the discriminating move was letting the *trajectory* and the direct-bilirubin shift redirect him.
- **Missed nursing concern as an error mode ([[Diagnostic Error|diagnostic error]]), averted.** The case is rescued by a first-day nurse's instinct; Reza names the failure to trust bedside nursing observation as a genuine route to diagnostic delay, and models the opposite.
- **Honesty about a deferred exam.** Rabih's transparent admission that he skipped the abdominal exam initially — as he does for most chest-pain patients — is offered as the episode's central takeaway: normalizing mistakes and learning from them with pride rather than fear, rather than performing infallibility.

## Teaching Discussion

- Name the chest-pain killers before anything else with the [[4+2+2 Chest Pain Schema|4+2+2 schema]], then let *tempo* do the early triage: a single, self-resolved episode retires pneumothorax, tamponade, takotsubo, and esophageal rupture/impaction, while a transient PE and ACS survive because both can insult and settle or wax and wane.
- Treat an established treatment as pretest evidence: a patient reliably anticoagulated for AFib is already receiving PE therapy, which lowers PE's posterior probability before imaging.
- An AST/ALT elevation is a single time-point on a curve, and "liver enzymes" are not liver-specific — AST especially is ubiquitous, so MI, rhabdomyolysis, and hemolysis belong on the differential and a CK is cheap insurance when the complaint is chest pain.
- Gallbladder-wall thickening with deranged liver enzymes should not be filed as simple cholecystitis; uncomplicated cholecystitis barely touches the LFTs, so a hepatocellular pattern signals a complication (choledocholithiasis, cholangitis) or a separate primary process, with the wall edema often a downstream consequence.
- The absence of ductal dilation does not exclude an obstructing stone. Imaging sensitivity for choledocholithiasis rises from ultrasound to CT to MRCP to EUS to ERCP, stones are missed on the lower-sensitivity modalities, and an AST/ALT rise can *precede* the alkaline phosphatase and bilirubin — so the trajectory of the enzymes, not a single negative scan, should drive escalation.
- Cholangitis is a drainable, life-threatening cause of jaundice — an abscess in the biliary tree — and demands source control (ERCP) alongside antibiotics that cover gram-negatives and anaerobes; jaundice is often invisible until bilirubin exceeds ~3, so waiting for it is waiting too long.
- Upper-abdominal and biliary disease can refer as chest pain in a minority of patients, which is the humbling reversal here: we readily invoke extra-abdominal causes of abdominal pain (heart failure, PE, pneumonia) but rarely reach for an abdominal cause of chest pain — and the first clue was the routine liver enzymes.
- The soft skills are the medicine: a first-day nurse's instinct cracked the case, a deferred abdominal exam finally localized it, and the host's willingness to say "I don't know what I'd do differently" models the humility that makes learning from a near-miss possible.

## Evergreen Note Updates

- [ ] Create **[[4+2+2 Chest Pain Schema]]** — Rabih's chest-pain framework: 4 cardiac (ACS, aortic dissection, tamponade, takotsubo), 2 pulmonary (PE, pneumothorax; + severe pneumonia as an honorary third), 2 esophageal (rupture, impaction); clear the life-threatening causes first, then filter by tempo.
- [ ] Create **[[Ascending Cholangitis]]** — obstruction + infection of the biliary tree; Charcot's triad (fever, RUQ pain, jaundice) and Reynolds' pentad; behaves like a biliary abscess requiring drainage (ERCP) plus antibiotics; jaundice often absent until bilirubin > 3.
- [ ] Create **[[Choledocholithiasis]]** — CBD stone; can present with an AST/ALT rise *before* ALP/bilirubin; one of the most common causes of transaminitis in the VA (Tony Brew); often missed on US/CT with normal-caliber ducts.
- [ ] Create **[[Extrahepatic Biliary Imaging Sensitivity]]** — Lindsay Shipley's schema: US → CT → MRCP → EUS → ERCP, with rising sensitivity for stones and the caveat that a negative low-sensitivity study does not exclude obstruction.
- [ ] Create **[[Hepatocellular vs Cholestatic Pattern]]** — AST/ALT-predominant vs ALP/bilirubin-predominant; AST:ALT ratio and half-lives; extrahepatic sources of AST/ALT (MI, rhabdomyolysis, hemolysis).
- [ ] Create **[[Parallel Reasoning]]** — running multiple diagnostic hypotheses simultaneously ("two 12-mile jogs at once") rather than anchoring on one path; contrast with premature closure.
- [ ] Update **[[Referred Pain]]** — add biliary/upper-abdominal pathology as an under-recognized cause of *chest* pain (the reverse of the usual extra-abdominal-causes-of-abdominal-pain reflex).
- [ ] Update **[[Anchoring Bias]]** and **[[Framing Effect]]** — with the resisted-chest-pain-anchor and patient-self-framing exemplars from this case.

## Handbook Opportunities

- A **4+2+2 chest pain** quick card — the ten causes on one page, annotated with which are excluded by a transient/self-resolved tempo.
- An **AST/ALT elevation** reference — hepatocellular pattern, AST:ALT interpretation, and the extrahepatic mimickers (MI, rhabdo, hemolysis) with the "send a CK" reflex.
- A **choledocholithiasis / cholangitis** card — the trajectory clue (enzymes rise before dilation/jaundice), imaging sensitivity ladder, and the drainage-plus-antibiotics management.
- A **biliary imaging sensitivity** ladder — US → CT → MRCP → EUS → ERCP, with test characteristics for detecting CBD stones.
- A **"chest pain with abnormal LFTs"** pattern card — when biliary disease masquerades as chest pain, and the exam/lab clues that should redirect the workup.
