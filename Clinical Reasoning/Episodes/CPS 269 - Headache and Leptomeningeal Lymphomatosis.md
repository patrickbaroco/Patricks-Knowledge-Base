## Summary

> [!summary] Scan Summary
> A neurology Virtual Morning Report with neurologist **Aaron Berkowitz** on the pairing of **[[Headache|headache]] and [[Altered Mental Status|altered mental status]]** — two chief concerns that, together, are a red flag. A **52-year-old woman with high-risk diffuse large B-cell lymphoma** three months into treatment (last chemotherapy two weeks earlier) has three months of occipital headache that, over the week before admission, became holocephalic and severe, with nausea, vomiting, and progressive somnolence until she no longer recognized her daughter and spoke nonsensically. Berkowitz frames headache ([[Secondary Headache|primary vs secondary]]) and AMS ([[Level vs Content of Consciousness|level vs content of consciousness]]) through his master equation — differential = [[Anatomical Approach|localization]] × [[Illness Tempo|time course]] — and invokes his mentor's aphorism that **"the first ten things on the differential for a neurologic symptom in a cancer patient are the cancer,"** while warning against the [[Anchoring Bias|anchoring]] that follows. He organizes [[Cancer and the Nervous System|how cancer hits the nervous system]] into three buckets — direct/metastatic involvement, treatment complications ([[PRES|PRES]], neuropathy, checkpoint-inhibitor/CAR-T neurotoxicity), and paraneoplastic plus the [[Trousseau Syndrome|hypercoagulability]] of malignancy — layered over the **immunosuppression** that reopens fungal, TB, and bacterial infection. The exam is non-focal but shows **bilateral papilledema and nuchal rigidity**, confirming raised [[Intracranial Pressure|intracranial pressure]] and a meningeal process; [[Kernig and Brudzinski Signs|Kernig/Brudzinski]] are absent (and ~5% sensitive anyway). MRI shows **leptomeningeal enhancement with cerebral edema**, and the LP is the crux: 140 WBC (mononuclear-predominant), **glucose 20 (serum 96)**, protein 51 — a lymphocytic, [[Hypoglycorrhachia|hypoglycorrhachic]] profile that fits infection (TB, fungal), inflammation, *or* neoplasm equally. Empiric bacterial/Listeria coverage runs while the team weighs TB; repeat GeneXpert and cryptococcal studies are negative twice, and **flow cytometry confirms meningeal infiltration by the B-cell lymphoma** — **[[Leptomeningeal Lymphomatosis|leptomeningeal lymphomatosis]]**, a form of [[Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|neoplastic meningitis]] with a grim prognosis, treated with intrathecal chemotherapy.

## Pearls

- **[[Headache|Headache]] + [[Altered Mental Status|altered mental status]] together is a red flag — and both resolve to [[Anatomical Approach|localization]] × [[Illness Tempo|time course]].** Split headache into [[Secondary Headache|primary vs secondary]] (secondary = *in* the head — brain, meninges, vessels, ventricles; *of* the head/neck — eye, sinus, dental, dissection, cervicogenic; or systemic), and split AMS into [[Level vs Content of Consciousness|level of consciousness]] (arousal: alert → somnolent → stupor → coma) versus **content** (attention, memory, language — a patient may look "confused" from amnesia or aphasia). Tempo then sorts the causes: sudden → vascular, acute → meningitis/encephalitis, subacute → neoplasm/fungal/TB, chronic → degenerative.
- **In a cancer patient, "the first ten things on the differential are the cancer" — but name the mechanism and beware [[Anchoring Bias|anchoring]].** [[Cancer and the Nervous System|Cancer hits the nervous system]] three ways: (1) **direct** — metastases, primary CNS or cord tumor, compression, or leptomeningeal spread; (2) **treatment** — chemotherapy ([[PRES|PRES]], neuropathy), radiation, and checkpoint-inhibitor/CAR-T neurotoxicity; (3) **paraneoplastic**, plus the [[Trousseau Syndrome|hypercoagulable state]] of malignancy ([[Cerebral Venous Sinus Thrombosis|CVST]], stroke). Layered on top is **immunosuppression** from the cancer and its treatment, reopening fungal, TB, and bacterial infection.
- **Raised [[Intracranial Pressure|intracranial pressure]] comes from one of three compartments (Monro-Kellie: ~80% brain, ~10% CSF, ~10% blood).** A non-focal patient with headache, somnolence, nausea/vomiting, and **papilledema** (which takes days to develop, dating the process) points to global raised ICP — from a mass, from **hydrocephalus** (e.g., meningeal disease impairing CSF egress), or from the venous side (**[[Cerebral Venous Sinus Thrombosis|cerebral venous sinus thrombosis]]** — never call papilledema "idiopathic intracranial hypertension" until an MRV excludes CVST).
- **A lymphocytic, low-glucose CSF is the great tie — infection vs neoplasm — and the LP profile alone cannot break it.** [[Hypoglycorrhachia|Low CSF glucose]] (< ~40, or < ~⅓ of a paired serum glucose — here 20 against a serum of 96) classically means bacterial, fungal, or TB meningitis, but also occurs in some viral infections, **leptomeningeal malignancy**, and sarcoid. Match the test to the bug: **cryptococcal antigen** (not insensitive India ink), **GeneXpert PCR** (not acid-fast smear) for TB, and coccidioides serologies where endemic.
- **Diagnosing [[Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|neoplastic meningitis]] takes tissue and persistence — CSF cytology is insensitive.** It can take **up to three lumbar punctures** to reach reasonable cytologic sensitivity for CNS/leptomeningeal lymphoma, and **false negatives run ~36% when CSF sits refrigerated > 48 hours** — so send fresh, send flow cytometry (CD phenotyping), and repeat. [[Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|Leptomeningeal carcinomatosis]] complicates ~8% of solid tumors and ~15% of hematologic cancers and carries a grim (often ~3-month) prognosis.
- **Treat first, ask questions later — empiric meningitis therapy does not wait for the LP.** When bacterial meningitis is plausible, start vancomycin + ceftriaxone + **ampicillin** (Listeria coverage for age > 50 or immunocompromise) ± acyclovir immediately: delay worsens bacterial-meningitis outcomes and the CSF chemistry/culture yield persists for days. Escalating to empiric anti-TB or amphotericin depends on whether the patient is deteriorating in front of you while cultures are pending.

## Problem Representation

A 52-year-old woman with high-risk diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (bone-marrow-infiltrated), three months into treatment with her last chemotherapy two weeks prior, presents with one week of worsening headache on a three-month background — an occipital headache that became holocephalic and severe with nausea, vomiting, dizziness, and progressive somnolence, disorientation, and language decline. She is afebrile and hemodynamically stable; her exam is non-focal but shows a fluctuating somnolent-inattentive mental status, bilateral papilledema, and nuchal rigidity without Kernig or Brudzinski signs. MRI demonstrates diffuse leptomeningeal enhancement and cerebral edema without hydrocephalus, and CSF shows a lymphocyte-predominant pleocytosis (140 WBC) with marked hypoglycorrhachia (glucose 20 against serum 96), mildly elevated protein, and an elevated opening pressure. With infectious studies (GeneXpert, cryptococcal antigen, bacterial cultures) negative on repeat, flow cytometry confirms meningeal infiltration by her B-cell lymphoma — leptomeningeal lymphomatosis (neoplastic meningitis) — treated with intrathecal chemotherapy against a poor prognosis.

## Case Narrative

A CP Solvers Neurology VMR with **Aaron Berkowitz** teaching, **Valeria** presenting, and **Joy** and neurology resident **Mattias** discussing. The chief concern is a loaded pair: **headache and altered mental status**.

**Framing both concerns.** Berkowitz builds two schemas before any history. **[[Headache|Headache]]**: [[Secondary Headache|primary]] (migraine, tension, and the trigeminal autonomic cephalalgias — cluster and its rarer cousins — which lack a biomarker but demand distinction because their treatments differ) versus **secondary** (a cause you can name), which he subdivides into pathology *in* the head (brain, meninges, vessels, ventricles), *of* the head and neck (eye strain, sinusitis, dental disease, glaucoma, orbital cellulitis, cervicogenic pain, carotid/vertebral dissection), and *systemic* (even a post-vaccine or dehydration headache). **[[Altered Mental Status|Altered mental status]]**: he and Mattias divide it into **[[Level vs Content of Consciousness|level of consciousness]]** — arousal graded alert → somnolent → stupor → coma — and **content of consciousness** — the cognitive modalities (attention, memory, language) whose focal failure can masquerade as global confusion (an amnestic patient disoriented because they record no new memories; an aphasic patient mistaken for confused). Over both he lays the master equation — differential = [[Anatomical Approach|localization]] × [[Illness Tempo|time course]] — preferring tempo (sudden → vascular/seizure/toxic; acute → infectious/inflammatory; subacute → neoplastic/fungal/TB; chronic → degenerative) to a cause-mnemonic like [[MIST Mnemonic|MIST]], and stresses that **context** (ED vs clinic, hyperacute vs chronic) shapes but can also mislead the read.

**HPI.** Valeria presents a **52-year-old woman** with, three months earlier, a new diagnosis of **high-risk diffuse ("giant B-cell") lymphoma** with bone-marrow infiltration — originally found after leg weakness left her unable to walk (records unavailable) — now three courses into chemotherapy, the last finishing **two weeks before admission**. She has had a **three-month occipital-predominant headache** that, one week before admission, became **holocephalic and more intense**, with nausea, vomiting, and dizziness; an ED visit six days earlier did not admit her. Over that week her daughter watched her grow **progressively somnolent**, sleeping through the day, until she could no longer recognize her daughter and spoke nonsensically. She is afebrile with no weight loss and an unremarkable social history.

**The cancer lens — and its trap.** Joy raises a chemotherapy complication; Berkowitz endorses it and generalizes with his mentor's line — **"if there's a neurologic symptom in a cancer patient, the first ten things on the differential are the cancer"** — immediately pairing it with the caution that this invites [[Anchoring Bias|anchoring and premature closure]], since cancer patients still get ordinary diseases. He lays out **[[Cancer and the Nervous System|three ways cancer strikes the nervous system]]**: (1) **direct** — brain/cord tumor, metastases, compression, or leptomeningeal spread; (2) **treatment-related** — chemotherapy ([[PRES|PRES]], peripheral neuropathy), radiation toxicity, and the expanding neuroimmunology of checkpoint-inhibitor and CAR-T neurotoxicity; and (3) **paraneoplastic**, into which he folds the **[[Trousseau Syndrome|hypercoagulability of malignancy]]** (with Marty Samuels' lore of Trousseau self-diagnosing his own fatal gastric cancer from a migratory DVT). Layered atop all three is the **immunosuppression** of the cancer *and* the chemotherapy, reopening fungal, tubercular, and bacterial infection. Mattias works the tempo — a subacute, progressive headache escalating to a disorder of arousal in an immunosuppressed host — and generates space-occupying infection, **[[Cerebral Venous Sinus Thrombosis|CVST]]** (lymphoma/chemo hypercoagulability), CNS localization of the lymphoma, and [[PRES|PRES]] (the occipital-to-holocephalic evolution), while noting common bugs like strep would be too fast for a week-long course.

**Raised ICP as the unifying physiology.** With headache, somnolence, and vomiting but (anticipated) no focality, Berkowitz reframes the problem as **rising [[Intracranial Pressure|intracranial pressure]]** and teaches the Monro-Kellie compartments — ~80% brain, ~10% CSF, ~10% blood (mostly venous). ICP can rise from a **mass**, from **hydrocephalus** (perhaps meningeal infection or leptomeningeal lymphoma impeding CSF egress), or from the **venous** side (CVST, which — unless it causes a stroke or bleed — need not localize). The exam's job, then, is to detect any focality and to confirm the meningeal/ICP process.

**The exam.** BP 100/60, HR 65, afebrile, ill-appearing and dehydrated. Mentally she is **somnolent and disorientated with fluctuating attention** — sometimes answering, sometimes staring past the examiner — but with intact naming, repetition, fluency, and comprehension when engaged. Cranial nerves are normal; there is **bilateral papilledema**. Motor exam shows non-focal generalized weakness (4/5 in all limbs) with normal reflexes, no Babinski or Hoffman, and no sensory or cerebellar deficit. There is **nuchal rigidity**, though the ED had recorded no Kernig or Brudzinski. Berkowitz reads this as a **hypothesis-confirming, non-localizing exam**: the papilledema proves (chronically) elevated ICP, and the neck stiffness raises concern for a meningeal process — while modeling the metacognition of predicting exam and imaging results in advance ("are you ordering this to rule in or rule out?") to make surprises informative. On [[Kernig and Brudzinski Signs|Kernig and Brudzinski]] he repeats the [[Test Interpretation|JAMA Rational Clinical Examination]] teaching: ~5% sensitive though highly specific, so their absence is meaningless — more common historically and in low-access settings where patients arrive late with advanced meningitis.

**Imaging and the pivotal LP.** With the CT scanner broken, an **MRI with and without contrast** shows **diffuse leptomeningeal enhancement and cerebral edema — no hydrocephalus, no mass**. The imaging clears the path for a **[[Lumbar Puncture|lumbar puncture]]** (the mental status and ICP mandated imaging first): **opening pressure 25**, transparent CSF, **140 WBC (65% mononuclear)**, 15 RBC, **protein 51**, and **glucose 20 against a serum of 96** — a lymphocyte-predominant, markedly **[[Hypoglycorrhachia|hypoglycorrhachic]]** profile. Berkowitz teaches that low CSF glucose is classic for bacterial/fungal/TB meningitis but also occurs in some viral infections, **leptomeningeal malignancy**, and sarcoid — so the profile ties infection against neoplasm and cannot break it alone.

**Working the tie.** He frames a **treat-first** posture: empiric bacterial coverage should already be running from the ED (vancomycin, ceftriaxone, **ampicillin** for Listeria given age and immunocompromise, ± acyclovir), because delay worsens bacterial-meningitis outcomes and does not erase CSF yield for days. For the fungal/TB arm he matches tests to organisms — **cryptococcal antigen** over insensitive India ink, **GeneXpert PCR** over acid-fast smear for TB, coccidioides serologies where endemic — and for the neoplastic arm, cytology plus **flow cytometry**, cautioning that CSF cytology is insensitive (**up to three LPs** for CNS lymphoma). In the final votes, Mattias and Berkowitz lean infection (Berkowitz, "going Bayesian," guesses TB given the setting and low glucose, half-joking about "Valeria bias" toward TB cases), while Joy holds out for cancer/inflammation.

**Resolution.** The infectious arm collapses: **GeneXpert negative twice, cryptococcal studies negative twice, Gram stain and cultures negative**. The repeat cytology is degraded (the samples had to be processed off-site), but **flow cytometry shows a phenotype compatible with meningeal infiltration by a chronic B-lymphoproliferative disorder** — the patient's **[[Leptomeningeal Lymphomatosis|lymphoma seeding the meninges]]**. Valeria's teaching points frame the diagnosis: **[[Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis|leptomeningeal carcinomatosis]]** (breast, lung, melanoma, and hematologic cancers) complicates ~8% of solid tumors and ~15% of hematologic malignancies, causes a characteristic pleocytosis with hypoglycorrhachia and elevated protein, and carries a grim prognosis; **cytology false-negatives reach ~36% when CSF is refrigerated > 48 hours** (as happened here), and the eponymous **[[Bing-Neel Syndrome|Bing-Neel syndrome]]** is the leptomeningeal variant seen with Waldenström/lymphoplasmacytic disease. She was started on **intrathecal chemotherapy** but remains hospitalized with a poor outlook — and, poignantly, her three-month headache in the setting of high-risk lymphoma may have been an earlier, missed signal of meningeal disease.

### Illness Scripts and Bias

- **[[Localization × Time Course|Localization × time course]], applied to two concerns at once.** Both headache and AMS localize intracranially; the *subacute, progressive* tempo in an immunosuppressed cancer host collapses the differential to meningeal infection versus neoplastic meningitis — the same Berkowitz frame anchoring [[CPS 153 - Intermittent Altered Mental Status and Non-Lesional Autoimmune Epilepsy|CPS 153]], [[CPS 359 - Altered Mental Status and Anti-NMDA Receptor Encephalitis|CPS 359]], [[CPS 386 - Rapidly Progressive Dementia and Intravascular Large B-Cell Lymphoma|CPS 386]], and [[CPS 402 - Altered Mental Status and Disseminated Varicella-Zoster|CPS 402]].
- **The [[Cancer and the Nervous System|cancer-and-the-nervous-system]] schema.** "The first ten things are the cancer" organizes the search into direct, treatment-related, and paraneoplastic mechanisms plus immunosuppression — a completeness heuristic that keeps [[PRES|PRES]], [[Cerebral Venous Sinus Thrombosis|CVST]], and opportunistic infection all on the board rather than fixating on one.
- **[[Anchoring Bias|Anchoring]], named and resisted.** Berkowitz flags the pull of the cancer history toward premature closure, and Mattias openly names "a bad case of anchoring" on infection — modeling the practice of stating one's bias so surprises retain their diagnostic force.
- **[[Base Rate|Base rates]] and setting.** The reasoning tunes probabilities to tempo and host (a week-long course is too slow for untreated pyogenic meningitis; immunosuppression raises fungal/TB priors) while acknowledging that "context can trick us."
- **[[Test Interpretation|Test interpretation]] under overlap.** The hypoglycorrhachic lymphocytic CSF is read for what it is — nonspecific across TB, fungal, viral, and neoplastic meningitis — with the serum:CSF glucose ratio, the CrAg-over-India-ink and GeneXpert-over-smear rules, and the insensitivity/false-negative pitfalls of CSF cytology all made explicit; [[Kernig and Brudzinski Signs|Kernig/Brudzinski]] are correctly discounted.
- **[[Diagnostic Humility|Diagnostic humility]] and treating under uncertainty.** With infection and malignancy genuinely tied, the team treats empirically (bacterial/Listeria coverage, weighing anti-TB and amphotericin against deterioration) rather than forcing a premature label — and closes the loop on a missed early signal (the three-month headache).

## Teaching Discussion

- Treat headache plus altered mental status as a high-yield danger pairing, and work each with a schema: headache as primary vs secondary (in the head / of the head and neck / systemic), and AMS as level vs content of consciousness — then let localization × time course, not a cause-mnemonic, drive the differential.
- In any cancer patient with a neurologic symptom, remember that the first considerations are the cancer itself — direct or metastatic involvement, treatment complications (PRES, neuropathy, radiation, checkpoint-inhibitor/CAR-T neurotoxicity), and paraneoplastic syndromes plus malignancy-associated hypercoagulability — while guarding against anchoring, since immunosuppressed patients also get ordinary and opportunistic infections.
- Localize raised intracranial pressure to one of the Monro-Kellie compartments (brain, CSF, blood): a non-focal patient with headache, vomiting, somnolence, and papilledema has global raised ICP from a mass, hydrocephalus, or cerebral venous sinus thrombosis — and never diagnose idiopathic intracranial hypertension without an MRV to exclude CVST.
- Read a lymphocyte-predominant, low-glucose CSF as a tie among tuberculous, fungal, viral, neoplastic, and inflammatory meningitis: interpret glucose against a paired serum value (low being < ~40 or < ~⅓ of serum), and recognize that hypoglycorrhachia occurs in leptomeningeal malignancy and sarcoid, not only in pyogenic infection.
- Match the diagnostic test to the organism — cryptococcal antigen rather than India ink, GeneXpert PCR rather than acid-fast smear for TB, coccidioides serologies where endemic — and recognize the insensitivity of CSF cytology for neoplastic meningitis (up to three LPs), sending fresh specimens and flow cytometry because refrigeration > 48 hours drives false negatives up to ~36%.
- Start empiric meningitis therapy before the LP when bacterial meningitis is plausible (vancomycin, ceftriaxone, ampicillin for Listeria in age > 50 or immunocompromise, ± acyclovir), since delay worsens outcomes and does not erase CSF yield for days, and escalate to empiric anti-TB or antifungal therapy based on trajectory while cultures and flow cytometry are pending.
- Recognize leptomeningeal carcinomatosis/lymphomatosis as a diagnosis that rewards persistence and early suspicion — it complicates ~8% of solid tumors and ~15% of hematologic cancers, carries a poor prognosis, and here a three-month headache in a high-risk lymphoma patient was a missed early signal worth acting on.
