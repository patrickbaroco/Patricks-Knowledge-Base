## Pearls

- **Split infection into [[Commensal vs Environmental Organisms|commensal vs environmental (exogenous)]] organisms.** Commensals (staph, E. coli) live on us in a balanced battle and cause *focal* disease (pneumonia, UTI, cellulitis) that disseminates leaving a residual imprint; **environmental** organisms the body has never seen (rickettsial, tick/flea-borne, granulomatous) **disseminate fast with systemic symptoms and little localization** — which is why a **well-appearing, systemically-ill** patient (you wouldn't look this good with staph everywhere) suggests an exogenous bug. ([[RLR 073 - Fever and Murine Typhus with Secondary Pulmonary Alveolar Proteinosis|RLR 073]])
- **Viruses shouldn't fever beyond ~1–3 weeks — repivot when they do.** A prolonged febrile systemic illness forces a choice between a **disseminated commensal** (easier to culture/image) and a **non-viral environmental/atypical** organism; the latter deserves **empiric [[Doxycycline|doxycycline]]** because several are rapidly lethal. ([[RLR 073 - Fever and Murine Typhus with Secondary Pulmonary Alveolar Proteinosis|RLR 073]])
- **Get the differential: [[Bandemia|bands]] on a normal total WBC signal a robust bacterial response.** A normal white count with **68% bands** is invisible without a diff, and immature neutrophils mark severe (often bacterial) infection — so "viral" is off the table. Pair it with **[[Thrombocytopenia in Infection|thrombocytopenia out of proportion to sepsis]]** and **transaminitis** (the liver's nonspecific stress marker), and the **[[Rickettsial Infections|exogenous/rickettsial]] pattern** emerges. ([[RLR 073 - Fever and Murine Typhus with Secondary Pulmonary Alveolar Proteinosis|RLR 073]])
- **The electrolytes tell you which GI loss dominates.** Vomiting causes a **hypokalemic, hypochloremic metabolic alkalosis** — not by losing K in vomit, but via volume loss → RAAS → aldosterone-driven renal K/H wasting — while diarrhea causes a **non-gap metabolic acidosis**; a "normal" bicarbonate can hide *both*. And a severe AKI can **protect** the potassium (masking worse hypokalemia), just as it can mask the hypertension of a nephritic process. ([[RLR 073 - Fever and Murine Typhus with Secondary Pulmonary Alveolar Proteinosis|RLR 073]])
- **A hyper-acute pulmonary process plus renal disease is a [[Pulmonary-Renal Syndrome|pulmonary-renal syndrome]] until proven otherwise.** New hematuria with nephritic-range proteinuria (interpret the [[Urine Protein-to-Creatinine Ratio|UPCR]] cautiously in AKI) alongside [[ARDS]] raises anti-GBM (Goodpasture), ANCA vasculitis, and lupus — and multi-organ involvement favors the alveolar substance being **[[Diffuse Alveolar Hemorrhage|blood (DAH)]]** over water (normal troponin/BNP) or a lung-restricted cellular/protein process. ([[RLR 073 - Fever and Murine Typhus with Secondary Pulmonary Alveolar Proteinosis|RLR 073]])
- **[[Ground Glass Opacities|Ground-glass opacities]] mean alveolar filling — and the pattern narrows the cause.** GGO = alveolar liquid/pus/blood; with pneumonia think **virus** first (GGO was a COVID screen), then **[[Pneumocystis Pneumonia|PJP]]** (especially with a very high **[[LDH]]**), disseminated **strongyloides**, and **[[Leptospirosis|leptospirosis]]** (GGO + DAH). Add **crazy paving** and **PAS-positive macrophages** and think **[[Pulmonary Alveolar Proteinosis|PAP]]**. ([[RLR 073 - Fever and Murine Typhus with Secondary Pulmonary Alveolar Proteinosis|RLR 073]])
- **[[Pulmonary Alveolar Proteinosis|PAP]] is primary or secondary — the anti-GM-CSF antibody splits them.** **Primary/autoimmune** PAP (classically younger women) is **anti-GM-CSF positive**; **secondary** PAP is **antibody-negative** and driven by an inflammatory/infectious insult. A negative antibody with PAS-positive macrophages should send you hunting the secondary cause — here **[[Murine Typhus|murine typhus]]**, a **never-reported** trigger. ([[RLR 073 - Fever and Murine Typhus with Secondary Pulmonary Alveolar Proteinosis|RLR 073]])
- **Transaminitis + thrombocytopenia + hyponatremia should trigger the tick/flea-borne–rickettsial script — and empiric doxycycline saves lives.** [[Murine Typhus|Murine typhus]] (cat-flea-borne, endemic in Texas and LA County) causes renal involvement (hematuria/proteinuria) in ~⅓, is usually mild but rarely multisystemic (with HLH), and its case-fatality drops below 1% with **doxycycline** — so treat on pattern and confirm later. ([[RLR 073 - Fever and Murine Typhus with Secondary Pulmonary Alveolar Proteinosis|RLR 073]])

## Problem Representation

A 50-year-old woman from Texas who fosters cats and dogs presents with one week of fever, headache, myalgias, and multiple daily episodes of non-bloody vomiting and diarrhea, initially labeled viral. She becomes hypotensive and, over 24–48 hours, develops hyper-acute hypoxemic respiratory failure requiring intubation. Laboratory findings include a normal total white count with 68% bands (later frank neutrophilia), thrombocytopenia (to 32k), transaminitis with an evolving direct hyperbilirubinemia, hyponatremia (124), hypokalemia (2.5), a rising creatinine (to 3.2), a very high LDH and ferritin, hypofibrinogenemia, and nephritic-range proteinuria with hematuria; imaging shows bilateral ground-glass opacities with crazy paving, and a bloody, darkening BAL shows PAS-positive macrophages with a negative anti-GM-CSF antibody. The unifying diagnosis is murine typhus (Rickettsia typhi) with secondary pulmonary alveolar proteinosis and diffuse alveolar hemorrhage — a previously unreported association — with dramatic improvement on empiric doxycycline started early in the course.

## Case Narrative

Austin Rezigh presents a slide-based case (from his wife, a pulmonary fellow): a 50-year-old Texan woman, a housekeeper who fosters cats and dogs, with a week of fever, headache, myalgias, and non-bloody vomiting and diarrhea, no localizing symptoms, a sick contact, and negative COVID tests. Reza sets the frame — **fever = inflammation = infection**, with the GI symptoms localizing to a **gastroenteritis** — and flags that persistent diarrhea would later force non-infectious inflammatory causes.

Rabih delivers the case's organizing idea: the **[[Commensal vs Environmental Organisms|commensal-vs-environmental]]** dichotomy. Commensals (staph, E. coli) live on the body in a balanced battle and cause **focal** disease that, even when it disseminates, leaves a residual imprint (a UTI with sepsis); **environmental** organisms the body has never met **disseminate quickly with systemic symptoms and no localization**. The reason we reflexively say "viral" is exactly this pattern — systemic symptoms in a **well-appearing** patient (staph everywhere would look far worse) — so he calls it a **disseminated process from an environmental organism**, with the caveat that the "**viral fever shouldn't last beyond ~1–3 weeks**" rule will force a repivot to a **disseminated commensal** or a **non-viral atypical** (rickettsial, tick-borne, granulomatous) organism if it persists. The past history is read for **infection vulnerabilities** (emphysema → pneumonia), and the animal-fostering social history is **pinned** for later.

The urgent-care data escalate it. Vitals show **SBP <90** with fever — beyond simple viral illness, raising **distributive shock** (though fluid-responsive, consistent with volume loss). Reza then mines the [[Basic Metabolic Panel|chemistries]]: **Na 126** (symptomatic-range hyponatremia), **K 2.5** (moderate-severe hypokalemia → arrhythmia risk), and a **BUN/Cr disconnect** (a low-ish BUN from poor intake atop a real AKI). He teaches the acid-base logic — **vomiting → hypokalemic, hypochloremic metabolic alkalosis** (volume loss → RAAS → aldosterone-driven renal K/H wasting, *not* K lost in vomit; the chloride of 89 supports it), **diarrhea → non-gap metabolic acidosis**, and a "normal" bicarbonate of 24 possibly hiding **both** — and notes the **AKI is protecting the potassium** from being even lower. He'd send her to the ED.

Rabih catches the **hidden clue**: **68% bands** on a normal total WBC — a robust response invisible without a diff, and immature neutrophils marking **severe bacterial** infection. Now the **[[Thrombocytopenia in Infection|thrombocytopenia]]** (56) becomes pivotal: pyogenic commensal sepsis lowers platelets *proportional to severity*, so a **deep thrombocytopenia without proportional sepsis** marks an **exogenous** organism (arbo/tick-flea-borne, rickettsial, ehrlichial, typhus). Add the **transaminitis** ("the liver's ATN"), and the **environmental pattern is born** → **empiric [[Doxycycline|doxycycline]]**. Reza layers a parallel thread — thrombocytopenia + a possibly-masked anemia + an AST/ALT pattern + severe diarrhea could be **Shiga-toxin HUS/MAHA** — prompting a smear, LDH, and haptoglobin. On the vitals, both note a **relatively low heart rate** for the fever/hypotension — the [[Faget Sign|Faget sign]] of an exogenous organism (with the caveat of possible cardiac involvement).

She returns far worse — **T 38.3, HR 143, RR 46, SpO₂ 77%** — in **acute hypoxemic respiratory failure**, and is **intubated within six hours**; an ABG (pH 7.26/PCO₂ 36/PaO₂ 72 on 100%) confirms **[[ARDS]]**. Reza stresses the **hyper-acute tempo** (well → intubated in a day) as the key, and reads the CXR/CT as **alveolar filling**. The [[Center of Gravity|kidney then becomes the center of gravity]]: a rising creatinine with **UA blood + protein** (nephritic-range, UPCR ~1.5 — interpreted cautiously in AKI) and low WBC/negative culture arguing against UTI, pointing to a **[[Glomerulonephritis|glomerulonephritis]]**. Pulmonary + renal → a **[[Pulmonary-Renal Syndrome|pulmonary-renal syndrome]]** (anti-GBM/**Goodpasture**, ANCA vasculitis — GPA/MPA/EGPA, lupus), with Reza even recalling Ernest **Goodpasture's** first name. The absence of schistocytes and a **direct** (not indirect) hyperbilirubinemia steer away from TMA.

The CT shows **bilateral diffuse ground-glass with interlobular septal thickening (crazy paving)** and lower-lobe consolidation. Reza works the **very high [[LDH]] (968)** (hemolysis unlikely with a direct bili and normal Hgb; consider infarction, lymphoma, and **PJP**) and the **[[Ground Glass Opacities|GGO]]** differential (**virus** first — GGO as a COVID screen; then **PJP** with high LDH — but HIV is negative; **strongyloides** given the GI illness; **[[Leptospirosis|lepto]]** with DAH), while running the ARDS causes and excluding acute eosinophilic pneumonia and hypersensitivity pneumonitis. Rabih reasons that **multi-organ** involvement makes the alveolar substance **blood ([[Diffuse Alveolar Hemorrhage|DAH]])** rather than water (normal troponin/BNP) or a lung-restricted protein/cellular process, and builds strong momentum for **[[Leptospirosis|leptospirosis]]** (leukocytosis, thrombocytopenia, renal, hepatic, DAH, animal exposure), noting the big-gun antibiotics don't cover lepto (keep doxy/ceftriaxone) and to send **[[Karius|Karius cell-free DNA]]** and serology (with its timing caveats).

The **[[BAL|BAL]]** is **orange, darkening with each aliquot** (a DAH signature, though the samples are lost) with **93% neutrophils** and many RBCs; Gram/AFB and the respiratory panel are negative. Complements show a **mildly low C3**, and the entire autoimmune panel (ANCA, ANA, anti-GBM) is **negative** — but the **cytology shows PAS-positive pulmonary macrophages**, and the **anti-GM-CSF antibody is negative**. Reframed as **crazy paving + PAS-positive macrophages + negative anti-GM-CSF**, the picture is **[[Pulmonary Alveolar Proteinosis|pulmonary alveolar proteinosis]]** — and the **negative antibody** makes it **secondary** (vs primary autoimmune PAP in younger women; with **[[Lipoid Pneumonia|lipoid pneumonia]]** as the PAS-positive mimic). Serum **lepto IgM** is negative, and Rabih broadens the "devastating exogenous pneumonia" list (measles/hantavirus; scrub typhus — Texas; lepto; the granulomatous Coxiella/**Q fever**, Chlamydia psittaci, and a cat-associated organism he can't recall).

The **final test** is a **Rickettsia typhi** antibody — **[[Murine Typhus|murine typhus]]** — with the **secondary PAP** as a **never-before-reported** complication. The vector fits (**cat fleas**; she fosters cats), murine typhus is **endemic in Texas and LA County**, and the ED had started **doxycycline early**, producing **dramatic improvement** (AKI, hyponatremia, imaging all resolved), with confirmatory **IgG seroconversion and a fourfold IgM rise**. Reza, chagrined he'd studied murine typhus for hours and "missed it twice at morning report," notes it *was* voiced early (cat fleas + the transaminitis/thrombocytopenia pattern), and that renal involvement (hematuria/proteinuria) occurs in ~⅓ — explaining the UA — with case-fatality <1% on doxycycline (rarely multisystem/HLH). Rabih frames rickettsia as **vasculotropic** — a "vasculitic fingerprint" (DAH + glomerular bleeding) — and both draw the meta-lessons: **never lock into one diagnosis** (weighted probabilities; the same case could be lepto another time), the **kidney was partly a distractor**, **DAH was the right illness-script anchor** (no PAP script exists to reach for), the **BAL color change is not sensitive**, and **sending the cytology is what caught the PAP** — with empiric doxycycline the life-saving act regardless of the final name.

### Illness Scripts and Bias

- **[[Schema-Based Reasoning|Framework upon framework]].** Commensal-vs-environmental, the GI acid-base logic, the thrombocytopenia-in-infection rule, the pulmonary-renal syndrome, and the GGO/alveolar-filling schema each carve the chaos — and the environmental-organism pattern (systemic, well-appearing, thrombocytopenia, transaminitis, bands) is the throughline.
- **[[Tempo-based reasoning|Tempo as the linker]].** The "viruses don't fever past weeks" rule and, decisively, the **hyper-acute** pulmonary onset (well → intubated in a day) are what connect the GI prodrome to the lung catastrophe and keep the process unified.
- **[[Test Interpretation|Reading the hidden data]].** Bands on a normal WBC, thrombocytopenia *out of proportion*, a UPCR caveated in AKI, a sky-high LDH, PAS-positive macrophages, and the anti-GM-CSF split — each result is interpreted for meaning, and the **diff** and **cytology** are the tests that changed the case.
- **[[Center of Gravity|Shifting center of gravity]].** The core moves from GI to hematologic (thrombocytopenia) to pulmonary (ARDS) to renal (GN) as data arrive — with the discipline to keep asking *which findings must I still explain*.
- **[[Diagnostic Humility|Never lock in; treat empirically]].** Explicit humility that the same presentation could be lepto another time, that the kidney was partly a distractor, and that **empiric doxycycline** — not diagnostic certainty — saved the patient; plus openness to a **never-reported** association.
- **Vasculotropism as a unifying lens.** Reframing rickettsia as vasculotropic ties the DAH and glomerular bleeding into a single "vasculitic fingerprint," a script Rabih traces to a formative Dhaliwal case.

## Teaching Discussion

- Classify infections as commensal versus environmental: systemic illness in a well-appearing patient, especially with thrombocytopenia out of proportion to sepsis and transaminitis, suggests an exogenous (rickettsial/tick-flea-borne) organism.
- Repivot from "viral" when fever persists beyond one to three weeks or when a diff reveals bandemia on a normal white count; treat the atypical/rickettsial pattern empirically with doxycycline because several are rapidly lethal.
- Use the electrolytes to identify the dominant GI loss (vomiting → hypokalemic hypochloremic alkalosis via RAAS; diarrhea → non-gap acidosis), and remember an AKI can mask both worse hypokalemia and a nephritic hypertension.
- Treat a hyper-acute pulmonary process with renal disease as a pulmonary-renal syndrome (anti-GBM, ANCA, lupus), and reason that multi-organ involvement favors alveolar blood (DAH) over edema or a lung-restricted process.
- Narrow ground-glass opacities by pattern and company — virus first, then PJP (with a high LDH), strongyloides, and leptospirosis — and add crazy paving plus PAS-positive macrophages to reach pulmonary alveolar proteinosis.
- Split PAP into primary (anti-GM-CSF positive, younger women) and secondary (antibody negative, driven by an inflammatory/infectious insult), and hunt the secondary cause when the antibody is negative.
- Recognize the transaminitis-plus-thrombocytopenia-plus-hyponatremia pattern of tick/flea-borne rickettsial disease (murine typhus, endemic in Texas and LA County), and treat empirically with doxycycline while confirming with serology/PCR.
- Hold diagnoses as weighted probabilities, treat the lethal possibilities empirically, and let the cytology and the diff — the easily overlooked tests — change the case.
