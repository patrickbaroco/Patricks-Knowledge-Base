## Summary

> [!summary] Scan Summary
> A middle-aged non-smoking man presents with six weeks of recurrent fevers, night sweats, fatigue, and 20-lb weight loss, with a **chronic left-lower-lobe consolidation** that persists through two courses of antibiotics, plus a small pleural effusion and pleural thickening — and a remote construction / steel-mill exposure history. Rabih filters the weight loss as a *severity* marker of an [[Inflammatory Weight Loss|inflammatory syndrome]] (infection, autoimmunity, or malignancy), quickly demotes autoimmunity (few autoimmune diseases produce prominent B symptoms), and fixes the [[Center of Gravity|center of gravity]] on the chronic consolidation — whose differential is narrow and dominated by [[Mucinous Adenocarcinoma|mucinous adenocarcinoma]] and [[Lymphoma|lymphoma]]. A PET-CT then scatters the picture: mediastinal/hilar and abdominal [[Lymphadenopathy|lymphadenopathy]], a lytic iliac lesion, diffuse skeletal uptake, a left adrenal mass, and an IgG-lambda [[Monoclonal Gammopathy|paraprotein]] — a "strong case for cancer." But the pieces come apart under biopsy: the iliac lesion is a benign chondromyxoid fibroma, the marrow is clean, and EBUS-TBNA of the nodes shows **non-caseating granulomas**. With infection excluded and the dings against Hodgkin too strong, Rabih invokes [[Illness Script Flexibility|illness-script flexibility]] to land on [[Sarcoidosis|sarcoidosis]] — a diagnosis later cemented when the patient develops anterior [[Uveitis|uveitis]] and responds beautifully to methotrexate (with an incidental [[Monoclonal Gammopathy|MGUS]] along for the ride).

## Pearls

- **Weight loss with inflammatory features tells you *how sick*, not *what's wrong* — it opens exactly three doors: infection, autoimmunity, and malignancy.** Because prominent B symptoms are rare in autoimmune disease (only the vasculitides and inflammatory myositides really qualify), the absence of rash, arthritis, or adenopathy early on lets you close the autoimmune door fast and spend your attention on infection versus cancer.
- **A consolidation earns diagnostic weight only once it proves *chronic* — ordinary bacterial pneumonia can take weeks to clear, so persistence, not mere presence, is the signal.** The differential for a truly chronic consolidation is narrow: [[Cryptogenic Organizing Pneumonia|COP]], [[Chronic Eosinophilic Pneumonia|chronic eosinophilic pneumonia]], alveolar [[Sarcoidosis|sarcoid]], [[Lipoid Pneumonia|lipoid pneumonia]], and — the notorious *unilateral* causes — [[Mucinous Adenocarcinoma|mucinous adenocarcinoma]] and [[Lymphoma|lymphoma]].
- **"[[Diseases Travel Together|When you assume everything is linked, you discover something is separate.]]"** The lytic iliac lesion looked like the metastatic keystone of a cancer story; biopsy returned a benign chondromyxoid fibroma, and dropping that red herring reshaped the whole differential. A unifying diagnosis is a hypothesis, not an obligation.
- **A negative bone marrow excludes *metastatic* lymphoma, not lymphoma itself.** Lymphoma is a cancer of mature lymphocytes that live *outside* the marrow, so a clean marrow only rules out marrow involvement — extranodal and nodal disease can still be present, and tissue from the node is what settles it.
- **Non-caseating granulomas in the US come down to three things: infection, [[Sarcoidosis|sarcoidosis]], and [[Lymphoma|lymphoma]].** With infection excluded and lymphoma's dings too strong (a node biopsy that overt should not miss it; Hodgkin rarely makes a consolidation), sarcoid wins — an exercise in [[Illness Script Flexibility|illness-script flexibility]], since no one would draft *this* as a classic sarcoid case.
- **[[Priming|Priming]] the next clinician is a diagnostic act.** Naming, at discharge, the feature you're waiting on ([[Uveitis|uveitis]], a skin lesion at a scar or tattoo, a suppressed [[Parathyroid Hormone|PTH]]) turns a future finding into an instant confirmation — exactly how anterior uveitis later clinched this case.

## Problem Representation

A middle-aged non-smoking man with hypertension and hyperlipidemia presents with six weeks of recurrent fevers, night sweats, fatigue, and ~20-lb weight loss, notable for a chronic left-lower-lobe consolidation refractory to two antibiotic courses, a small bilateral-predominant-left pleural effusion with pleural thickening, and a remote construction/steel-mill (asbestos-relevant) exposure; labs show neutrophilic leukocytosis, mild normocytic anemia, elevated ESR/CRP and ferritin ~1200, a negative broad infectious workup, and later an IgG-lambda paraprotein — with PET-CT revealing mediastinal/hilar and abdominal lymphadenopathy, a lytic iliac lesion, diffuse skeletal uptake, and a left adrenal mass, ultimately diagnosed as sarcoidosis (non-caseating granulomas on EBUS-TBNA, confirmed by later anterior uveitis responsive to methotrexate) with an incidental MGUS.

## Case Narrative

Reza presents a case written up with Dr. Kelly Chong: a middle-aged man with six weeks of recurrent fevers, night sweats, fatigue, and roughly 20 pounds of weight loss. The illness began six weeks earlier with a fever and an ED visit; a chest x-ray showed a left-lower-lobe consolidation with a small left pleural effusion, and he was admitted, treated with ceftriaxone and azithromycin, defervesced, and went home. Four weeks later the fever recurred, the consolidation was unchanged on repeat imaging, and he was given a 14-day course of levofloxacin. Two weeks after that he returned with persistent fever, fatigue, and the cumulative weight loss. Review of systems is essentially nil — no cough, dyspnea, diarrhea, headache, or rash. His history is only hypertension and hyperlipidemia (on antihypertensives and a statin).

On exam he is febrile to 101.5°F and tachycardic to 110, with a normal respiratory rate and oxygenation; lungs are clear and the skin, liver, and spleen exams are unremarkable. Labs show a neutrophil-predominant leukocytosis of 13,000, hemoglobin 10 with a normal MCV, normal platelets, elevated ESR and CRP, ferritin ~1200, and normal renal and hepatic chemistries; a fourth-generation HIV antigen/antibody test is negative. CT confirms the left-lower-lobe consolidation with an associated small pleural effusion, a slight right effusion, and left pleural wall thickening. The one rich piece of epidemiology: 10–15 years ago he worked in construction on the East Coast, and before that in a steel mill; animal and water exposures are negative.

Rabih opens by prizing the realism of receiving the "whole whopping dose" of data at once. He filters the weight loss as a marker of *severity*, not etiology: this is **[[Inflammatory Weight Loss|weight loss with inflammatory features]]**, which means infection, autoimmunity, or malignancy. He then reasons that prominent B symptoms are rare in autoimmune disease — only the vasculitides and inflammatory myositides really produce a night-sweats syndrome — and, hearing no lymphadenopathy, rash, or arthritis, he sets autoimmunity aside. That leaves infection and malignancy. The infections that behave this way come in two flavors: occult **[[Abscess|abscesses]]** hidden somewhere in the body, and *microscopic* abscesses hiding within **[[Granuloma|granulomas]]**, the latter usually tied to an epidemiologic exposure (Ohio River basin/Blastomyces, TB, and the like).

He then names the true **[[Center of Gravity|center of gravity]]**: not the non-specific neutrophilia but the **chronic consolidation**. He tempers it with a caveat — ordinary bacterial pneumonia often takes weeks to clear, so a consolidation must earn its significance by *persistence* — and here it has. The differential for a chronic consolidation is narrow: [[Cryptogenic Organizing Pneumonia|COP]] (usually migratory — a ding here, though the recent infection is supportive), [[Chronic Eosinophilic Pneumonia|chronic eosinophilic pneumonia]] (typically bilateral with peripheral eosinophilia — absent), alveolar [[Sarcoidosis|sarcoid]] (usually bilateral), and the notorious *unilateral* causes, **[[Mucinous Adenocarcinoma|mucinous adenocarcinoma]] and [[Lymphoma|lymphoma]]**, with [[Lipoid Pneumonia|lipoid pneumonia]] as the classic miss. The **pleural thickening** adds a second lever: it signals inflammatory pleural disease, again pointing to infection and malignancy over autoimmunity, and — with the construction/steel-mill history — raises a unifying [[Asbestos-Related Pleural Disease|asbestos]] theory (mesothelioma or mucinous adenocarcinoma). Being practical, Rabih wants tissue quickly by bronchoscopy or biopsy, prioritizing exclusion of a morbid mucinous adenocarcinoma.

Reza loads the next aliquot. The exhaustive infectious workup is uniformly negative: SARS-CoV-2 PCR; blood, urine, sputum bacterial and fungal cultures; urine Legionella, pneumococcal, and Histoplasma antigens; QuantiFERON; a fungal serology panel including Coccidioides IgM/IgG; rickettsial serologies; and a febrile antibody panel (Salmonella, Brucella, Rocky Mountain spotted fever, Rickettsia). A **PET-CT** then complicates everything: upper-abdominal and enlarged mediastinal/hilar lymphadenopathy with uptake suspicious for neoplasm, a **lytic expansile lesion in the left iliac bone**, diffuse intense skeletal uptake, and an **enlarged left adrenal gland** with uptake. An SPEP shows increased alpha-1, alpha-2, and beta-2 fractions; immunofixation reveals an **IgG-lambda monoclonal band**; and the beta-2 microglobulin is elevated.

Rabih works the new data. Bone lesions in a systemically ill patient usually mean metastatic cancer, though osteomyelitis (plausibly from a granulomatous pulmonary infection like TB) deserves a parallel workup. The **left adrenal mass breaks symmetry** and pushes malignancy further. Base rate then argues for lung cancer — by far the most common US malignancy — which could plausibly generate consolidation, adenopathy, adrenal disease, and bone metastases; but his non-smoking status tempers it. The **[[Monoclonal Gammopathy|paraprotein]]** cuts two ways: at his age there's roughly a 5% chance of [[Monoclonal Gammopathy|MGUS]] (so the test partly just "reconfirms his age"), but 20–30% of lymphomas also carry a paraprotein, nudging lymphoma up. His plan: get tissue — often the easiest is a lesion "wide open to the world," here the bronchus.

The final aliquots dismantle the unified story. Biopsy of the iliac lesion favors a **chondromyxoid fibroma**, a benign tumor — a red herring, separate from everything else. A bone marrow biopsy shows normal cellularity, no dysplasia, and no monotypic plasma-cell population on flow. Finally, **EBUS-TBNA** of the nodes shows histiocytes and **non-caseating granulomas**. Rabih reflects on the humility of it: "when you assume everything is linked, you find something is actually separate." He also flags the bone marrow teaching — a negative marrow excludes *metastatic* lymphoma, not lymphoma itself, because lymphoma is a cancer of mature lymphocytes living outside the marrow.

Non-caseating granulomas in the US, he reasons, mean infection, sarcoidosis, or lymphoma. Infection is largely excluded; lymphoma is a can't-miss but the dings are strong (a node biopsy this overt shouldn't miss it, and Hodgkin — the lymphoma that could suppress PTH — rarely causes a consolidation and is rarely extranodal). He therefore invokes **[[Illness Script Flexibility|illness-script flexibility]]**: sarcoid's script bends to fill a space nothing else occupies, even against atypical features (a chronic consolidation is ~1% of sarcoid; a concurrent pleural effusion is classically a reason to think of *another* diagnosis). To raise his sarcoid probability he would query for overt or subclinical [[Uveitis|uveitis]], subtle skin lesions at scars or tattoos, and a suppressed **[[Parathyroid Hormone|PTH]]** — the last explained, via a Lawrence Tierney pearl, by near-universal hypercalciuria in sarcoid from granulomatous vitamin-D activation. He cautions that ~15% of patients initially called sarcoid later develop lymphoma ([[Sarcoidosis-Lymphoma Syndrome|sarcoid-lymphoma syndrome]]), so vigilance must persist even after immunosuppression.

The case resolves. The fever spontaneously resolves; the patient is discharged to rheumatology, who — reaching the same hypothesis but with the patient asymptomatic — hold off on treatment. Weeks later he develops **anterior uveitis**, is started on **methotrexate**, and does phenomenally well; the biopsy shows no cancer and no infection. Rabih notes that uveitis is a powerful symmetry-break: most uveitis is idiopathic, ~40% autoimmune, ~20% infectious (essentially syphilis and TB, both excluded here), so its appearance strongly favors autoimmune disease and fits sarcoid beautifully. The final diagnosis is **[[Sarcoidosis|sarcoidosis]]**, with an incidental **[[Monoclonal Gammopathy|MGUS]]** — and both hosts close on how humbling the diagnosis was, since sarcoid was never truly on the radar until the tissue came back.

### Illness Scripts and Bias

- **[[Illness Script Flexibility|Illness-script flexibility]] — the episode's headline concept.** Rabih argues that some diseases lack a single defining feature, so their script can *bend* to occupy diagnostic space that nothing else fills. He is explicit that this is not a classic sarcoid case — chronic consolidation is ~1% of sarcoid and a pleural effusion classically argues *against* it — yet with infection excluded and the dings against Hodgkin too strong (an overt node biopsy shouldn't miss it; Hodgkin rarely consolidates or goes extranodal), sarcoid becomes the residual best fit. Flexibility is licensed *because* the alternatives are more strongly contradicted, not because the fit is clean.
- **[[Framing Effect|Framing]] via the weight-loss filter and the center of gravity.** Weight loss is reframed from an etiologic clue into a *severity* marker that opens the infection/autoimmune/malignancy triad, and the neutrophilia is deliberately discarded as non-localizing so the [[Center of Gravity|center of gravity]] (the chronic consolidation) can organize the differential. Naming the frame prevents the flashy-but-uninformative data from steering the case.
- **[[Base-Rate Neglect|Base-rate reasoning]], used and then tempered.** Rabih invokes the base rate (lung cancer as the most common US malignancy) to prioritize adenocarcinoma, then immediately tempers it with the patient's non-smoking status and with the competing paraprotein data — base rate as a starting weight, adjusted by the specifics.
- **Resisting the [[Unifying Diagnosis Bias|unifying-diagnosis]] pull ([[Occam's Razor vs Hickam's Dictum|Occam vs Hickam]]).** "When you assume everything is linked, you find something is actually separate." The lytic iliac lesion looked like the metastatic keystone; its benign biopsy forced decoupling of a finding the mind had already welded to the cancer narrative. Parsimony is a hypothesis to be tested, not a rule to be obeyed.
- **Symmetry-breaking findings.** Two findings function as high-information pivots: the left adrenal mass (breaks toward malignancy) and, at the end, the anterior uveitis (breaks toward autoimmunity — most uveitis idiopathic, ~40% autoimmune, ~20% infectious/syphilis-TB). "When uveitis shows up, it's autoimmune; when a bone lesion shows up, it's cancer" is the heuristic — with the caveat that the bone lesion here was the exception.
- **[[Priming|Priming and anticipatory diagnosis]].** Rabih's move of naming — at the moment of the biopsy — the confirmatory features he is *waiting* for (uveitis, scar/tattoo skin lesions, suppressed PTH) is what let a later uveitis instantly clinch the diagnosis. Reza's parallel story (documenting a proteinuria/ESR concern that later declared itself as ANCA-associated vasculitis) shows priming as a deliberate reasoning tool, not luck.
- **Diagnostic humility.** Both hosts stress that sarcoid "wasn't on the radar" until tissue returned, that no biopsy is perfect (time may be needed to confirm), and that the case is fundamentally humbling — "tissue is a real issue," but even tissue must be read against the whole story.

## Teaching Discussion

- The weight-loss filter is portable: inflammatory weight loss signals infection, autoimmunity, or malignancy, and because prominent B symptoms are rare in autoimmune disease (vasculitides and inflammatory myositides aside), a night-sweats syndrome without rash/arthritis/adenopathy is mostly an infection-versus-cancer problem.
- A consolidation must be *chronic* to carry weight — bacterial pneumonia can take weeks to clear. The chronic-consolidation differential is short and worth memorizing: COP (migratory), chronic eosinophilic pneumonia (bilateral + peripheral eosinophilia), alveolar sarcoid (bilateral), lipoid pneumonia (bilateral), and the notorious unilateral pair, mucinous adenocarcinoma and lymphoma.
- Pleural thickening always signals inflammatory pleural disease and skews toward infection and malignancy over autoimmunity; paired with an asbestos-relevant exposure it should raise mesothelioma and mucinous adenocarcinoma.
- A negative bone marrow biopsy excludes metastatic lymphoma, not lymphoma — lymphoma is a cancer of mature lymphocytes residing outside the marrow, so nodal/extranodal disease can persist despite a clean marrow. Tissue from the involved node is the answer.
- Non-caseating granulomas in the US mean infection, sarcoidosis, or lymphoma. Sarcoid is a diagnosis of exclusion with a famously flexible script; you augment its probability by seeking uveitis (overt or subclinical), skin lesions at scars/tattoos, and a suppressed PTH.
- The PTH pearl (via Lawrence Tierney): hypercalciuria is near-universal in sarcoid even when hypercalcemia is not, driven by granulomatous vitamin-D activation, and the resulting calcium load suppresses PTH. Among lymphomas, only Hodgkin tends to do this — and Hodgkin rarely consolidates.
- Maintain vigilance after diagnosing sarcoid: roughly 15% of patients initially labeled sarcoid go on to develop lymphoma (sarcoid-lymphoma syndrome) — whether missed at outset or as a premalignant evolution — so a sarcoid label is not a permanent all-clear.
- Uveitis is a strong symmetry-break toward autoimmune disease (most uveitis idiopathic, ~40% autoimmune, ~20% infectious/syphilis-TB) and fits sarcoid well. And priming the next clinician — documenting at discharge exactly which feature would confirm the diagnosis — converts a future finding into an efficient, immediate confirmation.
