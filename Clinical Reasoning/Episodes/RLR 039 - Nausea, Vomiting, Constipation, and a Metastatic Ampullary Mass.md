## Summary

> [!summary] Scan Summary
> A 51-year-old man who spends six months a year in the Philippines is driven straight from the airport to the ED after about a week of nausea, vomiting, and [[Constipation|constipation]] (obstipation) that began two days before Christmas. He looks *too well* and *too young* for the "routine constipation" the triage note implies — a mismatch Rabih flags by his own metacognition (a brand-new suitcase overturns the reflex stereotype of a marginally-housed patient). Reza sets his [[Center of Gravity|center of gravity]] on the constipation — a 51-year-old presenting to the ED for it is an **[[Alarm Symptoms|alarm sign]]**, not new [[Irritable Bowel Syndrome|IBS]] — and moves first to exclude [[Bowel Obstruction|obstruction]] at the bedside. But the abdomen is **flat, not distended**, with only mild diffuse tenderness, and the patient is well-appearing with normal vitals — deflating the obstruction hypothesis. A **[[Lipase|lipase]] of 2424** then lands like a hammer: it *raises* [[Acute Pancreatitis|acute pancreatitis]], yet by [[Illness Script|Lindsay Shipley's illness script]] (epigastric pain radiating to the back, lipase >3× normal, imaging — any two) the patient meets only one, so the hosts hold the number against the story rather than closing on it. The rest of the labs turn the case: **bilirubin 3 (direct 2.2), alkaline phosphatase 170, normal AST/ALT, normal calcium** — a [[Hepatocellular vs Cholestatic Pattern|cholestatic]] pattern with a predominantly [[Direct Hyperbilirubinemia|direct hyperbilirubinemia]] and normal transaminases, pointing at an **[[Extrahepatic Biliary Obstruction|extrahepatic]]** block; only now does Reza reopen the [[Framing Effect|geographic frame]] he had deliberately pinned, adding biliary [[Biliary Parasites|parasites]] to a duct-anatomy differential. CT delivers the unifier: a **large ampullary mass** (duodenal vs pancreatic), **several hepatic metastases**, and **marked [[Gastric Outlet Obstruction|gastric outlet obstruction]]**. Rabih's retrospective ([[Cognitive Autopsy|cognitive autopsy]]) is the teaching spine — *bowel-obstruction features + biliary-obstruction features + pancreatic features have only one convergence point, the [[Ampulla of Vater|ampulla of Vater]], so by [[Unifying Diagnosis|Occam]] a **[[Periampullary Adenocarcinoma|periampullary malignancy]]** was the answer before the scan* — an answer he anchored past in the moment ("young, healthy → alcohol, gallstones, triglycerides") and Reza reasoned toward. An NG tube relieves him; biopsy is pending.

## Pearls

- **A 51-year-old who comes to the *emergency department* for constipation has already given you an [[Alarm Symptoms|alarm sign]].** [[Constipation|Constipation]] is the commonest of complaints and usually benign, but new organic constipation severe enough to drive an ED visit at 51 is not first-presentation [[Irritable Bowel Syndrome|IBS]] — it demands a cause. Here *more is more, not less*: this patient earned a CT, not a laxative script and a "follow up with your PCP."
- **When several obstructions converge on one anatomic point, [[Unifying Diagnosis|Occam's razor]] can hand you the diagnosis before the imaging does.** Features of **bowel obstruction** (esophagus-to-anus), **biliary obstruction** (sphincter-to-liver), and **pancreatic disease** share exactly one crossroads — the [[Ampulla of Vater|ampulla of Vater]], where the common bile duct and pancreatic duct meet the duodenum. Bowel + biliary + pancreatic obstruction ≈ an **[[Periampullary Adenocarcinoma|ampullary mass]]**; "there's only one real possibility."
- **A sky-high [[Lipase|lipase]] is a raised hypothesis, not a closed diagnosis — hold the number against the [[Illness Script|illness script]].** [[Acute Pancreatitis|Acute pancreatitis]] needs two of three (epigastric pain radiating to the back, lipase >3× ULN, characteristic imaging); a lipase of 2424 supplies one, but a patient whose dominant symptom is *constipation* rather than pain fits poorly — prompting the questions *can lipase be spuriously high?* and *what will the CT show?* rather than premature closure.
- **A predominantly [[Direct Hyperbilirubinemia|direct hyperbilirubinemia]] with a high alkaline phosphatase and *normal* transaminases reads as [[Hepatocellular vs Cholestatic Pattern|cholestatic]] and points [[Extrahepatic Biliary Obstruction|extrahepatic]].** Walk the biliary tree by the [[Anatomical Approach|anatomical approach]] — intrahepatic ducts → common hepatic → cystic → common bile duct → ampulla — and split causes into things *filling the lumen* (stones, [[Biliary Parasites|parasites]], tumor) versus things *compressing from outside* ([[Pancreatic Adenocarcinoma|pancreatic]] mass, [[Cholangiocarcinoma|cholangiocarcinoma]], nodes). The normal AST/ALT and the high lipase both push toward an extrahepatic, ampullary block.
- **Deliberately *pin* a biasing frame, then reopen it on purpose.** Reza consciously kept "the Philippines" out of his time-zero differential to avoid an [[Framing Effect|exotic-organism]] reflex — and only reintroduced travel (biliary parasites) once the cholestatic labs made it earn its place. Bias management is not ignoring data; it's controlling *when* a salient fact is allowed to steer.
- **[[Gastric Outlet Obstruction|Gastric outlet obstruction]] can mask a second diagnosis.** The ampullary mass explains the nausea and vomiting through gastric outlet obstruction *and* the lipase through pancreatic-duct obstruction; the outlet obstruction may be exactly why the classic epigastric-pain-to-the-back phenotype of pancreatitis never surfaced — a reminder that when processes stack, one can hide the fingerprint of another.

## Problem Representation

A 51-year-old man, otherwise healthy, on no medications, who spends half of each year in the Philippines, is driven from the airport to the ED with roughly a week of nausea, vomiting, and progressive constipation (obstipation) that began two days before Christmas, still passing flatus. He is well-appearing with normal vitals and a **flat**, only mildly diffusely tender abdomen, making mechanical bowel obstruction unlikely on exam despite a constipation-first presentation. Labs show a markedly elevated **lipase (2424)** that meets only one criterion of the pancreatitis illness script, together with a **cholestatic pattern** — predominantly direct hyperbilirubinemia (total 3, direct 2.2), alkaline phosphatase 170, normal AST/ALT, normal calcium and CBC — indicating extrahepatic biliary obstruction. CT reveals a **large ampullary mass** (duodenal vs pancreatic origin), **several hepatic metastases**, and **marked gastric outlet obstruction**: a working diagnosis of metastatic periampullary adenocarcinoma that unifies the bowel-, biliary-, and pancreatic-obstruction features, managed acutely with NG decompression, hydration, NPO status, GI consultation for the outlet obstruction, staging (CT chest), and tissue via endoscopic ultrasound with biopsy (pending).

## Case Narrative

The episode opens away from medicine — a running bit about Reza being repeatedly, mercilessly beaten at ping-pong in Iceland by Rabih, whose mother was the under-18 Lebanese youth champion twice over — before Rabih walks into the room and does the real clinical work of the case before a single lab returns: he notices himself *noticing*. The triage complaint is constipation, "a visit that actually is routine," and a lot of older people are constipated; but the patient looks younger than expected and turns out to be 51, and there is a **suitcase**. Rabih narrates the stereotype it triggers — marginal housing — and then, crucially, refuses to lock it "in a concrete box": the bag is brand-new and immaculate, the man freshly showered and shaven. The suitcase is not a sign of homelessness but of *travel*. This patient had flown in from the Philippines, where he stays six months a year, and was driven straight from the airport to the ED. About six days earlier — two days before Christmas — he had developed nausea, vomiting, and worsening constipation, unable to move his bowels and too nauseated to eat; a local ER without diagnostic capability could only hydrate him, and once he felt well enough to hide it he boarded a plane. He is, reassuringly, still passing gas.

Reza praises the [[Metacognition|metacognition]] — recognizing an [[Representativeness Heuristic|unconscious stereotype]] and holding it loosely is the whole move — and then sets his [[Center of Gravity|center of gravity]]. Why prioritize the [[Constipation|constipation]] over the nausea and vomiting? Because the N/V can be *explained through* the constipation and any attendant obstruction, while the constipation is the harder, load-bearing symptom; the honest version is to weight each complaint, but his instinct puts constipation first. And that reframes the case immediately: a 51-year-old presenting to the **ED** for constipation is itself an [[Alarm Symptoms|alarm sign]]. This is not the [[Irritable Bowel Syndrome|IBS]] everyone has experienced (and IBS rarely debuts at 51); there is an etiology to find. His time-zero differential: first and foremost, *is the patient obstructed?* — after a recent run of small- and large-[[Bowel Obstruction|bowel obstruction]] episodes, and with the caveat that passing gas does **not** exclude a partial obstruction, he expects a distended abdomen and makes the physical exam his tier-one test. Then medications (opiates, iron, anticholinergics — the commonest organic causes) and electrolytes (hyper­calcemia, hypokalemia — anything that slows transit).

The exam refuses to cooperate with the obstruction story. The past history is *none*; medications *none*; occasional social alcohol and tobacco; no family history. Vitals are normal, he is not dehydrated, and the belly is **flat — not distended at all** — with only mild tenderness "a little bit everywhere," reassuring enough that Rabih files it as a normal exam. For Reza this substantially lowers [[Bowel Obstruction|bowel obstruction]], where he would have expected distension and a pain/vomiting-dominant rather than constipation-dominant picture. He explicitly notes a de-biasing choice: even though Rabih keeps repeating "the Philippines," he consciously keeps it *out* of his frame at time zero to avoid reflexively reaching for an exotic organism — a pin to be pulled later if needed ([[Framing Effect|framing]] control). His next moves are more history (what does this patient mean by constipation; when was the last bowel movement) plus basic labs for an [[Acute Kidney Injury|AKI]], electrolyte derangement, or [[Hypercalcemia|hypercalcemia]], and imaging held in reserve.

Rabih plays it as it happened: reassured by the exam, he does **not** run to the scanner. He writes for ondansetron, a liter of fluids, and basic labs, thinking maybe this is just gastritis and constipation. Walking to another patient, the nurse stops him — "did you see that lipase?" — and, before he can reach the rest of the results, he is holding a single, memorable number in his mind: **[[Lipase|lipase]] 2424**. Reza's reaction (a joke about nearly kicking his sister's shih tzu) precedes the reasoning: a lipase of 2424 is a striking abnormality that warrants thought, and lipase, in his mind and the listeners', represents [[Acute Pancreatitis|acute pancreatitis]]. But he immediately tests the number against the [[Illness Script|illness script]] — [[Test Interpretation|Lindsay Shipley's]] two-of-three: epigastric pain radiating to the back, lipase >3× the upper limit, or characteristic imaging. This patient has the nausea but *not* the epigastric-to-back pain; he reports **constipation** instead. So far that is one of three. Reza deliberately pauses on two questions rather than closing: *can anything cause an artificially high lipase?* (he admits he isn't sure), and *what if the CT is normal — can I still diagnose pancreatitis on the number alone?* He would obtain a contrast CT of the abdomen and pelvis to confirm pancreatic inflammation, and if confirmed, start the journey of *why* — the top causes being alcohol, gallstones, hypertriglyceridemia, and idiopathic.

Rabih then poses the exercise that, by his own admission, he *failed in real time and only solved in retrospect*: what is the overlap between pancreatitis (or "pancreas NOS") and the vibe of a bowel obstruction — constipation, nausea, vomiting, and a very high lipase? Reza works it live. Mechanically: could a lesion block the **pancreatic duct** and *partially* obstruct the small bowel at once? He floats [[Gallstone Ileus|gallstone ileus]] and discards it (the patient isn't sick enough), then a **pancreatic cancer** compressing both the duct and the bowel — though that would be an odd way to produce *constipation*. Non-mechanically: [[Hypercalcemia|hypercalcemia]] could tie pancreatitis, nausea, and constipation together (so he wants the calcium). Pushed further, and now focused on the constipation, he reaches for a zebra — [[Acute Intermittent Porphyria|acute intermittent porphyria]] — which can cause constipation but not really pancreatitis, and dismisses it. His summary: alcohol can't explain everything, triglycerides can't, gallstones *might*, and a mechanical obstruction plus an electrolyte abnormality remain in play.

The rest of the labs redirect the case. CBC normal (normal hemoglobin, normal white count); electrolytes normal, **including a normal calcium** (deleting the hypercalcemia bridge). But the **bilirubin is 3 with a direct of 2.2**, the **AST and ALT are normal**, and the **alkaline phosphatase is 170**. Reza reads this cleanly as a [[Hepatocellular vs Cholestatic Pattern|cholestatic]] pattern of liver injury with a predominantly [[Direct Hyperbilirubinemia|direct hyperbilirubinemia]]. The reflex question for direct hyperbilirubinemia is *intrahepatic vs [[Extrahepatic Biliary Obstruction|extrahepatic]]* — and here an inflamed pancreas (high lipase) *and* normal transaminases both push hard toward extrahepatic. Intrahepatic causes he names (viruses, drugs, infiltrative processes); extrahepatic causes (stones, strictures, masses, enlarged nodes). **Now** he pulls the geographic pin: the Philippines reopens the differential to biliary [[Biliary Parasites|parasites]] that can infiltrate the biliary system and obstruct both the bile and pancreatic ducts. He works the [[Anatomical Approach|biliary anatomy]] explicitly — right and left intrahepatic ducts to the common hepatic, the cystic duct joining to form the common bile duct, tapering to the [[Ampulla of Vater|ampulla of Vater]] where the pancreatic duct also ends — and sorts causes into things *filling the duct* (stones, parasites, cancers) versus *compressing from outside* ([[Pancreatic Adenocarcinoma|pancreatic cancer]], [[Cholangiocarcinoma|cholangiocarcinoma]]). He also asks whether anyone performed an ERCP or endoscopy in the Philippines that could have caused [[Post-ERCP Pancreatitis|post-procedure pancreatitis]] or biliary trauma — they did not. His pre-CT summary: a middle-aged, recently-traveled man with a week of nausea, vomiting, and constipation, a benign exam, a very high lipase, and a cholestatic pattern most consistent with **extrahepatic** obstruction — next step, imaging for a mass.

The CT confirms it and worse: a **large ampullary mass**, hard to call duodenal versus pancreatic, **several hepatic metastases**, and **marked [[Gastric Outlet Obstruction|gastric outlet obstruction]]**. Reza reads the unifier immediately — the mass explains the nausea and vomiting (outlet obstruction) *and* the very high lipase (pancreatic-duct obstruction), and he wonders aloud whether the gastric outlet obstruction is precisely why the classic epigastric-pain-to-the-back phenotype never appeared: when multiple processes stack, one can mask another. The dreaded possibilities are [[Cholangiocarcinoma|cholangiocarcinoma]], [[Pancreatic Adenocarcinoma|pancreatic adenocarcinoma]], or [[Duodenal Adenocarcinoma|duodenal adenocarcinoma]], all with a natural drainage route to the liver via the portal vein (hence [[Hepatic Metastasis|hepatic metastases]] as an expected first site). He keeps a short differential for non-adenocarcinoma ([[Lymphoma|lymphoma]], less likely without lymphadenopathy) and non-cancer ([[IgG4-Related Disease|IgG4-related disease]] — which he explicitly flags as the steroid-treatable mimic *not* to miss, though the hepatic metastases argue this is not an isolated pancreatic mass). Management: inpatient [[Endoscopic Ultrasound|endoscopic ultrasound]] with biopsy for tissue; a CT chest to complete staging; a GI consult around how obstructed he is and whether a **stent** can relieve the outlet obstruction; NPO with IV fluids; early thought to [[Parenteral Nutrition|parenteral nutrition]] given an uncertain time to eating; and [[Tumor Markers|tumor markers]] ([[CA 19-9|CA 19-9]], CEA) sent — acknowledged as tracking rather than screening tools. An **NG tube** was placed and the patient felt considerably better.

Rabih closes on the reflection that gives the episode its title. This "bread and butter" case was set up to be slammed as a bowel obstruction, then softened by the flat abdomen, then hijacked by a huge pancreatic signal — genuinely tricky. In the moment he anchored: pancreatitis in a young, otherwise-healthy man → alcohol, gallstones, triglycerides. When the CT returned a malignancy, he was floored. Spending New Year's Eve on a [[Cognitive Autopsy|cognitive autopsy]] of the case, he realized malignancy had been the *only* reasonable possibility all along — the man didn't drink, had no biliary colic, no reason to be hypertriglyceridemic, and yet had biliary obstruction *and* bowel-obstruction features *and* pancreatic disease. "What's bowel obstruction plus biliary obstruction plus pancreatic obstruction? Ampullary mass. There's only one real possibility" — and, by [[Unifying Diagnosis|Occam]], the answer was reachable *before* the scan. Reza had connected the dots; Rabih hadn't, and names the growth honestly. The shared human note is the hardest part: telling an otherwise-healthy 51-year-old, across the room, that he likely has cancer. Biopsy is pending as of a Friday; an update is promised. The final teaching line is the one from the top — for a 51-year-old with debilitating constipation, *more is more*: this patient needed a CT, not reassurance.

### Illness Scripts and Bias

- **[[Unifying Diagnosis|Occam over Hickam]] — one anatomic crossroads.** The episode's spine is that features of **bowel obstruction** (esophagus-to-anus), **biliary obstruction** (sphincter-to-liver), and **pancreatic disease** intersect at a single point, the [[Ampulla of Vater|ampulla of Vater]]; a lesion there produces all three, so the parsimonious explanation is a [[Periampullary Adenocarcinoma|periampullary mass]]. Rabih's insight is that this was deducible *before* imaging — the [[Cognitive Autopsy|cognitive autopsy]] revealing that the answer "could only be one thing."
- **[[Center of Gravity|Center of gravity]] plus the [[Alarm Symptoms|alarm-symptom]] reframe.** Choosing constipation as the load-bearing complaint (because N/V can be explained through it) and recognizing that a 51-year-old in the ED for constipation is itself a red flag converts a "routine" visit into a mandatory hunt for an organic cause — the anti–[[Search Satisficing|premature-disposition]] move.
- **[[Illness Script|Illness-script matching]] and [[Test Interpretation|test interpretation]] of the lipase.** A lipase of 2424 is a *raised hypothesis*, not a closed case. Applying Shipley's two-of-three script (the patient meets one) and asking whether lipase can be spuriously elevated keeps the number in tension with the story rather than triggering closure — the discipline that leaves room for the real diagnosis.
- **[[Anatomical Approach|Anatomical reasoning]] through the biliary tree.** A predominantly [[Direct Hyperbilirubinemia|direct hyperbilirubinemia]] with high ALP and normal transaminases ([[Hepatocellular vs Cholestatic Pattern|cholestatic pattern]]) is localized by walking duct anatomy to the ampulla and sorting causes into *intraluminal* (stones, [[Biliary Parasites|parasites]], tumor) versus *compressive* ([[Pancreatic Adenocarcinoma|pancreatic]] mass, [[Cholangiocarcinoma|cholangiocarcinoma]], nodes).
- **[[Framing Effect|Framing control]] — pinning and reopening the geography.** Reza's deliberate exclusion of "the Philippines" at time zero (to avoid an exotic-organism reflex), then its purposeful reintroduction once cholestatic labs earned it, is a model of *managing when* a salient datum is allowed to steer — bias control, not data suppression.
- **[[Representativeness Heuristic|Representativeness] and [[Metacognition|metacognition]] at the door.** Rabih narrates and then overrides two stereotypes: the suitcase as marginal housing (corrected by its newness), and — the one that caught him — "young and healthy pancreatitis → alcohol/gallstones/triglycerides," a representativeness prior that anchored him away from malignancy until the CT.
- **[[Gastric Outlet Obstruction|Masking].** Stacked processes obscured each other: the outlet obstruction plausibly suppressed the classic epigastric-pain-to-back phenotype, so the pancreatitis presented only as a number — a caution against reading an *atypical* presentation as evidence against a diagnosis when a second process can explain the atypia.

## Teaching Discussion

- Set the center of gravity on the hardest-to-explain complaint. Nausea and vomiting can be downstream of many things; a 51-year-old presenting to the ED for constipation is the load-bearing, alarming datum — treat new organic constipation at that age as a red flag warranting a cause, not a laxative and a discharge.
- Keep a very high lipase in tension with the illness script. Two of three (epigastric pain to the back, lipase >3× ULN, characteristic imaging) diagnoses acute pancreatitis; when only the number is present, ask whether it can be spuriously elevated and what the imaging will show before closing — the magnitude of a test result does not substitute for a matching story.
- Localize a cholestatic pattern anatomically. Predominantly direct hyperbilirubinemia with a raised alkaline phosphatase and normal transaminases points extrahepatic; walk the biliary tree to the ampulla and divide causes into intraluminal (stones, parasites, tumor) versus extrinsic compression (pancreatic mass, cholangiocarcinoma, nodes), letting normal AST/ALT and a high lipase argue for an ampullary-level block.
- Use Occam deliberately when obstructions converge. Bowel-, biliary-, and pancreatic-obstruction features meet at one place — the ampulla of Vater — so a single periampullary lesion is the parsimonious unifier; the diagnosis was reachable from the pattern before the CT confirmed it.
- Manage biasing frames by controlling their timing. Consciously excluding travel history early to avoid an exotic-organism reflex, then reintroducing it once the labs demanded (biliary parasites on a cholestatic pattern), is disciplined framing — and pairs with holding first-glance stereotypes (the suitcase; "young, healthy pancreatitis") loosely rather than in a concrete box.
- Expect stacked diagnoses to mask one another. Gastric outlet obstruction explains the vomiting and may be why the pancreatitis lacked its classic pain; an atypical presentation is weak evidence against a diagnosis when a concurrent process can account for the atypia.
- Treat the whole obstructing malignancy, not just the label. Relieve the gastric outlet obstruction (NG decompression now, endoscopic stent as feasible), keep NPO with fluids and anticipate parenteral nutrition, obtain tissue by EUS-guided biopsy, complete staging with a CT chest, and send tumor markers (CA 19-9, CEA) understanding they track disease rather than screen for it — while carrying the human weight of disclosing likely cancer to an otherwise-healthy 51-year-old.

## Handbook Opportunities

- A **periampullary / ampullary mass** card — the ampulla as the one crossroads of bile, pancreatic duct, and duodenum; the bowel + biliary + pancreatic "triple-obstruction" signature and its differential (ampullary, pancreatic head, duodenal, distal cholangiocarcinoma).
- A **cholestatic pattern** card — direct hyperbilirubinemia + high ALP + normal transaminases → extrahepatic; the intraluminal-vs-compressive duct schema and when to add travel/parasite causes.
- An **acute pancreatitis** card — the two-of-three criteria, lipase interpretation (magnitude vs script; spurious elevation), and the alcohol/gallstone/triglyceride/obstructive causal tree.
- A **constipation red flags** card — age of new onset, ED presentation, weight loss, anemia, and the "more is more" threshold for imaging.
- A **gastric outlet obstruction** card — causes, NG decompression, endoscopic stenting, and its masking of coexisting disease.
