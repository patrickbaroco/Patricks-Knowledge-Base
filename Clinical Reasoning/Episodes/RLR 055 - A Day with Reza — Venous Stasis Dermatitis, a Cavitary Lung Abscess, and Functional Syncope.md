> A **"day in the life of a teaching attending"** — Reza narrates **three real cases from a single day**, and Rabih reasons through each. *(Note: despite RLR 054's teaser, this episode does **not** resolve the post-TMJ hyperthermia cliffhanger.)* **Case 1 — the red legs.** A **cognitively intact 92-year-old** has **10 days of bilateral, symmetric, foot-to-mid-shin erythema** that failed augmentin and Bactrim (and got vancomycin) — non-blanching, warm, painful — two weeks after a **mechanical fall** left him **bed-bound**, on a background of **venous insufficiency**. Rabih's pivot: **bilateral cellulitis requires two independent simultaneous infections** (unlike bilateral pneumonia/pyelonephritis, which share a central connection) and is **overwhelmingly rare**, so this is a **cellulitis mimic**. The mimics sort into **non-infectious inflammation** (crystalline — gout/pseudogout with soft-tissue spread; eosinophilic cellulitis), **vascular/plumbing** (the common one: **venous stasis dermatitis**), and **irritant** (contact dermatitis, e.g., repeated hydrogen peroxide). With **near-normal inflammatory markers** and the story of new **dependency/immobility**, the diagnosis is **[[venous stasis dermatitis]]** — confirmed by **dramatic improvement with hours of leg elevation** (dermatology initially called it cellulitis; the rash **resolved in 4 days** on **elevation + topical steroid**). **Case 2 — the cachectic man with a cavity.** A **76-year-old** (CAD, HTN, CKD3, **blind**, grieving a beloved caregiver who died a year ago) with **80 lb of weight loss**, profound **cachexia**, and **incontinence from weakness** — worked up for a **catabolic solid tumor vs depression** — has **elevated ESR/CRP**, a **K of 2.5 with Mg 1.6 and normal pH** (**repleted Mg first**, then K), and a **CT showing a cavitary lung lesion**. The cavity means **necrosis** (inflammation — cancer/infection/immune — or **ischemia**); chronicity + minimal inflammation raises **cancer**, but the paradox — **so cachectic yet no metastases** — argues against it, and a **BAL grows MSSA**: a **[[lung abscess]]**, treated with plan to re-image (PET-CT lit only that lesion). **Case 3 — the rapid response.** A **man in his 50s** with reactive airway disease on around-the-clock albuterol develops a **narrow-complex regular tachycardia (100→140)**, is **hypertensive**, and **passes out after coughing** with brief left-arm shaking — then **wakes instantly and says "ow" to a pinch**. Rabih builds the **narrow-complex-tachycardia** differential (AVNRT/AVRT/atrial tach/flutter; **adenosine** as diagnostic *and* therapeutic), the **"S's" of transient LOC** (seizure, sugar, syncope — incl. **cough syncope**), and the tachycardia-plus-hypertension **don't-miss** list (substances, withdrawal, **hyperthyroidism**). Reza lands it: **tachycardic and hypertensive throughout, instant recovery, no postictal state** → a **[[functional neurological disorder|functional/psychogenic]]** event; chart review reveals **severe PTSD** (on sertraline/lorazepam), and by next morning his sleeping heart rate was 60 — a **pseudo-pheochromocytoma** physiology, handled with **de-stigmatized** psychiatry involvement. Threaded throughout: **base rate**, **NPV of inflammatory markers**, **intellectual honesty about made-up mechanisms**, and the humanity of the isolated patient.

**Pearls**

- **Bilateral "cellulitis" should trip an alarm.** Cellulitis is a **local, single-limb** event; **bilateral** disease would require **two independent simultaneous infections**, which is vanishingly rare — unlike bilateral **pneumonia** or **pyelonephritis**, where a **central connection** (trachea → both bronchi; bladder → both ureters) makes bilaterality plausible. **Bilateral red legs are a mimic until proven otherwise.**
- **The three buckets of [[cellulitis mimics]]:** **non-infectious inflammation** (crystalline disease — **gout/pseudogout** with peri-articular soft-tissue spread; **eosinophilic cellulitis/Wells**), **vascular/"plumbing"** (**[[venous stasis dermatitis]]** — the commonest), and **irritant** (**contact dermatitis** — classically **repeated hydrogen peroxide** on a post-fall abrasion).
- **[[Venous stasis dermatitis]] is an *-itis* — it looks inflammatory but isn't infected.** Suspect it with **bilaterality, known venous disease, and new dependency/immobility**; confirm with a **low systemic inflammatory signature** (near-normal WBC/CRP, ESR not high) and — the bedside test — **dramatic improvement after 1–2 hours of leg elevation**. Treat with **elevation** (most important), **emollients**, and **topical steroids** for the flare.
- **Name your made-up mechanisms.** Attendings "see something so often they invent an explanation." The honest move — *"that makes sense in my head, but I'll read about it"* — models intellectual humility and separates **pattern recognition** from **verified pathophysiology**.
- **Cachexia is a catabolic problem until proven otherwise.** Severe unintentional weight loss with a **catabolic** state and **no prominent inflammatory fingerprint** (no fevers/sweats) most worries for a **solid tumor** — but keep **depression**, **frailty**, and mechanical contributors (BPH/constipation → poor intake, AKI) on the list. **ESR/CRP have real negative predictive value** to gate cross-sectional imaging.
- **Fix magnesium before potassium.** **Hypokalemia with a normal pH** means potassium is either **shifted intracellularly** or being **renally wasted due to hypomagnesemia** (low Mg releases the **ROMK** channel's brake, wasting K). Without an insulin/β-agonist shift, suspect **[[hypomagnesemia and hypokalemia|Mg-driven K wasting]]** — **replete Mg first**, or the K won't hold.
- **A cavity means necrosis — two roads in: inflammation or ischemia.** Inflammation = **cancer, infection, or autoimmune**; ischemia = a (rare) **cavitating PE**. A **chronic (>3-month) cavity with minimal inflammatory symptoms** most worries **cancer** — but **"so sick yet no metastases"** argues against cancer and toward **infection** (anaerobic **[[lung abscess]]** with poor dentition/aspiration; TB/endemic mycoses by exposure). Here a **BAL grew MSSA**.
- **Not all transient LOC with limb-shaking is a seizure.** **Tachycardic *and* hypertensive throughout, instant lucid recovery, and no postictal state** point away from seizure/vasovagal (which trends **brady**) and toward a **[[functional neurological disorder|functional/psychogenic]]** event — a **pseudo-pheochromocytoma** adrenergic surge (often **PTSD**-driven, sometimes with elevated metanephrines). Diagnose it **positively and without stigma**, not by exclusion alone. Still screen the **tachycardia-plus-hypertension** don't-miss: **substances/withdrawal** and **hyperthyroidism**.

**Problem Representation**
Three same-day cases: (1) a 92-year-old with antibiotic-refractory bilateral leg erythema and near-normal inflammatory markers → venous stasis dermatitis (a cellulitis mimic) that resolved with elevation; (2) a 76-year-old with profound cachexia, hypomagnesemic hypokalemia, and a cavitary lung lesion → an MSSA lung abscess rather than metastatic cancer; and (3) a 50-something man with a narrow-complex tachycardia and cough-associated transient LOC → a functional/psychogenic (pseudo-pheochromocytoma) event on a background of PTSD.

## Case Narrative

The framing device is **"a day with Reza"** — three cases lived across one attending shift, presented in real time, with Rabih (on call, interruptible) as discussant. *(Contrary to RLR 054's promise, the post-operative TMJ hyperthermia case is not revisited here.)*

**Case 1 — the red legs.** A **cognitively superb 92-year-old** presents with **10 days of worsening bilateral lower-extremity erythema** despite **augmentin then Bactrim** (and vancomycin in the ED). The rash is **bilateral and nearly symmetric**, running **foot to mid-shin**, deeply erythematous and coalescing, **non-blanching, warm, and painful**. Two weeks earlier he'd had a **mechanical fall** (foot caught, no LOC) and became **sedentary**; he has known **venous insufficiency**. Rabih anchors on the **bilaterality**: cellulitis arises when a **locally compromised limb** lets bacteria through, and to invoke that **rare event happening independently and synchronously on both legs** is "overwhelmingly rare" — unlike bilateral **pneumonia** or **pyelonephritis**, where a **central connection** makes two-sided disease easy. So he **tosses cellulitis** and reaches for the **mimic** framework: **non-infectious inflammation** (crystalline **gout/pseudogout** with soft-tissue extension; **eosinophilic cellulitis**), **vascular** (**venous stasis dermatitis**, the common one), and **irritant** (**contact dermatitis** — the classic **repeated hydrogen peroxide** on a post-fall abrasion). Given **known venous disease + new immobility/dependency**, the working diagnosis is **[[venous stasis dermatitis]]** — "the last word is *dermatitis*; it's an *-itis*, so it looks inflammatory." He would confirm two ways: a **low systemic inflammatory signature** and the **bedside elevation test** (1–2 hours in Trendelenburg → "incredible" improvement). Asked the *mechanism* of stasis-with-dermatitis vs bland edema, Rabih models **intellectual honesty**: he's "made up an explanation" (abrupt venous-pressure rise → fluid leak → secondary inflammation) that he'll **read about later**. The labs fit — **WBC ~8k (baseline 4), CRP up but ESR not, no acidosis**. In real life Reza got **dermatology**, who called it cellulitis and said "get ID," but his own read (discordant angry-rash-well-patient) held: with **elevation + triamcinolone/hydrocortisone**, the rash **improved by day 1 and resolved by day 4** — extravasated red cells and stasis inflammation, not infection.

**Case 2 — the cachectic man with a cavity.** A **76-year-old** with **CAD, HTN, CKD3**, and **blindness** lost his **beloved live-in caregiver** a year ago, spiraled into **depression**, and **lost ~80 pounds** — now **profoundly cachectic** (temporal wasting, visible ribs, thighs "the size of a bicep"), with **poor appetite** and **urinary/bowel incontinence** attributed to **weakness** ("can't make it to the bathroom"), **no sensory deficit or focal weakness**, and **no adenopathy/thrush**. Rabih frames **cachexia as a catabolic problem** — most worrisome for a **solid tumor without an inflammatory fingerprint** — while explicitly holding **depression** and **mechanical** contributors (BPH/constipation → AKI, poor intake) open, and prizing the **welcome** owed to an isolated man. He leans on the **NPV of ESR/CRP** to gate imaging. Labs show **inflammation (elevated ESR/CRP), anemia, mild leukocytosis**, and a striking **K 2.5, Mg 1.6, normal pH**. Rabih reasons the **normal pH** excludes a big acid-base shift, so the hypokalemia is either an **intracellular shift** (no insulin/β-agonist here) or **hypomagnesemia-driven renal wasting** — the latter, so **replete Mg first, then K** (which normalized after Mg, as Reza confirms; the K 2.5 had prolonged the QTc on telemetry). The patient **declines an MRI spine** (capacity respected). A **contrast CT chest/abdomen/pelvis** reveals a **cavitary lung lesion** near the anterior chest wall. Rabih's cavity logic: a cavity means **necrosis**, whose two roads are **inflammation** (cancer/infection/autoimmune) and **ischemia** (a rare cavitating **chronic PE**); a **chronic cavity with minimal inflammatory symptoms** worries **cancer**, but the **paradox** — a man *this* devastated with **no metastatic disease** — argues *against* cancer, favoring **infection** (an **anaerobic lung abscess** from suboptimal dentition/aspiration, or TB/endemic mycoses by exposure). The radiologist favored **atypical bacteria (Actinomyces/Nocardia)**, thought it "**not squamous, not TB**," and flagged **tiny incidental PEs** ("don't anticoagulate — the cavity may bleed"). A **BAL grew MSSA** → an **[[lung abscess]]**; a **whole-body PET-CT lit only that lesion**; plan was **targeted antibiotics and re-imaging at 4 weeks**, with **CT-guided biopsy** if it grows.

**Case 3 — the rapid response.** Mid-thought on the potassium, a **rapid response** pulls Reza away: a **man in his 50s** with **reactive airway disease** (COPD/asthma), just admitted and on **around-the-clock albuterol**, whose heart rate jumped **100 → 140**. The rhythm is **narrow-complex, regular, without clear P waves**, and he's **hypertensive**; he reports **passing out after coughing**. As they work, he has a **coughing fit, closes his eyes, "passes out," and shakes his left arm** — then a **firm pinch snaps him awake** with an "**ow**." Rabih separates the strands. The **narrow-complex regular tachycardia** differential: **atrial tachycardia, junctional, atrial flutter with fixed block, AVNRT, AVRT** — often adrenergically driven, frequently self-terminating or managed with **AV-nodal blockers**, and **adenosine** as both **diagnostic** (transient AV block unmasks P-wave morphology/RP interval) and **therapeutic** (terminates most AVNRT/AVRT, ~30% of atrial tach). The **transient LOC** invokes the **"S's"** — **seizure** (the arm-shaking), **sugar** (always check glucose), and **syncope**, including **cough syncope** (high vagal tone) — with the **instant, lucid recovery** favoring a non-epileptic cause. And a crucial **don't-miss** for **tachycardia + hypertension**: **substances** (cocaine/amphetamine), **withdrawal** (alcohol/benzodiazepines), and **hyperthyroidism** ("one thing I'll never miss again"). Reza then supplies the resolution: the patient was **tachycardic *and* hypertensive throughout** (vasovagal syncope should trend **brady**), recovered **instantly with a pinch**, and had **no postictal state** — pointing to a **[[functional neurological disorder|functional/psychogenic]]** event. Chart review (JLV) showed **severe PTSD** on **sertraline and lorazepam**; by the next morning his **sleeping heart rate was in the 60s**. He frames it as a **pseudo-pheochromocytoma** adrenergic surge (PTSD-driven, sometimes with **elevated metanephrines**) — **real, not malingering or factitious** — and got **psychiatry/CL** involved early and **without stigma**. Rabih affirms the humane, **positive** diagnosis of functional disorders ("originating in the prefrontal cortex," a disorder of motor planning), and Matt closes.

### Illness Scripts and Bias

The unifying lesson across the day is **base rate plus discordance**. In **Case 1**, the base rate of **bilateral independent cellulitis** is so low that its mere presence flips the leading diagnosis, and the **discordance** between an **angry-looking rash** and a **well-looking patient with bland markers** seals it — the elevation test converting suspicion to proof. Rabih's **"I made up this mechanism"** aside is a small masterclass in **calibration**: distinguishing what you *do* (a correct, reliable management move) from what you can *explain* (an unverified just-so story), and refusing to conflate them. **Case 2** is a study in **holding two frames** — cachexia screams cancer, yet the **"too devastated to have no metastases"** discordance re-weights toward infection, and the humble truth (an **MSSA abscess** amplified by **age, blindness, CAD, and frailty**) resolves it; the **Mg-before-K** rule is the electrolyte-reasoning gem. **Case 3** is the day's **anti-anchoring** finale: the seductive scripts (SVT, seizure, vasovagal) are each **contradicted by a vital-sign or recovery detail**, and the **functional** diagnosis is reached **positively** — via the *pattern* (sustained adrenergic surge, instant lucid recovery, PTSD, normalized sleeping heart rate) — rather than by exclusion, while still **respecting the don't-miss** organic causes (substances, withdrawal, **hyperthyroidism**). The connective tissue is **humanism**: welcoming the isolated cachectic man, a "loving pinch," and a **de-stigmatized** functional diagnosis treated as a real disease of the brain.

## Teaching Discussion

**The bilateral-red-leg problem.** Lower-extremity cellulitis is **overdiagnosed**; up to a large fraction of "cellulitis" admissions are **mimics** ("pseudocellulitis"). The single most useful heuristic is **laterality**: **unilateral** favors cellulitis; **bilateral** strongly favors a **mimic**, because true cellulitis is a **local** process and simultaneous independent bilateral infection is rare. Sort the mimics into **non-infectious inflammation** (crystalline gout/pseudogout with soft-tissue spread, eosinophilic cellulitis/**Wells syndrome**, panniculitis), **vascular/"plumbing"** (**stasis dermatitis**, lipodermatosclerosis, DVT, chronic venous insufficiency), and **irritant/contact** (repeated antiseptics, adhesives). Supportive discriminators for **stasis dermatitis**: bilaterality, chronic venous disease, medial "inverted-champagne-bottle" changes, **bland inflammatory markers**, and **improvement with elevation** — the closest thing to a bedside diagnostic test.

**Cachexia and unintentional weight loss.** Frame severe involuntary weight loss as **catabolic vs anabolic-deficient**. A **catabolic** state without an obvious inflammatory fingerprint most worries **malignancy** (classically a **solid tumor**), but the differential must include **depression** and **social/functional** drivers (isolation, food insecurity, dysphagia, dementia) and mechanical contributors. **ESR/CRP** are imperfect but have useful **negative predictive value** for gating cross-sectional imaging when the pretest probability of an occult catabolic disease is moderate.

**Hypokalemia with a normal pH, and the magnesium rule.** For hypokalemia, first ask **shift vs loss**: a **normal pH** excludes large acid-base–driven shifts, so consider an **insulin/β-agonist** shift (absent here) or **renal/GI loss**. **Hypomagnesemia causes refractory hypokalemia**: low intracellular Mg **releases inhibition of the ROMK channel** in the distal nephron, accelerating **potassium secretion** — so potassium **won't correct until magnesium is replaced**. Practically: **replete Mg first (or concurrently), then K**. Severe hypokalemia (here **2.5**) prolongs the **QT** and predisposes to arrhythmia — telemetry is warranted.

**The cavitary lung lesion.** A cavity implies **tissue necrosis**, reachable by **inflammation** (**cancer** — especially squamous; **infection** — anaerobic **lung abscess**, TB, endemic mycoses/**molds**, **Nocardia/Actinomyces**, septic emboli; **autoimmune** — GPA, rheumatoid nodules) or **ischemia** (a cavitating **pulmonary infarct**). Two tempo/context clues steer it: **chronicity >3 months with minimal inflammatory symptoms** raises **cancer**, and the **exposure history** shapes the infectious workup (TB contacts, travel/endemic regions, dentition/aspiration risk). The **"too sick, too local"** paradox — profound cachexia with **no metastases** — is a real argument **against** metastatic cancer. Workup pairs a **thorough infectious evaluation** (AFB, fungal serologies, cultures) with a decision about **empiric anaerobic coverage + interval imaging** vs **early tissue** (BAL cytology/culture or biopsy); here **BAL grew MSSA**, and **PET-CT** avidity confined to the lesion supported a localized process.

**Narrow-complex regular tachycardia and adenosine.** The **regular, narrow-complex** SVTs are **AVNRT, AVRT, atrial tachycardia, junctional tachycardia, and atrial flutter with fixed block**. **Adenosine** is doubly valuable: **diagnostically**, transient AV block **unmasks** flutter waves or defines the P-QRS relationship (RP interval); **therapeutically**, it **terminates** the majority of **AVNRT/AVRT** and ~30% of **atrial tachycardia**. Rate-control/termination otherwise uses **AV-nodal blockers** (β-blocker/CCB), with **ablation** for recurrent cases. An abrupt **100 → 140** step favors a **paroxysmal SVT** over gradual sinus tachycardia.

**Cough syncope and the "S's" of transient LOC.** Sort transient loss of consciousness into **Seizure**, **Sugar (hypoglycemia)**, and **Syncope** — adding **functional/psychogenic** as a modern fourth. **Cough syncope** is a **reflex (vasovagal)** faint driven by high intrathoracic pressure and vagal tone, classically in men with obstructive lung disease and paroxysmal cough. Vasovagal events usually show **bradycardia/hypotension** and a brief recovery; **sustained tachycardia and hypertension** through the event argue against it.

**Functional neurological disorder — a positive, de-stigmatized diagnosis.** **[[Functional neurological disorder|Functional/dissociative]]** events are diagnosed by **positive features**, not exclusion: **instant, oriented recovery**, **inconsistency/distractibility**, **absence of a postictal state**, and physiology discordant with the proposed organic cause (here, an **adrenergic surge** with normal sleeping heart rate). The **pseudo-pheochromocytoma** phenotype — paroxysmal hypertension, tachycardia, and even **elevated metanephrines** without a tumor — is frequently **PTSD-associated**. Diagnose it **empathetically and affirmatively** ("real, not feigned; a disorder of brain function/motor planning"), engage **psychiatry**, and still **screen the organic don't-miss list** for tachycardia + hypertension: **stimulant intoxication, alcohol/benzodiazepine withdrawal, and hyperthyroidism**.
