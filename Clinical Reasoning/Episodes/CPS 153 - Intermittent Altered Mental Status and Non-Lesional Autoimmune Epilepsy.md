> A neurology-focused Virtual Morning Report — the first of the CP Solvers "**end [[Neurophobia|neurophobia]]**" series with neurologist **Aaron Berkowitz** — built around a framework as much as a diagnosis. A 68-year-old retired, high-functioning man presents with three-to-five weeks of *intermittent* confusion: word-finding trouble, inability to give directions home, and finally an episode of unresponsiveness. Berkowitz teaches the case through his "**[[Localization × Time Course|E=mc² of neurology]]**" — differential = [[Anatomical Approach|localization]] × [[Illness Tempo|time course]] — and walks the [[Altered Mental Status|altered mental status]] differential bucket by tempo (instant = vascular/[[Seizure|seizure]]/toxic-metabolic; acute = infection/inflammatory; subacute = neoplasm/antibody-mediated/indolent infection; chronic = degenerative), with the first branch always **neurologic vs non-neurologic**. The single word *intermittent* reframes everything toward seizure, and a striking bedside detail seals it: examined mid-episode the patient is mute and unresponsive with a sinus tachycardia to 113 and transient hypoxia, then ~45 minutes later is fully conversant with a completely normal neurologic exam. Labs, CT, LP, and MRI are all unrevealing; a [[Benzodiazepine Challenge|benzodiazepine challenge]] paradoxically *sedates* rather than wakes him; spot EEG shows frontal discharges and continuous video EEG during the episodes captures rhythmic 3–4 Hz polyspike **seizures**. With imaging non-lesional, the landing is **new-onset [[Non-Lesional Epilepsy|non-lesional epilepsy]] in an older adult, presumed [[Autoimmune Encephalitis|antibody-mediated / autoimmune]]** (anti-LGI1, anti-GABA-B, anti-NMDA on the differential) — antibody confirmation still pending when the presenter was pulled off service for COVID.

## Pearls

- **Differential = [[Anatomical Approach|localization]] × [[Illness Tempo|time course]] — Berkowitz's "[[Localization × Time Course|E=mc² of neurology]]."** Localization answers *where* (here, brain → [[Altered Mental Status|altered mental status]]); time course answers *what*. Localization takes years to master, but tempo is quick and high-yield — and it works across all of medicine, not just neurology.
- **Bucket the AMS differential by tempo.** *Instant* ("[[Seizure|seizure]], vascular, toxic-metabolic, trauma — instant neurology, just add water" for stroke); *acute* hours-to-days (bacterial/viral infection, inflammatory/demyelinating flares); *subacute* weeks-to-months (neoplasm, antibody-mediated/paraneoplastic, indolent fungal/TB/HIV infection, the rare rapidly-progressive prion dementia); *chronic* (degenerative). **Toxic-metabolic fits every bucket** — which is why it must be named, not waved at.
- **First branch in altered mental status: neurologic vs non-neurologic.** Every organ system can cause AMS, so before localizing, Berkowitz's discipline is to decide whether this is a brain problem at all — and if you invoke "toxic-metabolic," name *which* toxin or metabolite rather than signing off. (A discussant separately offered the [[MIST Mnemonic|MIST]] schema — metabolic, infectious, structural, toxin — as a complementary way to name the cause; it is *not* part of Berkowitz's framework, whose signature is the time-course buckets below.)
- **The word *intermittent* changes the case.** Intermittent AMS with a return toward baseline points at [[Seizure|seizure]]; here the episodes lengthened with *incomplete* recovery between them, suggesting recurrent (often [[Non-Convulsive Status Epilepticus|non-convulsive]]) seizures superimposed on a background process. Not all seizures shake — focal seizures with altered awareness and post-ictal states can present as pure confusion.
- **New-onset seizure in an older adult with a non-lesional MRI = antibody-mediated epilepsy until proven otherwise.** [[Autoimmune Encephalitis|Autoimmune/antibody-mediated syndromes]] (anti-LGI1 with faciobrachial dystonic seizures + hyponatremia, anti-GABA-B causing isolated epilepsy, anti-NMDA) are *not* all paraneoplastic — many have <5–50% cancer association — so hunt for a tumor but don't require one; send serum **and** CSF and call the reference lab.
- **Biopsy your gut before the test.** Predict *normal vs abnormal* for the MRI/EEG/LP before ordering, so a surprise becomes a [[Test Interpretation|calibration]] moment; remember a truly normal MRI is rare (incidentalomas abound), that [[Seizure|seizures]] themselves can produce cortical MRI changes and autonomic swings (the heart-brain "cardunculus"), and watch [[Anchoring Bias|anchoring]] on a first impression as data accrues.

## Problem Representation

A 68-year-old retired, previously high-functioning man with only well-controlled hypertension presents with three-to-five weeks of intermittent, stereotyped episodes of confusion — expressive difficulty (word-finding, sentence formation, inability to give directions) — that progressively lengthened with incomplete recovery between them, culminating in an episode of unresponsiveness. He is afebrile with no focal complaints, no travel, no toxic exposures, and a non-contributory family history. Examined during an episode he is mute and unresponsive with sinus tachycardia and transient hypoxia; ~45 minutes later he is fully conversant with a normal neurologic exam and normal vitals. Broad metabolic labs, CT, lumbar puncture, and contrast MRI are unrevealing; a benzodiazepine challenge paradoxically sedates him, and spot then continuous video EEG capture rhythmic 3–4 Hz polyspike seizures during the episodes. The syndrome is new-onset, non-lesional epilepsy in an older adult, presumed antibody-mediated (autoimmune) and pending antibody confirmation.

## Case Narrative

The session opens as the inaugural case of the CP Solvers effort to **[[Neurophobia|end neurophobia]]** — a term coined by Ralph Josefowitz (1994) for the fear of clinical neurology that arises when teaching fails to bridge basic neuroscience and the bedside. Berkowitz frames his antidote: neurology lets you localize a problem to within centimeters at the bedside, and once you know *where*, the differential for *what* follows. His organizing equation — "the **[[Localization × Time Course|E=mc² of neurology]]**" — is that **differential diagnosis = [[Anatomical Approach|localization]] × [[Illness Tempo|time course]]**.

**Chief complaint (deliberately bare): altered mental status.** Before any history, Berkowitz works the discussants through first principles. Two axes matter immediately. First, **localization** — [[Altered Mental Status|AMS]] points at the brain (this is not, say, a neuromuscular-junction problem unless respiratory failure produced hypercarbia). Second, **[[Illness Tempo|time course]]**, which he subdivides more finely than the usual acute/subacute/chronic: *hyperacute/instant* (truly sudden), *acute* (fast but not instant, hours-to-days), *subacute* (weeks-to-months), and *chronic*. He then loads each bucket. **Instant:** vascular (ischemic or hemorrhagic stroke — "instant neurology, just add water"), [[Seizure|seizure]] (including the post-ictal state of an unwitnessed seizure), toxic-metabolic, trauma, and migraine. **Acute (hours-to-days):** bacterial/viral infection (meningitis, encephalitis, abscess) and inflammatory/demyelinating flares (an MS relapse, transverse myelitis). **Subacute (weeks-to-months):** indolent infection (fungal, tubercular, HIV-related), antibody-mediated/paraneoplastic inflammation, and neoplasm (a mass — metastatic more often than primary) — plus the exception that proves the rule, the rapidly-progressive prion dementia. **Chronic:** neurodegenerative dementias. The through-line the discussants (Caitlin, Ninad) surface — a [[MIST Mnemonic|MIST]]-style schema (metabolic, infectious, structural, toxin) and the primacy of timing — sets up the central discipline: **is this neurologic or non-neurologic**, because every organ system can cause AMS.

**HPI.** The history (from the wife) reveals a 68-year-old, formerly a CEO, retired three years, highly functional (daily crosswords, Sudoku, camping, hiking, running), who over 3–5 weeks became intermittently confused — word-finding difficulty, trouble forming sentences, and on one occasion an inability to give directions home — prompting a 911 call today when he suddenly stopped responding and looked distressed (no syncope). No fever, headache, focal weakness, numbness, vision change, or trauma; no recent travel (Colorado); some vaguely described family stress. Past history is thin: hypertension on lisinopril, cancer screening up to date, vasectomy, no tobacco/drugs (including no marijuana), only social alcohol, and a non-contributory family history. Berkowitz zeroes in on one word — **intermittent** — and asks the pivotal clarifier: is the patient *normal between* episodes, or is there interval decline? The answer sharpens everything: initially normal between, but the episodes lengthened and deepened with **incomplete recovery**, and he has not recovered from the current one. This pattern — intermittent with progressive background decline — pushes hard toward **[[Seizure|seizure]]** (focal seizures with altered awareness need not shake), with the differential now "intermittent seizure with progressive altered mental status." In a 68-year-old with new seizures the dominant worries become a **mass** (metastatic or primary) or, if imaging is clean, an **antibody-mediated** process; a [[Prion Disease|prion disease]] is kept on as the subacute-dementia exception. Berkowitz names the metacognitive risk explicitly: a strong first impression can be right (you resisted red-herring distraction) or a trap ([[Anchoring Bias|anchoring]]) — so hold hypotheses loosely as data arrive.

**Exam — in two acts.** During an episode, the patient stares past the resident's extended hand, mute and unresponsive; vitals show BP 128/74, **HR 113**, RR 14, **SpO2 88% on room air**, temp 37 °C, with an otherwise unremarkable systemic exam. Roughly 45–60 minutes later he is **fully conversant** — frustrated and aware only that he has been confused — with a completely normal neurologic exam (motor, sensory, reflexes, palmomental, cerebellar testing, and an ataxia-free gait to the bathroom) and normalized vitals. The discussants read the transient tachycardia and hypoxia as most likely *ictal autonomic* phenomena rather than a primary cardiopulmonary driver (the oxygen is not low enough to explain the AMS), and the "normal-between, altered-during" pattern as classic for recurrent seizures with post-ictal recovery. Berkowitz adds his mentor Marty Samuels' "heart-brain" teaching — a putative insular "cardunculus" where ictal discharges drive tachy- or brady-arrhythmia — to explain the autonomic swings, arguing the causal vector runs brain → heart here, not the reverse.

**Testing — predict before you peek.** Berkowitz makes the discussants *vote* on each study first ("biopsy your gut"), teaching [[Test Interpretation|pretest calibration]]: a surprise then becomes a durable learning moment. Broad labs (CBC, BMP, LFTs, lactate, TSH), UA, troponin, VBG, CXR, and EKG (sinus tachycardia, no ischemia) are unremarkable, and non-contrast **CT** shows only age-related change — all as predicted, arguing against a medical/toxic-metabolic driver. The neurology reflex is **MRI + EEG ± LP**. The **LP** is bland (no pleocytosis, normal protein/glucose), which lowers meningoencephalitis but does not exclude antibody-mediated disease. The **MRI** (with and without contrast) is non-lesional — only age-related change — which, rather than reassuring, *raises* the pretest probability of an antibody-mediated syndrome (autoimmune encephalitis can spare the MRI or show only medial-temporal signal). **Psychiatry** finds a normal mini-mental status exam between episodes (no interval dementia) and performs a **[[Benzodiazepine Challenge|benzodiazepine challenge]]** for catatonia: instead of the paradoxical *awakening* seen in catatonia or non-convulsive status, the benzo *sedated* him — consistent with his not actively seizing at that moment. A **spot EEG** shows occasional frontal 3 Hz discharges (3 Hz generalized spike-wave is the classic childhood-absence signature; adult-onset absence is rare), and the definitive **continuous video EEG** captures, *during the episodes*, generalized continuous rhythmic **3–4 Hz polyspike discharges** — unambiguous seizures.

**Resolution.** The synthesis is **new-onset, non-lesional epilepsy in an older adult** with intermittent (largely non-convulsive) seizures on a progressive background — a picture that, absent a lesion, is **antibody-mediated / [[Autoimmune Encephalitis|autoimmune]] until proven otherwise**. Berkowitz reviews the candidate antibodies — anti-NMDA (younger patients, "brain on fire," orofacial dyskinesias, ~50% with teratoma/cancer), anti-LGI1 (faciobrachial dystonic seizures with hyponatremia, steroid-responsive), anti-GABA-B (can cause isolated new-onset epilepsy), anti-VGKC-complex — stressing that many autoimmune neurologic syndromes are *not* paraneoplastic (per Josep Dalmau's expanding catalog), so one hunts for cancer but does not require it. The workup plan is serum **and** CSF autoantibodies sent to a reference lab (with a call to its experts). At the time the presenting student was pulled from service for the COVID shutdown, the patient had been transferred to a higher level of care with **autoimmune antibodies pending** and the neurology team most suspicious of an antibody-mediated epilepsy — a working diagnosis, not yet a confirmed one.

### Illness Scripts and Bias

- **[[Localization × Time Course|Localization × time course]] as the master script.** The whole case is a demonstration that fixing the *where* (brain) and the *tempo* (subacute, intermittent) collapses an enormous AMS differential into a short, testable list before a single result returns — Berkowitz's explicit antidote to [[Neurophobia|neurophobia]].
- **[[Illness Tempo|Tempo]] bucketing, with toxic-metabolic as the universal.** Each etiologic class is pinned to a characteristic speed (vascular/seizure instant, infection/inflammatory acute, neoplasm/antibody-mediated subacute, degenerative chronic), while toxic-metabolic is flagged as fitting *every* bucket — hence the discipline of naming the specific toxin or metabolite ([[MIST Mnemonic|MIST]]) rather than deferring.
- **Neurologic vs non-neurologic as the first gate.** Because any organ system can cause [[Altered Mental Status|AMS]], the reasoning first excludes a medical driver (normal labs, VBG, CT) before committing to a primary brain process — the move that keeps neurology from reflexively signing off as "toxic-metabolic."
- **The single discriminating word — *intermittent*.** Reframing "altered mental status" as "*intermittent* altered mental status" is the hinge that summons [[Seizure|seizure]]; the follow-up (normal vs declining between episodes) then distinguishes discrete post-ictal recovery from a superimposed progressive process.
- **[[Test Interpretation|Pretest calibration]] ("biopsy your gut").** Predicting normal-vs-abnormal for each study converts results into calibration: the predicted-normal CT/labs argue against a medical cause, while the *non-lesional* MRI is read as evidence *for* autoimmune epilepsy — and the teaching that seizures can themselves produce MRI and autonomic changes guards against reversing cause and effect.
- **[[Anchoring Bias|Anchoring]], named at the bedside.** Berkowitz explicitly distinguishes a first impression that survives scrutiny from one clung to past its usefulness, modeling [[Diagnostic Humility|humility]] — including comfort ending on a *pending*, antibody-unconfirmed working diagnosis rather than forcing closure.

## Teaching Discussion

- Anchor every neurology case in differential = localization × time course: localize first (here, brain → altered mental status), then let the tempo — hyperacute, acute, subacute, or chronic — select the etiologic bucket; the framework generalizes to internal medicine as well.
- For altered mental status, decide neurologic vs non-neurologic before anything else, and if you invoke "toxic-metabolic," specify the toxin or metabolite; a MIST schema (metabolic, infectious, structural, toxin) keeps the categories explicit.
- Treat the descriptor "intermittent" as diagnostically loud: intermittent AMS suggests seizure, and clarifying whether the patient returns to baseline (discrete post-ictal recovery) or declines between episodes (a superimposed progressive process) shapes the differential; not all seizures are convulsive.
- In an older adult with new-onset seizures, worry first about a mass (metastatic more than primary), and when the MRI is non-lesional, shift to antibody-mediated autoimmune epilepsy — anti-LGI1 (faciobrachial dystonic seizures with hyponatremia), anti-GABA-B (isolated epilepsy), and anti-NMDA — recognizing that many such syndromes are not paraneoplastic.
- Interpret the ancillary tests in context: a bland LP does not exclude autoimmune encephalitis, a normal inter-ictal mini-mental argues against a progressive dementia, a benzodiazepine challenge that sedates (rather than awakens) argues against active catatonia or non-convulsive status at that moment, and continuous video EEG is the confirmatory test when spells are the phenomenon.
- Predict each study's result before ordering it to calibrate judgment, and remember the bidirectional pitfalls — a truly normal MRI is uncommon, and seizures themselves can produce cortical MRI changes and ictal autonomic swings (the heart-brain connection), so keep the causal vector straight.
- Send both serum and CSF for autoantibodies and use the reference-lab experts; hunt for an associated cancer but do not require one to accept an autoimmune neurologic diagnosis.
