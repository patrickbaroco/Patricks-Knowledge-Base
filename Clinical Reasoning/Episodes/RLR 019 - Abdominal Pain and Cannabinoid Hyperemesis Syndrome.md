## Summary

> [!summary] Scan Summary
> A young university student presents with four weeks of abdominal pain and prominent, refractory nausea, no diarrhea and no fever. He looks like he has been suffering for weeks, yet his vitals, abdominal exam, and a full metabolic screen (CBC, renal panel, liver enzymes, lipase, lactate, urinalysis) are all normal. An empiric PPI trial fails, and a contrast CT of the abdomen and pelvis is normal — leaving [[Image-Negative Abdominal Pain|image-negative abdominal pain]]. Reza works the case as a masterclass in structure: life-threatening causes first (excluded by tempo and sex), then an anatomic approach, then the metabolic/toxic/functional schema that a negative CT unlocks. The diagnosis is broken open not by another test but by a triage nurse who, removing his shirt for an EKG, finds a burn on his back — [[Erythema Ab Igne|erythema ab igne]] from compulsive very-hot showering, the only thing that relieves his pain. The [[Hot Shower Sign|hot-shower relief]] narrows to a short list, and the answer is [[Cannabinoid Hyperemesis Syndrome|cannabinoid hyperemesis syndrome]] in a patient smoking multiple joints a day. The episode pairs a diagnostic clinic with two meta-lessons: it is harder to *withhold* imaging than to order it, and the humility to admit you have forgotten a detail (does heat make these conditions better or worse?) is where good clinicians should be comfortable living.

## Pearls

- **A four-week tempo and male sex quietly retire the abdominal-pain emergencies.** [[Acute Mesenteric Ischemia|Mesenteric ischemia]], obstruction, perforation, and — in women — ectopic pregnancy are the can't-miss causes to name first, but a month-long indolent course argues against all of them, letting the reasoner move from a resuscitation posture to a deliberate, anatomic one.
- **Nausea is usually noise, but *debilitating, refractory, intrusive* nausea earns a seat at the table — and it is diagnosed by the symptom it travels with.** Nausea plus headache points one way (raised ICP, glaucoma), nausea plus chest pain another (ACS); nausea plus abdominal pain keeps GI pathology central while leaving room for referred cardiac pain even in the young.
- **Normal vitals and a normal CBC, together, say "not inflamed."** The absence of fever, tachycardia, and leukocytosis makes cholecystitis, pancreatitis, and colitis unlikely and pushes the reasoner toward a *non-inflammatory* cause of pain; a normal lipase and normal liver chemistries then subtract pancreatitis and hepatic parenchymal disease specifically.
- **A normal CT converts the problem into [[Image-Negative Abdominal Pain|image-negative abdominal pain]] — a distinct schema, not a dead end.** Its branches are metabolic ([[Diabetic Ketoacidosis|DKA]], [[Hypercalcemia|hypercalcemia]], [[Adrenal Insufficiency|adrenal insufficiency]], [[Acute Intermittent Porphyria|AIP]], uremia), medications/toxins (opiates on *or* off, anticholinergics, lead, alcohol), functional (IBS, abdominal migraine), and episodic/mucosal disease that only shows *during* pain ([[Angioedema|angioedema]], [[Intussusception|intussusception]]).
- **Hot water is the pivot, but the direction of the association is the discriminator.** [[Cannabinoid Hyperemesis Syndrome|Cannabinoid hyperemesis]] is *relieved* by hot showers, whereas the mast-cell and myeloproliferative itch of [[Polycythemia Vera|polycythemia vera]] and [[Systemic Mastocytosis|systemic mastocytosis]] gets *worse* — heat triggers histamine release ([[Aquagenic Pruritus|aquagenic pruritus]]) rather than relieving it.
- **It is harder to *withhold* a CT than to order one.** ED evidence supports delaying imaging for 2–3 days when the exam and labs are reassuring, trialing empiric therapy and safety-netting the return — and the humility to say "I've lost track of which direction this sign points" is the posture to aspire to, not to hide.

## Problem Representation

A young male university student presents with four weeks of abdominal pain and prominent, unremitting nausea without diarrhea, fever, or other localizing symptoms; he appears chronically unwell but with normal vital signs and a benign abdominal exam, and a full first-pass evaluation — CBC, renal panel, liver enzymes, lipase, lactate, and urinalysis — is entirely normal. An empiric PPI trial over two days brings no relief, and a contrast-enhanced CT of the abdomen and pelvis obtained during pain is normal, defining image-negative abdominal pain; the discriminating findings are compulsive relief with very hot showers and a resulting erythema ab igne burn on the back, in the setting of heavy daily cannabis use — consistent with cannabinoid hyperemesis syndrome.

## Case Narrative

The episode opens away from the case, with Reza reading a passage from his book *Finding Joy in Medicine: Becoming a Doctor*. In it, an acquaintance texts him a photograph of a net-like rash on the backs of both legs — [[Livedo Reticularis|livedo reticularis]] — and his first instinct is an autoimmune workup and biopsy. The story disconfirms itself: she has no fevers, night sweats, or joint pain and feels great. His mentor Gurpreet Dhaliwal, glancing at the photo, names the mimicker — [[Erythema Ab Igne|erythema ab igne]], a rash of chronic heat exposure — and the patient reveals a month of heating-pad use for exercise cramps. The vignette seeds three teaching lines Reza states outright: a diagnostician must interpret the data that exist and not fabricate findings to confirm a hunch ([[Confirmation Bias|confirmation bias]]), there is no room for arrogance and one should have a low threshold to ask for help, and the *sequence* of events matters as much as the *tempo*.

Rabih then presents the case as a challenge, releasing data in deliberate increments. The opening frame is spare: four weeks of abdominal pain in a young man with a great deal of nausea, no diarrhea, no fever.

Reza's discussion is structural. He notes that nausea is a nonspecific complaint, so he starts with the abdominal pain and filters it *through* the nausea. Before any anatomy, he names the life-threatening causes he must not miss — vascular catastrophes like [[Acute Mesenteric Ischemia|acute mesenteric ischemia]], obstruction, perforation, and in women ectopic pregnancy — and then discharges them on tempo: a four-week course makes these unlikely. He proceeds to an anatomic approach that would be governed by the pain's location (still unknown), illustrating with a right-upper-quadrant example (cholecystitis, choledocholithiasis, hepatitis, liver abscess), and insists on considering [[Referred Pain|extra-abdominal organs]] — heart, lung, GU tract — that project onto the abdomen. He flags that most such cases end in a CT, which is sensitive, and that a negative scan (with extra-abdominal sources excluded) activates an [[Image-Negative Abdominal Pain|image-negative schema]] he holds in reserve. Turning to the nausea, he explains that he rarely incorporates it into reasoning, but does so here because Rabih has framed it as *debilitating and refractory*; nausea is best worked by the symptom it accompanies, and nausea-plus-abdominal-pain keeps GI pathology central while still permitting referred cardiac pain — even in a young person, via non-plaque mechanisms like [[Spontaneous Coronary Artery Dissection|SCAD]], vasospasm, and non-obstructive disease. He also lists extraluminal drivers of pain and nausea: electrolyte disturbances, hypercalcemia, thyroid disease, and toxins (present or in withdrawal).

Rabih supplies the neighboring-hospital data: normal vitals; a patient who looks very uncomfortable and chronically unwell but with a relatively unremarkable abdominal exam; and a CBC, renal panel, liver enzymes, lipase, lactate, and urinalysis that are all normal. Reza reads this as a coherent negative: no fever, no tachycardia, and no leukocytosis together say the patient is *not inflamed*, making cholecystitis, pancreatitis, and colitis unlikely; the normal lipase subtracts pancreatitis specifically, and normal liver chemistries argue against hepatic parenchymal disease. He is reassured by the vitals but grounded by the degree of suffering, and concludes he would image this young patient and obtain an EKG.

Rabih uses the moment to teach imaging restraint. He cites ED literature that a reassuring exam and reassuring labs permit delaying a CT for two or three days while trialing empiric therapy — which is exactly what the outside hospital did, giving an empiric PPI and safety-netting the return. The patient came back after two days without improvement; a contrast CT of the abdomen and pelvis, obtained while symptomatic, was normal. Reza names the meta-point that both hosts endorse: it is harder to *withhold* imaging than to obtain it, and internists should thank their ED colleagues rather than reflexively second-guess the scan.

Now firmly in [[Image-Negative Abdominal Pain|image-negative abdominal pain]], Reza recites its structure: metabolic causes ([[Diabetic Ketoacidosis|DKA]], [[Hypercalcemia|hypercalcemia]], [[Adrenal Insufficiency|adrenal insufficiency]], [[Acute Intermittent Porphyria|acute intermittent porphyria]], uremia); medications and toxins (opiates whether on or off, anticholinergics, lead, alcohol); functional disease (IBS, abdominal migraine, abdominal seizure, dyspepsia); and an "other" category of episodic or mucosal disease — diabetic polyradiculopathy, [[Angioedema|angioedema]], zoster, and [[Intussusception|intussusception]] — with the caveat that angioedema and intussusception must be captured on a CT obtained *during* the pain, which here was the case. He anchors himself in the metabolic branch and prepares to march the checklist.

The case then turns on a person, not a test. Rabih credits the triage nurse, Terri, for insisting on an EKG (which is normal) — and, in helping the patient remove his shirt, noticing a burn on his back. Asked about it, the patient explains that the only thing that relieves his pain is showering in very, very hot water, so much so that he has burned his skin: [[Erythema Ab Igne|erythema ab igne]] again, this time as the diagnostic signature rather than a book anecdote.

Reza reframes the crux: image-negative, debilitating abdominal pain *relieved by hot showers*. He builds the differential of conditions tied to hot water, recalling that myeloproliferative disease — [[Polycythemia Vera|polycythemia vera]] — and [[Systemic Mastocytosis|systemic mastocytosis]] are associated with showering, and reaches for [[Cannabinoid Hyperemesis Syndrome|cannabis]] as well. Crucially, he displays the episode's signature humility: he cannot recall whether the mast-cell conditions get *better* or *worse* with heat, and says so with a smile. Rabih supplies the mechanism: heat triggers mast cells to release histamine, so polycythemia vera and mastocytosis pruritus get *worse* in the shower ([[Aquagenic Pruritus|aquagenic pruritus]]) — whereas cannabinoid hyperemesis is *relieved* by it. Reza lands the plane: rather than another test, the next step is a single question about cannabis use.

The patient — a hardworking student carrying real stigma around drug use — turns out to be smoking multiple joints a day, and is diagnosed with [[Cannabinoid Hyperemesis Syndrome|cannabinoid hyperemesis syndrome]]. The hosts note there is no long-term follow-up, but the combination of a negative CT, heavy cannabis use, and hot-shower relief — its signature literally burned onto his back — is a powerful triad. They close on humility as the destination: most clinicians lag behind their knowledge in the *comfort* to say "I don't remember," and the deepest gap is believing you have no gaps.

|---|---|---|
| Four-week tempo, male | Mesenteric ischemia, obstruction, perforation, ectopic | Lowered (retire the emergencies) |
| Prominent, refractory nausea | Nausea promoted from noise to a discriminating modifier | Raised as a reasoning axis |

### Illness Scripts and Bias

- **[[Framing Effect|Framing]] by symptom prominence.** Reza explicitly says he *usually* discards nausea as nonspecific, but incorporates it here *because Rabih framed it as debilitating and refractory*. The framing of a symptom's severity — not just its presence — legitimately changed its diagnostic weight. This is framing used well: an intentional, stated reweighting rather than an unconscious drift.
- **[[Anchoring Bias|Anchoring]] pre-empted by the "mimicker" reflex.** The book anecdote is a deliberate anti-anchoring parable: livedo reticularis *looks* autoimmune, and only Gurpreet's "don't forget its mimicker" (erythema ab igne) breaks the anchor. In the case proper, the same discipline keeps Reza from anchoring on a single luminal source once the CT is negative, and instead opening a structured schema.
- **[[Availability Heuristic|Availability]] surfaces the right leads — and a half-remembered one.** Reza's stored associations (hot water ↔ polycythemia vera, mastocytosis, cannabis) are exactly what availability is supposed to provide: fast recall of the short list tied to a rare clue. But availability delivered the *items* without the *vector* — he recalled the associations while forgetting whether heat helps or hurts. Recognition is not the same as a worked mechanism.
- **[[Confirmation Bias|Confirmation bias]], named and resisted.** The book passage states the principle directly — "you cannot fabricate elements of the story to confirm your initial hunch" — when the absence of systemic symptoms refuses to support the autoimmune read. The case echoes it: the normal labs are allowed to *mean* "not inflamed" rather than being explained away.
- **[[Premature Closure|Premature closure]] averted by the patient and the system.** The outside hospital's reasonable normal-workup pathway could have ended in dismissal; instead the patient's insistence on returning, and a triage nurse's attention to a burn, reopened the case. The reasoning was rescued less by a test than by persistence and teamwork.
- **Mechanistic anchoring beats rote recall.** Rabih's teaching — heat triggers mast-cell histamine release, so the itch *worsens* — is offered as the durable fix for the exact gap Reza displayed: once you understand *why* heat provokes these conditions, you never again confuse the direction. Understanding the mechanism inoculates against the half-remembered association.

## Teaching Discussion

- Structure the undifferentiated abdomen before you localize it: name the life-threatening causes (mesenteric ischemia, obstruction, perforation, ectopic in women), discharge them on tempo and sex when appropriate, then move to an anatomic differential, then remember the extra-abdominal organs that refer pain. The four-week course here does most of the early triage work.
- Treat nausea as a modifier, not a diagnosis. Alone it is nonspecific ("anything from head to toe"), but severe, refractory nausea deserves incorporation, and it is best worked by the symptom it accompanies — with abdominal pain keeping GI central while still permitting referred cardiac pain even in the young.
- Read a full set of normals as a positive statement. Normal vitals and CBC together mean "not inflamed," which subtracts the inflammatory abdomen (cholecystitis, pancreatitis, colitis); the normal lipase and liver chemistries then remove pancreatitis and hepatic parenchymal disease specifically. Absence of inflammation is itself a branch point.
- Learn image-negative abdominal pain as its own schema — metabolic, medications/toxins, functional, and episodic/mucosal — and note that a subset (angioedema, intussusception) is only visible on imaging obtained *during* pain, so the *timing* of the CT relative to symptoms is part of its interpretation.
- The hot-shower sign is high-yield but bidirectional: cannabinoid hyperemesis is relieved by heat, while polycythemia vera and systemic mastocytosis are worsened by it because heat drives mast-cell histamine release. Anchor on the mechanism so the direction is never in doubt.
- Diagnostic stewardship cuts against instinct: withholding a CT is harder than ordering one, and there is evidence to support a 2–3 day empiric trial with return precautions when exam and labs are reassuring. The internist's reflex to criticize an ED scan should give way to gratitude for the workup.
- The soft skills are the case: a triage nurse's observation cracked it, a patient's persistence reopened it, and disclosing stigmatized cannabis use required a trusting single question rather than another test. And the modeled humility — cheerfully admitting a forgotten detail — is presented as the goal state, not a lapse.

## Evergreen Note Updates

- [ ] Create **[[Cannabinoid Hyperemesis Syndrome]]** — heavy chronic cannabis use, cyclic vomiting/abdominal pain, pathognomonic relief with hot bathing/showers; contrast with cyclic vomiting syndrome and the paradox of an antiemetic drug causing emesis.
- [ ] Create **[[Erythema Ab Igne]]** — reticular hyperpigmentation from chronic low-grade heat (heating pads, laptops, hot showers); a *behavioral* sign that points back to the reason for the heat, and mimicker of livedo reticularis.
- [ ] Create **[[Image-Negative Abdominal Pain]]** — Reza's schema: metabolic / medications-toxins / functional / episodic-mucosal, with the "image during pain" caveat for angioedema and intussusception. (Reza's schema is on the CPSolvers website — link it.)
- [ ] Create **[[Hot Shower Sign]]** — differential of symptoms modified by hot water, split by *direction*: relieved (cannabinoid hyperemesis) vs worsened (polycythemia vera, systemic mastocytosis, aquagenic pruritus).
- [ ] Create **[[Aquagenic Pruritus]]** — water/heat-triggered itch; mechanism via mast-cell histamine release; association with polycythemia vera and mastocytosis.
- [ ] Update **[[Hypercalcemia]]** — as a metabolic branch of image-negative abdominal pain.
- [ ] Update bias notes — **[[Framing Effect]]**, **[[Anchoring Bias]]**, **[[Availability Heuristic]]**, **[[Premature Closure]]**, **[[Confirmation Bias]]** — with the nausea-reweighting, mimicker, half-remembered-association, and rescued-by-persistence exemplars.
