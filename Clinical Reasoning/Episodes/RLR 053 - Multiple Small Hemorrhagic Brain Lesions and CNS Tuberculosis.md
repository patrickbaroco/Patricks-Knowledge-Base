> [!summary] Scan Summary
> *(Guest episode — the mirror image of RLR 050: this time **Aaron Berkowitz presents** a case to Reza and Rabih, "how we got it" — imaging first.)* A woman in her **early 70s** with hypertension is worked up after **~1 day of word-finding difficulty** (largely resolved by arrival) when an **MRI shows multiple small ring-enhancing lesions in both cerebral hemispheres, at the gray-white junction, some hemorrhagic**, with a trace of **leptomeningeal enhancement** in the cerebellum. Rabih lays out the **10,000-foot brain-mass differential** — **cancer, infection (pus not tumor), autoimmune, vascular (a subacute stroke can enhance and masquerade), radiation** — and Reza foregrounds **tempo, radiologist detail (ring completeness, DWI), and host immune status**. The gray-white distribution and hemorrhage scream **metastasis** (mnemonic **MRCT** — Melanoma, Renal, Choriocarcinoma, Thyroid — over the base-rate champion, lung), so they send **HIV (negative), RPR (negative), a skin exam for melanoma**, and a **CT chest/abdomen/pelvis** that returns a **blizzard of findings**: thyroid nodule, two lung nodules, a **2 cm breast mass**, a **10 cm pelvic mass**, and **diffuse abdominal lymphadenopathy**. The frame hardens to **metastatic cancer**; the plan is to **biopsy the pelvic mass**. Then the case unravels: she becomes **fluctuatingly delirious** (a real UTI, then a frank **seizure**, EEGs without status, steroids that *worsen* her), the **pelvic mass biopsies as a fibroid**, and an **LP shows a lymphocytic pleocytosis** (360 WBC, 88% lymphs, protein 600, glucose 44/126). Over two weeks she deteriorates into **florid hydrocephalus** requiring an EVD, with **thick basilar leptomeningeal enhancement** on repeat MRI. The buried signal — a **QuantiFERON-gold positive** drawn early and dismissed — finally surfaces when a **second CSF TB-PCR returns positive**: the unifying diagnosis is **CNS tuberculosis** (tuberculous meningitis with miliary tuberculomas — hemorrhagic on SWI — hydrocephalus, and TB vasculitis; the abdominal nodes were TB, not tumor). She received **maximal TB therapy plus steroids** but never recovered, developed **vasculitic strokes**, and died. The teaching spine: **an ultrasensitive CT paints "noise" that anchors and frames**; **signal-vs-noise is the real work**; **tempo and morbidity should have re-opened infection**; and a **positive QuantiFERON is a "record-scratch" moment** — TB can mimic metastatic cancer.

**Pearls**

- **The brain-mass differential, agnostic to base rate:** **cancer**, **infection** ("if it's not tumor cells, it's pus"), **autoimmune** (demyelinating and systemic), **vascular** (a **subacute stroke can enhance and mimic a mass**, but usually lacks mass effect and stays in a vascular territory), and **radiation necrosis**. Base rate then makes you *think cancer* — but the list is what protects you.
- **Let the radiologist's detail prioritize the differential.** **Completeness of the ring**, **DWI restriction** (an abscess restricts; most tumor and TB do not the same way), location (**gray-white junction** = distal end-arterial → metastases *and* emboli), and **hemorrhage** each reorder the list. The internist need not read the MRI — only know these features *exist* and ask for them.
- **Host immune status broadens, not narrows, the differential** — adding **opportunistic infections and malignancies** (e.g., toxoplasmosis in HIV, which can be treated empirically without biopsy). Send the **HIV** before you see the patient; it changes everything.
- **Hemorrhagic metastases: [[MRCT mnemonic|MRCT]]** — **M**elanoma, **R**enal, **C**horiocarcinoma, **T**hyroid — but **base rate still wins**: the commonest hemorrhagic met is **lung**, simply because lung is so common. "Hemorrhagic ⇒ one of these four" is a tendency, not a rule.
- **Melanoma earns a dedicated skin exam.** A cancer that seeds the brain diffusely and hemorrhagically, with **nothing on labs or cross-sectional imaging**, may be **melanoma** — look at the back and places the patient can't see.
- **Ultrasensitive imaging generates *noise* that anchors you.** A pan-CT that lights up thyroid/lung/breast/pelvic lesions can **frame the case as metastatic cancer** when the true disease is elsewhere. The core cognitive task becomes **signal vs noise** — and here, nearly the entire periphery (thyroid, lung, breast, a **10 cm fibroid**) was **noise**.
- **Tempo and morbidity re-open the differential.** Normal on day 1 → obtunded by day 5–7 is **too fast for a paraneoplastic syndrome**, and small hemorrhagic metastases **don't explode into hydrocephalus overnight**. When the *behavior* of the illness contradicts the label, the label is wrong.
- **A positive [[QuantiFERON]] in an unexplained neuro case is a "record-scratch" moment.** IGRA positivity only proves **prior exposure** (a third of the world is infected) and is easy to dismiss — but paired with an **unexplained meningitis, mass lesions, or hydrocephalus**, it should force the question: *could this be TB?* **CNS TB can mimic metastatic cancer**, and its CSF (**lymphocytic pleocytosis, high protein, low-ish glucose**) overlaps with leptomeningeal malignancy.

**Problem Representation**
A woman in her early 70s with transient word-finding difficulty found to have multiple small hemorrhagic gray-white-junction brain lesions and diffuse systemic imaging abnormalities, initially framed as metastatic cancer, who progressed over two weeks to fluctuating encephalopathy, seizures, and florid hydrocephalus — ultimately diagnosed with CNS tuberculosis (tuberculous meningitis with tuberculomas and vasculitis), with an early positive QuantiFERON as the missed signal.

## Case Narrative

In a reversal of the usual format — and a callback to **RLR 050** — the **guest, Aaron Berkowitz**, is the one **presenting**, and he does so "how we got it": the neurology team was consulted **for an imaging finding**. An ED **MRI** showed **multiple small ring-enhancing lesions in both cerebral hemispheres, predominantly at the gray-white junction near the cortical surface, some with associated blood products**, plus a **trace of leptomeningeal enhancement in the cerebellar folia**. Before any history, he asks the hosts to reason from the picture.

Rabih offers the **10,000-foot view of a brain mass**: **base rate always says cancer**, but in parallel one must ask whether the "mass" is **pus rather than tumor** (infection), whether it is **autoimmune** (the demyelinating diseases, plus systemic disorders that seed the CNS), and — humbly — whether the mass **isn't a mass at all** but **ischemic/subacute stroke** or **radiation necrosis**, both of which have fooled many. Reza layers on three modifiers he wants *before* committing: the **tempo** of the illness (which "can really help us make progress"), the **radiologist's fine detail** (is the ring complete? does DWI restrict? — features that generate a **prioritized differential** the internist needn't read himself), and the **host's immune status**, which **broadens** the differential to **opportunistic** disease (toxoplasmosis in HIV being the classic "treat-without-biopsy" example).

Berkowitz fills in the history: a **woman in her early 70s with hypertension**, whose only symptom was **~1 day of word-finding difficulty** that had **largely resolved** by arrival (out of the stroke window); a month of intermittent orthostatic **dizziness** and some **gait trouble** attributed to a **known femur fracture** and cane. The exam was **unremarkable**. The **temporal dissociation** is stark — a day of transient aphasia cannot explain a brain full of chronic-looking lesions. Rabih names the deeper unease: lesions are **"peppered throughout her brain,"** strongly implying a **source beneath the brain**, yet she has **no systemic symptoms at all** — indeed, **not even neurologic symptoms** from devastating-looking disease — so the absence of systemic complaints should **not** reassure. He adds the one exam he'd insist on: a **thorough skin exam for melanoma**, the hemorrhagic brain-seeder that hides from labs and scans.

Reza and Berkowitz work the **hemorrhagic-metastasis** logic — the **[[MRCT mnemonic|MRCT]]** cancers (melanoma, renal, choriocarcinoma, thyroid) are the classic bleeders, but **base rate still favors lung** because it is so common, so "hemorrhagic ⇒ MRCT" is a lean, not a law. The **gray-white junction** distribution fits **metastases** (distal end-arterial territory) — but Reza notes it *also* fits **embolic** disease (endocarditis; the post-cardiac-surgery **"starry-night"** shower; watershed zones), a thread Berkowitz entertains and sets aside (no diffusion restriction, no clear cardiac source). So, **before** even meeting her, the team sends an **HIV** (to reframe if immunosuppressed) and a **CT chest/abdomen/pelvis** to "go hunting for a primary."

The pan-CT returns a **blizzard**: a **thyroid nodule**, **two lung nodules** (one 6 mm), a **2 cm breast mass**, a **10 × 6 × 12 cm anterior pelvic mass** (read as likely extraperitoneal), and **diffuse abdominal, retroperitoneal, pelvic, and inguinal lymphadenopathy** with adrenal thickening. **HIV and RPR are negative.** Rabih runs the **signal-vs-noise** exercise aloud: a **10 cm pelvic** lesion and a **2 cm breast** lesion are unlikely to be noise; **thyroid nodules and small lung nodules** usually are. He plays the **"where does metastasis *not* go?"** game (breast is a rare metastatic *target*, suggesting **breast as a primary**), then checks himself — could there be **two simultaneous primaries** (a **BRCA** breast-plus-ovarian)? — and even name-drops the **Krukenberg tumor** (GI → ovary). The frame consolidates: **metastatic cancer, site unknown**; the practical question becomes **"can we diagnose without tissue?"** — answered *no* — so the plan is to **biopsy the safest, highest-yield site**, the **large pelvic mass**, with an **LDH** and tumor markers sent alongside.

Then the case **comes apart**. Admitted for an expedited malignancy workup, she becomes **fluctuatingly delirious** — somnolent, then confused, then agitated — and is found to have a **real UTI**. Reza gives the **delirium** framework (dementia/poor cognitive reserve as the biggest risk factor; hunt common causes; review meds), but flags that with **known brain lesions** the threshold to **re-image** (CT first, for a new bleed or mass effect, then MRI) is **low**. Rabih raises **non-convulsive status epilepticus** as a **delirium mimic** and asks Berkowitz's threshold for **EEG**; Berkowitz teaches the nuance — the **comatose** phenotype and the **"wandering, confused"** phenotype, the value of **prolonged** EEG (patients cycle in and out; a postictal record can look like bland encephalopathy), and the humility that most such patients are *not* seizing. They **CT** (stable), **EEG** (no status), and later, after a **frank seizure**, start **Keppra**. **Steroids** (tried by oncology for presumed edema) make her **markedly worse** — an argument *against* vasogenic edema and *for* something else.

The hinge: the **pelvic biopsy returns a fibroid** — benign. The dominant "signal" was **noise**. An **LP** (which the team had resisted, doubting its yield for parenchymal disease) shows a **lymphocytic pleocytosis**: **360 WBC, 88% lymphocytes, protein 600, glucose 44** against a serum of **126**. Reza narrows a lymphocytic pleocytosis to **cancer vs infection**: among cancers, **lymphoma** ("you have to respect lymphoma"); among infections, the **non-bacterial** universe (viral — but the syndrome doesn't fit — **fungal**, **mycobacterial**, zoonotic), pushing for **fungal/mycobacterial cultures, broad PCR, cryptococcal antigen, LDH**. Rabih, wrestling with the **intraparenchymal signature** (hemorrhagic gray-white lesions don't sit comfortably with either leptomeningeal lymphoma *or* a bland CNS infection), begins to suspect the **periphery is noise** and this is a **CNS-predominant** process — invoking, by tempo and morbidity, aggressive **fungal** (Cryptococcus **gattii** in a normal host), **amoebic** (Balamuthia), and rare mold possibilities.

She continues to deteriorate. By two weeks she is **obtunded** with **sluggish pupils**; a **fourth CT** shows **new florid hydrocephalus** with hugely dilated ventricles → **ICU and an external ventricular drain**; a **repeat MRI** shows **thick basilar leptomeningeal enhancement**. Berkowitz voices the crux: **leptomeningeal disease "can do a lot of things," but it should not blow up the ventricles in a moment** — and small hemorrhagic metastases shouldn't behave this way either. The **tempo** has broken the cancer frame.

The resolution is a **cognitive autopsy** in real time. Early in the admission, oncology (prepping her for presumed chemo) had drawn a **QuantiFERON-gold** that was **positive** — but with **no TB symptoms**, an unremarkable chest CT, and a **negative induced sputum**, she'd been told to follow up as an outpatient, and the result **sat, ignored, for the whole hospitalization**. As she crashed, **ID** recommended **empiric TB therapy** (and empiric antifungals). A **CSF TB-PCR** was sent: **the first was negative**; the **second returned positive**. The final diagnosis was **CNS tuberculosis** — **tuberculous meningitis** with **miliary-pattern intracranial tuberculomas** (the small lesions, **hemorrhagic on susceptibility-weighted imaging**, an unusual but documented feature), **communicating hydrocephalus** from gummed-up basilar meninges, and **TB vasculitis**; the **abdominal lymphadenopathy was TB, not tumor**. Despite **maximal TB therapy, steroids, and the EVD**, she **never regained consciousness**, suffered **vasculitic strokes**, and **died**.

Berkowitz frames the takeaway with hard-won humility: the **QuantiFERON was the signal from day one**, drowned in a **spew of ultrasensitive incidental findings** that **framed** every brilliant specialist toward metastatic cancer. Reza and Rabih add their lenses — the **danger of oversensitive tests** (had she only had a chest x-ray, none of the noise would have appeared), the impossibility of AI catching **misleading data a human must recognize as misleading**, and the discipline, when a patient is **dying before your eyes without a diagnosis**, to **pause, ask for help, and go aggressively after tissue**. Going forward, Berkowitz vows: a **positive QuantiFERON in an unexplained neurologic case will be a record-scratch moment**. Rabih closes the (long) episode.

### Illness Scripts and Bias

This is a **master class in framing and anchoring** delivered as a **cognitive autopsy**. The single most important lesson is meta-diagnostic: **oversensitive imaging manufactured a differential**. A pan-CT's incidental thyroid, lung, breast, and (benign fibroid) pelvic findings created an overwhelming **metastatic-cancer frame** that captured neurology, medicine, oncology, ID, and two of the sharpest diagnosticians in the field — precisely because the frame was *coherent* (older woman, small enhancing brain lesions, multi-organ "lesions"). Rabih's repeated **signal-vs-noise** discipline was the right instinct, but even he "would have bet the house" the answer lived in the body. The **corrective signals were behavioral, not radiographic**: a **tempo** too fast for paraneoplasia, a **steroid response** that pointed away from edema, and a **hydrocephalus** that leptomeningeal cancer "shouldn't cause on a moment's notice." The **buried QuantiFERON** is the case's emblem of **base-rate/verification neglect** — a positive IGRA is so common and so nonspecific that it was reasonably discounted, yet its *conditional* value skyrockets in an **unexplained meningitis**. The hosts model the virtues that this disease punishes the absence of: **diagnostic humility**, **asking for help when a patient deteriorates undiagnosed**, **aggressive pursuit of tissue**, and the recognition — as Berkowitz's mentor **Allan Ropper** taught — that **modern "imaging-first" medicine** still needs a clinician to decide *what the picture means*.

## Teaching Discussion

**A structured approach to the brain mass.** Reason in two passes. First, **agnostic to probability**: **neoplasm** (primary or metastatic), **infection** (abscess/granuloma — "pus, not tumor"), **inflammatory/autoimmune** (demyelinating tumefactive lesions; neurosarcoid; vasculitis), **vascular** (a **subacute infarct** that enhances and mimics a mass — but usually respects a vascular territory and lacks true mass effect), and **treatment effect** (radiation necrosis). Second, apply **base rate** (→ cancer) *and* three modifiers: **tempo**, **radiologic detail**, and **host immune status**.

**Read the imaging with the radiologist.** Features that reprioritize the list: **ring completeness**, **DWI/ADC** (pyogenic abscess restricts centrally; most neoplasm and TB do not in the same pattern), **location** (**gray-white junction** = distal end-arterial → metastases and emboli; periventricular → lymphoma; basilar meningeal enhancement → TB/fungal/carcinomatous), and **hemorrhage/SWI**. The internist's job is not to over-read the MRI but to **ask for these descriptors** and let them generate a prioritized differential.

**Hemorrhagic metastases and the MRCT rule.** Classic bleeders are **[[MRCT mnemonic|MRCT]]** — **melanoma, renal cell, choriocarcinoma, thyroid** — but **base rate** means the *most common* hemorrhagic met is still **lung**, because lung metastasizes so often; melanoma has the highest *per-lesion* hemorrhage tendency. A diffusely hemorrhagic, brain-seeding cancer invisible on labs/scan should **prompt a skin exam for melanoma**.

**Signal versus noise in the age of ultrasensitive imaging.** Pan-body CT/PET routinely surfaces **incidentalomas** (thyroid nodules, sub-cm lung nodules, small adrenal lesions) that are usually **noise**. The discipline is to weight findings by **prior probability of relevance**, to ask **"where does metastasis rarely go?"** (breast as a metastatic *target* is rare → suggests breast *primary*), and to remember that **the biggest, most confident-looking lesion can be benign** (here a **10 cm fibroid**). Oversensitive tests can **anchor and frame** an entire team toward the wrong disease.

**Delirium with structural brain disease.** Work the common causes (infection, meds, metabolic, sensory/dis-orientation; **dementia** is the biggest predisposing factor via low reserve), but with **known brain lesions**, keep a **low threshold to re-image** (CT for a new bleed/mass effect, then MRI) and consider **[[non-convulsive status epilepticus]]** — a genuine **delirium mimic**. Suspect NCSE when **coma is out of proportion** to imaging/labs, or in a **"wandering, confused"** patient with a **focal lesion** or prior brain insult; use **prolonged** EEG (patients cycle; a postictal EEG can look blandly encephalopathic); a **benzodiazepine trial** that dramatically clears a comatose patient supports it. Importantly, most delirious inpatients are **not** seizing.

**"There are only four tools in neurology."** Imaging, **CSF**, **EEG**, and **EMG**. When a hard case stalls, enumerate them: if imaging, EEG, and EMG can't explain it, the remaining move is often the **LP**. And — a subtle point Reza makes — **finding something on the CSF does not retroactively prove the LP was indicated**; good reasoning and a useful test can coincide without the reasoning having been correct.

**CSF lymphocytic pleocytosis.** The buckets are **infectious** (viral; **fungal**; **mycobacterial/TB**; spirochetal; some parasitic) and **non-infectious** (**leptomeningeal malignancy**/lymphoma; neurosarcoid; autoimmune). A **low CSF glucose** narrows toward **TB, fungal, and leptomeningeal carcinomatosis** (and sarcoid). Cytology/flow for leptomeningeal cancer is **insensitive** — often requiring **three large-volume taps** — so a negative tap does not exclude it.

**CNS tuberculosis — the mimicker.** **[[Tuberculous meningitis]]** classically produces a **basilar meningitis** with **lymphocytic pleocytosis, high protein, and low glucose**, complicated by **communicating hydrocephalus** (exudate obstructing CSF flow) and **arteritis → infarcts** (the vasculitic strokes that killed this patient). Parenchymal disease takes the form of **tuberculomas**, occasionally in a **miliary** pattern that can **mimic metastases** and — atypically but reported — show **hemorrhage on SWI**. Diagnosis is hard: **CSF AFB smear is insensitive**, culture is slow, and **NAAT/PCR (e.g., Xpert MTB/RIF Ultra)** is **specific but imperfectly sensitive**, so **serial CSF sampling** raises yield (as here — the *second* PCR was positive). Crucially, a **positive IGRA/[[QuantiFERON]]** signifies **infection/exposure**, not active disease, and a third of the world is latently infected — but in an **unexplained meningitis or CNS mass**, it should **markedly raise** suspicion for CNS TB and prompt empiric therapy while confirmation is pursued. **Adjunctive corticosteroids** reduce mortality in TB meningitis, but outcomes in advanced disease with hydrocephalus and vasculitis remain grim.
