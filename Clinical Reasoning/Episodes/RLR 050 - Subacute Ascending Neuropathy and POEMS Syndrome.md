> *(Guest episode — Reza is away, so Rabih presents to neurologist **Aaron Berkowitz**.)* A man in his 60s, 10 years after a liver transplant for NASH cirrhosis and a "model" recovery on tacrolimus/prednisone/mycophenolate, presents to the transplant clinic with **3–4 months of progressive, symmetric, ascending numbness and tingling** — feet to knees, now the fingertips — with new **leg weakness** severe enough that he arrives in a wheelchair. Berkowitz localizes stepwise: symmetric, below-the-neck, sensory-predominant, length-dependent → **peripheral nervous system**, most likely an **axonal** neuropathy (the [[length-dependent neuropathy|length-dependent]] "longest-axons-first" pattern of toxic/metabolic causes), with **[[demyelinating neuropathy|demyelinating]]** kept in pencil. The **tempo** (months, not the days-to-weeks of [[Guillain-Barré syndrome|GBS]]) makes it **subacute** and "when-to-worry." Two quiet chart findings — **bilateral optic disc swelling** on an MRI done for mild headache and a **rising platelet count (~550k)**, plus **leg edema** — are noted but not forced into the story. The exam then breaks the axonal anchor: **areflexia everywhere (ankles, knees, and biceps)** with severe distal-worse weakness. An **EMG reads demyelinating**, and the **SPEP shows an IgA-λ paraprotein** — the unifying diagnosis is **[[POEMS syndrome]]** (Polyneuropathy, Organomegaly, Endocrinopathy, M-protein, Skin — with the patient's **papilledema, thrombocytosis, and edema** as supporting features). The reasoning spine: **localize then hypothesis-test on exam**, **tempo triages the differential**, **don't force incidental findings**, and the **"take one thing out"** move for an overwhelming picture.

**Pearls**

- **Localize before you differentiate.** Symmetric, below-the-neck, sensory-predominant, length-dependent symptoms with no cognitive/cranial-nerve/bowel-bladder involvement point to the **peripheral nervous system** (nerve/root/DRG) over cord or brain.
- **[[Axonal vs demyelinating neuropathy|Axonal vs demyelinating]] is the pivotal split.** **Axonal** = **[[length-dependent neuropathy|length-dependent]]** (longest axons first: toes → shins before fingers), typically **toxic/metabolic** (diabetes, B12, chemo). **Demyelinating** = **non-length-dependent** (proximal *and* distal, hands *and* feet together; loss of *proximal* reflexes), typically **inflammatory** (GBS, [[CIDP]], **paraproteinemic**).
- **The "paint-splatter" image**: throw paint at your traced outline and you hit both long and short nerves at once — why demyelinating disease gives **diffuse, proximal-plus-distal** findings (e.g., an absent **biceps** reflex) rather than a purely distal picture.
- **Tempo triages neuropathy.** Days-to-weeks (nadir 2–4 wk) → **[[Guillain-Barré syndrome|GBS]]**; a neuropathy "moving along" over weeks-to-months is **subacute** and worrisome — reversible, findable causes cluster here (inflammatory, **paraproteinemic**).
- **Every distal symmetric neuropathy earns B12, a diabetes test, and an SPEP with immunofixation** (AAN) — the **SPEP is the commonly-missed** one, sent to *not miss* a paraprotein even though it is usually normal.
- **Don't force incidental findings.** The optic disc swelling, thrombocytosis, and edema *stood out only because everything else was pristine* — resisting a premature unifying leap is a virtue, and in retrospect they were **POEMS** features.
- **[[POEMS syndrome]] is λ-restricted, VEGF-driven, and demyelinating.** Suspect it when a **subacute demyelinating polyneuropathy** pairs with a **paraprotein** and systemic clues (**papilledema, thrombocytosis, volume overload**, organomegaly, endocrinopathy, sclerotic bone lesions); **serum VEGF** supports it.
- **[[EMG]] is a *live* extension of the exam** — the electromyographer decides the next muscle to test in real time — so **order it with a precise question** (axonal vs demyelinating; root vs nerve vs plexus), not "patient weak."

**Problem Representation**
A man in his 60s, a decade post-liver-transplant, with a subacute, progressive, symmetric sensorimotor polyneuropathy that proves demyelinating on EMG and is associated with an IgA-λ paraprotein, papilledema, thrombocytosis, and edema — POEMS syndrome.

## Case Narrative

With Reza away, Rabih presents a case from his internship on the liver-transplant service to **Aaron Berkowitz**. A man in his 60s comes to the transplant clinic having **seen three clinicians in three months** for **numbness and tingling in the lower extremities** — progressive in severity and in extent (up to the knees), now involving the **fingertips**, with new **leg weakness** he finds alarming. Berkowitz's first move is to ask whether the **hands started after the legs** (they did), because the *sequence* informs pattern recognition.

He localizes out loud. Numbness/tingling are **sensory**, traceable anywhere from peripheral nerve → dorsal root ganglion → cord → brainstem → brain. But **symmetric, below-the-neck, sensory-predominant** symptoms with **no cognitive, cranial-nerve, or bowel/bladder** involvement make brain and brainstem unlikely and point to the **peripheral nervous system** (cord still possible, but a purely sensory, no-bowel/bladder picture argues against it). Within neuropathies, the pivotal classification is **[[axonal vs demyelinating neuropathy|axonal vs demyelinating]]**: **axonal** neuropathies are **[[length-dependent neuropathy|length-dependent]]** (longest axons fail first — toes, then soles, then ankles, and fingers only once the shins are involved, because neck-to-fingertip ≈ low-back-to-mid-shin), the classic pattern of **toxic/metabolic** causes (diabetes worldwide, B12, chemotherapy). **Demyelinating** neuropathies are **non-length-dependent**; Berkowitz teaches the **paint-splatter analogy** — throw paint at your chalk-traced outline and you hit long *and* short nerves at once, producing **proximal-plus-distal, hands-and-feet-together** findings and **loss of proximal reflexes**, the fingerprint of **inflammatory** causes (GBS, [[CIDP]], paraproteinemic).

Then **tempo** — his "E = mc², the space-time continuum." Days-to-weeks would mean evolving **[[Guillain-Barré syndrome|GBS]]** (nadir 2–4 weeks); **months** makes this **subacute**. So from the history alone: a **subacute, length-dependent-sounding (axonal-leaning) sensorimotor neuropathy** — with demyelinating still erasable-in-pencil pending the exam. He frames the **"when to worry"** principle: an idiopathic distal neuropathy that has smoldered for years can be watched, but one **moving along quickly** demands a hunt for a **reversible** cause — and the standard net (diabetes testing, **B12**, **SPEP with immunofixation**) is cast precisely so as not to miss a paraprotein.

Rabih then supplies the **context** he had withheld: the transplant, the immunosuppressants, a decade of excellent health. Berkowitz reasons through the **"neurology of liver transplant / immunosuppression"** Venn diagram and finds it unhelpful for a neuropathy — **tacrolimus** causes tremor, encephalopathy, and PRES (not neuropathy); **prednisone** a myopathy; **mycophenolate** little; opportunistic infections rarely cause this pattern (**leprosy** would give mononeuritis multiplex in cool regions). He explicitly declines to let the transplant explain the nerves, modeling **hypothesis-driven examination**: go to the exam to *prove or refute* a length-dependent neuropathy.

Rabih adds two chart findings noticed only **because everything else was pristine**: mild **headaches** had prompted an MRI (normal parenchyma/meninges but **bilateral optic disc swelling**; ophthalmology was underwhelmed, vision intact) and a **rising platelet count (~550k)** with **leg edema**. Berkowitz affirms the **red-flag "image-and-tap the immunosuppressed headache"** teaching (recalling Marty Samuels's error case of cryptococcal meningitis hidden by steroids — the patient calmly unpacking a suitcase) but **honestly declines to force** the disc finding or thrombocytosis into the neuropathy story, naming the **cognitive bias** of over-weighting the only abnormality in an otherwise clean patient.

The **exam** breaks the anchor: normal above the neck; ~50% reduced pin/light-touch in the fingertips; **1–2+ leg edema**; distal sensory loss (light touch, proprioception, vibration) at the ankles/feet; **weakness in all leg groups, distal worse than proximal** (proximal ~4−, distal near anti-gravity), and — decisively — **no ankle, knee, *or* biceps reflex**. The **areflexic biceps** is the crack in the length-dependent façade. Berkowitz works the tension aloud (still leaning axonal given the severe distal-vs-proximal gradient, but conceding demyelinating could masquerade as length-dependent because leg nerves are simply longer), and orders the test that adjudicates: an **[[EMG]]**.

Rabih reveals the data (out of order, for teaching): the **EMG is solidly demyelinating**. That flips the differential toward **inflammatory/paraproteinemic** — but developing an inflammatory neuropathy *on triple immunosuppression* "stretches the imagination," so Berkowitz uses the **"take one thing out"** technique: strip away the transplant context and just ask *what is a subacute demyelinating neuropathy?* → **paraprotein-associated** disease, [[CIDP]], paraneoplastic — and reaches, again, for the **SPEP**. It returns an **IgA-λ paraprotein**. Berkowitz names it: **[[POEMS syndrome]]** — **P**olyneuropathy (demyelinating), **O**rganomegaly, **E**ndocrinopathy (untestable here — the adrenal axis was blunted by steroids), **M**-protein (the **λ-restricted** paraprotein, a near-defining feature), **S**kin changes — with the incidental **papilledema**, **thrombocytosis**, and **edema** retrospectively snapping into place as recognized POEMS features, and **serum VEGF** as the confirmatory test. The patient — profoundly debilitated, areflexic everywhere — ultimately achieved **disease arrest** with (complicated) immunologic therapy.

### Illness Scripts and Bias

The episode is a master class in **anchoring, named and managed**. Berkowitz commits early to a **length-dependent axonal** read, narrates how "thinking out loud self-reinforces," and then lets the **areflexic biceps** and the **demyelinating EMG** force a revision he initially resisted — "clinical reasoning on steroids" when conflicting data reopen a nearly-dismissed hypothesis. Two disciplines counter the bias: **hypothesis-driven examination** (go to the exam to test, not to browse) and the **"take one thing out"** heuristic — remove the transplant context (or a single exam finding) and re-reason, converting an intimidating "what pentad is this?" into a solvable problem and guarding against **forcing incidental findings** (optic discs, platelets) into a premature unifying story. The **tempo variable** is treated as first-class ("E = mc²"), and **distributed humility** appears throughout (ophthalmology underwhelmed, the SPEP as a "rule-out," calling hematology once the paraprotein returns). The redemption of the "ignored" findings — papilledema, thrombocytosis, edema — as **POEMS** features shows the payoff of *holding* rather than *forcing* data until the framework arrives.

## Teaching Discussion

**Localize, then differentiate.** The neurologic method starts with **where**, not **what**: symmetric, below-the-neck, sensory-predominant, length-dependent symptoms without cognitive, cranial-nerve, or sphincter involvement place the lesion in the **peripheral nervous system**. Only after localizing does the differential open — and even then it is **structured by pathophysiology**.

**Axonal vs demyelinating is the hinge.** **[[Axonal vs demyelinating neuropathy|Axonal]]** neuropathies are **[[length-dependent neuropathy|length-dependent]]** (longest-first) and skew **toxic/metabolic** (diabetes, B12, chemotherapy); **demyelinating** neuropathies are **non-length-dependent**, hitting **proximal and distal together** with **early proximal reflex loss**, and skew **inflammatory** ([[Guillain-Barré syndrome|GBS]], [[CIDP]], paraproteinemic) — with genetic exceptions like [[Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease|CMT]]. The **paint-splatter** image explains why demyelinating disease can still *look* distal (more "geography" in the long leg nerves) yet betrays itself with an **absent biceps reflex** — the finding that cracked this case.

**Tempo is a first-class variable.** Days-to-weeks with a 2–4-week nadir is **[[Guillain-Barré syndrome|GBS]]**; a neuropathy visibly worsening over weeks-to-months is **subacute** and belongs in the **"when to worry"** bin, where **reversible** causes (inflammatory, **paraproteinemic**, and the short list of truly rapid ones — GBS, HIV seroconversion, arsenic/heavy metals) concentrate. A years-long, stable, distal sensory neuropathy with absent ankle jerks can be watched; one "taking off" cannot.

**The neuropathy workup, and the commonly-missed SPEP.** For a distal symmetric polyneuropathy, the **AAN** essentials are **B12**, a **glucose/A1c or glucose-tolerance test**, and an **SPEP with immunofixation** — the last sent to *not miss* a paraprotein, usually normal, but occasionally the key. Escalation to **[[EMG]]** is warranted when axonal-vs-demyelinating is genuinely uncertain and would change the workup, or when localization (root vs nerve vs plexus) is muddy.

**EMG is a living exam.** Uniquely among diagnostics, the **electromyographer performs and adapts the study in real time**, choosing each next muscle from the pattern emerging — a true extension of the physical exam and of localization (e.g., disambiguating C6/C7/C8, median/ulnar/radial, or root vs plexus). The practical corollary: **order it with a precise question** ("axonal vs demyelinating?" or "root vs nerve?"), because a vague "weak hand" request yields a study that may not answer what you need.

**"Take one thing out."** When a picture has too many moving parts (transplant + immunosuppression + neuropathy + optic discs + platelets), **remove one element and re-reason**: strip the transplant context and "subacute demyelinating neuropathy with a paraprotein" resolves cleanly to a small differential; strip a single exam finding and a hidden **tetrad** may emerge from a confusing pentad. It is the antidote to both **anchoring** and the morning-report reflex to **force everything into one line**.

**POEMS syndrome.** [[POEMS syndrome|POEMS]] is a paraneoplastic manifestation of a **λ-restricted** monoclonal plasma-cell disorder driven by **VEGF**: **P**olyneuropathy (typically **demyelinating**, sensorimotor, often severe), **O**rganomegaly, **E**ndocrinopathy, **M**-protein, **S**kin changes — plus the frequently-present extras that appeared here: **papilledema**, **thrombocytosis/polycythemia**, **extravascular volume overload** (edema/effusions/ascites), and **sclerotic bone lesions**/Castleman disease. Diagnosis requires the two mandatory criteria (**polyneuropathy + monoclonal plasma-cell disorder**) plus a major (**Castleman, sclerotic bone lesion, or elevated VEGF**) and a minor. The clinical trigger to *think* of it is exactly this case's shape — a **subacute, worsening demyelinating neuropathy with a paraprotein and systemic features** — and it matters because, unlike many severe neuropathies, it is **treatable**.
