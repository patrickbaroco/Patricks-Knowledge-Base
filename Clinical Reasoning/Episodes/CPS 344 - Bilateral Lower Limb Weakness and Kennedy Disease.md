## Summary

> [!summary] Scan Summary
> A Neurology VMR on **"bilateral lower limb weakness for 20 years"** that is a two-part masterclass — first the **[[Anatomical Approach|localization]] of bilateral leg weakness**, then a full **[[Myopathy|muscle-vs-nerve]] work-through** — landing on the rare **[[Kennedy Disease|Kennedy disease]]** (X-linked spinobulbar muscular atrophy). A **67-year-old man** has **two decades of slowly progressive, proximal-onset leg weakness** that later adds **ptosis, nasal voice and dysphagia (bulbar), hand-grip loss, facial/tongue fasciculations, and gynecomastia** — with **no sensory loss, no bowel/bladder involvement**, and a striking **X-linked pedigree** (brothers and maternal-line male cousins similarly affected). Berkowitz frames bilateral leg weakness as **"thoracic spine or below"** — most likely **spine** (cord *or* **[[Cauda Equina|cauda equina]]**), then **peripheral** (nerve, **[[Neuromuscular Junction|NMJ]]**, muscle), and least likely a **[[Parasagittal Meningioma|midline brain]] lesion** (parasagittal meningioma or **[[Azygos Anterior Cerebral Artery|azygos-ACA]]** bilateral ACA strokes — the "gourmet" causes, since the leg homunculus sits medially). The **20-year tempo** narrows the chronic differential to **structural** (spondylosis — the [[Base Rate|common]] cause), **genetic** (**[[Hereditary Spastic Paraplegia|HSP]]**), and **slow infection** (**[[HTLV-1 Associated Myelopathy|HTLV-1 myelopathy]]**). But the exam **relocalizes**: **no sensory and no bowel/bladder** with **face and bulbar involvement** takes it *out of the spine*, and **fasciculations + areflexia with retained strength** take it *out of muscle* — because in **[[Muscle vs Nerve|muscle disease reflexes are preserved]]** (unless the muscle is too weak to move the joint), whereas here they are gone. Along the way Berkowitz teaches the **[[Myopathy|four categories of myopathy]]** (inflammatory / toxic / genetic / systemic), the **[[Myasthenia Gravis|myasthenia]] eyelid signs** (**[[Curtain Sign|curtain]]**, **[[Cogan Lid Twitch|Cogan]]**, **[[Hering's Law|Hering's law]]**; excluded by no fatigability and 20-year course), and the **[[Lambert-Eaton Syndrome|Lambert-Eaton]]** post-exercise reflex facilitation he's "always wanted to see." **[[Fasciculations|Fasciculations]]** (including **[[Tongue Fasciculations|tongue]]**) + **areflexia** + retained strength put this in the **[[Anterior Horn Cell|anterior horn / motor nerve]]**, opening the **[[Motor Neuron Disease|motor-neuron-disease]]** spectrum — **[[ALS|ALS]]** (upper+lower, but a 20-year course excludes it), **[[Progressive Muscular Atrophy|progressive muscular atrophy]]** (pure lower), **[[Primary Lateral Sclerosis|primary lateral sclerosis]]** (pure upper). The clincher is the **triad**: **pure-motor lower-motor-neuron disease + gynecomastia + X-linked inheritance** = **[[Kennedy Disease|Kennedy disease / SBMA]]**. A mildly elevated **CK 928** is correctly *not* re-anchored to muscle ("**the exam is smarter than the labs**" — CK rises modestly from denervation), **[[EMG|EMG]]** shows **widespread chronic anterior-horn-cell denervation**, and genetics confirm an **[[Androgen Receptor Gene|androgen-receptor]] CAG-repeat expansion (47 repeats)** — a **[[Trinucleotide Repeat Disorder|trinucleotide-repeat disease]]** (as in [[CPS 299 - Clumsiness and Spinocerebellar Ataxia Type 7|CPS 299]]'s SCA7). The coda is Marty Samuels's dictum that **"Google knows the answer"** — the neurologist's job is **localization + time course + family history**; feed "**motor neuron disease, genetic, X-linked, gynecomastia**" to a search and Kennedy disease appears.

## Pearls

- **Bilateral leg weakness is "thoracic spine or below" — spine most likely, periphery possible, brain a gourmet last resort.** The leg homunculus is medial, so a *brain* cause needs a **[[Parasagittal Meningioma|midline parasagittal]] lesion** or **bilateral ACA strokes** (**[[Azygos Anterior Cerebral Artery|azygos-ACA]]** variant). Say **"spine"** not "cord" — it can be **[[Spinal Cord|spinal cord]]** *or* **[[Cauda Equina|cauda equina]]**; below that lie **nerve, [[Neuromuscular Junction|NMJ]], and muscle**.
- **Use the [[Illness Tempo|20-year tempo]] to prune: chronic bilateral leg weakness is structural, genetic, or slow-infectious.** [[Base Rate|Commonest]] is **degenerative [[Spondylosis|spondylosis]]** (lumbosacral); then **[[Hereditary Spastic Paraplegia|hereditary spastic paraplegia]]** and other genetic causes; and the slow retrovirus **[[HTLV-1 Associated Myelopathy|HTLV-1 myelopathy]]** (imaging-negative, inflammatory CSF). Acute vascular and most metabolic causes don't fit 20 years.
- **Sensory, bowel/bladder, and face/bulbar findings *relocalize* — let them override the first impression.** **No sensory loss** removes muscle/NMJ from being *excluded* and keeps you honest; **no bowel/bladder + face/bulbar involvement** takes you **out of the spine**. This case looked spinal by the chief concern and muscular by the proximal onset, but the exam moved it to the **[[Anterior Horn Cell|anterior horn]]**.
- **[[Muscle vs Nerve|Reflexes distinguish muscle from nerve]] — and [[Fasciculations|fasciculations]] are a nerve/anterior-horn sign, never muscle.** In **[[Myopathy|muscle disease]] reflexes are preserved** (unless the muscle is too weak to move the joint); in motor-nerve disease they are **diminished/absent**. Retained strength *with* areflexia and **[[Tongue Fasciculations|tongue fasciculations]]** points squarely at the **[[Motor Neuron Disease|lower motor neuron]]**.
- **Organize [[Myopathy|myopathy]] into four buckets: inflammatory, toxic, genetic, systemic.** Inflammatory (**[[Dermatomyositis|dermatomyositis]]**, polymyositis, **[[Inclusion Body Myositis|IBM]]**, **[[Immune-Mediated Necrotizing Myopathy|IMNM]]** — anti-HMGCR/anti-SRP, statin-linked); toxic (**[[Statin Myopathy|statins]]** #1, steroids, tacrolimus, hydroxychloroquine); genetic (**[[Muscular Dystrophy|dystrophies]]** — Duchenne/Becker, **[[Oculopharyngeal Muscular Dystrophy|oculopharyngeal]]**, **[[Myotonic Dystrophy|myotonic]]**, limb-girdle; mitochondrial; metabolic; distal); and systemic (**[[Hypothyroid Myopathy|hypothyroid]]**, Cushing, overlap).
- **A mildly elevated [[Creatine Kinase|CK]] does not re-open the muscle door — the exam outranks the labs.** CK 928 would normally suggest muscle, but with fasciculations and areflexia everything else says nerve; motor-neuron disease raises CK modestly (denervation), so **don't let one lab drag you back** to a hypothesis the exam excluded.
- **Localize, time-course, and pedigree first — then the rare name is *lookup-able* ("Google knows the answer").** Pure-motor **[[Motor Neuron Disease|lower-motor-neuron]] disease + gynecomastia + X-linked inheritance** = **[[Kennedy Disease|Kennedy disease (SBMA)]]**, an **[[Androgen Receptor Gene|androgen-receptor]] CAG-repeat** disorder. The clinician's job is the localization; "motor neuron disease, genetic, X-linked, gynecomastia" retrieves the diagnosis.

## Problem Representation

A 67-year-old man presents with **20 years of slowly, steadily progressive bilateral lower-limb weakness** that began **proximally** (difficulty climbing stairs and rising from squatting) and, over the most recent five years, extended to **proximal arm weakness, ptosis, nasal voice and dysphagia (especially to liquids), hand-grip weakness, facial and tongue fasciculations, and frequent falls** (one with fracture) — **without any sensory symptoms, bowel or bladder dysfunction, ocular-motility complaints, or fatigability**, and with **gynecomastia** and a **striking X-linked family history** (brothers and maternal-line male cousins similarly affected). Examination shows **mild ptosis with orbicularis oculi/oris weakness, tongue atrophy and fasciculations, perioral fasciculations, proximal > distal weakness progressing over years, and reflexes that were reduced and became absent** (with flexor plantar responses), **no sensory deficit**, and gynecomastia. The pattern is a **pure-motor, non-length-restricted lower-motor-neuron syndrome with bulbar involvement** — localized (by preserved sensation, absent bowel/bladder, face/bulbar involvement, and areflexia with fasciculations) to the **anterior horn cell / motor neuron** rather than spine, muscle, or NMJ. A mildly elevated **CK (928)**, a negative acetylcholine-receptor antibody, and **EMG evidence of widespread chronic anterior-horn-cell denervation** support **motor neuron disease**, and the triad of **lower-motor-neuron disease + gynecomastia + X-linked inheritance** with a confirmed **androgen-receptor CAG-repeat expansion (47 repeats)** establishes **Kennedy disease (X-linked spinobulbar muscular atrophy)**.

## Case Narrative

Aye (A) opens with **"bilateral lower-limb weakness for 20 years,"** and Berkowitz has Valeria localize. She runs the motor pathway (cortex → corona radiata → brainstem → cord → root → nerve → **[[Neuromuscular Junction|NMJ]]** → muscle) and, for *bilateral leg* weakness, favors **spine or periphery**, offering two "gourmet" brain exceptions — a **[[Parasagittal Meningioma|midline parasagittal]] lesion** and an **[[Azygos Anterior Cerebral Artery|azygos-ACA]]** bilateral-ACA stroke — because the leg fibers are medial in the homunculus. Berkowitz endorses the framework and refines the rule: bilateral leg weakness is **"thoracic spine or below"** (a cervical lesion would take the arms; a brainstem lesion would add cranial nerves), and he insists on saying **"spine"** rather than "cord," since it can be **[[Spinal Cord|spinal cord]]** *or* **[[Cauda Equina|cauda equina]]**, with **nerve, NMJ, and muscle** below that.

On **[[Illness Tempo|tempo]]**, the **20-year** course points to **chronic** categories: **structural** (Berkowitz supplies the [[Base Rate|common]] answer Valeria's differential lacked — **degenerative [[Spondylosis|spondylosis]]**), **genetic** (**[[Hereditary Spastic Paraplegia|hereditary spastic paraplegia]]**), and **slow infection** — Valeria's excellent call of **[[HTLV-1 Associated Myelopathy|HTLV-1 myelopathy]]** (endemic in Peru, Japan, the Caribbean; imaging-negative with inflammatory CSF; the same entity built in [[CPS 312 - Frequent Stumbling and HTLV-1-Associated Myelopathy|CPS 312]]). They agree on the history targets: is it truly legs-only; proximal vs distal; sensory involvement; and — Berkowitz's favorite spine question — **bowel and bladder** (present with cord/cauda, absent with peripheral nerve/NMJ/muscle).

The **history** reshapes everything. The weakness began **proximally** (a muscle-flavored start Valeria and Berkowitz both note), then over five years added **arm weakness, nasal voice, ptosis, dysphagia to liquids, hand-grip loss, frequent falls, and facial "twitching,"** with **no sensory symptoms and no bowel/bladder involvement**. Valeria reads the proximal onset and cramps toward **[[Myopathy|muscle]]**, but the **ptosis, [[Bulbar Weakness|bulbar]] features, and possible fasciculations** unsettle that. Berkowitz uses the moment to teach the **[[Myopathy|four categories of myopathy]]** — **inflammatory** (**[[Dermatomyositis|dermatomyositis]]**, polymyositis, **[[Inclusion Body Myositis|inclusion body myositis]]**, **[[Immune-Mediated Necrotizing Myopathy|immune-mediated necrotizing myopathy]]** with anti-HMGCR/anti-SRP), **toxic** (**[[Statin Myopathy|statins]]** first, steroids, tacrolimus, hydroxychloroquine), **genetic** (**[[Muscular Dystrophy|muscular dystrophies]]** — Duchenne/Becker, Emery-Dreifuss, FSHD, limb-girdle, and the case-relevant **[[Oculopharyngeal Muscular Dystrophy|oculopharyngeal]]** with ptosis + bulbar weakness and a French-Canadian founder population; mitochondrial; metabolic; and the rare **distal myopathies**), and **systemic** (**[[Hypothyroid Myopathy|hypothyroid]]**, Cushing, overlap). He and Valeria then *pull* the muscle hypothesis apart: **[[Myasthenia Gravis|myasthenia]]** is excluded (no fatigability, no fluctuation, 20-year course), and **[[Fasciculations|fasciculations]]** — if that's what the twitching is — are a **lower-motor-neuron** sign, not muscle.

The **exam** confirms the reframe. There is **mild ptosis with orbicularis oculi/oris weakness, tongue atrophy and fasciculations, perioral fasciculations**, a **proximal > distal weakness** that has progressed over years, and **reflexes that were reduced and then became absent** with **flexor plantars**, **no sensory deficit**, and **gynecomastia**. Berkowitz walks through the **[[Myasthenia Gravis|myasthenic eyelid signs]]** A had checked and found absent — the **[[Curtain Sign|curtain sign]]**, the **[[Cogan Lid Twitch|Cogan lid twitch]]**, and **[[Hering's Law|Hering's law]]** — and the **[[Lambert-Eaton Syndrome|Lambert-Eaton]]** finding (post-exercise reflex facilitation) he has "always wanted to see." The decisive synthesis: **retained strength but absent reflexes** excludes muscle (a myopathy able to move the joint should still elicit a reflex), and **tongue/perioral fasciculations** localize to the **[[Anterior Horn Cell|anterior horn / motor nerve]]**. That opens the **[[Motor Neuron Disease|motor-neuron-disease]]** spectrum — **[[ALS|ALS]]** (upper *and* lower motor neuron; but a **20-year** course excludes it — ALS survival is ~3–5 years, worse with bulbar onset), **[[Progressive Muscular Atrophy|progressive muscular atrophy]]** (pure lower), and **[[Primary Lateral Sclerosis|primary lateral sclerosis]]** (pure upper).

The **family history** is the hinge: affected **brothers and maternal-line male cousins only** — an **X-linked** pattern. Combined with a **pure-motor lower-motor-neuron** syndrome and **gynecomastia**, Berkowitz assembles the triad and names the **[[Kennedy Disease|Kennedy disease (spinobulbar muscular atrophy)]]** — a slowly progressive, ALS-mimicking lower-motor-neuron disease with **anti-androgen features** (gynecomastia, erectile dysfunction, infertility). The workup follows the **[[Test Interpretation|"exam-over-labs"]]** discipline: a mildly elevated **[[Creatine Kinase|CK 928]]** is *not* allowed to re-anchor the case to muscle (motor-neuron disease raises CK modestly via denervation — "the exam is smarter than the labs"); acetylcholine-receptor antibody is negative; and **[[EMG|EMG/nerve conduction studies]]** show **widespread chronic denervation across four limbs and the tongue — anterior-horn-cell disease**. **Genetic testing** confirms an **[[Androgen Receptor Gene|androgen-receptor]] CAG-repeat expansion of 47** — a **[[Trinucleotide Repeat Disorder|trinucleotide-repeat disorder]]** (the same disease mechanism as SCA7 in [[CPS 299 - Clumsiness and Spinocerebellar Ataxia Type 7|CPS 299]]).

Berkowitz closes with **Marty Samuels's "Google knows the answer"** teaching: the neurologist's essential work is the **localization + time course + family history**; once you can say "**genetic, X-linked, motor neuron disease, gynecomastia**," the rare name (Kennedy disease) is retrievable from any search or textbook — whereas "weakness, fasciculation, tongue" is not enough. We can't know every disease, but if we localize and characterize the tempo and pedigree, **someone has described it**, and we can find the test to confirm it. A adds the teaching points: Kennedy disease is **purely motor, lower-motor-neuron only** (unlike ALS's upper+lower), **very slowly progressive** with a normal lifespan (unlike ALS), and marked by **anti-androgen features**, with no disease-modifying treatment yet.

## Teaching Discussion

- Localize bilateral leg weakness to the thoracic spine or below, treating the spine (cord or cauda equina) as most likely and a midline brain lesion (parasagittal meningioma, azygos-ACA bilateral strokes) as a rare last resort, since the leg fibers sit medially in the homunculus.
- Prune a 20-year course to chronic mechanisms — degenerative spondylosis (commonest), hereditary spastic paraplegia and other genetic diseases, and slow HTLV-1 myelopathy — and let sensory, bowel/bladder, and face/bulbar findings relocalize away from the spine when they appear.
- Distinguish muscle from nerve with the reflexes and fasciculations: muscle disease preserves reflexes unless the muscle is too weak to move the joint, motor-nerve disease diminishes or abolishes them, and fasciculations (especially of the tongue) are a lower-motor-neuron sign never produced by muscle.
- Organize myopathy into inflammatory, toxic, genetic, and systemic categories, and remember the ptosis-plus-bulbar genetic mimics (oculopharyngeal and myotonic dystrophy) that can imitate a neurogenic bulbar syndrome.
- Refuse to let a single mildly elevated CK re-open the muscle hypothesis when the exam says nerve — motor neuron disease raises CK modestly through denervation, and the exam outranks the lab.
- Recognize the triad of pure-motor lower-motor-neuron disease, gynecomastia, and X-linked inheritance as Kennedy disease (spinobulbar muscular atrophy), an androgen-receptor CAG-repeat disorder, and remember Samuels's lesson that once you nail the localization, time course, and pedigree, even a disease you have never seen is findable and confirmable.
