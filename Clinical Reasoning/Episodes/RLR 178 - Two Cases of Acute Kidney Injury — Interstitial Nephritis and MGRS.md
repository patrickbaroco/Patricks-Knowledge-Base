## Pearls

- **Proportionality first: ask how the patient feels, because silence is ominous.** A patient who feels *great* with a deeply elevated creatinine likely has a **[[Silence Is Ominous|silent intrinsic renal process]]** (glomerular, vascular, or tubulointerstitial disease — the diseases you don't feel), whereas one who feels *terrible* often has a diffuse, **[[Prerenal Azotemia|pre-renal]]**, reversible insult with the kidney as innocent bystander; a very low baseline creatinine (0.6 in a 70-year-old) additionally flags low muscle mass and less reserve.
- **For out-of-the-blue AKI in a well patient, think the two Ps — pharmacy and plumbing.** Hemodynamic **[[Prerenal Azotemia|pre-renal]]** disease is unlikely without symptoms, so meticulously review meds (**[[NSAID Nephrotoxicity|NSAIDs]]**, ACE/ARB, antibiotics) and interrogate for obstruction; a normal **[[Bladder Scan|bladder scan]]** excludes only bladder-outlet obstruction, so **bilateral ureteral obstruction** still warrants imaging for **[[Hydronephrosis|hydronephrosis]]**.
- **The urinalysis is really a glomerular analysis.** A bland **[[Urinalysis|UA]]** effectively excludes **[[Glomerulonephritis|glomerular disease]]** — the one category the UA rules out by itself — but says little about tubular, interstitial, vascular, pre-, or post-renal disease, so a "stone cold normal" UA redirects you to those compartments.
- **Use blood pressure to localize intrinsic renal disease.** Lump **[[Glomerulonephritis|glomerular]] + [[Renovascular Disease|vascular]]** disease (typically **hypertensive** — renal ischemia drives hyperaldosteronism and often refractory HTN) versus **[[Tubulointerstitial Nephritis|tubular + interstitial]]** disease (typically "mellow," normotensive); a normotensive, non-edematous, "sleepy" creatinine bump is almost always **[[Tubulointerstitial Nephritis|tubulointerstitial]]** — the deadly exception being **[[Monoclonal Gammopathy of Renal Significance|paraprotein]]** infiltration.
- **Put drug-induced interstitial nephritis on a timeline — acuity predicts reversibility.** Abrupt exposures (**[[Immune Checkpoint Inhibitor Nephritis|checkpoint-inhibitor]]** AIN — little fibrosis, steroid-responsive) reverse; chronic exposures (months of a **[[Fluoroquinolones|fluoroquinolone]]**; **[[PPI and Interstitial Nephritis|PPIs cause *chronic* TIN, not acute AIN]]**) are fibrotic and minimally reversible — which is why a months-on-levofloxacin patient is neither biopsied nor given steroids, just de-prescribed and accepted at a new baseline. Acquired tubulointerstitial disease in adults is "**meds, meds, meds**."
- **Hematuria + AKI has only two linking explanations — glomerulonephritis or clot obstruction — and in a monoclonal gammopathy it screams MGRS.** A *focal* bleed (stone, bladder/prostate cancer) can't take down both kidneys, so **[[Hematuria and AKI|hematuria with AKI]]** means bleeding *everywhere* (**[[Glomerulonephritis|glomerulonephritis]]**) or a clot blocking the bladder; with a paraprotein, suspect **[[Monoclonal Gammopathy of Renal Significance|MGRS]]**, remembering the clone can damage the kidney *indirectly* (dysregulating **[[Complement|complement]]** → **[[C3 Glomerulopathy|C3 glomerulopathy]]**), so a **polytypic** biopsy doesn't exclude it and **[[Clone-Directed Therapy|clone-directed therapy]]** may be required.

## Problem Representation

Two men in their 70s–80s are admitted the same day for an incidentally discovered, asymptomatic rise in creatinine. **Patient 1** is an 81-year-old with a chronic left-foot osteomyelitis (hardware in place, on months of antibiotics — ertapenem then levofloxacin plus cefadroxil) whose creatinine rose from a baseline of 0.6 to 4.1; he feels well, with a normal blood pressure (101–120 systolic), a bland urinalysis (isosthenuria, specific gravity ~1.010), a normal bladder scan, no hydronephrosis and normal-sized kidneys on ultrasound, negative urine eosinophils, and only partial improvement (to ~3.2) with several liters of fluid — a fluid-refractory, normotensive, UA-negative intrinsic AKI most consistent with chronic drug-induced tubulointerstitial nephritis, managed by stopping the offending antibiotics (no biopsy, no steroids) with stabilization at a new baseline of ~2.3. **Patient 2** is an elderly man with biclonal MGUS (IgG-kappa and IgG-lambda) and recurrent DVTs on a DOAC whose creatinine rose from 1.3 to 3.7; he feels well but is hypertensive (160), with hematuria (>180 RBCs) plus mild albuminuria and pyuria — the ominous hematuria-plus-AKI combination — and a renal biopsy showing necrotizing crescentic glomerulonephritis with IgG and C3 deposits, focal thrombotic microangiopathy, RBC casts, and patchy chronic tubulointerstitial nephritis; the deposits are polytypic (not matching his monoclonal proteins), with negative ANCA and anti-GBM and normal complement, no solid cancer, and 5% marrow plasma cells — a picture best explained by monoclonal gammopathy of renal significance (potentially via indirect, complement-mediated injury), for which clone-directed therapy is being weighed (outcome pending).

## Case Narrative

*A paired-case episode: two same-day AKI admissions used to build a general localization framework. Reza presents both; Rabih reasons through each in turn.*

**Framework.** Rabih anchors on **proportionality**: since patients don't *feel* their kidneys, his most valuable first move is to walk in and ask how they're doing. **Feeling well with a badly dysfunctional kidney is ominous** — it implies a **[[Silence Is Ominous|silent, intrinsic]]** process (glomerulonephritis, vasculitis, tubulointerstitial disease), the diseases the body doesn't announce — whereas **feeling terrible** counterintuitively carries a *better* prognosis, because the kidney is usually the innocent bystander of a diffuse, dilute, reversible (**[[Prerenal Azotemia|pre-renal]]**) insult. He also flags that a baseline creatinine of **0.6 in a 70-year-old** signals low muscle mass/frailty and a truly lower GFR with less reserve, and that the **depth** of injury (into the 4s) makes full reversibility less likely. For **out-of-the-blue AKI** in a well patient, hemodynamic pre-renal disease is improbable, so he invokes the **[[Two Ps|two Ps — pharmacy and plumbing]]** (review NSAIDs/ACE/ARB/antibiotics; hunt for obstruction), using the **[[Urinalysis|UA]]** as a "**glomerular analysis**."

**Case 1 — creatinine 0.6 → 4.1, chronic antibiotics → drug-induced interstitial nephritis.** The 81-year-old has a **chronic left-foot [[Osteomyelitis|osteomyelitis]]** with hardware, on antibiotics for months (ertapenem → **[[Fluoroquinolones|levofloxacin]]** + cefadroxil), sent in when a pre-angiogram BMP showed the rise. He feels **well**; the **bladder scan is normal** (excluding bladder-outlet obstruction, not bilateral ureteral), the **UA is stone-cold normal** with **isosthenuria** (SG ~1.010, a tubular concentrating defect), the **renal ultrasound shows no hydronephrosis** and normal-sized kidneys, **urine eosinophils are negative**, and he is **fluid-refractory** (4 → 3.2 with three liters). Rabih walks the systematic ladder — not pre-renal (fluid-refractory), not post-renal (no hydro), not glomerular (bland UA) — leaving **[[Tubulointerstitial Nephritis|tubulointerstitial]]** or **[[Renovascular Disease|vascular]]** disease, and vascular is unlikely in an oligosymptomatic patient (vascular renal disease tends to be acute, hectic, and **hypertensive**). So this is **[[Drug-Induced Interstitial Nephritis|drug-induced tubulointerstitial nephritis]]** — "acquired TIN in adults is **meds, meds, meds**," with **[[Fluoroquinolones|quinolones]]** a frequent offender. He teaches the two-category split — **non-inflammatory** tubulointerstitial nephropathy (toxins: heavy metals, herbals, **[[Analgesic Nephropathy|analgesic nephropathy]]** from acetaminophen/NSAIDs; genetic) versus true **inflammatory** TIN (autoimmune, infection, and — dominantly — drugs) — and the crucial **[[Tempo-Based Reasoning|timeline]]** point: **checkpoint-inhibitor** AIN is abrupt with little fibrosis and strong steroid response, whereas **[[PPI and Interstitial Nephritis|PPIs cause *chronic* TIN]]** and a **months-long fluoroquinolone** course produces fibrosis with minimal reversibility. Accordingly, nephrology and ID **did not biopsy and did not give steroids**, simply **stopped the antibiotics**; three weeks out the creatinine sits at **2.3** — a new baseline and a sequela, exactly as chronicity predicts (Reza's spinal-cord-injury analogy: chronic → irreversible, acute → salvageable). Confirming the localization, his **blood pressure was 101–120** — normotensive, fitting tubulointerstitial rather than glomerular/vascular disease.

**Case 2 — creatinine 1.3 → 3.7, hematuria + AKI in biclonal MGUS → MGRS.** The second man has **[[MGUS|biclonal MGUS]]** (IgG-kappa and IgG-lambda) followed in a multidisciplinary clinic and **recurrent DVTs** on a **DOAC**, found on a routine visit to have the rise. He too feels well, but his **[[Urinalysis|UA]]** is different: orange urine, mild albuminuria (protein 30), pyuria (WBC 10), and **hematuria (>180 RBCs)**. Rabih seizes on **[[Hematuria and AKI|hematuria plus AKI]]** — a combination with only **two** linking explanations, since a *focal* bleed (stone, bladder/prostate cancer) cannot compromise *both* kidneys: either the kidney is **bleeding everywhere** (**[[Glomerulonephritis|glomerulonephritis]]**) or a **clot has blocked the bladder**. Most often the two findings are coincidental (a stone plus pre-renal AKI), but **persistent, unexplained** AKI with hematuria in a patient with a paraprotein is the deadly combination pointing at **[[Monoclonal Gammopathy of Renal Significance|MGRS]]** — the "two N's" a monoclonal gammopathy threatens being **neuropathy and nephropathy**. A **[[Blood Pressure as a Localizing Clue|blood pressure of 160]]** independently favors a glomerular/vascular process. He undergoes a **renal biopsy** (bridged with heparin given his clot history): **necrotizing crescentic glomerulonephritis** with rare active cellular crescents, **IgG and C3 deposits**, **focal [[Thrombotic Microangiopathy|thrombotic microangiopathy]]**, RBC casts, tubular hemosiderosis, and patchy chronic TIN. Crucially, the **IgG is polytypic** (not monoclonal, not matching his serum IgG-kappa/IgG-lambda clones).

Rabih works the glomerulonephritis: autoimmune causes dominate GN in older adults — **[[ANCA-Associated Vasculitis|ANCA-associated vasculitis]]** (pauci-immune) and **[[Anti-GBM Disease|anti-GBM]]** — but the biopsy fits neither cleanly (no linear IgG for anti-GBM; immune deposits argue against pauci-immune, though "pauci" means *little*, not *absent*). He sends **[[ANCA-Associated Vasculitis|MPO/PR3-ANCA]]**, **[[Anti-GBM Disease|anti-GBM]]**, and **[[Complement|complements]]** (recalling that **[[Hypocomplementemia|hypocomplementemic GN]]** is *infection until proven otherwise*, plus lupus, cryo, and C3 disease). All return reassuringly *negative/normal*: **ANCA negative, anti-GBM negative, C3/C4 normal**, no solid cancer, and **5% marrow plasma cells** (below myeloma criteria). The key conceptual move: **[[Monoclonal Gammopathy of Renal Significance|MGRS]]** does not require the clone to *deposit* in the kidney — the paraprotein can act *indirectly*, e.g., as an autoantibody **dysregulating the [[Complement|complement]] cascade** to produce **[[C3 Glomerulopathy|C3 glomerulopathy]]**, so "you're looking for the paraprotein in the wrong place." He notes the well-described overlap of **[[Crescentic Glomerulonephritis|crescentic GN]] with [[Thrombotic Microangiopathy|TMA]]**, and the nephritic paraprotein diseases (type 1/2 **[[Cryoglobulinemia|cryoglobulinemia]]**, C3 glomerulopathy, **[[PGNMID|PGNMID]]**), alongside the classic nephrotic **[[AL Amyloidosis|AL amyloidosis]]** (only ~15% of whom have overt myeloma). The therapeutic fork: **autoimmune** GN responds to anti-inflammatory therapy (steroids), whereas **hematologic/MGRS** disease needs **[[Clone-Directed Therapy|B-cell/plasma-cell-directed therapy]]** (rituximab, myeloma-type regimens) — and a normal complement pushes away from C3-mediated disease while the negative autoimmune serologies weaken the ANCA-negative-pauci-immune alternative. Rabih's honest conclusion: with **isolated kidney disease, no provable alternative, and a background clone**, he would **not** settle for steroids alone and would worry about a **hematologic** driver despite the sub-threshold marrow ("a small number of really bad actors"), treating **urgently** to prevent dialysis; the only tension is the *polytypic* biopsy (sampling error vs indirect complement mechanism vs a truly different disease). Treatment had not yet been given at recording — **outcome pending**.

### Illness Scripts and Bias

- **Proportionality and "[[Silence Is Ominous|silence is ominous]]."** The single most valuable datum is how the patient feels: asymptomatic profound AKI implies a silent intrinsic process, inverting the usual comfort of a well-appearing patient.
- **[[Anatomical Approach|Localization ladder]].** Pre-renal (fluid response) → post-renal (**[[Hydronephrosis|hydro]]**) → glomerular (**[[Urinalysis|UA]]**) → tubulointerstitial vs vascular — with the **[[Two Ps|two Ps]]** (pharmacy/plumbing) as the front-line screen in a well patient.
- **[[Blood Pressure as a Localizing Clue|Blood pressure as a localizer]].** Glomerular + vascular disease runs **hypertensive** (renal ischemia → hyperaldosteronism); tubulointerstitial disease is **normotensive/mellow** — Case 1 (101–120) vs Case 2 (160) map cleanly onto their biopsies-in-waiting.
- **[[Test Interpretation|The UA as a glomerular analysis]].** A bland UA excludes glomerular disease and *only* glomerular disease; **isosthenuria** signals tubular dysfunction; **[[Hematuria and AKI|hematuria + AKI]]** is a two-answer syndrome (GN or clot obstruction).
- **[[Tempo-Based Reasoning|Drug-TIN on a timeline]].** Reversibility tracks acuity: abrupt **[[Immune Checkpoint Inhibitor Nephritis|checkpoint-inhibitor]]** AIN responds to steroids; chronic **[[Fluoroquinolones|fluoroquinolone]]**/**[[PPI and Interstitial Nephritis|PPI]]** exposure fibroses and doesn't — dictating *not* to biopsy or steroid Case 1.
- **[[Base Rate|Base rate]] with a foreground/background discipline.** A background **[[Monoclonal Gammopathy of Renal Significance|paraprotein]]** must be connected to the *foreground* disease only when justified; here the foreground (crescentic GN + TMA + hematuria/AKI) plus the exclusion of alternatives *earns* the MGRS link.
- **[[Monoclonal Gammopathy of Renal Significance|MGRS]] and indirect injury.** The clone can damage the kidney directly (deposition — **[[AL Amyloidosis|amyloid]]**, **[[PGNMID|PGNMID]]**, **[[Cryoglobulinemia|cryo]]**) or *indirectly* (**[[Complement|complement]]** dysregulation → **[[C3 Glomerulopathy|C3 glomerulopathy]]**), so a **polytypic** biopsy and sub-threshold marrow do **not** exonerate it — and the treatment fork (steroids vs **[[Clone-Directed Therapy|clone-directed therapy]]**) hinges on that distinction.
- **[[Diagnostic Humility|Humility]].** Reza's initial note ("maybe just biclonal MGRS") is refined, not overturned; both hosts hold the genuine uncertainty (ANCA-negative pauci-immune vs indirect-MGRS) and the pending outcome openly.

## Teaching Discussion

- Begin every AKI by asking how the patient feels: an asymptomatic patient with profoundly reduced kidney function likely has a silent intrinsic process (glomerular, vascular, or tubulointerstitial), whereas a symptomatic one more often has a reversible pre-renal insult, and a very low baseline creatinine signals low muscle mass and reduced reserve.
- Screen out-of-the-blue AKI in a well patient with the two Ps — pharmacy and plumbing — reviewing nephrotoxic medications and interrogating for obstruction, remembering that a normal bladder scan excludes only bladder-outlet obstruction and not bilateral ureteral obstruction.
- Treat the urinalysis as a glomerular analysis: a bland UA effectively excludes glomerular disease but not tubular, interstitial, vascular, pre-, or post-renal disease, and isosthenuria indicates a tubular concentrating defect.
- Use blood pressure to localize intrinsic renal disease, grouping glomerular and vascular disease (hypertensive, via renal-ischemia-driven hyperaldosteronism) against tubulointerstitial disease (normotensive and mellow), while remembering paraprotein infiltration as the dangerous quiet exception.
- Attribute acquired adult tubulointerstitial nephritis to medications until proven otherwise, distinguish non-inflammatory nephropathy (analgesics, heavy metals, herbals) from inflammatory interstitial nephritis (drugs, infection, autoimmune), and place drug-induced disease on a timeline, since abrupt exposures (checkpoint inhibitors) are steroid-responsive while chronic exposures (fluoroquinolones, PPIs) fibrose and are minimally reversible — arguing against biopsy or steroids.
- Read hematuria plus AKI as a two-answer syndrome — glomerulonephritis or clot obstruction — because a focal bleed cannot compromise both kidneys, and pursue a glomerular cause when the two findings are persistent and unexplained.
- Evaluate a rapidly progressive glomerulonephritis in an older adult for ANCA-associated vasculitis and anti-GBM disease, send complements (with hypocomplementemic GN treated as infection until proven otherwise), and recognize that a monoclonal gammopathy can cause renal significance either by direct deposition or by indirect complement dysregulation, so a polytypic biopsy and sub-threshold marrow do not exclude MGRS.
- Match therapy to mechanism: autoimmune glomerulonephritis responds to anti-inflammatory/steroid therapy, whereas MGRS and hematologic disease require clone-directed (B-cell/plasma-cell) therapy, and many MGRS patients warrant urgent treatment despite not meeting criteria for overt malignancy.
