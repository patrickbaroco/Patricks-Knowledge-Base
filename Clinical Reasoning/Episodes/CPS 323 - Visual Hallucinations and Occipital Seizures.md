> A neurology Virtual Morning Report with neurologist **Aaron Berkowitz** on **visual hallucinations** — and a case that ends, deliberately, in ambiguity. A **60-year-old man** has weeks of stereotyped episodes: an **oscillating red light that grows**, then **angry unfamiliar faces** he knows are not real, in the **left visual field**, **4–10 times an hour**, lasting under a minute, preceded by a **right parieto-occipital headache** — on a background of a year of blurred vision, poorly controlled diabetes, and heavy alcohol use. Berkowitz "**[[Round to the Nearest Schema|rounds to the nearest schema]]**" (visual hallucinations → [[Altered Mental Status|altered mental status]], and monocular-vs-binocular from the visual toolkit) and teaches the **[[Visual Hallucinations|typology of visual hallucinations]]**: [[Charles Bonnet Syndrome|Charles Bonnet]] (release hallucinations of visual loss), [[Dementia with Lewy Bodies|Lewy body]] (little people/animals), [[Peduncular Hallucinosis|peduncular hallucinosis]] (midbrain, dreamlike), alcohol withdrawal (bugs), and [[Occipital Seizure|occipital seizure]] (primitive geometric shapes). The **episodic, focal, stereotyped** pattern points to **[[Occipital Seizure|occipital seizures]]** ("anything episodic may be seizure") from a right parieto-occipital structural lesion, with [[Migraine|migraine]] reframed as a possible **secondary** phenomenon. The data muddy it: **HbA1c 20** with glucose spikes that **correlate with the episodes** ([[Hyperglycemic Seizures|hyperglycemic focal seizures]]), a confusing left/right localization ([[Gaze Deviation|gaze deviation]], [[Todd's Paralysis|Todd's]] phenomena), [[Diabetic Retinopathy|diabetic retinopathy]] (a [[Charles Bonnet Syndrome|Charles Bonnet]] substrate), and a **left occipital [[Diffusion Restriction|diffusion-restricting]] lesion** that could be **stroke or seizure-related change**. Berkowitz lands on **[[Hickam's Dictum|multiple coexisting diagnoses]]** — occipital seizures provoked by hyperglycemia on a possible stroke, plus retinopathy-driven release hallucinations — treated empirically (anticonvulsant, glucose control, stroke prevention) with the motto "**[[Embrace the Ambiguity|embrace the ambiguity]]**."

## Pearls

- **[[Visual Hallucinations|Visual hallucinations]] belong on a neurology differential — know the types.** [[Charles Bonnet Syndrome|Charles Bonnet]] (release hallucinations from visual loss — complex, non-threatening, insight preserved; think diabetic retinopathy, macular degeneration, glaucoma); [[Dementia with Lewy Bodies|Lewy body]] (little people or animals, non-disturbing, often early); [[Peduncular Hallucinosis|peduncular hallucinosis]] (midbrain lesion, dreamlike "augmented reality"); alcohol/[[Delirium Tremens|withdrawal]] (disturbing bugs); and [[Occipital Seizure|occipital seizure]] (primitive geometric shapes). **Auditory** hallucinations, by contrast, are strongly psychiatric.
- **Episodic + focal + stereotyped = [[Seizure|seizure]] until proven otherwise — and seizures can do anything.** Recurrent brief (<1 min) events several times an hour, always the same, in one visual field, point at **[[Occipital Seizure|occipital seizures]]**, whose classic phenomenology is a **primitive geometric shape** (a bright circle, square, or spinning "beachball") rather than a formed image. A structural lesion can generate them, so the visual "aura" may be ictal, not migrainous.
- **[[Migraine|Migraine]] is a syndrome that can be *secondary*.** New migrainous visual phenomena in an older adult are atypical; the same symptomatology (via [[Cortical Spreading Depression|cortical spreading depression]]) can be provoked by a structural lesion — or, as Berkowitz has seen, be an occipital **seizure** masquerading as migrainous aura ("secondary, even tertiary migraine"). Image the older adult with new "migraine."
- **[[Gaze Deviation|Conjugate gaze deviation]] localizes — and [[Todd's Paralysis|Todd's phenomena]] flip it.** Each [[Frontal Eye Field|frontal eye field]] drives the eyes to the opposite side, so ictal spread deviates the eyes **away** from the lesion (a right-hemisphere seizure → eyes left), and after the seizure the exhausted field lets the eyes drift **back toward** the lesion with post-ictal (Todd's) deficits — which can be weakness, hemianopia (occipital), or neglect (parietal), not just a weak limb.
- **[[Diffusion Restriction|Diffusion restriction]] is not synonymous with stroke.** Its differential includes acute [[Ischemic Stroke|ischemic stroke]] (cytotoxic edema), **[[Seizure|seizure]] activity itself**, [[Brain Abscess|abscess]] (dense inflammatory cells), and **[[Primary CNS Lymphoma|primary CNS lymphoma]]** in the immunocompetent (dense B cells). A gyriform, non-vascular-territory pattern favors a seizure-related change; when a lesion could be *either* a stroke provoking seizures *or* a seizure-induced change, **repeat imaging in months** (looking for encephalomalacia) resolves it.
- **When one diagnosis won't fit, invoke [[Hickam's Dictum|Hickam's dictum]] and [[Embrace the Ambiguity|embrace the ambiguity]].** This patient plausibly has *several* things at once — occipital seizures, **[[Hyperglycemic Seizures|hyperglycemia]]** lowering the seizure threshold (HbA1c 20, episodes tracking glucose spikes), a possible occipital **stroke**, and **[[Diabetic Retinopathy|retinopathy]]**-driven **[[Charles Bonnet Syndrome|Charles Bonnet]]** faces. Treat the treatable in parallel (anticonvulsant, glucose control, stroke prevention) and let follow-up imaging adjudicate rather than forcing a single unifying answer.

## Problem Representation

A 60-year-old man with poorly controlled diabetes, hypertension, a year of blurred vision, and heavy alcohol use presents with weeks of stereotyped, brief (<1 minute) episodes occurring 4–10 times an hour: an oscillating enlarging red light followed by unfamiliar accusatory faces he recognizes as unreal, confined to the left visual field and preceded by a right parieto-occipital headache. His neurologic exam is normal at baseline but during episodes shows left gaze deviation with nystagmoid movements and a transient right visual-field loss, and ophthalmology finds diabetic retinopathy. Work-up reveals marked hyperglycemia (HbA1c 20) with glucose spikes correlating with episode frequency, a small left periventricular CT hypodensity, and an MRI showing left occipital diffusion restriction. The picture is best explained as focal occipital seizures — provoked by hyperglycemia and possibly by an occipital stroke — with a superimposed retinopathy-related Charles Bonnet component, a multifactorial diagnosis treated empirically with an anticonvulsant, glucose control, and stroke prevention amid genuine localization and etiologic ambiguity.

## Case Narrative

A CP Solvers Neurology VMR with **Aaron Berkowitz** teaching, **Valeria** presenting, and **Gregory** (Ghana) and **Umbish** discussing. The chief concern: **visual hallucinations**.

**Rounding to the nearest schema, and the typology.** Because "visual hallucinations" has no tidy framework, Berkowitz **[[Round to the Nearest Schema|rounds to the nearest]]** — it's a form of [[Altered Mental Status|altered mental status]] (systemic/[[Delirium|delirium]], psychiatric, or neurodegenerative), and it's *visual*, so even the monocular-vs-binocular question from the diplopia toolkit can matter. He teaches the **[[Visual Hallucinations|typology]]**: **[[Charles Bonnet Syndrome|Charles Bonnet / release hallucinations]]** (visual loss leaves a gap the cortex fills — like an amplified blind spot); **[[Dementia with Lewy Bodies|Lewy body]]** hallucinations (little people or animals, non-disturbing, often preceding other features — and "the presence"); **[[Peduncular Hallucinosis|peduncular hallucinosis]]** (a midbrain lesion producing dreamlike "augmented reality" — the hospital tunnel becoming synchronized swimmers and a spaceship that was the MRI); alcohol **[[Delirium Tremens|withdrawal]]** (disturbing crawling bugs); and neuropsychiatric overlaps ([[Anti-NMDA Receptor Encephalitis|NMDA]]/infectious encephalitis in the young). He notes the clinical rule that **auditory** hallucinations are strongly psychiatric while **visual** ones range across neurology.

**HPI.** A **60-year-old man** has weeks of episodes beginning with an **oscillating red light that grows**, then **angry unfamiliar faces** he interprets as accusatory but knows are unreal, in the **left visual field**, **4–10 times an hour**, waking him at night, each lasting **under a minute**, and preceded by a **right parieto-occipital headache**; he also reports a year of blurred vision. Umbish flags the **focal, one-sided** phenomenology (left visual field → right hemisphere) and the occipital headache. Berkowitz presses the discriminating feature — this is **episodic**, several times an hour — and after the primitive geometric quality of the "red light" surfaces, reframes it: this is not (only) [[Migraine|migraine]] but likely a **[[Occipital Seizure|focal occipital seizure]]** from a structural lesion. He teaches that **[[Migraine|migraine]] is a syndrome that can be secondary** (a lesion driving [[Cortical Spreading Depression|cortical spreading depression]]), that **occipital seizures** classically produce primitive geometric shapes (a colleague's patient saw the spinning "beachball" cursor, with an occipital EEG correlate), and that "**seizures can do anything**." He keeps the neurodegenerative visual syndromes on the table — Lewy body, **[[Posterior Cortical Atrophy|posterior cortical atrophy]]** (the visual variant of Alzheimer's), the Heidenhain variant of CJD — but the **weeks-long, headache-associated** tempo favors something structural.

**Localization and the structural differential.** The history localizes **right and posterior** (left-field phenomena, right occipital headache). Over weeks, Gregory raises **neoplasm** (glioblastoma; more commonly metastases) and **infection** (TB/tuberculoma, abscess); Berkowitz adds the [[Glioblastoma|glioblastoma]] pearl (Marty Samuels: deficits mild relative to a large mass, often presenting with seizure). The social history — **heavy alcohol** (to the point of losing consciousness) and **poorly controlled diabetes** with limited insulin access — raises alcohol-withdrawal hallucinations and the aside that **[[Hyperglycemic Seizures|hyperglycemia can cause focal seizures]]** (and hemichorea-hemiballism), though he expects the focality to reflect an underlying lesion.

**The exam and the left/right puzzle.** Baseline exam is normal; **during episodes** there is **left gaze deviation with nystagmoid movements** and a transient **right visual-field loss**. Berkowitz works the [[Gaze Deviation|gaze deviation]]: a **[[Frontal Eye Field|frontal eye field]]** drives the eyes contralaterally, so ictal spread to the right FEF deviates the eyes **left** (away from the lesion), and post-ictally the eyes drift back toward the lesion with **[[Todd's Paralysis|Todd's]]** phenomena (weakness, or a hemianopia from occipital, or neglect from parietal involvement). But the pieces **don't fully square** — left-field hallucinations and left gaze deviation argue right hemisphere, yet the ictal **right** visual-field loss and later imaging point left. He names the discomfort honestly and teaches the bedside tools for extent (homonymous field cut, clock-drawing for left neglect).

**The muddying data.** Labs show **glucose 496**, evening spikes of 300–400, and a stunning **HbA1c of 20**; carbamazepine and insulin are started, and **episode frequency correlates with glucose spikes** (though reporting is inconsistent — and the "field loss" is itself unreliable on formal testing). **EEG** shows posterior attenuation (side reported inconsistently). **CT** shows only a small hypodensity near the posterior left ventricle; **ophthalmology** confirms **[[Diabetic Retinopathy|diabetic retinopathy]]**; and a later **MRI** (obtained off-site) shows **left occipital hyperintensity with diffusion restriction**. Berkowitz teaches the **[[Diffusion Restriction|diffusion-restriction differential]]** — [[Ischemic Stroke|stroke]] (cytotoxic edema), **seizure activity itself**, [[Brain Abscess|abscess]], and **[[Primary CNS Lymphoma|CNS lymphoma]]** in the immunocompetent — and the core dilemma: is the left occipital lesion a **stroke provoking seizures**, or a **seizure-induced** change? A gyriform, non-vascular-territory pattern favors the latter; when unclear, **repeat imaging in months** (encephalomalacia = old stroke) settles it. He illustrates with his own case of a man whose "migrainous aura" turned out to be occipital seizures, and another whose only sign of seven seizures an hour was being subtly "off."

**Resolution — embrace the ambiguity.** Valeria's team lands on **[[Hickam's Dictum|multiple coexisting problems]]**: signs suggestive of an occipital **stroke** (started on secondary prevention), **[[Hyperglycemic Seizures|hyperglycemic]]**-provoked focal **seizures** (continued carbamazepine, which helped), and **[[Diabetic Retinopathy|retinopathy]]**-related **[[Charles Bonnet Syndrome|Charles Bonnet]]** faces — with diabetes that stayed hard to control. Berkowitz endorses treating the treatable in parallel and letting follow-up imaging adjudicate the diffusion-restriction question, closing on his motto "**[[Embrace the Ambiguity|embrace the ambiguity]]**." Valeria's teaching points and Maria's Oliver Sacks reading (*Hallucinations*; Charles Bonnet describing his grandfather's "theatre") round out a case whose lesson is the method, not a single answer.

### Illness Scripts and Bias

- **[[Round to the Nearest Schema|Rounding to the nearest schema]] plus a [[Visual Hallucinations|typology]].** With no dedicated framework, folding visual hallucinations into AMS *and* into the visual system, then deploying the hallucination typology, is what generated a workable differential — the same move used in [[CPS 395 - Behavior Change and Neurocysticercosis|CPS 395]].
- **Episodic-and-stereotyped → [[Seizure|seizure]].** The pattern (brief, frequent, identical, one-field) overrode the "hallucination = psychiatric" prior and named occipital seizures — "anything episodic may be seizure," echoing [[CPS 395 - Behavior Change and Neurocysticercosis|CPS 395]].
- **[[Anatomical Approach|Localization]] held loosely.** The gaze-deviation/Todd's reasoning localized toward the right hemisphere, but the ictal right-field loss and left occipital MRI didn't square — and Berkowitz modeled naming the inconsistency rather than forcing it.
- **[[Test Interpretation|Test interpretation]] as the crux.** The [[Diffusion Restriction|diffusion-restriction]] differential (stroke vs seizure vs abscess vs lymphoma) and the plan to **repeat imaging** to distinguish a causal stroke from a seizure-induced change is the case's central diagnostic lever.
- **[[Hickam's Dictum|Hickam's dictum]] over a forced unifier.** Rather than a single [[Occam's Razor|Occam]] diagnosis, the reasoning accepts several coexisting contributors (seizure, hyperglycemia, stroke, retinopathy/Charles Bonnet) — appropriate when a comorbid, poorly-controlled patient accumulates overlapping problems.
- **[[Diagnostic Humility|Diagnostic humility]] — "embrace the ambiguity."** The case ends without a definitive diagnosis; the discipline is to treat every treatable thread in parallel and let time and repeat imaging adjudicate, a model of acting well under uncertainty.

## Teaching Discussion

- Put visual hallucinations on a neurologic differential and use the typology: Charles Bonnet/release hallucinations from visual loss (diabetic retinopathy, macular degeneration, glaucoma), Lewy body hallucinations (little people or animals), peduncular hallucinosis (midbrain, dreamlike), withdrawal (disturbing bugs), and occipital seizure (primitive geometric shapes) — while remembering auditory hallucinations are strongly psychiatric.
- Treat episodic, brief, stereotyped, one-field phenomena as seizure until proven otherwise; occipital seizures classically produce primitive geometric shapes, and a structural lesion can drive them, so an apparent visual aura may be ictal rather than migrainous.
- Regard migraine as a syndrome that can be secondary — new migrainous visual phenomena in an older adult warrant imaging, since a structural lesion (or even a seizure) can produce migrainous aura.
- Localize conjugate gaze deviation to the contralateral frontal eye field (eyes deviate away from the lesion during a seizure and drift back toward it post-ictally), and expect Todd's phenomena to include not only weakness but hemianopia (occipital) or neglect (parietal).
- Interpret diffusion restriction with its full differential — ischemic stroke, seizure activity itself, abscess, and immunocompetent CNS lymphoma — and, when a lesion could be either a stroke provoking seizures or a seizure-induced change, repeat imaging in months (looking for encephalomalacia) to resolve it.
- Consider hyperglycemia (and hypoglycemia) as a provocateur of focal seizures and movement phenomena (hemichorea-hemiballism), while still searching for an underlying structural focus that the metabolic derangement is unmasking.
- Apply Hickam's dictum when a single diagnosis won't fit a comorbid patient — treat coexisting contributors (seizure, hyperglycemia, stroke, retinopathy-related Charles Bonnet) in parallel and embrace the ambiguity, letting follow-up adjudicate rather than forcing premature closure.
