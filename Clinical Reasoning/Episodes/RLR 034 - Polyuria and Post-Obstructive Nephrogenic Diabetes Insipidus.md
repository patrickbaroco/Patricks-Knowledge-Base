## Summary

> [!summary] Scan Summary
> A 79-year-old man with a chronic indwelling Foley (placed weeks earlier for [[Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia|BPH]] urinary retention that had itself caused confusion) is brought in for five hours of [[Altered Mental Status|confusion]] — but the labs (WBC 30k, creatinine 0.8→2.4, anion gap 21) prove the [[Illness Tempo|disease is older than the symptom]]. Rabih reframes the tempo from hyperacute to acute, which swaps the differential from intrinsic-brain causes to the extrinsic [[MIST Mnemonic|MIST]] infections, makes the leukocytosis the [[Center of Gravity|center of gravity]], and runs the "six common infections" schema to the urinary tract. The [[Law of Proportionality|AKI out of proportion]] to the man's hemodynamics flags a post-renal cause: the Foley isn't draining (600 cc on bladder scan). Replacing it, plus ceftriaxone, resolves the confusion fast; cultures grow [[Klebsiella|Klebsiella]] in urine and blood — a [[Catheter-Associated UTI|catheter-associated UTI]] with bacteremia that behaves like [[Ascending Cholangitis|cholangitis]] (obstruction under pressure), improving as quickly as it declared. Then the "main dish": days of massive dilute urine (6–11 L/day, specific gravity 1.001). Rabih works [[Polyuria|polyuria]] as DM-or-DI, and since the glucose is far too mild, it's [[Diabetes Insipidus|diabetes insipidus]]. A tension follows — [[Base Rate|base rate]] and an incidental pituitary finding on MRI pull toward *central* DI, but a slam-dunk nephrogenic trigger sits right there: [[Post-Obstructive Uropathy|post-obstructive uropathy]]. Intact anterior-pituitary hormones, no bitemporal hemianopsia, and the [[Test of Time|test of time]] (plus stopping the output-matching fluids driving a solute diuresis) confirm **nephrogenic DI from post-obstructive downregulation of aquaporins** — with the 1.7 cm [[Pituitary Macroadenoma|macroadenoma]] an incidental, non-functioning [[Anchoring Bias|red herring]].

## Pearls

- **The time course of the *disease* is not the time course of the *symptom report*.** Five hours of confusion with a WBC of 30k and a creatinine tripled from baseline cannot have evolved in five hours — the process was brewing (Aaron Berkowitz's [[Analogical Reasoning|pen rolling off the table]]). Reframing the tempo from *hyperacute* (which skews to intrinsic-brain causes — stroke, seizure, acute hydrocephalus, hypoglycemia, opioids) to *acute* opens the extrinsic [[MIST Mnemonic|MIST]] infections. ([[Illness Tempo|tempo]] as the master variable.)
- **A leukocytosis of 30k routes you to six common infections that hospitalize people** — respiratory, urinary, skin/soft-tissue, hepatobiliary, C. diff, and bacteremia without a source — and get blood cultures when the count is that high. Here the [[Center of Gravity|dominant finding]] and the schema converge on the urinary tract.
- **A Foley being *in* does not mean it's *working*, and a [[Catheter-Associated UTI|catheter-associated UTI]] rewrites the symptom set.** Work *up* the catheter (obstruction → suprapubic tenderness → flank pain) rather than hunting dysuria/frequency; an [[Law of Proportionality|AKI out of proportion]] to the patient's hemodynamics and intake points to a post-renal obstruction, so bladder-scan the Foley. Like [[Ascending Cholangitis|cholangitis]], obstruction under pressure seeds bacteremia — and unclogging it resolves everything as fast as it came.
- **[[Polyuria|Polyuria]] (>3 L/day off diuretics) is DM or DI — split them with the glucose and the specific gravity.** Hyperglycemia far too mild to explain a massive *dilute* output means [[Diabetes Insipidus|diabetes insipidus]]; a serum sodium that isn't low argues against psychogenic polydipsia.
- **[[Post-Obstructive Uropathy|Post-obstructive uropathy]] causes nephrogenic DI.** Beyond the classic solute (urea/sodium) diuresis, obstruction *downregulates [[Aquaporin|aquaporin]] expression* at the collecting duct (with a distal/hyperkalemic RTA next door), so ADH — even when released — has no channels to act on; the dilute urine (specific gravity 1.001) is the tell it's more than a solute washout, and stopping output-matching IV fluids stops iatrogenically feeding the diuresis.
- **When the sequence of events and an incidental image disagree, weigh the base rate and don't anchor on the scan.** [[Central Diabetes Insipidus|Central DI]] is more common and more abrupt, and this man had a [[Pituitary Macroadenoma|pituitary macroadenoma]] on MRI — but intact anterior-pituitary hormones, no bitemporal hemianopsia, and a slam-dunk nephrogenic trigger make it nephrogenic (a [[Copeptin|copeptin]] or water-deprivation test could formally split them). Keep [[Hickam's Dictum|AND, not OR]] in view — with that adenoma he could harbor both.

## Problem Representation

A 79-year-old man with HFpEF, poorly controlled diabetes (A1c 13), BPH, and a chronic indwelling Foley — placed weeks earlier for urinary retention that had itself presented as confusion, with an incidental MRI showing a prominent sella — is brought in for five hours of confusion, feeling "terrible," afebrile and hemodynamically stable, with a WBC of 30k (neutrophil-predominant), sodium 131, bicarbonate 18 with an anion gap of 21, creatinine 2.4 (baseline 0.84), and a turbid urinalysis (nitrite-negative, leukocyte-esterase-positive, >100 WBC and RBC, many bacteria). A bladder scan shows 600 cc behind an obstructed Foley; replacement plus ceftriaxone rapidly clears the confusion, and urine and blood cultures grow Klebsiella (a catheter-associated UTI with bacteremia), with the leukocytosis, AKI, and anion gap normalizing within days. He then develops massive polyuria (6–11 L/day) with a specific gravity of 1.001, a urine osmolality of 179, only mild hyperglycemia, and a normal-to-high serum sodium — diabetes insipidus — and, against a base-rate and MRI pull toward central DI, a normal visual field and normal anterior-pituitary panel plus the temporal link to relieved obstruction and spontaneous resolution establish nephrogenic diabetes insipidus from post-obstructive uropathy (aquaporin downregulation), with a subsequently confirmed 1.7 cm non-functioning pituitary macroadenoma as an incidental finding.

## Case Narrative

Reza presents a case he says he *misdiagnosed*, opening on a confession that "sometimes thinking too much leads to unnecessary workup" — a nod to over-active System 2 reasoning, which Rabih answers with his tongue-in-cheek ER award for System 1 thinking. A 79-year-old man is brought by his daughter for five hours of confusion; he feels "terrible" but cannot elaborate, denies chest/abdominal pain and fever, and the daughter describes him being delayed and blank ("What?") when she asked about breakfast. Vitals are unremarkable (afebrile, normotensive, not in distress); the cardiac and lung exams are normal; he has a chronic indwelling Foley. Weeks earlier he had an identical episode of confusion, was found to have 800 cc of urinary retention, and normalized once a Foley drained the bladder — attributed to BPH, for which he was started on tamsulosin and finasteride and referred to urology; that admission's MRI (done because of aphasia) had shown a prominent sella with a convex superior border and a recommendation for a dedicated pituitary protocol. Now he is oriented to self and place but not date. Labs: WBC 30k (neutrophils 28k), sodium 131, bicarbonate 18 with an anion gap of 21, creatinine 2.4 (baseline 0.84 two weeks prior), lactate 1.3, BNP 197; the urine is turbid, nitrite-negative but leukocyte-esterase-positive, with >100 WBC and RBC and many bacteria. EKG and chest film are normal.

Rabih immediately connects it to the prior AMS case ([[RLR 033 - Persistent Altered Mental Status and Spinal Epidural Abscess|RLR 033]]) and makes the [[Illness Tempo|tempo]] the opening move: a WBC of 30k and a creatinine tripling could not have arisen in five hours, so **the disease is older than the symptom** — Berkowitz's [[Analogical Reasoning|pen that rolled off the table]] had been rolling for a while. This matters because a truly *hyperacute* AMS skews toward intrinsic-brain causes (stroke, seizure, acute hydrocephalus, hypoglycemia, opioid toxicity), whereas an *acute* encephalopathy over days points to the extrinsic causes of [[MIST Mnemonic|MIST]] (metabolic, infectious, toxic). He reframes the case as acute, and asks whether a finding *dominates the landscape*: it does — the [[Center of Gravity|leukocytosis]] — and the patient has already proven he can be confused by a urinary problem. A WBC of 30k triggers his reflex schema of the **six common infections** that hospitalize people (respiratory, urinary, skin/soft-tissue, hepatobiliary, C. diff, bacteremia without source); respiratory, hepatobiliary, and skin are unsupported, C. diff is a thought given the abdominal complaints, and the urine data make the urinary tract the source. He would draw blood cultures and — crucially — check that the Foley is *working*, because the [[Law of Proportionality|degree of AKI is out of proportion]] to the man's hemodynamics and intake, raising a **post-renal obstruction from a plugged catheter**; two bedside checks (watch the bag drain; bladder-scan) settle it. He predicts a complicated UTI from an obstructed Foley.

Reza adds the [[Anatomical Approach|catheter-anatomy]] teaching: a catheter connecting bladder to the outside world *removes* the usual urinary symptoms (dysuria, frequency, urgency), so a [[Catheter-Associated UTI|catheter-associated UTI]] declares itself by working *up* the catheter — obstruction first, then suprapubic tenderness, then flank pain — with extra-urinary inflammation less specific; the unifying twist is that the CA-UTI can *cause* the obstruction rather than an obstruction causing the UTI. The ED bladder-scans 600 cc behind the Foley, replaces it, gives ceftriaxone and fluids, and the confusion resolves before the medicine team arrives. Cultures grow **Klebsiella in urine and blood**, sensitive to ampicillin-sulbactam; the WBC falls 30k→13k and the creatinine 2.4→1.2 within 72 hours, normalizing by day five. Rabih draws the [[Ascending Cholangitis|cholangitis]] analogy: an obstructed catheter under pressure seeds bacteremia (Klebsiella in urine and blood), and the complications vanish as fast as they came once the obstruction is relieved.

Then the "main dish." After the Foley is replaced, the man pours out urine — 6.8 L day one, 8.7 L day two, 11 L day three, 8 L day four — net negative liters daily despite matching fluids, with a specific gravity that falls to 1.001, a urine osmolality of 179, a urine sodium of 56, a serum glucose of only 241 (in a diabetic with an A1c of 13), and a *normal-to-high* serum sodium (136). Rabih works [[Polyuria|polyuria]] (>3 L/day) as a clean fork: a **solute diuresis** (glucose, sodium, mannitol, urea dragging water out) versus a **water/solvent diuresis** ([[Diabetes Insipidus|diabetes insipidus]]). Off diuretics, polyuria is essentially DM or DI — and the glucose here is far too mild to explain a massive *dilute* output, with the very low specific gravity supporting DI; psychogenic polydipsia is argued down by the hospital setting and a serum sodium that isn't low. So this is diabetes insipidus.

The remaining fork — [[Central Diabetes Insipidus|central]] (ADH deficiency) vs [[Nephrogenic Diabetes Insipidus|nephrogenic]] (ADH resistance) — carries a real tension. [[Base Rate|Base rate]] favors central (more common, more abrupt, and this presentation looks acute), and the MRI's pituitary abnormality pulls the same way. But a slam-dunk *nephrogenic* trigger sits right there: [[Post-Obstructive Uropathy|post-obstructive uropathy]], which injures the distal nephron to produce both a distal (hyperkalemic) RTA and ADH resistance — and need not have caused AKI to do so. Rabih flags that the responsible move is to admit diagnostic uncertainty, that a [[Copeptin|copeptin]] (an ADH surrogate — low in central, high in nephrogenic resistance) or a water-deprivation-plus-vasopressin-challenge can split them, and, most importantly, to screen for **occult pituitary hormone deficiencies** (a low cortisol, a high potassium, hyperpigmentation would force an urgent [[Adrenal Insufficiency|adrenal insufficiency]] workup). Reza, initially anchored by the MRI, went to a visual-field test (normal) and a full hypothalamic-pituitary panel (TSH, free T4, FSH, LH, prolactin, ACTH, cortisol all normal). Rabih reframes: ADH is made in the hypothalamus and secreted by the *posterior* pituitary, so an intact *anterior* pituitary and no optic-chiasm compression drop the probability of central DI sharply — though a non-secreting adenoma or isolated central DI (trauma, gestational, genetic, ICU vasopressin-withdrawal) remain formally possible.

The resolution came from the [[Test of Time|test of time]] and the literature rather than more testing. Nephrology had proposed a water-deprivation test and vasopressin challenge, but Reza — puzzled by how dilute the urine was for a supposed solute diuresis — read up and learned the mechanism he'd been missing: post-obstructive uropathy *downregulates [[Aquaporin|aquaporin]] channel expression*, so even released ADH has nothing to act on (distinct from the urea/sodium solute washout). He also recognized that *matching* the urine output with IV fluids was iatrogenically sustaining a solute diuresis, so he stopped the fluids and waited. The urine osmolality climbed and output fell to ~1.5–2 L/day; the man was discharged, and a dedicated pituitary MRI later showed a **1.7 cm macroadenoma** — an incidental, non-functioning finding, not the cause. In his own [[Cognitive Autopsy|cognitive autopsy]], Reza names two traps: interpreting a datum he doesn't usually obtain in post-obstructive diuresis (the urine osmolality), and lacking the aquaporin-downregulation script, which let the MRI pull him toward central DI. Rabih's counterpoint is that running a *little* toward central was reasonable — nephrogenic DI is almost never acute except with diuretics or post-obstructive uropathy — and that the biggest lesson is [[Hickam's Dictum|AND, not OR]]: with a macroadenoma (the commonest risk factor for central DI) the man could genuinely have both, and expediting the pituitary evaluation was not wasted.

### Illness Scripts and Bias

- **[[Illness Tempo|Tempo divorced from the symptom clock]].** The headline move is refusing to accept "five hours" as the disease's age: a WBC of 30k and a tripled creatinine reset the tempo to acute, which *changes the entire differential* (intrinsic-brain → extrinsic MIST). Reading the pace from the physiology rather than the history is the case's opening lesson.
- **[[Schema-Based Reasoning|Schemas stacked on the leukocytosis]].** The six-common-infections schema localizes the source; the catheter-anatomy schema rewrites the expected UTI symptoms; and the polyuria DM-or-DI fork, then the central-vs-nephrogenic fork, structure the "main dish." Each converts a broad problem into a few moves.
- **[[Law of Proportionality|Proportionality]] as the post-renal flag.** The AKI is *out of proportion* to the man's hemodynamics and intake, which is exactly what promotes a post-renal obstruction over the assumed prerenal picture — the disproportion, not the raw creatinine, does the work; and the dilute urine is *out of proportion* to a pure solute diuresis, forcing the DI reframe.
- **[[Base Rate|Base rate]] vs the [[Anchoring Bias|incidental image]].** Central DI is the more common, more abrupt entity, and the MRI pituitary finding anchors toward it — but the sequence of events (relieved obstruction → polyuria) and the normal anterior-pituitary axis re-weight toward nephrogenic. The macroadenoma is the classic incidentaloma trap: a real finding that is not the cause.
- **[[Test Interpretation|Interpreting unfamiliar data, and knowing the mechanism]].** Reza's [[Cognitive Autopsy|autopsy]] is candid: a datum he doesn't usually obtain (urine osmolality in post-obstructive diuresis) plus a missing script (aquaporin downregulation) let the MRI mislead him; the fix was reading the literature and letting mechanism, not a single scan, drive interpretation.
- **[[Test of Time|Test of time]] and [[Hickam's Dictum|AND-not-OR]].** Rather than a water-deprivation test, the diagnosis was made by stopping the output-matching fluids (which were feeding a solute diuresis) and watching the urine concentrate — and both hosts hold that the man could still harbor *both* nephrogenic and (future) central DI given the adenoma, so expediting the pituitary workup was not wasted [[Diagnostic Humility|effort]].

## Teaching Discussion

- Read the tempo from the physiology, not the symptom report: a large leukocytosis and a big creatinine rise cannot be five hours old, so reframe a "hyperacute" confusion as acute — which shifts the differential from intrinsic-brain catastrophes to the extrinsic MIST infections and metabolic causes.
- Let a WBC of 30k trigger the six-common-infection schema (respiratory, urinary, skin/soft-tissue, hepatobiliary, C. diff, bacteremia without source), draw blood cultures, and localize with the available data — here the urinary tract, in a man who has already been confused by a urinary problem.
- Never assume an indwelling Foley is patent: catheter-associated UTI removes the ordinary urinary symptoms and declares itself by obstruction, suprapubic tenderness, and flank pain, and an AKI out of proportion to volume status should prompt a bladder scan for post-renal obstruction; an obstructed catheter behaves like cholangitis, seeding bacteremia and resolving fast on relief.
- Work polyuria (>3 L/day off diuretics) as DM or DI, splitting them with the glucose and specific gravity; a dilute urine with only mild hyperglycemia is diabetes insipidus, and a serum sodium that isn't low argues against primary polydipsia.
- Know that post-obstructive uropathy causes nephrogenic DI through aquaporin downregulation (and a distal/hyperkalemic RTA), distinct from the classic urea/sodium solute diuresis — and that matching urine output with IV fluids can iatrogenically sustain the diuresis, so stopping fluids and using the test of time can both treat and diagnose.
- Weigh base rate against an incidental scan: central DI is commoner and more abrupt, but intact anterior-pituitary hormones and an intact visual field make a pituitary cause unlikely even when an adenoma is present; use a copeptin (low in central, high in nephrogenic) or a water-deprivation/vasopressin-challenge when the split matters, and screen for occult adrenal and thyroid deficiency before attributing everything to the kidney.
- Hold AND, not OR: a patient with a pituitary macroadenoma and a post-obstructive kidney can have both central and nephrogenic mechanisms, so expediting the pituitary evaluation is legitimate even once the acute cause is nephrogenic.
