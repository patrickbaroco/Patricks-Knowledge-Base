A single vital sign or lab value is a snapshot; its trajectory across repeat measurements is data of a different kind.

## How it operates

- **A single reading can suggest one diagnosis; a trend can rule it out.** A static, high value might fit a benign explanation, while an actively worsening trend argues for a more dangerous or evolving process — and vice versa.
- **Repeat vitals cheaply generate high-value data.** Before ordering advanced imaging or invasive testing, a repeat set of vitals in an hour can meaningfully re-rank the differential.
- **Trends discipline against premature reassurance.** A single normal-appearing value should not be allowed to close a workup if the trajectory leading up to it is unknown.

## Examples from RLR episodes

- **Escalation over time redirects the workup.** A heart rate rising from 120 to 150, alongside temperature climbing from 101.5°F to 105°F, argues against a benign, self-limited process and away from a diagnosis (pulmonary embolism) that a single static reading had suggested — prompting broad-spectrum antibiotics and closer scrutiny of a urine source that a single early value might not have justified.
