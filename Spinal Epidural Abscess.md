Evergreen concept note. Spinal epidural abscess — a commonly missed, time-critical diagnosis. Teaching points cited per episode.

## Why it is missed

- **It is among the most commonly missed and most-litigated diagnoses in medicine.** The classic triad (fever, back pain, neurologic deficit) is insensitive, and the presentation is easily attributed to the more common causes of back pain. ([[RLR 033 - Persistent Altered Mental Status and Spinal Epidural Abscess|RLR 033]])
- **Non-contrast CT of the spine essentially cannot exclude it** — sensitivity ~15% (vs ~80% for [[Vertebral Osteomyelitis|vertebral osteomyelitis]]), so a "negative" spine CT is near-worthless for the abscess. Match the modality to the target: **contrasted MRI**. ([[RLR 033 - Persistent Altered Mental Status and Spinal Epidural Abscess|RLR 033]])
- **The neurologic exam is limited in an encephalopathic patient** — the cord signs can hide under the confusion; use reflexes and withdrawal-to-pain, and go back to the history (progressive back pain, new weakness) when there is no anchor. ([[RLR 033 - Persistent Altered Mental Status and Spinal Epidural Abscess|RLR 033]])

## Mechanism and urgency

- **Hematogenous seeding — including from pyelonephritis via [[Batson Venous Plexus|Batson's valveless plexus]].** The vertebral venous plexus links pelvic/renal drainage to the spinal epidural space, a reported route from severe [[Pyelonephritis|pyelonephritis]] to epidural abscess. ([[RLR 033 - Persistent Altered Mental Status and Spinal Epidural Abscess|RLR 033]])
- **Cord compression makes it a time-critical emergency** (see [[Spinal Cord Compression]] and [[Myelopathy]]) — urgent contrasted MRI and neurosurgical washout; culture typically grows the seeding organism (Klebsiella in the worked case). ([[RLR 033 - Persistent Altered Mental Status and Spinal Epidural Abscess|RLR 033]])
- **It can drive profound encephalopathy** that resolves after decompression — an unexplained but proven link in the worked case. ([[RLR 033 - Persistent Altered Mental Status and Spinal Epidural Abscess|RLR 033]])
