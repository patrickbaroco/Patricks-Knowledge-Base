Anasarca (generalized edema) and the NSRCA framework for its causes — Nephrotic syndrome, Systemic (cardiac/hepatic) causes, Renal failure, Cardiac failure, and hypoAlbuminemia broadly — a rough mnemonic scaffold for a bilateral lower-extremity-edema-plus-systemic-edema presentation.

## How it operates

- **NSRCA is a starting checklist, not a verdict.** Nephrotic syndrome, liver disease, renal failure, cardiac failure, and hypothyroidism/hypoalbuminemia are all screened with a basic panel (BMP, urine protein, TSH, troponin, exam for JVP) before the leap to "anasarca" is accepted.
- **Disproportionality argues against a true anasarca pattern.** True anasarca from any of the NSRCA causes should distribute roughly symmetrically; edema disproportionately severe in one region (the face, sparing the arms) argues for a localized process instead, even when some peripheral edema is also present.
- **A "soft" leap to anasarca should be named as soft.** When the fit isn't clean, say so explicitly rather than letting the convenient, broad label quietly close off a narrower, more urgent diagnosis.

## Examples from RLR episodes

- **A leap deliberately not taken.** Facial and mild lower-extremity edema, absent orthopnea/PND/DOE and with normal CBC, BMP, urine protein, TSH, and troponin, is tempting to frame through NSRCA — but because the arms are spared and the face is disproportionately affected, the leap to anasarca is explicitly called "soft," preserving space for the correct diagnosis, SVC syndrome.
