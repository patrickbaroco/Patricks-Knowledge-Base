Evergreen concept note. Definition plus worked examples, drawn from the RLR / Clinical Problem Solvers series and elsewhere. Source episodes are cited per example.

## Definition

The representativeness heuristic is judging how likely a diagnosis is by how well the patient matches its *prototype* — the textbook picture — rather than by its actual probability. It misleads in two directions: an *atypical trigger or presentation* hides a common mechanism (the case doesn't "look like" the diagnosis, so it's dismissed), and a *classic-looking* case is over-diagnosed despite a low base rate. Described by Tversky and Kahneman, it is the cognitive cousin of [[Base-Rate Neglect|base-rate neglect]]: the prototype, not the frequency, drives the judgment.

## How it operates

- **The prototype filters out the truth.** A mechanism is rejected because the trigger or demographic doesn't fit the remembered picture, even when the physiology is textbook.
- **Age and demographics used as false filters.** Treating "this doesn't happen in this kind of patient" as an exclusion removes whole categories that differ in *frequency*, not in kind.
- **It pairs with [[Illness Script Flexibility|script rigidity]].** A script held too narrowly (severity, host, histology) is exactly what makes an atypical-but-real case look unrepresentative.
- **Resisting it is deliberate.** The counter-move is to keep a category alive *because* the physiology fits, explicitly overriding the "doesn't look like it" reflex.

## Examples from RLR episodes

- **An atypical trigger hides a textbook mechanism.** Cataract surgery doesn't match the prototype of an immobilizing event, so immobilization hypercalcemia was dismissed — until "sequential bilateral cataracts → two couch-bound weeks" revealed the classic mechanism behind the unrepresentative trigger. ([[RLR 014 - Hypercalcemia and Immobilization|RLR 014]])
- **Age as a false filter.** Assuming an older adult isn't exposed to toxins, toxidromes, or STIs is a representativeness trap that would miss HIV, syphilis, and ingestions; Reza keeps a urine tox and a home-exposure hypothesis explicitly alive, noting everyone follows similar behavioral patterns, differing in frequency, not in kind. ([[RLR 024 - Confusion and Ertapenem-Induced Encephalopathy|RLR 024]])

## Counter-moves

- **Keep a category because the physiology fits, not because the patient looks the part** — override "doesn't look like it" when the mechanism is present ([[RLR 014 - Hypercalcemia and Immobilization|RLR 014]]).
- **Don't let demographics truncate the differential** — age and "type of patient" change frequency, not kind ([[RLR 024 - Confusion and Ertapenem-Induced Encephalopathy|RLR 024]]).
