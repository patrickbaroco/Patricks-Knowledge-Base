Evergreen concept note. An approach to blood in the urine — localize the lesion like a GI bleed, separate glomerular from non-glomerular sources, and pursue source control. RLR / Clinical Problem Solvers teaching points are cited per episode; general clinical detail is scaffolding.

## The GI-Bleed Analogy
Approach hematuria the way you approach a GI bleed: **find the lesion and get mechanical source control** ([[RLR 064 - Hematuria and Post-TAVR Perivalvular Leak|RLR 064]]). Most hematuria is an **anatomical lesion** — the worst is **cancer** (urothelial/bladder, renal, prostate), while the commonest are **infection**, bladder-wall irritation/trauma, and bleeding on a vulnerable prostate (Foley catheter plus BPH) ([[RLR 064 - Hematuria and Post-TAVR Perivalvular Leak|RLR 064]]). Urology usually localizes and controls it endoscopically; when they **cannot** stop it, you are by definition in unusual territory and should step back to interrogate a systemic contributor — the blood or the [[Bleeding Framework|bridge]] ([[RLR 064 - Hematuria and Post-TAVR Perivalvular Leak|RLR 064]]).

## First Distinctions
- **Gross vs microscopic.** Visible (gross) hematuria carries a higher malignancy yield; microscopic hematuria is defined by ≥3 RBCs/hpf on a properly collected specimen.
- **Confirm it is truly blood.** A dipstick positive for "blood" with **few or no RBCs** on microscopy points to **myoglobinuria or free hemoglobin**, not bleeding (see [[Free Hemoglobin vs RBC Hematuria]]) ([[RLR 064 - Hematuria and Post-TAVR Perivalvular Leak|RLR 064]]).
- **Image-positive vs image-negative.** Is there a visible lesion that won't stop, or none identified? This fork organizes the workup ([[RLR 064 - Hematuria and Post-TAVR Perivalvular Leak|RLR 064]]).

## Glomerular vs Non-Glomerular
The most useful mechanistic split:
- **Glomerular** — **dysmorphic RBCs**, **RBC casts**, and **proteinuria**; often brown/cola-colored and painless. Points to glomerulonephritis (IgA nephropathy, post-infectious, vasculitis, thin basement membrane, Alport). Route to **nephrology**.
- **Non-glomerular (urologic)** — isomorphic RBCs, clots, no significant proteinuria. Sources anywhere along the urinary tract: **malignancy**, stones, infection, BPH, trauma, catheter. Route to **urology**.

## Workup
- **Urinalysis with microscopy** — RBC morphology, casts, protein, signs of infection.
- **Upper-tract imaging** — **CT urogram** is the study of choice to evaluate the kidneys, collecting systems, and ureters (MR urogram or renal ultrasound when contrast is contraindicated).
- **Cystoscopy** — evaluates the bladder and urethra for lower-tract lesions; standard for gross hematuria and higher-risk microscopic hematuria ([[RLR 064 - Hematuria and Post-TAVR Perivalvular Leak|RLR 064]]).
- **Cytology / additional studies** as directed by risk.

## Reasoning Note
When a structural lesion is present but bleeding is **out of proportion** or refractory to source control, look beyond the lesion to a systemic bleeding diathesis (platelet/coagulation/vWF) and to non-bleeding mimics like hemolysis ([[RLR 064 - Hematuria and Post-TAVR Perivalvular Leak|RLR 064]]; see [[Law of Proportionality]]). Temporizing measures like [[Continuous Bladder Irrigation|continuous bladder irrigation]] prevent clot obstruction but do not treat the cause.
